Longue Marche Plates

Grothendieck sketched a finite diagram for the fundamental groupoid of the moduli space of five points on the sphere. Our run made it explicit: 542 objects and 1,620 arrows, carried into themselves by all 120 relabellings of the five points. These plates show the whole diagram, its special points, its pattern, and a model you can turn and take apart.

The five points: 0 1 2 3 4

Plate I

The whole diagram, seen down its five-fold axis

Every dot is an object of the diagram; every strand is an arrow. Nothing was placed by hand. The positions come from the diagram itself: the vibration modes of the graph (eigenvectors of its Laplacian) fall into representations of the relabelling group S5, and the first five-dimensional one keeps all nine kinds of object apart.

We project it onto the axis and the rotation plane of the five-cycle 0→1→2→3→4→0. The picture therefore has exact five-fold rotational symmetry and five mirror lines. The two pentagonal objects that this cycle fixes, the pentagon 0 1 2 3 4 and the pentagram 0 2 4 1 3, sit on the axis at the centre.

The loop turns the model by 72°, one fifth of a turn. That turn maps the diagram onto itself, so the end of the loop is its beginning.

Plate II

The three most symmetric ways to put five points on a sphere

The 62 large objects of the diagram are the special points of the moduli space of five points on the Riemann sphere: configurations that a rotation of the sphere carries to themselves while permuting the labels. Up to relabelling there are three, and each has a polyhedron for a skeleton.

Plate III

The pattern: nine kinds of object, fourteen kinds of arrow

Divide out the relabellings and the 542 objects collapse to nine kinds, the 1,620 arrows to fourteen families. This is the diagram as Grothendieck wrote it on scan 31. Each circle gives the kind, its stabilizer and its number of copies; each arrow its family and its number of copies, drawn thicker for more.

The large objects (column I) are the three special configurations of Plate II. Arrows run from column III towards column I. The τ-arrows stay inside a column.

The run corrected one count. Grothendieck wrote 14 orbites next to the arrows, and the scan already strikes out the word: there are fourteen families but 16 orbits, because γr1′ splits into three (B.12).

Plate V

The same diagram, laid out by a general-purpose program

Graphviz (sfdp, about one second) knows nothing about relabellings. It draws every object and every arrow, in the same colours, and none of the symmetry survives. Plate I uses the same data. There the layout is computed from the symmetry itself, and the five-fold structure appears.

Plate VI

How connected each level of the tower is

EXIT_TOWER computes exactly how connected the rank-k piece Pk(g,n) of the tower is, for every genus g and number of marked points n (D.45). With d = 3g−3+n:

In genus 0 and 1 it is the diagonal k−1. From genus 2 on, the line drops to slope two thirds, ⌊2k/3⌋, losing one step every three ranks, until rank 3g−4; from rank 3g−3 it climbs at slope one again, as k−g. From rank d on, the piece is a cone, so it is contractible.

The dashed line is the bound proposed in our prompt (its Lead 4, min(k, 2g−2+n) − 1). Where it runs above the solid line, it is false. The run refutes it at (g,n,k) = (2,1,3) and everywhere after (D.6).

Plate VII

The three relations that define GT, drawn as strings

An element of the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group GT̂ is a pair (λ, f): an exponent λ for every crossing of two strands, and a profinite word f(x,y) in two letters for every change of bracketing. Each strand carries one of the labelled points, coloured as everywhere on this page. The pair must make each polygon close up: going once around gives the identity.

(I)f(x,y) f(y,x) = 1re-bracket and back
(II)f(z,x) zm f(y,z) ym f(x,y) xm = 1, with xyz = 1 and m = (λ−1)/2the hexagon: three re-bracketings and three crossings
(III)f(x01,x12) f(x23,x34) f(x40,x01) f(x12,x23) f(x34,x40) = 1the pentagon: five re-bracketings of four strands

A bracketing of four strands is a tree with five coloured ends, the four leaves and the root. It is a sphere with five points pinched as far as it goes, into three pieces. So the pentagon of (III) is the boundary of one of the twelve real pentagons of the moduli space of five points, and at its centre sits the regular-pentagon configuration: an I10 object of Plate II. EXIT_TOWER E.39 claims that every such pair (λ, f) acts on the whole tower of moduli spaces. E.43 and E.44 claim that genus one adds no new freedom, and E.49 that the character Δ vanishes on GT̂. All four are the run's claims, not yet refereed.

Where it comes from

The diagram, its counts and the connectivity formula are statements B.12, B.25 and D.45 of our research run EXIT_TOWER (GPT-6 Astra, ChatGPT Pro mode), checkpoint 28, on Grothendieck's tower of moduli spaces (cotes 143 and 146–148). They are the run's own claims and have not been refereed yet.

What we checked

From the run's data file we recovered the action of the 120 relabellings on the 542 objects. It is pinned down uniquely by the arrows: every relabelling maps the 1,620 arrows, family by family, onto themselves. The stabilizers come out as in the run's table: D5, S3, C4, and a double transposition for the four C2 kinds.
