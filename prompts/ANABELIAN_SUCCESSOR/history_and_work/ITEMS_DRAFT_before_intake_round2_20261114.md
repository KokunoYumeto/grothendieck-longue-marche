## Conventions

`k` a finite field of characteristic `p`, `C/k` smooth proper geometrically connected of genus `g ≥ 2`,
`E_C^(p′) → G_k` the geometrically prime-to-`p` group, `s_P` the section of `P ∈ C(k)`. A *collision* is a pair
`P ≠ Q` in `C(k)` with `s_P`, `s_Q` conjugate under the geometric group. For a finite extension `L/k` and a
character `χ : J_C(L) → Z/n`, `D_χ → C_L` is the pull-back, by the Abel map based at `P`, of the quotient of the
Lang isogeny of `J_C` over `L` cut out by `χ`: a `Z/n`-torsor, geometrically connected when `χ` has exact order
`n`, with rational deck group and the rational point `(P, 0)` over `P`. `L_P(χ, T)` is its twisted `L`-function, a
polynomial of degree `2g − 2` for `χ ≠ 1`. `P_n(D) = im ∏_{ℓ∣n}(1 − σ^{n/ℓ}) ⊂ J_D` is the primitive part.

## Results this builds on (confirm each where used, or give the exact error)

- **R1** (AP-A.1, AP-A.2, AP-A.3; proofs followed by the intake). A collision gives, on every finite étale cover
  `D → C_L` with geometrically prime-to-`p` Galois closure and every `P′ ∈ D(L)` over `P`, a point `Q′ ∈ D(L)` over
  `Q` with `[Q′ − P′] ∈ J_D(L)[p^∞]`; it is the only point over `Q` with a `p`-primary difference; and the
  condition for all such covers is equivalent to the collision.
- **R2** (AP-A.C2.7; proof followed). If a deck transformation `δ ≠ 1` of odd order of a Galois cover as in R1 fixes
  `T = [Q′ − P′]`, then `D` is hyperelliptic, which a free automorphism of odd prime order excludes. So `T` has
  trivial stabilizer in every deck group of odd order.
- **R3** (AP-A.C2.19, AP-A.C2.20; proofs followed). If `C` has, geometrically, a connected cyclic étale cover of odd
  degree `n` prime to `p` with `f_p(P_n) = 0`, there is no collision on `C`. The proof: `A·T = 0` for
  `A = ∏_{ℓ∣n}(1 − σ^{n/ℓ})` gives a function of degree `2^{ω(n)}` with divisor in two free orbits; the field
  generated by its translates has a curve of genus one; `D` is a double cover of it; `C` is a ramified double cover
  of a genus-one curve `E₀`. For odd `p` that case is AP-A.29l; for `p = 2` the Deuring–Shafarevich formula gives
  `f_2(P_n) = r₁φ(n) > 0`.

## Leads (drafter; each is to be proved or refuted where an item uses it)

- **L1** (the rational-torsion test; expected true). *Let `P ≠ Q` collide on `C/k`. Then for every finite `L/k`,
  every odd prime `ℓ ≠ p` and every non-trivial `χ : J_C(L) → Z/ℓ`, `p` divides `#J_{D_χ}(L) / #J_C(L)
  = ∏_{i=1}^{ℓ−1} L_P(χⁱ, 1)`.* Reason: by R1, `T = [Q′ − P′]` is an `L`-rational `p`-primary point of `J_{D_χ}`;
  by R2, `(1 − σ)T ≠ 0`; it is a rational `p`-primary point of the Prym `im(1 − σ)`, whose number of `L`-points is
  the displayed quotient. The return used only the geometric form (`p`-rank zero) and wrote that the rational form
  "would require a new controlled-field endgame"; for prime degree none is needed. So a single `(L, ℓ, χ)` with
  that product prime to `p` proves that `P` collides with no point.
- **L2** (the same for composite degree; expected true). If `C` is not a separable double cover of a curve of genus
  one, a collision forces `p ∣ #P_n(D_χ)(L)` for every `χ` of odd order `n` prime to `p`: the endgame of R3 uses
  only `A·T = 0`.
- **L3** (the test as a count). By the congruence `L_P(χ, T) ≡ det(1 − TΦ ∣ H¹(C, L_χ))` modulo a prime over `p`
  (`Φ` the `L`-linear Frobenius), the test of L1 fails for `χ` exactly when `Φ` has the eigenvalue 1 on some
  `H¹(C, L_{χⁱ})`. In genus two this reads: for some `i`, `Σ_{x ∈ C(L)} χⁱ([x − P]) ≡ −1`, the sum taken in `F̄_p`
  through a reduction of `μ_ℓ`. If `p` is a primitive root modulo `ℓ`, a collision then forces, for
  `B = J_C(L)/ℓ` and `N_b = #{x ∈ C(L) : [x − P] ∈ b}`: `N_b ≡ N_0 + 1 (mod p)` for every `b ≠ 0`.
- **L4** (heuristic). For a given `χ` the test fails with probability about `(ℓ − 1)/p^f` over the primes above `p`
  (`f` the order of `p` modulo `ℓ`). So every `(C, P)` should have witnesses with small `ℓ`, and R6 should follow
  from point counts with `ℓ ≤ 13`.
- **L5** (towards a proof). With `ℓ^r = #B > #C(L)` and `p` a primitive root modulo `ℓ`, L3 says that every
  non-zero coset of `ℓ J_C(L)` contains a number of points of `C(L) − P` divisible by `p`. The pairs `x ≠ y` with
  `x − y ∈ ℓJ_C(L)` are at most `N²/ℓ^r + ((2g − 2)² − 1)#L` by the Weil bound, and at least `(p − 1)(N − N_0)`.
  For `p > (2g − 2)²` this is a contradiction as soon as such an `ℓ` exists for one `L`.
- **L6** (higher genus, geometric form). `f_p(P_n) = 0` if and only if, for every orbit `O` of multiplication by
  `p` on the primitive characters, the composite of the `#O` Frobenius maps around `O` is nilpotent on the
  `(g − 1)`-dimensional space `H¹(C, L_χ)`.

## Parts

**Part A. Point sections in the geometrically prime-to-`p` group (scans 35–36). The main question.**
- **A1.** Prove or refute: no smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus ≥ 2 over a finite field has a
  collision. With R1 and AP-A.8 state and prove the corollary for every hyperbolic curve over every field finitely
  generated over `F_p`, which decides his "Il est cependant possible que l'injectivité de (*) reste valable" and
  "Il se pourrait qu'on puisse en déduire `P = Q`". A refutation is a curve and a pair with the conjugacy proved in
  the whole group.
- **A2.** Prove or refute L1 and L2, with every field of definition and every twist written (the cover must have a
  rational point over `P`; a change of base point twists `χ`).
- **A3.** Prove or refute: for every `C/k` of genus ≥ 2 and every `P ∈ C(k)` there are a finite `L/k`, an odd prime
  `ℓ ≠ p` and a non-trivial `χ : J_C(L) → Z/ℓ` with `∏_i L_P(χⁱ, 1)` prime to `p`. With A2 this is A1. Attack it by
  L3 and L5; determine exactly which pairs `(p, g)` L5 settles, and close the others.
- **A4.** Compute the test of L1 for every genus-two curve over `F_4`, `F_8` and `F_16`, for every smooth plane
  quartic over `F_3` and `F_5`, and for every rational point of each: for each `(C, P)` the witness `(L, ℓ, χ)` of
  least `ℓ`, or the list of all `(L, ℓ, χ)` tried with their values. Compare with R6 class by class. These
  computations are tests of A2 and A3 and do not replace them.
- **A5.** Prove or refute the existence statement of R3 in the form: every curve of genus ≥ 2 over `F̄_p` has a
  connected cyclic étale cover of odd degree prime to `p` whose primitive part has `p`-rank zero; with L6, and for
  genus two and `p = 2` in the theta form of R5 and R8.
- **A6.** The criterion for a Galois cover with non-abelian group `G` of order prime to `p`: state and prove what
  replaces R3 and L1 for an element of `Z[G]` supported on few elements (R2 gives `(1 − δ)T ≠ 0` for every `δ` of
  odd order), and decide whether it reaches a curve that A3 does not.

**Part F. Schemes from their étale topoi (scan 52).**
- **F1.** Prove or refute: a field `k` of characteristic 0 has closed-point-preserving reconstruction of all
  finite-type schemes if and only if, for every finite extension `E/k`, no non-trivial automorphism of `k̄`
  centralizes `G_E`.
- **F2.** Decide reconstruction for a field of finite transcendence degree over `Q` with a henselian valuation of

- **F3.** Give the criterion: a property of `k`, proved necessary and sufficient, that contains R9. By AP-F.42 it

- **F4.** His experiment of scan 52: prove or refute, for a smooth proper curve `X` of genus ≥ 2 over a number
  field `K₀` with function field `K`, that every outer automorphism of `G_K` over `G_{K₀}` that preserves the

  `X`; and say what it gives for the functor of scan 52 over the base `S = Spec K₀`.
