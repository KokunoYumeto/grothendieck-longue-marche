4. Forest collapse, precisely scoped

The five retained edges form the disjoint union of the Cayley trees
of the right T cosets in G. Collapsing that forest gives vertex module
Z[G/T], identified with Z[R_A x R_B], and edge module Omega^16. On
chains, the map simply discards the five retained rows. The boundary
of an A edge at coefficient(u,v,t) is the edge from(u,v) to
(u tau_t(x_(1,i)),v); a B edge uses beta(t). These are exactly DE's
sixteen rows, with the same G coefficient, not merely the same
augmentation.

The forest is acyclic. More explicitly its boundary map is injective
and has image ker(Z[G]->Z[G/T]). Thus every16-cycle has a unique
finite retained-edge completion to a21-cycle: its uncollapsed
boundary has zero sum in each forest component, so it is the boundary
of a unique finite tree-chain. This proves the H1 isomorphism.
It does not assert a G-homotopy equivalence of the spaces, because
collapsed vertices acquire T stabilizers.

An arbitrary actual old/source map need not preserve this forest.
Its exact operation on16-cycles is consequently: uniquely complete
the retained forest, apply the full21 semilinear Fox map, and then
discard the retained rows. Omitting the completion would lose actual
mixed conjugation terms. The involution does preserve the forest.
