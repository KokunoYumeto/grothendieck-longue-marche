# A.43 working proof: the actual first central refinement is sufficient

**Status:** independently reviewed by the parent and incorporated as formal A.43. This file preserves the working proof; formal A.43 is authoritative. A.42 contains the all-primes local conjugacy corollary.

Use any finite perfect-family quotient from A.40 or A.41. Thus Pi=C2 amalg C3, R=[R,Pi], C=Pi/R, W=R intersect ker(q), and D0=Pi/W=M semidirect C, where M is a finite product of nonabelian simple groups and C acts through A5 by regular factor permutations. Put W1=[W,Pi], W2=[W1,Pi]. The already proved A.40 gives

    E1=Pi/W1 = M1 semidirect C,

where M1=R/W1 is finite and perfect, its map onto the centerless M is central, and its kernel Z1=W/W1 is central in all of E1. The C-action on M1 factors through A5. The positive automorphism psi1=id_(M1) semidirect h_C fixes actual tau and preserves the s/r classes, retaining the prescribed C-frames.

## 1. The second central refinement collapses

Set E2=Pi/W2 and M2=R/W2. The map M2 -> M1 is central, with kernel A=W1/W2 central in all of E2. As in A.40, the canonical image of the finite universal central cover of M1 is a finite perfect normal subgroup P of M2, and M2=P*A. Since R=[R,Pi],

    M2=[M2,E2] <= P.

Hence M2=P is finite and perfect.

Let Z0=W/W2, the preimage of Z1 in M2. Because M is centerless, Z1=Z(M1). The preimage of the center under a central extension of a perfect group is central: for z in that preimage, the commutator map g -> [z,g] takes values in the central kernel A and is a homomorphism on the perfect M2, hence trivial. Consequently Z0 is central in M2. It is a finite abelian group, with exact sequence

    0 -> A -> Z0 -> Z1 -> 0.

Conjugation on Z0 by E2 factors through C, because M2 centralizes Z0. In fact it factors through A5. For an element c in ker(C -> A5), choose an E2-lift of its pure section element in E1=M1 semidirect C. Conjugation by that lift induces the identity on M1. An automorphism of the perfect central extension M2 inducing the identity on M1 is the identity, since its difference is a homomorphism from M2 to A. Thus this lift centralizes M2 and hence Z0.

The resulting A5-action on Z0 is the identity on both A and Z1: A is central in E2, and Z1 is central in E1. The group of automorphisms of the abelian extension Z0 which induce the identity on both A and Z1 is abelian. Explicitly, each is z -> z+d(z mod A) for some homomorphism d:Z1 -> A, and composition adds d. Since A5 is perfect, its action in this abelian group is trivial. Therefore Z0 is central in E2.

It follows that the image of [W,Pi] in E2 is trivial. Thus W1 is contained in W2, and the opposite inclusion is automatic. We have proved the exact equality

    [[W,Pi],Pi] = [W,Pi].

In particular all further relative central commutators stabilize after this first step. This is an actual kernel equality, not merely a vanishing assertion for an arbitrarily selected module.

## 2. The negative automorphism already squares to one at E1

The genuine standard c preserves W and W1. Let c1 be its actual action on E1. Define b1=psi1*c1. Its action on C is beta_C, so its square is the identity on C. On M1 its action is c1 restricted to M1, because psi1 is the identity there. Thus b1 squared is also the identity on M1. Both c1 and b1 invert actual tau, so b1 squared fixes tau.

An automorphism theta of E1 which is the identity on M1 and on the quotient C is determined by a central homomorphism d:C -> Z(M1). To see this, compare theta(i(c)) with i(c) for the chosen C-section. Their ratio centralizes M1 because theta fixes M1 pointwise, and hence lies in Z(M1)=Z1. This center is central in all of E1, so the ratio is an ordinary homomorphism. Now C^ab=Pi^ab=C6, since R=[R,Pi], and its abelianization is generated by tau_C. If theta also fixes actual tau, then d(tau_C)=1, so d is trivial.

Apply this to theta=b1 squared. It follows that b1 squared is the identity on E1. Its negative multiplier and retained Delta=1 make its actual outer image a nonstandard involution at this refined level.

## 3. A full ordinary lift retains this refined involution

Since W1=[W1,Pi], the central refinement required by A.3 for the quotient E1=Pi/W1 is E1 itself. The peripheral automorphism psi1 therefore passes the exact central criterion directly. Its full ordinary lift h1 can be normalized to fix tau and retain the C-frames, exactly as in A.40. Then a1=h1*c induces b1 on E1, of order two and parity one. This still does not assert a1 squared is trivial in the full group.

## 4. Compatibility for all the finite families

The automorphism psi1 is independent of the section chosen in the construction: it is uniquely characterized by fixing M1 pointwise, inducing h_C on C, and fixing actual tau. Indeed the ratio of any two such automorphisms is a central homomorphism C -> Z1 and the tau condition kills it, as above.

When a finite family J is enlarged, [W_J,Pi] decreases. The higher refined action therefore projects to the uniquely characterized lower refined action. Thus the family of refined positive automorphisms, and hence the negative involutions, is compatible.

Alternatively apply compactness directly to the full retained-frame solution sets for all finite unions J. Each is nonempty by step 3; finite intersections contain the set for the union; all conditions are closed exactly as in A.41. One full negative a of parity one consequently induces an involution on

    Pi / intersection_J [W_J,Pi],

while retaining the original C-frames. This strengthens A.41 through every actual scalar central refinement of the specified semisimple family.

## Scope of the remaining obstruction

The proof handles central kernels by their actual perfect cover and the perfect A5 action. It does not extend to a general noncentral elementary-abelian layer, where automorphisms of the kernel need not be a Hom group with trivial action and where the relevant C2 norm class can survive. It gives no cofinality statement and no full solution of the nonabelian norm equation.
