## Why an exact word solver recovers the geometric coefficient

An actual transported node word is an automorphic conjugate of a
commutator of two distinct free-basis meridians. The cyclically reduced
four-letter commutator is not a proper power. Thus its centralizer in
the generic free group is precisely the cyclic subgroup generated by
that word. Two conjugators producing the same oriented physical-mate
identity differ by a power of the mate relator. That relator is killed
in the actual quotient G. Consequently both conjugators have the same
G coefficient.

This uniqueness is applied after the geometric comparison establishes
the single-face map. It would not, on its own, rule out adding a
two-cycle to an arbitrary algebraic lift of the one-chain action.

Applying F twice gives a conjugator in that same cyclic centralizer.
Its G image is trivial, and the two orientation signs multiply to one.
Hence the signed semilinear face action has literal square one. The
conjugate-relator Fox identity supplies the boundary compatibility with
the full twenty-one-row action. These checks can therefore verify all
128 finite node faces without changing the geometric definition.
