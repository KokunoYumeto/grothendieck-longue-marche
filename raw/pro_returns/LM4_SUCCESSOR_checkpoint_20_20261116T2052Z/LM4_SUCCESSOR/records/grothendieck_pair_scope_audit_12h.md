# Grothendieck-pair route: hypothesis audit

Research record, 16 November 2026. This is not a counterexample to the
centralizer statement in task B.6. We searched for an actual residually
finite, good group with the required finite virtual cohomological and
cohomological finiteness properties. None of the examples below has
been verified to supply that complete input.

Bridson and Reid (2015, Theorem B, Theorem 6.1, and its proof in
Section 6.3) construct a finitely generated subgroup N of a
two-dimensional, virtually special hyperbolic group with the same
profinite completion, while H2(N,Q) is infinite-dimensional. The proof
is an actual Rips construction, not an inference from an abstract
isomorphism of completions. This example cannot simply inherit all
cohomological finiteness assertions of its ambient group. In
particular, finite generation of N and residual finiteness do not
establish its goodness. The same paper's finitely presented examples
in Theorem 4.1 also require a separate goodness audit; finite
presentation alone is insufficient. We make no claim here that every
Grothendieck-pair construction fails goodness.

Bridson (2024, Theorem A and Sections 2--3; author version dated
11 December 2023) constructs free-by-free groups of discrete
cohomological dimension two whose full completions can have much
larger cohomological dimension or torsion. For the explicit family
using Gamma=Z^d, d>=2, the completion has cohomological dimension d+1.
These particular examples are not good: goodness and discrete
cohomological dimension two would force continuous cohomology with
every finite coefficient module to vanish in degrees above two.
Thus this source supplies a concrete reason not to use arbitrary
free-by-free Grothendieck pairs in task B.6. Its infinite-rank free
kernel is not interchangeable with a finitely generated free kernel.

Jaikin-Zapirain and Lubotzky (2025, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2)
give representation-extension and Grothendieck-rigidity statements.
Those statements do not assert that an arbitrary proper member of a
Grothendieck pair is good. We do not infer finite-coefficient
cohomological equivalence in every degree merely from their
representation-extension conclusion.

Bridson and Reid (2025, Theorem A and Remark 7.4) give further
Grothendieck pairs inside products of hyperbolic three-manifold
groups, including the Weeks example. The paper distinguishes its
finitely generated constructions from the unresolved finitely
presented case in the Weeks product. We found no theorem there
supplying all of the goodness and finiteness hypotheses needed for
our proposed finite-subgroup-centralizer counterexample.

The missing step is concrete: construct a finite nonsolvable action
on an eligible discrete group, prove all hypotheses, identify an
actual completed fixed element, and prove it is outside the closure
of the discrete fixed subgroup. A dense proper subgroup, a failure
of subgroup separability, or an isomorphism of completions alone
does none of these four jobs. The actual icosahedral source work
continues independently of this bounded literature search.
