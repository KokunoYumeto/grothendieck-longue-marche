   Historically, the Coleman–Ihara formula arose from Ihara’s study of his universal
power series for Jacobi sums [Iha86]. From the coefficients of the logarithm of this
power series, Ihara obtained certain characters and local formulae relating them to
Coates–Wiles homomorphisms and Kubota–Leopoldt p-adic L-values. Combined with
Coleman’s identification of these characters with the restrictions of Soulé characters,
these formulae yield the comparison now known as the Coleman–Ihara formula, whose
proof relies on Coleman’s explicit reciprocity law for Hilbert norm residue symbols
[Col81, Iha86]. In subsequent work, Anderson, Coleman, and Ihara–Kaneko–Yukinari
developed explicit global formulae in this setting [And89, Col89, IKY87]. Although
these global formulae reveal deep connections with Iwasawa theory and the arithmetic
of cyclotomic fields, the Coleman–Ihara formula itself is essentially local: it describes
the restriction of a Soulé character to the image of the local reciprocity map. This local
viewpoint has led to several generalizations. Nakamura, Sakugawa, and Wojtkowiak
proved polylogarithmic analogues, replacing Soulé characters and Kubota–Leopoldt p-
adic L-values by p-adic polylogarithmic characters and Coleman p-adic polylogarithms,
respectively [NSW17a, NSW17b]. Burns and Sano formulated a higher-rank analogue
using Euler systems and generalized Stark elements, which recovers the classical formula
over Q and gives a conjectural extension to totally real fields [BS25].

   We prove the theorem by adapting the local method of Nakamura, Sakugawa, and
Wojtkowiak [NSW17a] to the CM elliptic setting. Namely, we compare two expressions
for a Hilbert norm residue symbol. The first arises from the Kummer property of ellip-
tic units and yields the elliptic Soulé character. The second is obtained from Coleman’s
explicit reciprocity law and is expressed in terms of Coleman power series. To identify
the resulting expression with the product of the two-variable p-adic L-function and the
two-variable Coates–Wiles homomorphism, we compare the Coleman power series aris-
ing on the cyclotomic and elliptic sides via an isomorphism between the multiplicative
formal group and the formal group of E.

the paper. In Section 3, we introduce the elliptic Soulé characters and review their basic
properties. Section 4 recalls the Coleman-theoretic ingredients needed for the proof:

procity law, and the two-variable Coates–Wiles homomorphisms and the two-variable
p-adic L-function. Finally, in Section 5, we combine these ingredients to prove the main
theorem and verify its compatibility with the formula obtained in [IG26].

Grössencharacter ε of infinity type (k, j), in the notation of [dS87, II.6.4], is
                                        k+j+1          ε̌(σ−δ )
                       Lp (ε) = dK N f 2 W p-adic (ε)            Lp (ε̌),
                                                         ε(σδ )
where f denotes the prime-to-p part of the conductor of ε. In particular, when f = (1),
the product of the factors multiplying Lp (ε̌) on the right-hand side is equal to 1. Hence,
                                         Lp (ε) = Lp (ε̌),
which is consistent with Katz’s functional equation (17).
