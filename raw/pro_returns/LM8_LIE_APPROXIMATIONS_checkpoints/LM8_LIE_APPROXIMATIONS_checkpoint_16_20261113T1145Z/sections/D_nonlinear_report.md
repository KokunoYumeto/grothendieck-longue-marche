# Nonlinear positive-dimensional-base audit: D.12–D.16

## Outcome and scope

The manuscript's unrestricted quasi-compact-base positive-power theorem is **refuted** by D.14. The later D.17–D.21 supplement proves the corrected assertion over every quasi-compact quasi-separated base for separated finitely presented groups, including all Noetherian and all affine bases. The distinction is visible on the original manuscript: scan 10 only requires a quasi-compact base, and the separatedness insertion modifies the group scheme; scan 11 then restricts the attempted argument to a Noetherian integral base. Exact original pages and their image concordance are preserved in `source_checks/140-4`.

The counterexample has a one-dimensional, quasi-compact, non-quasi-separated base obtained by doubling `Spec((∏_{p≥5} F_p[ε]/ε²)[z])` along the open disjoint union of its prime-indexed components. Glue additive groups using `α_p(t)=t+ε(t^p−t)`. These automorphisms fix every integer and therefore glue the section 1, but a global homomorphism has bounded polynomial degree on the two affine charts. A larger prime not dividing the proposed exponent forces both chart maps to be `mt`, which is incompatible with the gluing. This proves failure for every positive power. Multiplication by the elliptic curve `y²w=x³−xw²` gives a genuinely nonaffine separated smooth finitely presented group with connected geometric fibres. The relatively affine version is already a counterexample to the literal quantifier. The base is not affine, so no claim that every ring-base formulation is false is made.

## Positive results

**D.12: common dilation and descent.** The equalizer of two additive parameters into a separated locally finitely presented group is finitely presented and closed. Vanishing at all integers, followed by the Vandermonde adjugate identity, annihilates its finite set of polynomial coefficients by one integer. Consequently the parameters agree after a common dilation. This makes a local map descend along a single faithfully flat quasi-compact covering morphism and proves that local positive-power existence glues on quasi-compact quasi-separated bases. The actual overlap compactness is indispensable. In characteristic zero, or over a Z-flat base, the undilated map is unique.

**D.13: every finite-characteristic base.** If a quasi-compact base is killed by one nonzero integer, every fibrewise quasi-unipotent section is torsion. Constructible compactness supplies a uniform fibrewise order. The corresponding finitely generated equalizer ideal is nilpotent; its successive square-zero layers are killed by the base characteristic. The group is allowed to be nonflat and nonaffine, and the base need not be quasi-separated. This extends far beyond `Z/p^e`.

**D.15: Q[t] and nonlinear thickened curves.** Over a characteristic-zero DVR, a generic additive homomorphism whose value at 1 is an integral section extends uniquely. Nagata compactification first extends the map near the generic special point. Some integer section then meets that domain and proves that the image enters the original open group. Integer translates cover the special affine line because the residue characteristic is zero. Translated local maps agree generically and hence glue by separatedness. Spreading and uniqueness globalize this over every integral Noetherian regular one-dimensional Q-scheme. No flatness, smoothness, or affineness of the group is imposed.

Weil restriction along the finite free universal homeomorphism `S[ε]/ε^r → S`, with its successive vector-group kernels, extends the conclusion to standard nilpotent thickenings. The genuinely nonflat ring `Q[t,ε]/(ε^r,tε)` is also settled: it is the fibre product `Q[t] ×_Q Q[ε]/ε^r`; the parameters on the two closed pieces agree by characteristic-zero uniqueness and glue via the fibre product of their structure sheaves. This last step does not incorrectly assume that a nonflat extension admits the same Weil restriction argument.

**D.16: integer localization and arithmetic curves.** The mixed-characteristic problem admits a stronger argument. For a Noetherian local domain R and a nonunit integer n, start with a parameter over R[1/n]. Choose an affine identity chart V. Its inverse-image open contains the entire zero section, so its complement ideal contains a polynomial f with f(0)=1. Pullbacks of finitely many coordinates are a_i/f^k, whose constant terms are integral. Replacing the parameter by n^N times itself clears all n-denominators and makes the new F congruent to 1 modulo n. Thus D(F) contains every fibre over V(n), and it glues with the old map over R[1/n] to cover the entire affine line. Injectivity into the localization verifies the equations; separatedness and generic density verify the group law and the value at 1.

This local lemma globalizes by finite-presentation spreading and a finite cover. If S is integral Noetherian and its rational fibre S_Q is regular of dimension at most one, D.15 first supplies a parameter over S_Q, then finite-presentation descent supplies one over S[1/n], and the integer-localization lemma extends a further power over S. Positive generic characteristic is already torsion. Consequently all DVRs, all Dedekind bases, Spec Z[t], and its localization at (2,t) are settled, without affine or flat hypotheses on G. Standard finite free nilpotent thickenings of these bases are settled as well. This proof explicitly starts from R[1/n]; it does not incorrectly suppose that arbitrary fraction-field denominators in higher dimension are integer denominators.

## Completion of the base argument and model scope

D.17–D.21 settle the former next tests `Spec Q[s,t]_(s,t)` and `Spec Z[s,t]_(2,s,t)`, then all Noetherian and all quasi-compact quasi-separated bases. Rational interpolation first removes denominators over arbitrary Noetherian domains, even without finite normalization. Saturated primary filtrations and closed patching handle nilpotents and reducible bases. Finite relation, group-law, commutation and value errors extend the integer-localization argument to every local ring. Constructible images of finite-presentation parameter witnesses reduce arbitrary Q-algebras to Noetherian models. The proofs are in `D_surface.tex`; no rational-surface or non-Noetherian affine case remains listed as unresolved.

The actual smooth-model question occurs on scan 213, folio 754, and concerns the preceding factorization and character conditions (45)–(49). D.22 proves its coherent adelic version using a stable global lattice, schematic graph closure and the exact group-smoothening theorem of Bosch, Lütkebohmert, and Raynaud (1990, §7.1, Theorem 5). The stronger whole-M integral-point recap on scan 213 is corrected in D.23: an extended character would take integral points to {±1}, contradicting the already required open multiplier image. Conditions (55)–(64) are introduced later as supplementary hypotheses and then used in a definition; no universal construction obligation is inferred from them. D.4 and D.10 refute two proposed automatic consequences. D.9 and its ledgers now state this proved scope clarification.
