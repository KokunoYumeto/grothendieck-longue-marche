## Additional proposed notes (one to four sentences each)

### J08 — unpursued implication: two different quotients

**Location:** 57:275–277, especially 277 Corollary 1; **context:** C44:2 Th.3 and C44:8, with CN `D08`; **bears on:** WG2 §2.3 #12 and WG2 §2.4 #4 (both unchecked). The current quotient `Pic/Pic^τ` is described as “non ramifié” on 277, whereas C44:2 asks for an abelian-variety structure on algebraically trivial cycles modulo the proposed Albanese relation. Their shared use of representability and quotient language is a methodological link, but conflating the two quotients would erase the different numerator, denominator and geometric target.

### J09 — own idea: separate Picard dimension from its tangent dimension

**Location:** 57:285, 287–288; **context:** C44:2 Th.2 and C44:8 realization claim, CN `D09`–`D10`; **bears on:** WG2 §2.3 #12 (unchecked). Use the displayed Igusa example as a control case for any attempt to deduce the C44 dimension bound from `H¹(O)`: 57:285 records `dim H¹(O_X)=dim B+dim A` but `dim Pic_X=dim B`. **Confidence: high** in this diagnostic distinction because both dimensions are explicitly juxtaposed in the frozen text; it does not prove the bound, identify a reduced Picard component in every characteristic, or resolve the C44 realization conjecture.

### J10 — unpursued implication: the family problem is additional data

**Location:** 57:288, 309(f), 311, 313–315; **context:** C44:8’s fixed-variety `J^i` and C44:4’s fibration margin, CN `D12`; **bears on:** WG2 §2.4 #4 and WG H9 (unchecked). The warning “la formation de Pic variété n’est pas compatible avec la spécialisation” on 288 makes a relative extension of the C44 programme a separate problem, not an automatic consequence of a construction over a single field. The later questions about continuity, separation and properness sharpen that issue; they do not contradict the fixed-fibre statements in C44 because the hypotheses and objects have changed.

### J11 — own idea: test the correspondence construction first in its stated Picard case

**Location:** 57:283–284 and 313, last paragraph; **context:** C44:9, its final proposed test `T=Pic⁰(Y)` and its divisor correspondence `D=r_*(p^*z·q^*z)`, CN `D13`; **bears on:** WG H9 and WG2 §2.3 #12 (unchecked). A concrete comparison could start with the Picard parameter space explicitly offered by C44:9 and use the relative correspondence question on 57:313 (“Oui, par Raynaud ...”) to formulate the family of divisor correspondences precisely. **Confidence: medium** because the two source passages name compatible kinds of parameter objects, but existence of a correspondence space supplies neither the needed kernel quotient nor positivity, so this is a research route rather than a proof.

### J12 — unpursued implication: polarization language does not supply positivity

**Location:** 57:280–281; **context:** C44:2 Th.4, C44:8–9 and CN `D13`; **bears on:** WG H9 (unchecked “Believe”). The current change from classes of relatively ample line bundles to a cone of polarizations can serve as a target criterion for the divisor class constructed in C44:9. What remains to be shown is precisely that this particular class lands in the ample cone with the intended sign; the current discussion does not perform that calculation and does not upgrade the hunch relating Th.4 and Th.5.

### J13 — own idea: p-divisibility is an integral warning, not a height formula

**Location:** 57:289–290; **context:** C44:4 Th.5 and C44:9’s “modulo le facteur 2” qualification, CN `D12`–`D13`; **bears on:** WG H9 (unchecked) and Hua, findings register, `57-ns-p-divisibility-generization` (an AI-produced lead). The section whose special-fibre class becomes p-divisible while the global class does not suggests testing any integral normalization of a relative correspondence before dividing its class or transferring it between fibres. **Confidence: medium** as a proposed stress test because the source supplies the divisibility phenomenon, but it does not identify that section with the C44 correspondence or prove that the same obstruction occurs in its height construction.
