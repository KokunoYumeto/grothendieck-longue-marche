## More specific patterns within the broad categories

- Scans 061–063: an arrow crosses a table divider. Array-cell placement in both witnesses can preserve the named source and target yet change the visible relation to the ruled table. Read the rules and arrow together, not as independent decorations.
- Scans 060 and 062: both witnesses complete mathematical punctuation that the source leaves incomplete or mismatched. In 060 an opening subscript parenthesis has no visible mate; in 062 a point-set closes with a parenthesis and has no comma before the group. These are retained as written, with uncertainty where needed.
- Scans 058 and 061: an insertion connector can run above a live word or end in a hook resembling punctuation. Identify its continuous path before treating it as a strike or a comma.

- Scans 072–073: a long descender from the preceding line meets an accent position. In072 both independent arms establish a real hat; in073 the neighboring inserted M retains a hat/no-hat doubt. The crossing alone does not establish cancellation or the absence of a hat.
