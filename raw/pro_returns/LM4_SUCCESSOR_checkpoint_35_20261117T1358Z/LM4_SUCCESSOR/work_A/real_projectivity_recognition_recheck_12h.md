## A sharper elementary countermodel for the weaker hypotheses

Let D2 be the infinite pro-two dihedral group Z2 semidirect C2,
and put E0=C2 amalg D2 in the FULL profinite category. Then E0
is real projective, has exactly three involution classes, has
self-centralizing involutions, has minimal generator number three,
has abelianization C2^3, and is generated by three involutions.
These statements follow from the explicit dihedral action, the
full free-product torsion theorem, and the real-projectivity
criterion. The torsion-free orientation kernel is

  F0 = Z2 amalg Zhat,

by open Kurosh. For every odd prime p, H1(F0,Fp) has dimension one,
so F0 is NOT Fhat2.

This E0 cannot decompose as (C2 amalg C2 amalg C2) amalg P with a
projective remainder P. The three cyclic factors already account
for all of H1(E0,F2), so the orientation character kills P. The
retraction onto their full free product would then retract F0 onto
Fhat2. This contradicts the odd-prime H1 dimensions1 and2.

This is only a countermodel to the weaker recognition shortcut.
It expressly fails the full-Fhat2 orientation-kernel hypothesis
of the main question. A21 is complementary: it retains a full
Fhat2 orientation kernel but has only one involution class.
Neither countermodel settles the combined sharp hypotheses.

## What a full decomposition would actually imply

Suppose the desired group E=Fhat2 semidirect C2, with Eab=C2^3
and three involution classes, were shown to decompose as

  E = (C2 amalg C2 amalg C2) amalg P.

The orientation character kills P, since its mod-two abelianization
has no room beyond the three displayed cyclic factors. Retraction
onto W3 therefore induces a surjection from the original full Fhat2
to the torsion-free index-two subgroup Fhat2 of W3. Hopficity makes
this map an isomorphism. Since P lies in its kernel, P=1. This
would establish an abstract orientation-preserving E≅W3 and hence
an unmarked full Aut(Fhat2)-conjugacy of the selected involution to
the standard one, after permuting/refactoring the three torsion
classes as needed.

It would still require a separate argument to make that conjugator
peripheral for the actual named x,y,z. We have not identified a
full-category theorem proving the displayed decomposition under the
sharp hypotheses, and we do not conflate unmarked recognition with
that marked transfer.

## Final relative-degree check

The standard modular reflection extension has the presentation

  (C2 × C2) amalg_{C2} S3.

In a hypothetical full negative extension, the local s-normalizer
and r-normalizer supply the two finite vertex groups. A pure
transporter between the appropriate regular sections can align
their common involution, producing a homomorphism from this
reflection amalgam. This observation does not yet produce a map
of the specified peripheral Poincare duality pairs. Its source
cusp maps to the product formed from the aligned vertex-group
images. Requiring that product to lie in a conjugate of the
ACTUAL target cusp subgroup is precisely the missing third-cusp
condition. Without that inclusion there is no relative degree
for the two prescribed pairs to which the existing duality
argument can be applied. CE independently confirmed this typing
obstruction. No generation claim follows from local incidence,
the three section classes, or Euler characteristic alone.

## Thirteenth-hour finite-centralizer and accessibility recheck

The primary author version of Herfort and Zalesskii (2008), Theorem1,
was reopened at
https://mat.unb.br/~pz/arquivos/vfreepaper3.pdf . Its hypothesis is
explicitly **virtually free pro-p**. Section5, Lemma10, supplies the
full-profinite counterexample `S3 amalg_C2 S3`: every nontrivial torsion
centralizer is finite, but no finite group is a free factor. This example
has order-three torsion and therefore does not refute a special theorem
under our full index-two, torsion-free orientation hypothesis. The
pro-p proof uses a pro-p HNN embedding, a p-adic permutation-lattice
theorem, and the pro-p subgroup Kurosh theorem; replacing its final
category by the full profinite category is not justified.

Herfort, W., & Zalesskii, P. A. (2008). Virtually free pro-p groups whose
torsion elements have finite centralizer. *Bulletin of the London
Mathematical Society, 40*(6), 929–936.
https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/bdn070

A recent relevant primary source is:

Wykowski, J. (2026). Profinite subgroup accessibility and recognition
of amalgamated factors. *Groups, Geometry, and Dynamics, 20*, 1071–1097.
https://doi.org/10.4171/GGD/851
Publisher full text: https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/53276

Its Theorem1.1 proves that an accessible closed subgroup is the kernel
of a continuous derivation; it does not prove the converse for general
profinite groups. Theorem1.2 assumes an abstract finitely generated
virtually free ambient group. Section2.1, printed1079, explicitly
distinguishes arbitrary profinite tree actions from actions with finite
quotient graph: the latter give the required graph-of-groups structure,
whereas in general a spanning tree or its lift can fail. The general
structure theorem subsequently cited there is pro-p. Thus this new
source does not remove the missing realization step in our argument.

### Exact sufficient realization input

Suppose that our actual group `E=Fhat2 semidirect C2` admitted a proper
finite graph-of-finite-groups presentation (equivalently, a profinite
tree action with finite stabilizers and finite quotient in the applicable
Bass–Serre structure theorem). Every finite subgroup of E injects into
the orientation quotient, so all nontrivial vertex and edge groups
would be C2. Contract any nonloop nontrivial edge, whose endpoint map
is then an isomorphism. A remaining nontrivial loop would make its
infinite-order stable letter centralize C2, contradicting the already
proved self-centralizer theorem. Hence all edges become trivial.

The result would be `E=(amalg_{i=1}^k C2) amalg Fhat_b`. The actual
three torsion classes force k=3. Open Kurosh gives orientation-kernel
rank `k+2b−1=2`, hence b=0. This is a complete conditional abstract
recognition argument, and is the same reduction already used in A21.

## Augmentation-module route: exact unproved step

For the three involution classes, a tempting module is

    R = ker( direct_sum_i Zhat[[E/C_i]] → Zhat ).

Its restriction to the index-two full free subgroup has the rank
suggested by the three-reflection free product. This numerical fact
does not identify R with two copies of the completed group ring, and
does not produce generators with the required marked cusp product.
Any use of relative projectivity to prove an appropriate projectivity
statement for R would also require its exact module-category hypotheses;
we have not promoted that intermediate assertion here.

In particular, self-duality of R is not established. Dualizing the
displayed kernel sequence produces a cokernel presentation for its
dual, rather than another copy of the kernel. The existing peripheral
duality theorem pairs absolute and relative cohomological objects; it
does not identify them without an additional map. The natural finite
permutation-module pairing can have a radical on the augmentation
kernel in modular characteristic. Consequently a rank or character
calculation on permutation coefficients cannot silently remove an
ambiguity between distinct dual or Galois-conjugate simple summands.

CE independently confirmed this limitation in the thirteenth-hour
review. We record it as the boundary of this attempted module proof,
not as an identified complete obstruction to recognition. Neither
freeness of R nor a proper marked amalgam epimorphism has been proved.
What is missing is the **finite-quotient tree realization**, not another
torsion centralizer computation.

Haran's embedding supplies a restricted action on a profinite tree;
it does not supply that finite quotient graph. Nor does the finite
number of torsion classes by itself justify replacing its quotient by
a finite core. We found no applicable full-category theorem upgrading
these hypotheses. This statement records the outcome of the bounded
literature check, not a claim that no such theorem could ever be proved.

Even a successful abstract recognition would not identify the images
of the three specified source cusp subgroups. The separate marked
amalgam/third-cusp gate described above remains necessary.
