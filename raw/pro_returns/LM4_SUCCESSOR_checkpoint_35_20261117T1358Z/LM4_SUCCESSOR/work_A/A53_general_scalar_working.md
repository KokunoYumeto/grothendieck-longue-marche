# General two-step scalar stabilization over the modular group

Status: reviewed elementary derivation, superseded by the stronger formal A.53 and A53_general_abelian_working.md. No equivariant or negative-norm conclusion is included. The proof concerns actual iterated relative central refinements.

## Theorem

Let Pi=C2 amalg C3, let R be a closed normal subgroup satisfying R=[R,Pi], and put C=Pi/R. Let W be closed normal in Pi, contained in R, such that V=R/W is a finite elementary-abelian2 group. Regard V as a finite continuous C-module and assume

    H1_cont(C,V^vee)=0.

Put W0=W and W_(i+1)=[W_i,Pi], with all commutators closed. Then

    W3=W2.

The groups Z_i=W/W_i for i=1,2,3 are finite elementary-abelian2 groups. They are the derived subgroups of M_i=R/W_i and are central in M_i. Let H be the finite image of C on V, and K=O^2(H), the kernel of its maximal2-group quotient. Their actions factor through H/K, which is trivial or C2. More precisely,

    Z1=Z2/(s-1)Z2,
    W1/W2=(s-1)Z2.

Thus a genuine second scalar layer corresponds exactly to the regular F2[C2] summands of Z2. The scalar refinement is immediate if that action is trivial. No claim is made that Z2 equals a prescribed entire exterior-square quotient; it is an actual quotient of (wedge^2 V)_K.

## 1. The finite action head and the coinvariant condition

The finite head H is an actual quotient of Pi. Every2-group quotient of H kills r and is generated by the image of s of order at most2. Hence H/K is trivial or C2. Relative perfectness gives V_H=0. Taking K-coinvariants leaves a finite F2[H/K]-module with zero coinvariants. If H/K=C2 and T denotes its generator, then T-1 has square0; a module equal to (T-1) times itself is therefore0. Thus

    V_K=0.

This conclusion does not require semisimplicity, an odd normal subgroup, or an irreducibility assumption.

## 2. The abelianization induction, with finite-quotient rigor

Write E_i=Pi/W_i, M_i=R/W_i, and A_i=W_(i-1)/W_i. The new A_i is globally central in E_i. Relative perfectness descends to M_i=[M_i,E_i]. We prove inductively that M_i^ab=V for i=1,2,3.

Here is the exact finite argument. Choose a finite quotient of E_i retaining the prescribed V quotient of M_i. Such quotients are cofinal: since V is finite, a fixed open normal subgroup can be chosen whose intersection with M_i lies in the kernel of M_i->V; intersecting any other open normal subgroup with it preserves this condition. The induced finite quotient of E_(i-1) also retains V, because an element projecting into M_(i-1) already lies in M_i.

Assuming M_(i-1)^ab=V, the kernel A' of the finite new abelianization B->V is precisely the image of the NEW globally central A_i. Thus A' is a finite trivial C-module. It is irrelevant that an older center may no longer be globally central. The dual exact sequence is

    0 -> V^vee -> B^vee -> (A')^vee -> 0.

Relative perfectness gives (B^vee)^C=0. The stated H1(C,V^vee)=0, through the invariants exact sequence, forces (A')^vee=0. Therefore B=V. Cofinality proves the global assertion M_i^ab=V. Notice carefully that the hypothesis is cohomology of the INFINITE retained C, not merely H1 of its finite action image H.

Consequently Z_i=W/W_i is precisely the closed derived subgroup M_i' whenever this induction is applied.

## 3. The first center

The kernel Z1=A1 is globally central. Thus M1 is nilpotent of class at most2, with abelianization V and derived subgroup Z1. The commutator pairing is alternating and F2-bilinear: squares of lifts lie in the center, so every commutator has order at most2. Hence Z1 is a quotient of wedge^2 V and in particular finite elementary abelian. Its C-action is trivial.

## 4. The second center

At the second stage, [Z2,M2] lies in the new globally central A2. Thus gamma4(M2)=1 and [Z2,Z2]=1. The commutator therefore descends to a C-equivariant pairing

    Z1 tensor V -> A2.

Both Z1 and A2 are trivial C-modules, and V_H=0; the pairing vanishes. Thus Z2 is central in M2. As in the first stage, M2^ab=V makes Z2 an elementary-abelian quotient of wedge^2 V. Its action consequently factors through the finite head H.

The exact sequence 0->A2->Z2->Z1->0 has trivial H-modules on both ends. The image of H on Z2 is therefore a2-group (in a compatible basis it consists of upper triangular unipotent block matrices). It factors through H/K, so Z2 is K-trivial. This also proves that it is a quotient of (wedge^2 V)_K.

## 5. The third center and the exact relative commutators

The same abelianization induction gives M3^ab=V. The possible central commutator descends to

    Z2 tensor V -> A3.

The old Z2 and the new A3 are K-trivial, whereas V_K=0. The pairing vanishes. Hence Z3 is central in M3, elementary abelian and a quotient of wedge^2 V, with action factoring H.

The filtration 0<A3<Z3 with quotient Z2 has only trivial H composition factors. Thus the finite action image is a2-group, again factoring through H/K<=C2. Write I=T-1, with I=0 when that quotient is trivial. In either case I^2=0 on Z3. Relative commutators inside this actual abelian kernel give

    W1/W3 = I Z3,
    W2/W3 = I^2 Z3 = 0.

Therefore W2=W3. Using Z2 now gives the two claimed exact formulas for Z1 and W1/W2. Every F2[C2]-module is a direct sum of trivial one-dimensional and regular two-dimensional modules, so the regular summands are precisely the source of the possible second scalar step.

## Pronilpotent-base corollary

Let K0 be any closed normal subgroup of Pi and R=R_nil(K0). A34 gives R=[R,K0], and N=K0/R is a normal pronilpotent subgroup of C=Pi/R. For ANY W as above with V=R/W elementary2, the cohomology hypothesis is automatic.

Indeed V_N=0. Write N=N2 times Nodd, the product of its pro-two Sylow factor and all odd Sylow factors. These are actual closed normal subgroups of C, not just subgroups of a finite head. The finite module U=V_Nodd is acted on by a finite2-group, and U_N2=V_N=0. The finite2-group Nakayama argument gives U=0. Hence (V^vee)^Nodd=0.

Let z:C->V^vee be any continuous1-cocycle. Its restriction to the pro-odd Nodd is a coboundary, by finite averaging: a continuous cocycle into a finite module factors through a finite odd quotient after taking the core of an open subgroup on which both the action and cocycle vanish. Subtract this coboundary, so z vanishes on Nodd. For n in Nodd and g in C, normality and the cocycle identity imply

    n z(g)=z(ng)=z(g(g^-1 n g))=z(g).

Thus all z(g) lie in the zero Nodd-invariant subspace. Therefore z=0 and H1(C,V^vee)=0. The theorem applies and proves W3=W2 for every such finite elementary2 residual layer over the relative-pronilpotent base.

The corollary need not automatically apply to an arbitrary enlarged base whose residual is known only to be relatively perfect with Pi; there the conditional theorem requires its H1 hypothesis to be checked, as in A49–A52. The theorem also does not prescribe a negative involution, make the finite head action equivariant, or solve a nonabelian norm equation. It bounds the actual scalar refinement required once those data are specified.
