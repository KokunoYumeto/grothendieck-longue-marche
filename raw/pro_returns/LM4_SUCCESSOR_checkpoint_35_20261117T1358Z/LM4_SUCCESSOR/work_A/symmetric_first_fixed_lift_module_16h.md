## 2. The actual first obstruction module

Pull the diagram of spaces back to the universal cover Xtilde of X. The
one-sided covers Ytilde,Vtilde are connected, since their fundamental groups
map onto Q. Their groups R_A,R_B are free: they are the kernels of the
actual free-fiber filling F13->F5. Since Y and V are aspherical, these
covers are K(free,1)'s and are homotopy equivalent to graphs.

The intersection Ztilde is connected and has group K. Their union is Xtilde
minus all Q-lifts of the64 nodes. Xtilde is contractible. Relative excision
in real dimension four gives H3(union,Z)=Z[Q]^64 and all reduced homology
in degrees1,2 equal to zero. Mayer--Vietoris consequently gives

    H2(Ztilde,Z)=Z[Q]^64,
    H1(Ztilde,Z)=R_A^ab direct_sum R_B^ab.                (2)

The first isomorphism carries each local linking three-sphere to its local
linking two-torus, up to a consistent orientation. The involution exchanges
the two open sets; on these H2 classes the Mayer--Vietoris sign is minus
the permutation of nodes.

The Hopf map H2(Ztilde,Z)->H2(K,Z) is onto. The five-term homology sequence
for 1->M->K->R_A times R_B->1, together with (2) and freeness of R_A,R_B,
therefore gives

    C:=M/[M,K]
      = (R_A^ab tensor_Z R_B^ab)/(the actual local node tensors). (3)

Here [M,K] is the subgroup generated by the displayed commutators; it is
normal in Gamma and stable under F. The ordinary group H2 of the product
of the two free groups is their tensor product of abelianizations.

Codimension-two excision identifies R_A^ab with the free abelian group on
the connected components of the inverse image of A in Xtilde. Each such
component is a lift of an actual section A_i->B, so its group injects in Q
and its lift is contractible. The same holds for B=F(A). A local node tensor
is exactly the positive basis tensor of its two intersecting components.
Repeated intersections repeat that same basis relation and introduce no
extra coefficient. Thus (3) is the free abelian group on pairs of lifted
A/B components which do not intersect.

Under the involution the basis indexed by (C_A,C_B) is sent to the negative
of the basis indexed by (F(C_B),F(C_A)). The minus sign also follows directly
by applying F to a cross commutator and reversing the two arguments.

## 3. No fixed-index disjoint pair survives

Choose the lift Stilde0 of S0 through the actual basepoint in Xtilde.
Because pi1(S0) injects into Q by B87, Stilde0 is its simply connected
cover. The chosen lift of F fixes it pointwise, by unique path lifting.
The pullback of S is Stilde=Stilde0 minus its actual intersection points
with lifted A components. This is the covering of S corresponding to the
source filling kernel, so its image in R_A is exactly I.

Its H1 is generated by the small meridians of these intersection punctures.
Each maps to the meridian basis vector of the A component it meets; all
intersections are transverse. Surjectivity (1), and the meridian basis
from excision, force EVERY lifted A component to meet Stilde0. If C_A is
such a component and x is one such meeting point, then x=F(x) belongs to
both C_A and F(C_A). Hence C_A and F(C_A) are not disjoint.

A basis pair in (3) can be fixed by the index permutation only when
C_B=F(C_A). The preceding paragraph excludes exactly those pairs. Every
basis orbit therefore has two elements. Its signed-swap module is a free
Z[C2]-module after changing the sign of one basis element. We obtain

    H^1(C2,C)=0.                                           (4)

The coboundary solution is integral and explicit. On an orbit choose e,e'
with F(e)=-e' and F(e')=-e. A cocycle has equal coefficients a on e,e',
and equals m-F(m) with m=a e. Summing over its finite support gives an
actual discrete m. There is no division by2 and no claim of a compatible
continuous orientation of all completed basis orbits.

This is a result about the discrete actual module. No conclusion about an
arbitrary quotient of its full module completion is being imported.

For comparison, for a hypothetical disjoint matched pair and a meridian a,
the word a F(a) lifts the corresponding diagonal meridian; its cocycle
(a F(a))^-1 F(a F(a)) maps, up to sign, to that singleton basis tensor.
It would give the nonzero sign-module class modulo2. Actual (1) rules out
all those singleton indices, rather than merely testing selected meridians.

## 4. Discrete fixed lifting in this quotient

Let Gamma2=Gamma/[M,K] and E=G1 times_Q G2. The F-stable exact sequence

    1->C->Gamma2->E->1

has abelian kernel C. For any F-fixed target d and lift x, the element
x^-1 F(x) lies in C and is a C2 cocycle. Equation(4) lets one multiply x
by a suitable element of C to make it F-fixed. Therefore

    Gamma2^F -> E^F is onto.                              (5)

This does not assert the analogous map Gamma^F->E^F is onto.

## 5. The actual compact fixed-lift conclusion by density

The compact counterpart of (5) can be proved WITHOUT identifying the whole
completed coefficient module with a profinite permutation quotient. Use
B89's actual isomorphism hat E=hat G1 times_(hat Q) hat G2, and identify G2
with G1 by F. Then E^F is the diagonal copy of f^-1(H_b), where f:G1->Q;
B87 identifies hat E^F with the diagonal copy of hat f^-1(bar H_b).

The discrete f^-1(H_b) is dense in that compact preimage. Its closure
contains ker(hat f)=bar R_A. Its image in hat Q is bar H_b, since it
contains all actual H_b lifts and compact images are closed. A closed
subgroup containing the full kernel and projecting onto bar H_b equals
the full preimage of bar H_b.

The image of (hat Gamma2)^F in hat E is compact, hence closed, and by (5)
it contains the dense image of E^F in hat E^F. It therefore equals hat E^F.
By B89, completion of Gamma2 is the actual quotient

    hat Gamma / [mathcal M, mathcal K_s]_closed,

because closures and closed commutators agree by density; mathcal M is
also the actual compact intersection. Thus every actual compact fixed
target has a fixed lift after centralizing the intersection relative to
the kernel over Q. No assertion H^1(C2,mathcal C)=0 is needed or made.

## 6. Remaining obstruction

The defect for a chosen lifted fixed element is now confined to [M,K]
(or its actual closure). Iterating this statement would require new
arguments about the succeeding nonabelian layers; none is supplied here.
The original problem still requires fixed lifting through M itself and
the projected fixed-source equality. This calculation explains why the
first abelianized intersection cannot provide a missing dyadic norm, in
agreement with the inherited internal normal-generation theorem. It is
not a new finite census or a claim that solvable tests decide the problem.
