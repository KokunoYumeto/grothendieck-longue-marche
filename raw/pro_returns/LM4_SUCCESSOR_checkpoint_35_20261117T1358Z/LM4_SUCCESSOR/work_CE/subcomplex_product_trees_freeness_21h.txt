Subcomplexes of a product of two trees: the exact free-group lemma
CE research note, checkpoint21. This is an abstract lemma, not an
identification of the actual mixed kernel M with such a subcomplex.

1. Statement in the actual countable scope

Let T_A and T_B be countable simplicial trees, with their ordinary
one-dimensional CW structures. Give T_A x T_B its product square
structure with the weak topology of that square CW complex. For
trees of infinite valence this topology convention is explicit;
no different, coarser product-space topology is being used.
Let X be any cubical subcomplex. Then every connected
component of X has free fundamental group.

More precisely, if A subset A' and B subset B' are finite subtrees,
then each component of X intersect (A x B) maps injectively on pi1
as a free factor of the component of X intersect (A' x B') which
contains it. Components can merge; the assertion for one component
uses a basepoint in that component. This relative assertion is the
step that upgrades local freeness to actual freeness.

2. One relative leaf strip

Suppose A' is obtained from A by adding an edge e=[p,l], with p in A
and l a new leaf. Keep a finite B fixed, and put

  X_0=X intersect (A x B), X_1=X intersect (A' x B).

Every new square is e x f for some edge f of B. Its outer edge
{l} x f belongs to exactly that one square of X_1: e is the only
A' edge at l. It is not in X_0. Distinct new squares have distinct
outer edges. Thus each square and its outer edge are an elementary
free-face pair, and all these pairs can be collapsed relative to
X_0. Shared horizontal edges and endpoints do not change this:
none is the chosen free face, and no collapse adds another square.

After these finite elementary collapses, the remaining new cells
are a graph consisting of horizontal rungs, outer vertical edges
that belonged to no square, and their vertices. Its intersection
with X_0 consists only of vertices. An inner vertical edge of a
new square was already in X_0 because X is a subcomplex. Hence
no purported new graph edge is secretly shared with X_0.

This proves that X_1 is homotopy equivalent, relative to X_0, to
X_0 with a graph attached at vertices. The same proof works with
A and B interchanged.

3. The fundamental group consequence, including disconnected stages

Let Y be any CW complex and attach a graph at some of its vertices.
In each resulting component, van Kampen with a maximal forest in
the graph of old components gives

  pi1(new component) = (* pi1(old components in it)) * F(J).

The displayed inclusions are the actual inclusions, after choosing
paths to one basepoint; the group of the component containing a
specified unchanged basepoint is a free factor by its based
inclusion. This can also be seen by contracting a maximal tree in
the graph of components. It does not require the old component
groups to be free.

Apply this fact after the relative leaf-strip collapses. A finite
subtree extension can be made one leaf edge at a time, because each
component of the added edges is a rooted finite tree. First extend
A to A', then B to B'. This proves the precise relative assertion
in section1. Starting with vertices shows that every finite
rectangle intersection has free component groups. Alternatively,
its squares can all be collapsed by successively removing outer
leaf strips; any already exposed hanging graph edges do not prevent
a later square from having its designated outer free edge.

4. Countable exhaustion: why this is not merely a local argument

Choose roots and increasing finite subtrees A_n and B_n exhausting
both countable trees, adding finite paths as necessary. Include the
two coordinates of a chosen base vertex of X from the start. Let
C_n be the component of X intersect (A_n x B_n) containing that
vertex. Then C_n subset C_(n+1), and every pi1(C_n) is a free factor
of pi1(C_(n+1)). Choose a free basis at stage0 and extend that same
basis at each succeeding stage. The union of these bases is free.

Every based loop and every based null-homotopy in X has compact
image contained in a finite subcomplex, hence in some rectangle.
It follows that pi1 of the chosen component is the direct limit
of these based groups. Its free basis is exactly the compatible
union above. A filtered union of arbitrary free groups need not
be free; this proof uses the established free-factor inclusions.

Countability includes the actual Cayley trees of the countable
free groups R_A and R_B. No local finiteness or finite vertex degree
is assumed. No assertion about a non-CW subspace is made.

In fact every component is homotopy equivalent to a graph. Each
higher sphere and each choice of its basepoint lies in a finite
rectangle component, which collapses to a graph by section3. Thus
all higher homotopy groups vanish. A wedge of circles realizing
the compatible free basis maps to the component and is an
isomorphism on every homotopy group. The CW Whitehead theorem gives
the asserted homotopy equivalence. This is stronger than freeness
alone and is still only a statement about genuine subcomplexes.

5. Exact implication and current missing comparison

If one produces an actual based group isomorphism from M to pi1
of a cubical subcomplex X of the actual product trees, the lemma
proves M free. Such a comparison is not supplied by the lemma.
The actual C_E cannot itself be homotopy equivalent to one of
these subcomplexes: B105 proves a nonzero actual rank-two spherical
block in H2(C_E), whereas a graph has zero H2. Thus the viable
comparison is at the level of pi1/Tietze moves. It must allow
redundant two-relations to be removed or added, with their effect
on the two-complex accounted for separately, for example by the
corresponding higher cells. A direct homotopy equivalence of the
unchanged actual two-complex is already excluded.

The existing relative node complex is not automatically such an X.
Its attaching maps are the complete transported nonlinear node
words, with the actual coefficient section cocycle omega and
involution defect eta. They cannot be replaced by their abelian
two-chain supports. In particular, attaching one disk along the
outer boundary of two adjacent squares kills only the product of
their two independent graph loops; filling both squares kills each
loop separately. The interior graph edges already exist. Thus the
two operations generally give different fundamental groups even
though the outer boundary is the sum of the square boundaries.

A sufficient new gate would be a compatible graph homotopy/Tietze
comparison of the ENTIRE translated node family with selected
unit-square boundaries, retaining all same-pivot consistency and
allowing redundant relations. The128 individual primitive proofs
do not provide such a comparison. The earlier B100 cube relations
show that requiring full normal independence would be unnecessarily
strong; redundant relations can carry real spherical classes while
the quotient group remains free. No simultaneous primitivity,
RAAG presentation, actual M freeness or kappa vanishing is concluded.

6. Review and provenance

The proof was derived directly from elementary relative square
collapses. DE independently checked the finite-rectangle/free-factor
argument before this note was saved; a complete written independent
review is requested. No finite-depth computation, new node orbit,
or outside theorem about the actual complement is used.
