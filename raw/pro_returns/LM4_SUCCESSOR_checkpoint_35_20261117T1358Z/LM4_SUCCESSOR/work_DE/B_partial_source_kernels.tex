\statement{B.82}{Proved}{Actual partial-source images and complete partial filling kernels in two ordered free factors}
The two ordered families in B.81 have actual partial-source kernel images.
We specify all operators generating these images, exhibit a rank-eight
free factor whose two partial filling kernels are contained in the
corresponding source images, and compute the entire selected-family
intersection with an ordered rank-nine free factor. The same statements
hold for actual compact images in full completions. They do not prove
normality of a partial-source image in the entire fiber.

\begin{proof}
\emph{The actual partial maps and all source operators.}\par
Choose the ordered lift
\(v_*=1+\sqrt{99/100},\ w_*=1-\sqrt{99/100}\) of the B.81 source
basepoint. In a fixed \(q\)-fiber the ordered coordinate is \(v\), with
\(w=q/v\). Its group is \(F_{13}\), with punctures
\[
 0,\ \infty,\ \sqrt q,\ -\sqrt q,\quad
 v=u,\ v=q/u\quad(u\in\mu _5).
 \tag{B.82.1}
\]
Let \(N_m\) be the kernel of filling the four moving points
\(v=q/u\), \(u\ne1\), and \(N_c\) the kernel of filling the four
constant points \(v=u\), \(u\ne1\). Each actual quotient is \(F_9\).
Their product is the whole ordered filling kernel \(R\), with quotient
\(F_5\). The same product assertion holds for the closed kernels:
products of compact normal subgroups are closed.

On the source \(T=1\), the ordered-cover character is the winding of
\(1-q\) modulo two. It is odd precisely on \(\alpha _1\).
The actual ordered source is \(F_{19}\); filling one family gives
\(F_{15}\), and filling both gives \(F_{11}\). Define
\(J_m,J_c\) to be the images of these two actual partial-source
kernels. They are normal in the whole ordered source image \(H\),
and in particular in \(I\), the image of the source kernel filling
both families. We have
\[
 J_m\le N_m,\qquad J_c\le N_c,\qquad
 I=J_mJ_c,\qquad \alpha _1(J_m)=J_c.
 \tag{B.82.2}
\]
The source fillings are free retractions. Consequently their completed
kernels are the closures of their discrete kernels, and their compact
images are the actual closures of \(J_m,J_c,I\). This establishes the
same normality and product assertions in the specified completions.
It does not identify a source image with an intersection merely because
both maps have been completed.

The based family names can be checked geometrically. Along each actual
source-crossing whisker, the larger ordered root is
\(v=1+\sqrt{1-q}\), with the principal branch continued from the
basepoint. At \(q_u=2u-u^2\), \(u\ne1\), it gives
\(v=2-u=q_u/u\) and \(w=u\). The whiskers stay on the appropriate
side of the cut \([1,\infty)\), so this identifies the moving family
without an unbased arc choice. The actual crossing-count check confirms
that only \(\alpha _1\) changes the sheet. Thus all four \(V_j\) of
B.81 belong to \(J_m\), and \(\alpha _1\) exchanges the two ambient
and source family kernels.

Put \(\Gamma _0=\langle\alpha _0,\alpha _2,\ldots,\alpha _5\rangle\).
The eleven even old source operators are
\[
 \alpha_k,\quad \alpha_1\alpha_k\alpha_1^{-1}
 \quad(k=0,2,3,4,5),\qquad \alpha_1^2.
 \tag{B.82.3}
\]
The four original inverse source meridians are, literally,
\[
 w_4=V_4,\qquad w_1=V_1,\qquad
 w_2=\alpha_5^{-1}(V_2),\qquad
 w_6=\alpha_3^{-1}(V_6).
 \tag{B.82.4}
\]
Therefore \(J_m\) is exactly the subgroup generated by the orbit of
\(V_1,V_2,V_4,V_6\) under the eleven operators (B.82.3), the four
inner operators \(\operatorname{Inn}(\alpha_1(V_j))\), and all their
inverses. This follows from the Schreier free basis of the actual
\(F_{19}\) source: its four opposite-family meridians are the
\(\alpha _1\)-transports of the original four. Conjugation by the
moving subgroup itself adds nothing to a subgroup. In particular the
other-family source conjugations are not omitted or replaced by
arbitrary ambient conjugations.

\emph{An ordered free basis contained in the two source images.}\par\nopagebreak
Use the following Schreier marking of \(F_{13}\):
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 V_1,V_2,V_4,V_6,\quad W_j=AV_jA^{-1}\ (j=1,2,4,6),\\
 C,\quad E=ACA^{-1},\quad A^2,\quad P=BA^{-1},\quad Q=AB.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{B.82.5}
\]
Replace the four \(W_j\) by
\[
 O_1=\alpha_1(V_1),\quad U_2,\quad
 O_4=\alpha_1(V_4),\quad U_6.
 \tag{B.82.6}
\]
These lie in \(J_c\), with actual source witnesses. For \(O_1,O_4\)
this is the based family exchange. In the stored B.81 source-orbit list,
write \(r_i\) for its \(i\)-th word. The exact expressions are
\[
 U_6=r_1r_{48}r_1^{-1},\qquad
 U_2=(r_{21}r_{48})r_{36}(r_{21}r_{48})^{-1}.
 \tag{B.82.7}
\]
Here \(r_{36}=\alpha_1^{-1}(w_2)\) and
\(r_{48}=\alpha_1^{-1}(w_6)\) are actual constant-family source
meridians. All conjugators are in the ordered source image, which
normalizes \(J_c\). This proves source membership, not just membership
in an ambient family kernel.

The replacement is a literal Nielsen basis change. The identities
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 U_6=PW_6P^{-1},\qquad
 U_2=PW_6W_2W_6^{-1}P^{-1},\\
 O_4=h_4W_4h_4^{-1},\qquad O_1=h_1W_1h_1^{-1},
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{B.82.8}
\]
use
\[
 \begin{split}
 h_4&=C^{-1}V_6Q^{-1},\\
 h_1&=h_4W_4QV_6^{-1}Q^{-1}W_4^{-1}QV_2Q^{-1}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{B.82.9}
\]
First recover \(W_6,W_2\), then \(W_4\), and finally \(W_1\).
The exact checker verifies both inverse substitutions on all thirteen
generators. In particular the first eight new elements generate a
rank-eight free factor \(L_8\le I\). Normality in \(I\) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 J_m\cap L_8&=\ker(L_8\longrightarrow
                  \langle O_1,U_2,O_4,U_6\rangle),\\
 J_c\cap L_8&=\ker(L_8\longrightarrow
                  \langle V_1,V_2,V_4,V_6\rangle).
 \end{split}
 \tag{B.82.10}
\]
These maps kill the complementary four free generators. The upper
bounds are the restrictions of the actual ambient partial fillings;
the lower bounds use normality under \(L_8\). The free-factor
retractions and dense discrete filling kernels prove exactly the same
intersection formulas for actual compact images.

\emph{The entire selected-family kernel in the ordered \(D\)-block.}\par\nopagebreak
Put \(W=U*\langle D\rangle\), where \(D=BC\), and
\(W_e=W\cap F_{13}\). Its group is free of rank nine, with basis
\[
 U_j,\ DU_jD^{-1}\ (j=1,2,4,6),\qquad D^2.
 \tag{B.82.11}
\]
The whole \(D\)-kernel \(R_D=\ker(W\to\langle D\rangle)\)
is contained in \(I\) by B.81, and therefore normalizes \(J_m\).
The moving members in (B.82.11) are
\(U_1,U_4,DU_2D^{-1},DU_6D^{-1}\).

For the initial odd indices we use a whole-subgroup assertion with a
restricted scope. Let \(J_0=\langle\Gamma_0\cdot V\rangle\).
The four nodes give \(K_0=\ker(V*\langle C\rangle\to\langle C\rangle)\)
inside \(J_0\). Every generator of \(\Gamma_0\) and its inverse
sends \(C\) to \(kCk^{-1}\), with \(k\in K_0\). Successively
adjoin the images of \(K_0\) under these ten operators. If a stage is
\(C\)-normal, then so is every next image, because the inverse
operator sends \(C\) into that stage times \(C\) times that stage.
Their union is \(J_0\). Hence \(C\) normalizes the whole \(J_0\).
Now \(B^{-1}U_6B=V_6\) and
\(B^{-1}U_2B=V_6V_2V_6^{-1}\), so
\(D^{-1}U_2D,D^{-1}U_6D\in J_0\le J_m\).
Also \(U_1,U_4\in J_m\), by removing the \(U_6\)-conjugations
in \(V_1,V_4\) using normality under \(I\).

The exact B.81 action fixes \(D\) and restricts on \(U\) to
\(\operatorname{Inn}(D^{-1})\circ\theta\), where \(\theta\)
conjugates the first pair by \(K_{12}=U_2U_1\) and the second by
\(K_{46}=U_4U_6\). Both pair words are fixed by \(\theta\).
Consequently
\[
 \alpha_1^2(D^kU_jD^{-k})
   =D^{k-2}K_{\mathrm{pair}}^2U_jK_{\mathrm{pair}}^{-2}D^{-(k-2)}.
 \tag{B.82.12}
\]
The extra conjugator belongs to \(R_D\le I\), so normality of
\(J_m\) removes it. Using the inverse action as well proves
\(D^kU_jD^{-k}\in J_m\) for every even \(k\) when \(j=1,4\),
and every odd \(k\) when \(j=2,6\). Their normal closure in
\(W_e=R_D\rtimes\langle D^2\rangle\) is thus contained in
\(J_m\).

To identify that normal closure with the actual partial-filling kernel,
fill the moving \(V_j\) in (B.82.5). The five surviving images from
(B.82.11) are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 PW_6W_2W_6^{-1}P^{-1},\quad PW_6P^{-1},\\
 PEW_1E^{-1}P^{-1},\quad PEW_4E^{-1}P^{-1},\quad PEQC.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{B.82.13}
\]
Together with \(C,E,A^2,P\), these are a Nielsen basis of the actual
\(F_9\) quotient: recover \(W_6,W_2,W_1,W_4,Q\) successively.
Thus the quotient of \(W_e\) by the four selected normal generators
embeds as a free factor of \(F_9\). We obtain
\[
                       J_m\cap W_e=N_m\cap W_e.
 \tag{B.82.14}
\]
The rank-five free factor \(W\le F_7\) lifts to a connected subgraph
in the index-two cover, so \(W_e\) is a free factor of \(F_{13}\).
The surviving quotient is also a free factor, as just proved. These
full-topology comparisons and closure of the discrete filling kernel
prove the identical compact intersection formula. No completion of an
abstract infinitely generated kernel is substituted for an actual
closed ambient subgroup.

The actual complementary free generators can be chosen as
\[
        F_{13}=W_e*\langle C,\ DCD^{-1},\ AD^{-1},\ DA\rangle.
 \tag{B.82.15}
\]
Formula (B.82.14) does not show that these four elements normalize
\(J_m\). It does not even show that \(D^2\) normalizes the whole
\(J_m\): the uniform argument above controls its full kernel inside
\(W_e\). Those whole-subgroup extensions remain open. The rank-eight
and rank-nine intersections cannot simply be glued; their reviewed
rank-six overlap gives a rank-eleven join which still misses \(C\).

\end{proof}
