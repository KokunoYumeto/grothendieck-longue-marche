# Independent audit of the full profinite braid input to C.31

Reviewer: topology/census component. Date: 16 November 2026, approximately 04:13 UTC.

## Verdict and scope

The claim that the **full profinite completion** of the ordinary Artin braid group is torsion-free is correct. The prime-cycle contradiction proposed for C.31 is valid once the two stated operadic inputs are established: the induced braid endomorphism preserves its permutation, and it sends the full twist exactly to its lambda-th power. No automorphism or surjectivity assumption is needed for that contradiction.

This audit checks the full topology, the cohomological argument, the quotient construction yielding full braid groups, and the last algebraic contradiction. It does not independently re-prove Boavida de Brito–Horel–Robertson's ribbon-extension proposition; the CE component is auditing that proposition and its balancing formula directly. Thus the audit does not replace that source check by an assumption that a central element must stay central under a nonsurjective endomorphism.

## 1. Full induced topology in the two relevant exact sequences

Write P_n for the pure braid group and B_n for the ordinary Artin braid group. The finite-index inclusion P_n in B_n induces the full profinite topology: if V is a finite-index subgroup of P_n, then V has finite index in B_n, and its core in B_n is a finite-index normal subgroup contained in V. Hence

\[
1\longrightarrow\widehat P_n\longrightarrow\widehat B_n
 \longrightarrow S_n\longrightarrow1
\]

is exact. In particular, the profinite pure group in this sequence is its full completion, rather than a quotient arising from an unspecified induced topology.

The Fadell–Neuwirth sequence for ordered planar configurations splits:

\[
1\longrightarrow F_{n-1}\longrightarrow P_n
 \longrightarrow P_{n-1}\longrightarrow1.
\]

For clarity, full induced topology on this free kernel also has an elementary proof. Put F=F_{n-1} and Q=P_{n-1}, so P_n=F semidirect Q. Given a finite-index normal subgroup V of F, choose a characteristic finite-index subgroup W contained in V. Such W exists because a finitely generated group has only finitely many subgroups of a fixed finite index. The action Q on F/W factors through a finite quotient Q/U. The finite quotient

\[
 F\rtimes Q\longrightarrow(F/W)\rtimes(Q/U)
\]

has kernel intersecting F in W. Thus the full completion of F embeds in the full completion of P_n. The completed sequence is exact; the quotient is the full completion of Q by the universal property of finite quotients, and the original splitting extends continuously. This is a full profinite argument and uses no pro-p replacement.

## 2. Goodness of ordinary braid groups

Finite-rank free groups are good in Serre's sense. The preceding exact sequences and the discrete/continuous Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequences show inductively that P_n is good. For every finite coefficient module M, the groups H^j(F_{n-1},M) are finite and vanish for j greater than one, so the comparison of E_2 terms is legitimate in every total degree. Comparison for the finite quotient S_n then gives goodness of B_n from goodness of P_n. This last step does not infer finite cohomological dimension from a finite extension; it establishes goodness only.

Primary checks: Horel (2017), Proposition 5.10 and Corollary 5.11, author-manuscript pp.34–35, state the completion exactness/goodness result and the pure-braid conclusion. Marin (2012), Section 2.3 and Proposition 1, author-manuscript pp.9–10, explicitly includes goodness of ordinary and pure Artin braid groups and the finite-index equivalence. The elementary characteristic-quotient argument above independently addresses the potentially delicate topology of the free kernel.

## 3. Finite cohomological dimension of the full completion

The ordered configuration space Conf_n(C) is aspherical by induction on n using the forgetful fibration with punctured-plane fibre. Its free S_n quotient UConf_n(C) has the same contractible universal cover. It is a smooth manifold of real dimension 2n and hence has a CW structure of dimension at most 2n. Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{cd}(B_n)\leq 2n<\infty.
\]

The sharper classical bound n minus one is unnecessary here. In particular, this argument does not merely establish virtual cohomological dimension.

Fix a prime p. For every finite discrete p-primary continuous module M, goodness gives

\[
H^q_{\mathrm{cts}}(\widehat B_n,M)
 \cong H^q(B_n,M)=0\qquad(q>2n).
\]

Every discrete p-primary module for a profinite group is the filtered union of its finite invariant submodules: each element has finite orbit, and the subgroup generated by finitely many such finite orbits is finite. Continuous cochains from a compact profinite power have finite image. Therefore continuous cohomology commutes with this filtered union, extending the displayed vanishing to every discrete p-primary module. It follows that

\[
 \operatorname{cd}_p(\widehat B_n)\leq2n
 \quad\text{for every prime }p.
\]

A closed subgroup C_p would contradict this finite bound: p-cohomological dimension does not increase on closed subgroups, whereas H^q(C_p,F_p) is nonzero for every nonnegative q. Every nontrivial finite-order element has a power of prime order. Hence the full profinite group \widehat B_n is torsion-free. Morales (2024), Lemma 8.4, records precisely this finite-cd_p criterion for all primes.

This argument does not use the false general implication that the completion of every torsion-free group is torsion-free. Goodness and actual finite cohomological dimension are both used.

## 4. Why an object-fixing symmetric PaB endomorphism gives the required full braid map

Choose one binary parenthesization b in arity n and take the full subgroupoid on its S_n orbit. The action of S_n on this object set is free and transitive. The quotient groupoid has one object, and its automorphism group is B_n: its morphisms are all permutation cosets of P_n, with multiplication obtained by matching the intermediate labelled object. After completion, the automorphism group is \widehat B_n by the finite-index/full-topology result in Section 1.

An object-fixing endomorphism of a symmetric operad commutes with the S_n action. It therefore restricts to this subgroupoid and descends to a continuous endomorphism

\[
 \phi_n:\widehat B_n\longrightarrow\widehat B_n.
\]

Because source and target labelled objects are fixed, the morphism's permutation is unchanged. This quotient is essential: an individual PaB object's automorphism group is the pure braid group, so it would be incorrect to obtain the full braid map merely by taking that automorphism group.

## 5. The torsion contradiction

Assume for every n that \phi_n preserves the permutation and satisfies

\[
 \phi_n(z_n)=z_n^\lambda,
 \qquad z_n=\Delta_n^2.
\]

Suppose a prime p divides lambda in the precise sense lambda belongs to p times \widehat Z. There is a unique mu in \widehat Z with p mu=lambda, since multiplication by p on \widehat Z is injective with image p\widehat Z. In B_p put

\[
 \delta_p=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{p-1},\qquad
 \delta_p^p=z_p.
\]

The full twist is central in B_p and remains central in its completion by density. Therefore

\[
 a=\phi_p(\delta_p)z_p^{-\mu}
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
 a^p=\phi_p(z_p)z_p^{-p\mu}=1.
\]

Its permutation is the nontrivial p-cycle of delta_p, since the full twist is pure and the permutation is preserved. Thus a has exact order p, contradicting Section 3. This rules out every prime divisor and gives lambda in \widehat Z^times.

The argument uses arity p to rule out that prime. A construction at finitely many arities would rule out only the corresponding primes and would not by itself force lambda to be a unit. It also requires an actual continuous full profinite braid endomorphism, not an endomorphism of a pro-p or prounipotent quotient.
