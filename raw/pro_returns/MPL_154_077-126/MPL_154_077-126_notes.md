# Research notes — Montpellier 154, scans 077–126

## Scope, source status, and principal finding

The broader tame-topology programme is a relevant setting; identifying this package as an early version of the distinct 1986 *Vers une géométrie des formes* would require additional evidence.

### Classification of the package

#### C01 — Tame topology: a concrete motivating problem, with a narrower actual subject

**Anchor:** 154/086:4–15; 088:4–10; 090:10–46; 092:2–12. **Kind:** unpursued implication / classification. **Context search:** pseudodroites, modérée, stratifi.

The phrases “surface des pos. rel.” and “graphe topologique” identify an explicitly geometric and combinatorial problem about curves, cell structures, and changes of relative position. ESQ §5, marker 119/28, expressly lists pseudoline systems among stratified modular examples, so these pages **add a worked construction within that motivating family**, rather than merely sharing a title with it. They do not formulate axioms for tame subsets, a comparison of tame theories, or the full dévissage theorem of ESQ markers 119/32–34 and 119/39–41. This bears on the scope of RS **MPL_150_044-084 Q9** and the planning question in README: the positive classification is low-dimensional combinatorial/modular topology related to tame topology, with no claim that the package supplies its foundations.

#### C02 — The exact *Esquisse* match is §8's modular surface

**Anchor:** 154/086:4–15; 088:17–29; 090:26–46; 120:66–87. **Kind:** unpursued implication / historical identification. **Context search:** surface modulaire, positions relatives, affinisations.

ESQ marker 119/50, §8, describes a combinatorial “surface modulaire” classifying relative positions of an added pseudoline, including unstable positions; this is a much more specific match to the present construction than the word “tame” alone. The published passage is Grothendieck (1984/1997, p. 41), and the present package **adds the transport choices, failures, parity arguments, and counting calculations behind that announcement**. The association is strong at the level of construction and vocabulary, although these 49 pages do not prove that every variant tried here is the final object intended in the announcement. RS has no separate completed return for the missing earlier 154 probe; **Hua, findings register, 154-dihedral-monodromy-half-turn** supplies only the narrower monodromy lead examined in R12–R19.

#### C03 — The 1986 geometry of forms is not established as this package's framework

**Anchor:** 154/077–126, particularly 088:4–29, 118:24–50 and 119:30–43. **Kind:** reading question / bounded historical comparison. **Context search:** formes, réseaux, ateliers.

These scans contain curves, surfaces, graphs, polygons, collars, and gluing data, but do not present the later network/figure/workshop framework identified by the titles of Montpellier cotes 156-1–156-9. In his letter to Brown of 17 June 1986, Grothendieck describes the new approach as “different from topological spaces, from topoi and from moderate spaces” (Grothendieck, 1986, pp. 134–135), which rules out treating the two programmes as interchangeable names. A developmental connection remains possible, but **the present pages neither establish nor refute that lineage**; RS **MPL_148_029-072 R16** is only a comparative lead about realization, not evidence of such an identification. Thus README's second candidate remains unproved, while “neither” would miss the direct *Esquisse* §8 connection.

## Individual manuscript items and proposed tests

### Transport, duality, and the cancelled character calculations

#### R01 — The polygonal structure is declared evident

**Anchor:** 154/077:13–18. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “Structure polygonale … : évidente.” **Context search:** polygon, arêtes, carte.

The cyclic ordering of outgoing oriented edges is asserted rather than constructed in these lines. ESQ marker 119/12 supplies the general cartographic/drapeau setting and 119/50 the modular-surface announcement, so this **adds a local construction obligation**; no exact RS theorem settles it.

#### R04 — The straight-line dual arrangement is an explicit test question

**Anchor:** 154/078:15–41 and 079:2–20. **Kind:** conjecture / question. **Quote:** “Qu'en serait-il dans le cas … d'un vrai plan projectif réel.” **Context search:** projectif, droites, dualit.

#### R05 — The genericity assumptions in the dual test need to stay visible

**Anchor:** 154/078:21–28. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “quitte à perturber un peu.” **Context search:** stable, singulier, droites.

The discussion assumes noncollinear triples of dual points and simple intersections away from the distinguished dual points, after perturbation if needed. A verification must identify which degeneracies are excluded and whether the relative positions or circuits under study survive that perturbation; straight-line realizability is part of this test, not a property of every pseudoline arrangement. ESQ marker 119/50 includes both stable and unstable relative positions, so this **narrows that announcement for a particular calculation** rather than reducing the entire programme to the stable case; compare the distinct combinatorial and realizability questions in Goodman and Pollack (1980).

### Dihedral monodromy and the comparison with the Hua entry

#### R12 — Surjectivity with supersingular crossings is asserted as checked

**Anchor:** 154/086:4–23. **Kind:** unproved claim with a proof sketch. **Quote:** “J'ai vérifié”; “était surjectif.” **Context search:** 154-dihedral-monodromy-half-turn, monodrom, polyg.

Allowing the specified crossings of supersingular positions, the manuscript claims that loop transport realizes reflections associated with an edge and with a vertex, giving full dihedral monodromy. The group-generation step is elementary once those geometric loops and their actions are constructed, but this package does not spell out every exceptional gluing rule needed for that construction. It **supports the subject and the asserted surjectivity in Hua, findings register, 154-dihedral-monodromy-half-turn**, while R15–R16 prevent upgrading the whole geometric proof to a reviewed theorem.

#### R13 — Removing supersingular crossings initially gives only an index bound

**Anchor:** 154/086:23–45. **Kind:** self-flagged / unproved claim. **Quote:** “Ceci ne prouve pas”; “d'indice 1 ou 2.” **Context search:** supersing, index, demi.

For the cut surface, the first argument provides only the edge-reflection subgroup, which the text identifies as index two in the full dihedral group. The distinction between the enlarged surface and its cut version is essential: an argument establishing surjectivity for the former cannot simply be reused for the latter. This **refines the scope of Hua, findings register, 154-dihedral-monodromy-half-turn** and provides the exact gap addressed by the parity continuation on scan 87.

#### R14 — The half-turn formula in the Hua excerpt need not conflict with the frozen reading

### Relative positions, exceptional vertices, and descent

#### R20 — Equality of the set of met facets is asserted to characterize relative isotopy

**Anchor:** 154/088:11–26. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “Il f. et s.” **Context search:** isotopie, combinatoire, facette.

After introducing a compact surface with a cellular graph and a standard-position circle, the text says that equality of the sets of met facets is necessary and sufficient for the prescribed isotopy relation. The stated generality needs scrutiny because a set forgets multiplicities and cyclic order of intersections. ESQ marker 119/12 instead describes isotopy through combinatorial information together with fundamental-group data, while RS **EXIT_ONE_TYPE P4e** and **MPL_121_049-073+153+155 R121-08** record analogous losses from coarse data; the present assertion **differs in strength**, and R22 gives a specific test.

#### R21 — Ambient isotopy is a further equivalence, not merely a restatement

**Anchor:** 154/088:27–29. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “une famille à un paramètre … d'autom. de (X,X1).” **Context search:** isotopie, automorphisme, plongements.

The source also identifies relative isotopy with an isotopy of automorphisms of the pair carrying D to D′. Even if a parametrized isotopy of the circle has been specified, extension while preserving the graph and its relevant facets is a separate assertion needing local compatibility. ESQ markers 119/11–12 give the Ladegaillerie/isotopy background and 119/40–41 distinguish weak uniqueness from contractible choices; these pages **add a concrete relative extension problem**, not a proof of the general isotopy comparisons mentioned in RS **MPL_121_001-048 Q4.3**.

#### R22 — A torus test obstructs the literal unrestricted facet-set criterion

**Anchor:** 154/088:4–29. **Kind:** own idea, conditional counterexample. **Confidence:** high for the construction; conditional as an interpretation of the manuscript because additional conventions may precede scan 77. **Context search:** coarse maquette, isotopie, normal data.

On $T^2=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$, take $X_1$ to be the two coordinate circles and $D_1(t)=(t,t+1/3)$, $D_2(t)=(t,2t+1/3)$, with t taken modulo 1: both embedded circles avoid the graph vertex, cross its edges transversely, and meet the same three open facets. Their homology classes $(1,1)$ and $(1,2)$ differ even up to sign, so no isotopy from the identity can carry one to the other, although their sets of met facets agree. Thus the literal broad claim in R20 requires extra restrictions or richer incidence data; this does **not** refute a pseudoline-only version or a convention in omitted earlier pages. It **adds a specific, elementary test** to the general data-loss warning in RS **EXIT_ONE_TYPE P4e** and **MPL_121_049-073+153+155 R121-08**, without re-proving or upgrading those register entries.

### Cartographic involutions and local counting

#### R33 — Five involutions are proposed from an even-valency/even-face map

**Anchor:** 154/095:46–58. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “une suite circulaire de cinq involutions 2 à 2 commutantes.” **Context search:** involutions, cartographique, sigma.

#### R34 — Visualization of the pentagonal map is a stated difficulty

**Anchor:** 154/095:58–61. **Kind:** self-flagged / question. **Quote:** “Je n'arrive pas bien à visualiser.” **Context search:** carte, visual, formes.
