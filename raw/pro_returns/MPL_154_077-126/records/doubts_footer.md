# Legibility assessment

All 49 supplied pages have been read as whole pages and in their full-resolution halves, then checked again in a second visual audit. No OCR was used. Rapid cursive, compressed endings, pencil on patterned listing paper, cancellations, and coloured overpainting make some readings persistently uncertain; a successful LaTeX compilation does not validate those readings.

The least trustworthy passages are the heavily cancelled material on scans 82, 86, 92–93 and 118, the crowded notation on scans 94–95 and 119, small numerical/sign labels in scans 105 and 111–116, and the nearly clipped final words on scan 94. The register conservatively labels all unresolved readings “low”; this describes the local doubtful token, not a measured error rate for its entire page. It would be misleading to supply numerical confidence without an independent palaeographic adjudication.

The TikZ drawings preserve the observed components, labels, colours, direction marks, intersections and relative placement as closely as feasible, but are reconstructions rather than facsimiles. The most intricate overlap/routing reconstructions carry the permitted crop-candidate comments on scans 81, 83, 85, 94, 103 and 124; none is replaced by an embedded scan. Faint isolated marks found during the second reading have been included. A two-dimensional crossing in a reconstruction is not by itself a claim of mathematical incidence beyond the ink.

Foreground computer print on reused sheets is included, even when inverted relative to the handwriting. Clearly mirrored or translucent reverse-side show-through was not treated as foreground writing; this distinction is least secure where print and pale pencil overlap. The visible marginal numbering is reproduced without attributing it to Grothendieck. This package has no demonstrably authorial title sheet or explicit authorial page count.
