## Legibility and limits

The typed baselines are generally legible. Their principal difficulty is the coexistence of live type, old letters under mechanical x cancellations, displaced impressions, and pen replacements. A plausible grammatical sentence is especially unsafe at those locations: the pages retain wrong words, mismatched indices, missing letters and punctuation, and unfinished parentheses. Small round o and numeral0 are not assumed interchangeable. A doubled outline is distinguished from a heavy single impression before an alphabet is changed.

The least secure readings are compressed handwriting in the margins and insertions, text under repeated cancellations, the faint parts of handwritten formulas, and material cut off at the right or lower image edge. Doubt remains explicit where the available pixels do not decide. The second-pass records preserve both independent readings, including disagreements of scope or macro representation, and the main reader’s adjudication.

C usually retains substantially more cancelled and overwritten matter than H. H often supplies a fluent live reading while omitting old states, but it also provides useful competing readings—for example the last handwritten adjective on scan027. Neither witness is treated as independent proof of an agreed word. Counts in the changes log measure documented places, and are not estimates of the witnesses’ overall error rates. H’s editorial summaries, its page markers, and its transition to the next section are not manuscript ink.

No OCR, text-recognition service, outside mathematical source, or TeX compilation was used. Pale paper texture, isolated non-writing specks, and show-through are not promoted to text. The source’s missing or clipped characters have not been reconstructed merely to make a formula or sentence grammatical.
