# Observed handwriting and notation

Habits below are local observations, not automatic substitutions on other pages.

## Scan 002

- Scan002 only: the 4 in C₄ has an open upper angle and a long upright below the horizontal cross-stroke. The smaller 4 of (24 p) is rounder. Too few occurrences to establish a general habit.

## Scan 003

- Scan003: s often has a high hooked body and a descending low tail; the isolated initial s of sur can look like a comma. H adds a comma after G at L04; no separate mark is present.
- Scan003: abbreviated ss and sss use repeated compact hooked forms. H repeatedly expands ss to sous and reads both sss as ssi; the two sss occurrences have three full bodies.
- Scan003: x is often formed by two curved strokes resembling a small n at first glance (diagram x, x\prime, and torsor quantifier); this is consistent across those occurrences.
- Scan003: g can have an open upper bowl and a long lower curl; prose gx is stronger than the small boxed g/s subscript. The boxed glyph remains uncertain; this habit is not promoted to an all-occurrence resolution.
- Scan003: the empty-set sign is phi-shaped with a long descender, at both occurrences; C and H substitute standard empty-set glyphs.
- Scan003: word-final e and s can both collapse into a long low exit stroke; de/des in the torsor criterion remains unsettled rather than decided by grammar.

## Scan 004

- scan004: final t in sommet has a broad returning lower foot and detached rightward crossbar; this foot is not a final s (both the data line and axiom parenthesis). H pluralized both.
- scan004: accents may be compact detached marks; connait has only an i dot, whereas géom has a detached accent over e. H supplied a circumflex in connait.
- scan004: low n/m sequences often flatten into a long horizontal stroke (ens, un, sommets); tiny lower-case x in the seg coordinates resembles n at all three occurrences, still unresolved.
- scan004: p plus a low word tail can resemble a turnstile; the par/p./symbol place remains doubtful, so this is not established as a general habit.
- scan004: actual quotation marks are paired raised short strokes, not guillemets, at sommet, d’arêtes, attache, and Réalisation rectiligne.

## Scan 005

- Low n and m strokes are often flattened almost horizontally; tall l and t remain much more conspicuous.
- The g descender can return left beneath the word and mimic an underline; segment has such a return.
- Several replacements are written above a horizontal cancellation; separate circles and leaders locate multi, distincts and the conn. addition.
- The last e can be a long low horizontal exit, as in connexe.
- A final q-like descender can survive below a heavy cancellation even when its bowl is obscured.

## Scan 006

- The paired s abbreviation in ss-grpe can resemble two linked upright strokes; the g has a conspicuous deep loop and the r/p/e ending is compressed.
- Short words such as est may collapse to a long low run with an upturned terminal cluster, sometimes resembling a small arrow.
- Cancelled text is often struck with a long horizontal line and then sweeping diagonal or curved strokes; these intersect individual letters and require retained doubt.
- Interlinear additions are smaller and have return marks; the inverse sentence is written in the interline as a complete sentence.
- The written tt abbreviation has two tall strokes and a low connecting loop; it has no intervening o/u letter bodies.

## Scan 007

- The writer compresses n, u, and several joined lower-case letters into long nearly horizontal strokes with short upturns/downturns; small connectors can therefore resemble dashes.
- The g descender in générateur returns far to the left; this is a letter stroke, not an underline beneath the word.
- Small upper hooks and lower turns form the numeral 2; the numeral 3 has two rightward bowls. Numerals were inspected independently of the mathematical claim.
- The same main ink hand becomes lighter and more broken in the lower remarks, while tall l, p descenders and the crossed t remain useful letter anchors.
- A full stop is present after the first néc. but absent after the second néc, ens, autom, and the lower i.e.

## Scan 008

- scan008: tte contracts toute into two tall t stems and a small terminal e loop; H expands this abbreviation.
- scan008: autom terminates at m without a written stop or expanded orphisme(s), at all three occurrences checked.
- scan008: long s strokes in sss can resemble i; the last stroke has no separate dot and bends downward.
- scan008: sigma and omega are distinguished by the latter’s two rounded lower lobes; opposite omega pair confirms the shape.
- scan008: lower-case o in or is short relative to the neighboring mathematical capitals.
- scan008: short abbreviations and interlinear words use flattened joins that can hide r or other internal letters; corresponding doubts retained.
- scan008: arrow shafts were read at both ends; the marginal map has a terminal head, while the body map’s small terminal hook remains unresolved.
