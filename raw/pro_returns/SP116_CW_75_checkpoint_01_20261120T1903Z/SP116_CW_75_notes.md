Targeted check of the finite-graph realization claim: two distinct edges with identical endpoints would have the same straight segment, contradicting an embedding. Conversely, choose finitely many distinct points (t,t²,t³). Vandermonde determinants give no three collinear and no four coplanar; hence segments with disjoint endpoints do not intersect and segments sharing one endpoint meet only there. This proves the claimed existence in R³ for the strict graph. The unique-extension phrase presupposes the affine realization of each coordinate segment; a condition on the image alone would not uniquely specify its parametrization.

Targeted check of the contour classification: following the two incident edges at each vertex of a connected loopless degree-two graph either closes into a finite cycle (including two vertices joined by two edges) or continues as a doubly infinite path. Connectedness leaves no other vertices. Thus its cardinal is an integer at least 2 or ℵ₀, vertices and edges have equal cardinal, and strictness excludes exactly the two-vertex case. Exchanging the two incidence sets preserves degree two, which explains the ink’s heading Dualité.

The asserted equivalence of conditions 1° and 2° fails for the square under adjacency of distinct vertices or edges. Number the vertices 0,1,2,3 cyclically. The reflection (0 1)(2 3) sends every vertex to a neighbour, satisfying 1°, but fixes the edges {0,1} and {2,3}, violating 2°. Targeted check: in a cyclic group generated by g of finite order n≥2, g=g⁻¹ exactly when g²=1, hence exactly when n=2. Thus the stated inverse-generator pair has cardinal two precisely for n≠2; replacing the pair by an abstract two-element set avoids its collapse when n=2. The ink’s doubtful abbreviation sss/ss/ssi is retained despite the mathematical equivalence.

Two qualifications are needed if the claims are read with the earlier loopless-contour definition. The doubly infinite path from scan005 requires the infinite cyclic group, which is absent from the opening n∈N, n≥2 description unless that sentence is limited to finite contours. Also an arbitrary Z-action may have fixed points: a singleton with the identity action would create a loop, disallowed by two distinct endpoints per edge. For loopless oriented contours the corresponding permutation must have no fixed points; its orbits then give cycles of length at least two or doubly infinite paths. These are notes on the claims, not supplied restrictions in the transcription.

## Scan 008

The page headed “Orientation d’un contour” describes an orientation by choosing an origin for each edge, with exactly one originating edge at each vertex. It states there are two orientations of a connected contour, constructs the associated vertex and edge advances u_omega and their inverses, and relates these to cyclic groups, torsors, and circular order. The final sentence continues onto the next leaf. No date, author page number, or page count is written; penciled archive numeral 7 is retained as a comment.

Reading decisions: repeated omega has two rounded lobes and is resolved consistently with scans006–007. The cropped singular arc, lowercase or, avec, donc, singular élément, tte, and raised multiplication cross are retained. The short map shaft lacks a complete decisive arrowhead. The struck words and crowded interlinear annotation retain explicit alternatives. The visible closing brace after 2° has no corresponding opening set brace before g; none is supplied.

Source slip at the edge definition: the ink says “l’unique arc de S”. The definition starts with a∈A and constructs u_omega(a), while sigma has domain A; the expected phrase is “arc de A”. S remains in the transcription.

For a connected degree-two loopless graph, choosing one edge orientation forces the outgoing choice successively at each vertex. This gives the two directions on either a finite cycle or the bi-infinite path. The associated advance reverses when the orientation reverses; on a finite n-cycle its order is n. At n=2 the two orientations give the same advance on both the vertex and edge sets, explaining the exceptional equality without identifying the two orientation choices themselves.

The asserted equivalence and characterization by conditions 1° and 2° again have the four-cycle counterexample recorded on scan006: one type of reflection moves every vertex to a neighbor but fixes two edges, while the other fixes vertices but moves every edge to an adjacent edge. Thus the two conditions individually are insufficient here; the two quarter-turns satisfy both. This conclusion uses the existing explicit certificate and does not alter the ink. The finite cyclic-order formulation is read with its stated n; the permitted n=1 torsor needs separate treatment if it is to correspond to the preceding loopless contour definition.
