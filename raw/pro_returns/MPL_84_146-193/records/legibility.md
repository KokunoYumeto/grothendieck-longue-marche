The first sheet is a rough calculation sheet. Dense overwriting obscures several Greek letters and the beginning of the top formula. No OCR is used. Doubt marks preserve positive tentative readings, including inside diagrams; no mathematical completion is used to remove a doubt.

Several typescripts lose final letters at the right image edge. In these cases the first alternative records the visible prefix and the second a tentative reading; neither the missing letters nor punctuation are treated as secure. Pencil foliation is retained separately from typed page numbering.

The bottom paragraph of scan 154 is particularly compressed. Its word-by-word tentative readings should not be used as an established sentence or claim. In the upper diagram of that scan, the lower horizontal stroke is rendered leftward from its thick pointed left end; a plain dash is an alternative (low confidence). The sloping stroke through the abbreviated diagram annotation is provisionally treated as cancellation.

The most difficult mathematical sheets are 146, 154, 166, 176, 178, 180, 182, 184, 188, 190 and 192. Several contain abandoned computations written over one another, unclosed brackets, unexplained isolated symbols, and words reduced to a few joining strokes. The lower prose of 154, the coefficient computations and cancellations of 176, the lower prose and pairing computations of 180, the crossed conditions of 188, and the upper prose and overwritten quadratics of 192 are especially unreliable at the word or index level; the doubt register should be consulted before quoting or using them mathematically.

The typescripts are generally much clearer than the mathematical handwriting, but their literal misspellings, missing accents, grammatical mismatches, unfinished page continuations, inserted punctuation, and sometimes clipped last letters are retained. The crowded handwritten changes in 149, 153, 167, 175, 179, 189 and 191 require more caution than their surrounding typed prose. No source text outside the supplied scans was used to restore their wording.

Every whole page and both supplied halves were viewed during the initial transcription. Every page was then visually audited again, and selected difficult passages and recurring symbols were enlarged for a further direct comparison before freezing. The transcription is a single-reader diplomatic reading with a second audit by that same reader; the independent technical checks concern TeX syntax and rendered layout only, and do not certify the readings. Diagrams preserve nodes, edges, labels, cancellation states, and relative arrangements, but do not claim millimetric facsimile geometry.

No `\illegible` placeholder was needed: even the least secure marks have a positive tentative reading with `\unsure`. This does not imply that every mark is deciphered. In particular, a sequence of low-confidence guessed words is not a reconstructed sentence, and uncertain formula signs are not to be treated as established algebraic identities. Paper blemishes, stains and scan-edge artefacts without identifiable textual or drawn content are not promoted to characters.
