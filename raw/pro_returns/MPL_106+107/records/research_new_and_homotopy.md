## Appendix A. Verification of the telescope counterexample

This is a standard example, not a novelty claim. Fix a prime \(p\), let \(f_n:S^1\to S^1\) have degree \(p\), and form its mapping telescope \(T\). Let \(T_n\) be the finite telescope ending at its \(n\)-th circle, with \(T_0=S^1\). Each \(T_n\) is a finite CW complex deforming onto that terminal circle, each \(T_n\hookrightarrow T_{n+1}\) is an inclusion of subcomplexes, and the induced bonding map on \(H_1\) is multiplication by \(p\). Thus

\[
H_1(T;\mathbb Z)=\mathop{\rm colim}(\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{p}\cdots)=\mathbb Z[1/p],
\qquad H_2(T;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]

The universal-coefficient sequence, or Milnor's sequence applied to the finite subtelescopes, gives

\[
H^2(T;\mathbb Z)
\cong\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z[1/p],\mathbb Z)
\cong\varprojlim{}^1(\mathbb Z\xleftarrow{p}\mathbb Z\xleftarrow{p}\cdots)
\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}_p/\mathbb Z.
\]

Milnor (1962, Lemmas 1–2, pp. 337–339) verifies the limit and exact-sequence tools; Hatcher (2002, Examples 3F.3, 3F.7, 3F.9 and the following Ext calculation, pp. 312–316) explicitly treats this telescope. Here is a self-contained verification of the algebraic term, to fix the convention and exclude confusing \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}_p/\mathbb Z\) with a different completion quotient. A free resolution has differential

\[
\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathbb Ze_n\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathbb Ze_n,
\qquad e_n\longmapsto e_n-pe_{n+1},
\]

with cokernel \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\), by sending the target basis vector \(e_n\) to \(p^{-n}\). Dualization computes Ext as the cokernel of

\[
\delta:\prod_{n\ge0}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\prod_{n\ge0}\mathbb Z,
\qquad\delta(a)_n=a_n-pa_{n+1}.
\]

The convergent sum \(b\mapsto\sum_{n\ge0}p^nb_n\pmod{\mathbb Z}\) induces an isomorphism from that cokernel to \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}_p/\mathbb Z\). It is onto by p-adic digits; telescoping shows that it kills \(\delta(a)\); and if the sum is an integer \(m\), the integers

\[
a_n=\frac{m-\sum_{k<n}p^kb_k}{p^n}
\]

are well defined and satisfy \(\delta(a)=b\), proving injectivity on the quotient. The quotient is nonzero, indeed uncountable.

Representability of integral cohomology now yields

\[
[T,K(\mathbb Z,2)]\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}_p/\mathbb Z,
\qquad
[T_n,K(\mathbb Z,2)]\cong H^2(S^1;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]

Consequently the comparison map is the map from a nontrivial group to a singleton. Every nonzero class gives a nonnull map which is null on every finite subtelescope, and hence on every finite subcomplex: these are phantom maps. This use of unbased homotopy classes is legitimate because the target is simply connected; Hatcher (2002, Theorem 4.57 and the paragraph following it, pp. 393–394) states the unbased identification explicitly.

**Literal presheaf realization of the example.** Set \(X_n=\operatorname{Sing}(T_n)\) and \(Y=\operatorname{Sing}(K(\mathbb Z,2))\). In simplicial sets, the \(X_n\to X_{n+1}\) are monomorphisms/cofibrations and \(Y\) is Kan. The telescope height \(T\to[0,\infty)\) bounds the image of every singular simplex because its domain is compact, so \(\operatorname{Sing}(T)=\bigcup_n\operatorname{Sing}(T_n)\) **as a simplicial set**. Therefore this is exactly a chain of monomorphisms in \(\widehat\Delta\) with ordinary presheaf colimit, as requested at 107/019; no identification of “homotopy colimit” with “ordinary colimit in Hot” has been smuggled in.

## Appendix B. Verification that no ordinary Hot colimit exists

This is our short implication of the checked standard results. Work in the ordinary unbased category of weak homotopy types, equivalently CW homotopy types. Suppose that \(L\) is a colimit of \(S^1\xrightarrow{p}S^1\xrightarrow{p}\cdots\), so that, naturally in every target \(Y\),

\[
[L,Y]\cong\varprojlim_n[S^1,Y].
\]

Testing discrete sets \(D\) gives \(\operatorname{Set}(\pi_0L,D)\cong D\), naturally in \(D\), and therefore \(\pi_0L\) is a singleton by Yoneda. For every abelian group \(A\), functorial Eilenberg–MacLane models give

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(H_1L,A)
\cong H^1(L;A)
\cong[L,K(A,1)]
\cong\varprojlim(A\xleftarrow{p}A\xleftarrow{p}\cdots)
\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z[1/p],A).
\]

These are natural bijections in abelian-group homomorphisms \(A\to A'\), so Yoneda in \(\mathrm{Ab}\) implies \(H_1L\cong\mathbb Z[1/p]\). There is no unbased conjugacy defect here: conjugation is trivial in the abelian target, or one may directly use cohomological representability, including its unbased version (Hatcher, 2002, pp. 393–394). The universal-coefficient theorem, with no finite-generation hypothesis, then injects

\[
0\ne\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb Z[1/p],\mathbb Z)
\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}_p/\mathbb Z
\hookrightarrow H^2(L;\mathbb Z).
\]

On the other hand, testing \(Y=K(\mathbb Z,2)\) in the presumed universal property forces \(H^2(L;\mathbb Z)=0\), since every \([S^1,K(\mathbb Z,2)]\) is a singleton. This contradiction excludes **every** ordinary colimit object. The finite subtelescope sequence is isomorphic to the degree-\(p\) circle sequence as a diagram in Hot, by terminal-circle deformation retractions and the mapping-cylinder homotopies, so its image in Hot has no ordinary colimit either. A telescope still exists as a homotopy colimit of the coherent diagram.

## Appendix C. Exact rectification hypotheses and proof

### C1. Simplicial model categories

Fix a strict square and a proposed diagonal:

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
A&\xrightarrow{g}&E\\
\downarrow i&&\downarrow p\\
B&\xrightarrow{g'}&F,
\end{array}
\qquad pg=g'i,
\qquad\varphi:B\to E.
\]

Assume \(i\) is a cofibration with \(A,B\) cofibrant, \(p\) is a fibration with \(E,F\) fibrant, and the model category is simplicial. SM7 says that

\[
q:\operatorname{Map}(B,E)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Map}(A,E)\times_{\operatorname{Map}(A,F)}\operatorname{Map}(B,F)
\]

is a Kan fibration. If \(k:\varphi i\simeq g\) and \(h:p\varphi\simeq g'\) satisfy \(pk=h(i\otimes\Delta^1)\), the pair \((k,h)\) is a path in this fibre product from \(q(\varphi)\) to \((g,g')\). Lift this path from \(\varphi\); its endpoint is the required \(\bar\varphi\), satisfying the two equations **strictly** and remaining homotopic to \(\varphi\). Conversely, any homotopy \(H:\varphi\simeq\bar\varphi\) gives the compatible pair \((Hi,pH)\). Thus the existential “if and only if” does not require either \(i\) or \(p\) to be a weak equivalence, and it does not claim uniqueness of the diagonal.

For the weaker formulation in the manuscript margin, suppose that \(pk\) and \(hi\) are homotopic **rel endpoints** in \(\operatorname{Map}(A,F)\). Since \(\operatorname{Map}(A,p)\) is a Kan fibration, the induced map between path spaces with the specified fixed endpoints is a fibration (apply the same pushout-product/pullback-Hom axiom to \(\partial\Delta^1\hookrightarrow\Delta^1\)). Lifting the homotopy of projected paths adjusts \(k\), with endpoints unchanged, to \(\tilde k\) satisfying \(p\tilde k=hi\), after which the preceding argument applies. This is an existence statement about adjustable homotopies; an arbitrary prescribed incompatible pair need not be the restrictions of any homotopy of diagonals.

### C2. A formulation valid in an arbitrary model category

An ordinary model category need not have a literal simplicial tensor \(B\otimes\Delta^1\); compatible cylinders or framings must be specified. Choose a good cylinder \(CA\) on \(A\), form

\[
Q=(B\amalg B)\amalg_{A\amalg A}CA,
\]

and factor its canonical map to \(B\) as a cofibration \(Q\hookrightarrow CB\) followed by a weak equivalence \(CB\to B\). This gives a compatible cylinder on the cofibration \(i\), and

\[
B\amalg_A CA\longrightarrow CB
\]

is a trivial cofibration: it is a cofibration by construction and pushout stability, while two-out-of-three applies to its composite with \(CB\to B\), using the trivial cofibration \(B\to B\amalg_A CA\). Compatible homotopies \(k:CA\to E\), \(h:CB\to F\), together with \(\varphi\) at the initial endpoint, therefore extend to \(H:CB\to E\) by the lifting axiom against \(p\). This proves the same strict-compatible-data implication without a simplicial enrichment; the cofibrant-source/fibrant-target homotopy comparison supplies the existential converse. To use the margin's higher “homotopic” comparison literally, employ compatible higher cylinders/framings or the derived mapping-space formulation, rather than treating arbitrary cylinders as if they were one common tensor.

### C3. Why varying both homotopies cannot repair every square

For the square of H07, the two components of a homotopy \(k:\varphi|_{\partial I}\simeq g\) are paths in \(\mathbb R\) from 0 to 0 and from 0 to 1, respectively. Their projections to \(S^1\) have winding numbers 0 and 1, independently of how those paths are chosen. For every \(h:I\times I\to S^1\) from the constant map to itself, its restrictions at the two endpoints of the first interval are homotopic **rel endpoints**, through the first coordinate, so they have the same winding number. Hence no pair \(k,h\) can have equal—or even endpoint-relative homotopic—projections. The fixed strict base map and fixed endpoint map are essential: allowing \(g\) or \(g'\) themselves to change is a different lifting problem.

## Appendix E. Additional verifications for H12–H13

### E1. Finite presentation of [n] and preservation by the nerve

Let \((C_j)_{j\in J}\) be a filtered diagram of small categories. Its filtered colimit can be described on objects and arrows by the filtered colimits of those sets: source, target, identities, and composition descend because every finite collection of representatives and every finite equation can be placed at a common stage. For a functor \([n]\to\operatorname{colim}_j C_j\), choose representatives of its n generating arrows and n+1 objects, then move to a common stage where the required source and target equalities hold. Their composites define a functor \([n]\to C_j\), proving surjectivity of

\[
\operatorname{colim}_j\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Cat}}([n],C_j)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Cat}}([n],\operatorname{colim}_jC_j).
\]

If two representatives define the same functor in the colimit, their finitely many object and generating-arrow equalities hold at a common later stage, proving injectivity. This bijection is natural in [n]; it therefore gives the asserted isomorphism of simplicial sets. This is our elementary verification of the reason already supplied in the manuscript and restated in the companion commentary at V ms. 46, not a further external theorem or historical-priority claim.

### E2. A sequence of retracts with no retraction from its union

Use the literal simplicial example of Appendix A, and choose a simplicial representative \(f:X_\infty\to Y\) of a nonzero class, with \(Y=\operatorname{Sing}K(\mathbb Z,2)\) Kan and zero map \(c:X_\infty\to Y\). Every restriction \(f_i\) is simplicially homotopic to \(c_i\), since all simplicial sets are cofibrant and the target is Kan. Form the pushout

\[
Z_i=Y\amalg_{X_i\times\partial\Delta^1}(X_i\times\Delta^1),
\]

where the boundary map is \((f_i,c_i)\). The inclusion \(Y\to Z_i\) is a monomorphism. A retraction \(Z_i\to Y\) restricting to \(\mathrm{id}_Y\) is, by the pushout universal property, exactly a simplicial homotopy from \(f_i\) to \(c_i\); hence every stage admits such a retraction. The transition \(Z_i\to Z_{i+1}\) is the pushout of the monomorphism

\[
(X_i\times\Delta^1)\cup(X_{i+1}\times\partial\Delta^1)
\hookrightarrow X_{i+1}\times\Delta^1,
\]

so it too is a monomorphism. Colimits and the pushout formula give

\[
Z_\infty=Y\amalg_{X_\infty\times\partial\Delta^1}
(X_\infty\times\Delta^1).
\]

A retraction of this object onto Y would be a homotopy \(f\simeq c\), contrary to the chosen nonzero class; thus there is no global retraction.

For completeness, let \(R_i\) be the simplicial mapping space of retractions under Y, equivalently the path space from \(f_i\) to \(c_i\) in \(\operatorname{Map}(X_i,Y)\). These are nonempty Kan complexes, and each restriction \(R_{i+1}\to R_i\) is a Kan fibration by the displayed monomorphism and the mapping-space axiom. The ordinary limit \(\lim_iR_i\) is precisely the retraction space for \(Z_\infty\). If a compatible sequence of components existed, choose a representative at stage zero and, inductively, lift a path from a later representative’s restriction to the already chosen earlier representative; this produces strictly compatible representatives. It follows that \(\lim_i\pi_0R_i\) is empty here: the manuscript’s final obstruction persists even for a genuine tower of retraction spaces with all the stated cofibration and fibrancy properties.
