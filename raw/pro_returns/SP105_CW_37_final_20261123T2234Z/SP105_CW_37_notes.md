## Mathematical checks made in this run

### M93-1 — The asserted independence over B needs correction (scan 93)

The page says that the displayed generators give elements “linéairement indépendants” in `J_n/TJ_n` over `A_o=A/TA=B`. For this check, take `B=k[X_0,X_1,...]` over a nonzero field k: this makes explicit the polynomial-ring interpretation of the page's family notation, whose typed subscript literally reads `1≥0`. The assertion is false already for `n=1`. Put `J_1=(X_0,X_1T)` in `B[T]/(T^2)`. Then

`X_0 [X_1T] = [T(X_1X_0)] = 0` in `J_1/TJ_1`,

although `X_0` is nonzero in `B`. Thus the proposed generators are not B-linearly independent. The same relation holds if the displayed ideal is first considered in the polynomial ring before truncation. In the page's reduction modulo the other `X_j`, the coefficients `a_i` are reduced too: proving their reduced images vanish does not prove the original `a_i` vanish in B. This is a check of the argument on this sheet, not a proposed alteration of its transcription.

### M93-2 — The needed minimal-generator conclusion has a narrower proof (scan 93)

For the polynomial-ring example over a field, the `n+1` monomials `X_i T^i` are minimal ideal generators. In the quotient `J_n / (T,X_0,X_1,...)J_n`, they are linearly independent over `k`: none of these monomials is a positive-degree monomial multiple of another. Localizing at the maximal ideal `(T,X_0,X_1,...)` therefore still requires `n+1` generators, while the displayed list supplies that many. This verifies the minimal-generator conclusion separately from the stronger, false B-linear-independence assertion. It does not establish every implication of EGA I 10.10.5, whose full statement is not in these pages.

### M97-1 — The local-generation argument and its unfilled bound (scan 97)

Under the hypotheses expressed in the lemma, choose numerators `h_j^i` in M for the local generators, and let N be the submodule generated by all of them. For each `i`, localization makes `N_{f_i}=M_{f_i}`. Hence `(M/N)_{f_i}=0`. For any element of `M/N`, a power of each `f_i` kills it. Those finitely many powers generate the unit ideal because the `f_i` do, so the element is zero. Thus `N=M`, with at most `sum_i m_i` generators. This independently checks the local-generation argument.

The manuscript leaves the operation between `m` and `m_i` blank. The proof explains a possible intended bound but does not put a summation sign or equality on the page.
