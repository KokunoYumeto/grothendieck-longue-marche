# Legibility and limitations

All 43 pages were read visually in one stream, whole page followed by both supplied halves, and then audited again in the same order. No OCR was used, and no transcription or visual audit was delegated. Tooling assistance was limited to mechanical validation, hashing and packaging scripts.

The printed offprints (161-5/003–022) are substantially more legible than the handwritten sheets, although small round component exponents, an overwritten inequality, and marginal journal-title abbreviations remain uncertain. The handwriting in 93 is often compressed, particularly the first sheet, connective words, word endings and additions across cancellations. The least reliable passages are the dense cancellations and overwriting on 161-5/023 and 025–029, the short parenthesis on 027, and the small letters and subscripts in the final blue diagrams; these should receive priority in a human recheck.

Every uncertain formula symbol has a proposed reading in `\unsure`; no formula or diagram uses `\illegible` in place of a symbol. The few `\illegible` word counts concern densely obliterated prose after enlargement. Drawings and enclosing marks are TikZ approximations of the strokes, with no image embedded; spatial scale is approximate. Ordinary interline spacing and line endings have not been facsimile-reproduced.

This table and the final readings were fixed before context contents were opened. Later context-based reading questions belong only in the research notes. Mechanical checks cover page order, macros, balanced braces/environments, inventory membership and hashes; they do not certify palaeographical accuracy. A TeX compilation was not possible in this environment because the installed engines lack the required formats and packages; the requested deliverable remains a LaTeX fragment with the user's state macros undefined, ready for their own wrapper.
