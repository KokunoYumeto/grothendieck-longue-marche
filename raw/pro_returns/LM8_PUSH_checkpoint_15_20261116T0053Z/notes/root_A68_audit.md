## Actual covers and maps

We checked the degree-sixteen map from `v^2=w^16-6w^8+1` to the specified CM elliptic curve and its factorization as duplication after the degree-four Kummer map. The Kummer function has divisor `T0+T(-1)-T1-O`; these are four simple valuations. Its fourth root therefore gives degree four and ramification index four at exactly those points. Duplication has degree four and is unramified in characteristic zero. The composed map consequently has precisely the four stated cusps and is étale over the once-punctured elliptic curve.

The two displayed automorphisms preserve that actual map. Their orders and relation give sixteen distinct deck transformations, so the asserted dihedral group is the full group. The degree-sixteen squarefree hyperelliptic polynomial gives genus seven. Multiplication of `w` by a sixteenth root of unity identifies the plus and minus equations after a finite field extension, preserving the marked set. This field extension is allowed by the derived-subgroup comparison already proved in A.41.

The quotient to the genus-three curve uses the explicit function `u=w^2`. The genus-two quotient is an actual function-field map, with `T=w^2+w^-2` and `W=(w+w^-1)v/w^4`. Its equation and discriminant are exact polynomial identities. The intermediate invariant functions under inversion and the next involution also check; the argument does not infer a morphism from a resemblance of branch loci.

The pullbacks of the holomorphic forms give three independent even-index forms and two odd-index planes. The two planes are complementary because the eigenvalues on each of the pairs `(1,7)` and `(3,5)` are distinct. Thus the product of the three actual Jacobian maps has an invertible differential in characteristic zero and is an isogeny. This proves the claimed decomposition of the entire seven-dimensional Jacobian, with no missing complementary factor.

## Reduction and pointed geometry

The exact invariants are `(24,-24,1248,7344,-256)`. Liu's two auxiliary invariants are 1152 and -52641792. The three strict-positivity valuations are 20, 12 and 32; the two further integrality valuations are 48 and 20. Substitution in the theorem gives two smooth elliptic components meeting in one point, and both the product and sum of their j-invariants vanish in characteristic two. Each is therefore supersingular. The conclusion is about stable reduction of the curve, hence good reduction of its compact-type Jacobian.

We reread the relevant proper-factor paragraph of A.37: the genus-three quotient has elliptic factors with j-invariants among 1728, 8000, 10976 and 128. They all have integral j reducing to zero at two. The isogeny above therefore gives potential good reduction and zero 2-rank for the whole genus-seven Jacobian. Potential good reduction transfers by the auxiliary-prime Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion; zero 2-rank transfers through the isogeny of good special fibres. A semistable curve whose Jacobian has good reduction has a tree dual graph.

The four displayed products and their expansions at both zero and infinity give the exact principal divisors stated in the draft. Their relations connect all four cusps, so every difference is annihilated by eight. A good abelian variety of zero 2-rank has no nonzero geometric 2-primary torsion points in its special fibre. Thus all these differences specialize to zero. We reread the precise A.41 and A.42 hypotheses: positive compact genus, a distinct companion cusp, compact type, a rational path including both endpoint components, and the specified actual packet/open derived subgroup. Every hypothesis is supplied. The conclusion is the full fixed subgroup in every pro-prime completion, not merely a Lie or homology statement. ## Every intermediate cover

The inertia at a cusp is the order-four group generated by the square of the rotation. It is normal and equals the Frattini subgroup of the dihedral group of order sixteen. The addendum also gives a direct proof of the only Frattini property needed: a subgroup mapping onto the quotient of order four contains an odd rotation and a reflection, and therefore is the whole group. Thus a proper intermediate has at least two cusp double cosets. Its compact genus is positive because it maps nontrivially to an elliptic curve.

The norm on Jacobians is surjective, since its composite with pullback is multiplication by the degree. It transfers potential good reduction and zero 2-rank to the target. For actual points over an algebraically closed constant field, divisor pushforward has residue degree one, with no ramification multiplier. Taking the norm of a function with divisor eight times a source cusp difference proves that each target cusp difference is also eight-torsion. Separating the target marks only inserts rational components. A.41–A.42 therefore apply to each proper intermediate, while the whole-group quotient is the original elliptic curve, already covered by the established base theorem. Its geometric closure is a quotient of a finite two-group, so A.59 applies to normalizers as well.

The subgroup certificate enumerates all nineteen subgroups. Exhaustiveness also has a structural justification: intersection with the cyclic rotation group is cyclic, and a subgroup outside it is generated by that intersection and one reflection. Every subgroup therefore has at most two generators, exactly the range enumerated by the program.
