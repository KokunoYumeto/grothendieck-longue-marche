## Opened primary source

P. A. Zalesskii, *On virtually projective groups*, J. reine angew. Math. 572 (2004), 97–110, author-hosted published PDF: https://mat.unb.br/pz/arquivos/virproj.pdf . The introduction on printed p.97 explicitly distinguishes the full profinite category from the pro-p category: a virtually free profinite group need not act on a profinite tree. Its example is the semidirect product of the profinite integers by an involution which negates the 2-component and fixes the other prime components. Theorem 2.5, printed p.108, proves only that the quotient of a virtually projective group by the closed normal subgroup generated by its torsion is projective. It does not give the three-reflection presentation required in B.5.

## Exact consequence for the current frontier

For an actual negative peripheral involution alpha, the group F semidirect C2 is virtually free profinite. This fact alone does not justify treating it as the profinite completion of a discrete graph of finite groups. In particular, it does not supply three torsion representatives whose pairwise products generate the prescribed inertia groups and whose total subgroup is the whole extension. The generation criterion already recorded in Part B therefore remains a genuine additional gate. The counterexample above does not refute that criterion or the desired conclusion in the peripheral rank-two case: it has a different action. It prevents importing an unrestricted full-profinite realization theorem from the pro-p statements.

Next useful attack: establish a realization theorem with the actual peripheral marking, or construct the three sections and prove their full finite-quotient generation directly. No inference about involution conjugacy, torsion centralizers, or the existence of the proposed glued involution is promoted here.
