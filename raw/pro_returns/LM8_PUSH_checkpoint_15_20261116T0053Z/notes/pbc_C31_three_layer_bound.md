original generators g_(l+1),...,g_h as an F_3 basis. For each generator g_j
of G_l, read the matrix M_j directly from the original word for
`g_i ^ g_j`, after quotienting to G_h. All these matrices are independently
checked invertible. Rows use right conjugation. Replacing each action by its
inverse gives exactly the same coinvariant relation space, so this convention
does not affect the homology calculations.

`H_i(G_l,A) = H_i(G_l/N,A_N) = 0` for every i >= 0.

## Homology comparison and the exponent bound

All homology in this paragraph uses Z_3 coefficients. For a finite group,
this is its integral homology tensored with Z_3, so it retains precisely the
three-primary part. A finite normal 2-kernel has zero positive homology with
these coefficients. Its quotient map therefore induces natural isomorphisms
on homology in every degree.

For an elementary abelian 3-kernel A in E with quotient Q, the homology
Hochschild--Serre spectral sequence is

`E^2_(a,b) = H_a(Q,H_b(A,Z_3)) => H_(a+b)(E,Z_3)`.

In total degree two the terms are H_2(Q,Z_3), H_1(Q,A), and
H_0(Q,wedge^2 A). The last equality is the integral identity
H_2(A,Z)=wedge^2 A, followed by tensoring with Z_3.

For A_18, the last two terms vanish by the explicit calculations. The only
possible outgoing differential from E^2_(2,0) is d_2 to A_Q, which also
vanishes. There is no incoming differential to this bottom-row term, and no
later outgoing differential because its first index would become negative.
Consequently the ACTUAL quotient map induces an isomorphism

`H_2(G_104,Z_3) -> H_2(G_86,Z_3)`.

Combining the normal 2-kernel comparisons gives the natural isomorphism

`H_2(G_176,Z_3) -> H_2(G_26,Z_3)`.

This is stronger than an exponent estimate. It permits a three-primary
source-centralization computation in the actual 26-generator quotient,
provided the target and all genuine original correction words are RECOLLECTED
there and the resulting ORIGINAL source identity is fully replayed. Raw
central tails must not be truncated; the earlier 104-generator experiment
demonstrated why this shortcut is invalid.

For A_9 the H_1 term vanishes and the exterior-square coinvariants have
exponent 3. The spectral-sequence filtration therefore shows that the
exponent of H_2(G_26,Z_3) divides three times any annihilator of
H_2(G_17,Z_3). No splitting of this filtration is assumed. The normal
2^13 comparison identifies the latter with H_2(G_4,Z_3).

## Elementary base-group proof, independent of multiplier software

The actual quotient G_4 has relative orders (3,2,3,3). All four powers are
trivial. The original conjugation relations, truncated only as GROUP words,
are checked verbatim by the standalone program. They give

`A=<g_3,g_4>=C_3^2` normal, and `G_4/A=C_6` generated by g_1,g_2.

On the ordered A basis (g_3,g_4), g_1 acts by
`U=[[1,0],[1,1]]` and g_2 acts by `-I`. A generator g_1 g_2 of C_6
therefore acts by -U, and `-U-I=[[1,0],[2,1]]` modulo 3 is invertible.
The cyclic resolution gives H_1(C_6,A)=0. Equivalently, the order-two
subgroup acts by -I, hence has zero coinvariants and positive homology on A.
Also H_2(C_6,Z_3)=0. The same spectral sequence makes H_2(G_4,Z_3) a
subquotient of `(wedge^2 A)_(C_6)`, which is killed by 3. This proves the
needed base annihilator without a Schur multiplier implementation.
