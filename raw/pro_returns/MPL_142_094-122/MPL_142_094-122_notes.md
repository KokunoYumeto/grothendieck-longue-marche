## I. Lifting automorphisms and recognizing Galois actions (scans 094–100)

### R01 — The endomorphism/automorphism boundary

**Location:** scans 094, equations (48)–(49), and 096–098, reopening of the lifting problem. **Kind:** reading question. **Context search:** `endomorphisme`, `présentation-génération`.

The text reopens the problem with “un endomorphisme” (096), whereas the stabilizer criterion and subsequent bijections use an automorphism hypothesis that needs to be made explicit in a mathematical reconstruction of the argument. For a group homomorphism \(\theta:G\to G\), the formula \(F(aH)=\theta(a)gH\) is well defined when \(\theta(H)\subseteq gHg^{-1}\), but equality and bijectivity require additional hypotheses; taking \(H=G\ne1\) and \(\theta\) trivial already shows that an endomorphism of a one-point fibre can exist without \(\theta(H)\) being conjugate to \(H\). **Context relationship—adds:** `LM_140-3_text.tex`, scans 186–187, explicitly separates an “automorphisme (ou du moins un endomorphisme)” from the extra “présentation-génération” requirement, providing a close warning about the same logical distinction. This comparison does not authorize replacing any occurrence of *endomorphisme* in the frozen text.

### R02 — Proving the transporter criterion

**Location:** scan 094, equations (47)–(49). **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context search:** `Transp`, `transporteur`.

The assertion “\(\Theta(\pi'_0)\) conjugué à \(\pi'_0\)” is a precise lifting criterion once \(\Theta\) is assumed to be an automorphism and the connected fibre is identified with \(G/H\). Writing a semilinear bijective lift as \(F(aH)=\Theta(a)gH\) proves the condition \(gHg^{-1}=\Theta(H)\), and two choices give the same lift exactly when they differ by right multiplication by \(H\). **Context relationship—restates with a different direction convention:** `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 58, describes morphisms through a transporter modulo a subgroup acting on the left; the present right quotient must be checked from the chosen coset action, not copied from that earlier formula. The finite-cover interpretation lies in the Galois-category framework of Grothendieck (1971, Exposé V, §§4–5), while the displayed calculation supplies the local proof.

### R03 — The ambiguity of a lift is structured

**Location:** scan 094, equation (49) and right margin. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context search:** `Norm`, `normalisateur`, `action extérieure`.

The “choix possibles” form, when nonempty, a torsor for the group of \(G\)-equivariant automorphisms of \(G/H\), abstractly \(N_G(H)/H\) with the appropriate left/right convention. Thus existence of a lift does not select one, and a statement about an outer automorphism has still less data for selecting its induced action on a particular subgroup or pointed cover. **Context relationship—adds:** `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 480, explicitly observes that an outer action on \(\widehat\pi_{0,3}\) does not automatically induce an outer action on a specified open subgroup; equation (49) here exposes the finite-cover ambiguity behind that warning. A useful next step is to track how the torsor changes under composition, rather than treating independent choices of lifts as an action.

### R05 — Rational points require pointwise fixing

**Location:** scan 095, condition (a), upper middle. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context search:** `points rationnels`, `points.*fixes`, `admissible`, `rat.`.

### R12 — A concrete nonuniqueness test using G_m

**Location:** scan 096, “En fait, c'est faux pour \(\mathbf G_m\)” and uniqueness paragraph. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high, because the example is an explicit calculation with the power covers of \(\mathbf G_m\). **Context search:** `cyclotomique`, `chi_.*Gamma`, `non.trivialité`.

Take \(X=\mathbf G_{m,\mathbf Q}\) with the single base point \(1\): its geometric profinite fundamental group is \(\widehat{\mathbf Z}(1)\), and the based Galois action on the power-cover tower is multiplication by the cyclotomic character. Hence distinct elements of the nontrivial kernel of \(G_{\mathbf Q}\to\widehat{\mathbf Z}^{\times}\) induce the same automorphism, so uniqueness fails in this precise example even though this says nothing by itself about sufficiency of (a)–(b). **Context relationship—adds:** `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 85, explicitly stresses cyclotomic action on peripheral groups; the present test isolates the loss of information when the whole geometric group is peripheral and abelian. This is a diagnostic example, not a claim of novelty or an identification of the ambiguous marginal referent.

### R15 — Why extra transcendence can create a kernel

**Location:** scan 096, same field-of-moduli paragraph. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the conditional example, because the action factors through restriction to the smaller field by base change. **Context search:** `corps de définition`, `corps ... modul`, `extension ... base`, `isotriv`, `transcendance`.

The action of \(G_K\) therefore factors through \(G_L\), and the nontrivial geometric kernel of \(G_K\to G_L\), witnessed for example by the extension obtained by adjoining \(\sqrt t\) over \(\overline L(t)\), prevents uniqueness. **Context relationship—differs:** `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 24, replaces the base field by the finite constant field \(H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)\) to obtain geometric connectedness, whereas this example concerns descent across a transcendental extension; scan 502 provides the closest faithfulness question over a number field. The example supports a need to exclude superfluous transcendence but does not show that a field-of-moduli condition alone removes every kernel.

### R18 — A finite counterexample to image stability alone

**Location:** scan 098, upper insertion and equation (52). **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high, because the obstruction is an explicit orbit count. **Context search:** `multiplicité`, `cardinal.*fibre`, `orbites`, followed by `C_2`, `C_\{2\}`, `Z/2`.

### R19 — One chosen image per orbit determines the lift

## Scope of the topological literature comparisons

**Brown.** Theorem 3.4 is a groupoid pushout theorem for an interior-cover decomposition with representative base points and the specified transport data; §4 treats adjunction spaces, with the homotopy-extension condition in Remark 4.5 and CW applications in Remark 4.6. Lemma 1.1 concerns full representative subgroupoids, and §1 defines quotient relations through normal closure. These supply proof mechanisms, not automatic validation of the present drawings, degeneracies, equivariance, or uncertain formulas.

**Deligne.** The introduction, p. 85, distinguishes topological sector filters from the other realizations under positive rescaling. Section 15.9, pp. 233–235, treats tangential fibre functors and explicitly compares real blowups and their circles of directions; the subsequent sections separately construct the étale and de Rham versions. This comparison does not identify a ray with a normalized arithmetic vector or prove the manuscript's claim about actual canonical paths.
