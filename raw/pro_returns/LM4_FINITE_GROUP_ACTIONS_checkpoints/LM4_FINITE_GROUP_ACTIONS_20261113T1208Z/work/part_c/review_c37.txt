Conclusion: the proof passes in its stated scope, namely the whole closed set X_2. It does not by itself supply a global contraction on the complementary strata.

1. Primary source. I opened the full author PDF of Grandoni--Ravi--Singh--Zenklusen, New Approaches to Multi-Objective Optimization (17 July 2013), https://people.idsia.ch/~grandoni/Pubblicazioni/GRSZ13mp.pdf. Theorem 4.7 explicitly allows nonatomic signed measures. Its d=2 conclusion gives one circular interval. The text's inaccurate general counting sentence is not needed: on the single interval at this planar step, at most two cuts give at most three pieces, so one of the two equal-measure halves is a single interval. The complement and halving argument then gives all fractions. Polygonal approximation with fixed endpoints and compactness of the two interval endpoints gives C.37.3 for every continuous planar path.

2. Affine group. In the product (a,b)(a',b')=(a+a',b+e^a b'), conjugation by (0,b/(e^a-1)) sends (a,b) to (a,0). The displayed chart sends left multiplication by (ra,0) to translation by (r,0), and the translation chart does the same when a=0. Both alternatives in the vector identity give exactly the group words stated in C.37.2, in the displayed order. Applying this in the simply connected group gives equality of universal-cover endpoints, not merely equality after projection.

3. Compact word isotopies. The parameter simplex 0<=a,b<=t<=1 is compact. The path u -> f_(ut)^epsilon f_(ub) f_(ua)^(-1) is jointly continuous in the compact-open topology on maps and inverses. Its based lift is the product of the corresponding based lifted paths, so its endpoint is precisely the lifted fractional power selected by C.37.2.

4. Limit-orbit classification. The metabelian identity passes from U_2 to X_2 pointwise. In the nonabelian case the derived subgroup is path connected, abelian, normal, and has no common fixed point. Its orbits are connected nontrivial intervals or circles. An included interval endpoint would be fixed by the whole orbit action; hence these orbits are open and the derived subgroup is transitive. Closure plus open evaluation identifies a transitive abelian image with R or S^1, with its actual topology. The normalizer calculation then gives the full positive affine group on a line, and only rotations on a circle. Thus the Lie logarithms used in the limit argument are justified for the actual image.

5. Residual estimate. For each fixed n, the bounded integers floor(n r_i) admit a constant subsequence and differ from nr by at most one in the limit. The residual is again an exact lifted fractional power with fraction in [0,1), so its endpoint belongs to the same compact three-word family independently of n. On a line, intrinsic compact-open continuity makes this a compact subset of the identified Lie group. Its logarithms are bounded. The commutation identity and the one-parameter centralizer in Aff^+(R) justify addition of the logarithms and division by n.

6. Circle lifts. The residual word loop on O_i has identity endpoint in the universal covering group, so it is null-homotopic in N_(O_i). Evaluation at x_i and h x_i gives a two-point loop of winding zero. For fixed n the word paths converge uniformly on compact sets; the limiting two-point path stays separated, because each limiting word is a plane homeomorphism. Winding is therefore preserved. On the Jordan circle it is the signed real angle nA-m_nT-C_n. This proves the real identity, including when f_t restricts to the identity but its based angle is a nonzero integer.

7. Assembly. The density argument genuinely supplies continuity for parameters varying with points. The inverse loop has the identical orbit/operator decomposition and produces the orbitwise inverse logarithmic action. Applying the same argument proves inverse continuity. Equivariance already holds on U_2 and extends to its closure. The final paragraph correctly retains compatibility with the complementary strata as the unresolved part of C.12.
