C.43: exact local product, quotient, and global trivialization

The compatible closed Polish group from C.42 acts transitively on the covering plane and maps each connected level component to the corresponding component. A compact-arc displacement bound is an actual open identity neighborhood. Effros openness therefore supplies the exact arc embeddings required by the primary regular-family definition, not merely pointwise control or semicontinuity of a partition.

The transverse space in an Aarts--Oversteegen box is metrizable. The product-cancellation proof from C.33 applies to its product with an interval: local path connectivity descends, an embedded open arc is open by invariance of domain on its product, and the only alternative endpoint has the wrong local second homology. Hence the transverse space is locally an interval. F is constant on plaques, and its openness makes the transverse map an open continuous interval map. Such a map is strictly monotone. Reparametrizing by that map gives actual projection charts for the specified F.

The component quotient is proved open by transporting the compact leaf arc joining a given point to an open set. This argument does not assume a foliation quotient is open. Distinct plaques in an F-projection chart have distinct F-values, so they cannot be joined by a global level component outside the chart. The transversal therefore gives the claimed genuine local chart of the component quotient. The induced factor map to R is a local homeomorphism.

An open quotient of the second-countable plane is second countable. The actual compatible action induces transitivity on this quotient. Baillif--Gabard consequently applies with all its hypotheses verified. The resulting connected Hausdorff one-manifold is a line: the circle cannot have an onto open map to R. A local homeomorphism from a line onto a line has a constant local monotonicity sign and is a global homeomorphism. Thus whole lifted fibres, not only their components, are proper lines.

Plane orientation together with the real transverse coordinate orients all leaf charts coherently. The auxiliary complete flow has precisely those leaves by the inspected primary construction and Remark 4.4. Its orbit parametrizations are free, since a periodic orbit would be a circle. Saturating a small F-section gives a continuous bijection R times I onto F inverse I; invariance of domain proves this is the claimed homeomorphism.

The additive transition equation is consistent: sigma_j=R(a_ij,sigma_i) gives a_ij=a_ik+a_kj. The locally finite partition-of-unity averages b_i=sum rho_j a_ij satisfy b_i=a_ik+b_k, so R(b_i,sigma_i) is a single global section. The signs in this patching formula are correct. This proves the exact F-preserving product, without assigning operator equivariance to the auxiliary flow.

C.44: the actual deck action and all five displayed forms

The equality F gamma=F+eta(gamma) follows from the original covering equation. In the circle case, the difference is a continuous integer-valued function on a connected plane; in the line case it is zero. Its value is the actual image winding of a source loop, so no alternative abstract deck character is substituted.

Every deck map preserves plane orientation, while its real target shift is increasing; its fibre homeomorphisms are consequently increasing. Restriction of ker eta to one fibre is faithful, free, and proper. The closed discrete orbit and nearest-neighbor argument gives precisely the trivial group or Z. It excludes a dense translation subgroup by using properness, rather than just freeness.

For cyclic kernel, the displacement of the positive generator is positive on the whole connected plane. The displayed piecewise formula k_t^n(r k_t(0)) is continuous at integral parameters, strictly increasing and onto in every fibre, and a continuous bijection of the plane. Invariance of domain gives its inverse continuity. It conjugates the specified kernel generator to unit translation while leaving F fixed.

A nonzero base-shift quotient in this case would have a compact circle-cylinder strip mapping onto the complete source quotient. That would make the open planar orbit compact, an exact contradiction. When the kernel is trivial and the shift image is mZ, the interpolation rho_t and recursion rho_(t+m)=h_t rho_t match at both ends and yield a continuous F-preserving conjugacy to pure base translation. The final whole-fibre labels are exactly Z/eta(Gamma), with component R/ker eta in the established coordinates. In particular, surjectivity of each restricted source covering is proved from these formulas and is not presumed for arbitrary restricted coverings.

C.45: one common action, endpoint topology, and the three domains

Both target lifts are included in a single closed Polish compatible group. Each source lift has the two unique compatible target lifts after its initial values are chosen, so the resulting source action is genuinely transitive.

The stabilizer of one F1-level acts transitively on its whole proper line and on its F2-image. The image is an interval. A nondegenerate transitive interval for increasing homeomorphisms has no included endpoint, so it is a Polish open interval. The inclusion of actual point stabilizers and the two open orbit maps prove that the restriction of F2 is open. Its strict monotonicity gives injectivity. In the constant case, source transitivity transports constancy to every level and the two quotient maps prove that the induced real map is continuous, open, and onto.

In the independent case, invariance of domain makes the pair map an actual homeomorphism onto an open domain. The two coordinate sections are intervals. Each finite endpoint map is obtained by descending a continuous evaluation along the open first-coordinate orbit map. Its image is all R by transitivity of the second-coordinate action, and its stabilizer inclusion proves openness. This validates its claimed topology without an assumption about regular boundary graphs.

The monotonicity cases in the displayed coordinate changes are correct. With two decreasing endpoints the use of beta inverse reverses both inequalities and gives x<y<beta inverse alpha(x). With two increasing endpoints alpha inverse gives x<y<alpha inverse beta(x). In both cases the transition is increasing and has displacement positive everywhere; fundamental-interval coordinates give its exact conjugacy to unit translation. With one finite endpoint the same inverse and, when necessary, exchange give the ordered half-plane. The full increasing coordinate-pair groups follow by preserving their separate endpoint graphs. All changes are by separate real homeomorphisms, so the original source/target covering equations are retained.
