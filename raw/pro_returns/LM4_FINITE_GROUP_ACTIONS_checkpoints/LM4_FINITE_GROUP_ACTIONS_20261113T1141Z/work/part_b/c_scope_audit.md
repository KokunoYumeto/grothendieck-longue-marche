## 2. The exact map at level 0

Let `A = Homeo(X,S)` and let `u:Γ→A` be a strict action of an arbitrary discrete group. Put `H=u(Γ)`, `M=C_A(H)`, and base `M` at the identity. Let `N` be the weak automorphism groupoid of this action in the categorical group `Π₁A`, with its identity weak automorphism as base object. An automorphism of that base object is a loop class `z∈π₁(A,1)` satisfying the naturality equality

`u(γ) z u(γ)⁻¹ = z` for every `γ∈Γ`.

Consequently

`π₁N = π₁(A,1)^Γ`.

The comparison sends an actual centralizing loop to the same loop in `A`. Its exact map is

`ι_*: π₁(C_A(H),1) → π₁(A,1)^Γ`,

where `ι` is subgroup inclusion. The image lies in the displayed invariants because the representatives commute pointwise with every `u(γ)`. Finiteness of `Γ` is not used in this groupoid calculation. For a transporter based at any equivariant homeomorphism, translation by that homeomorphism identifies its based fundamental-group map with the corresponding automorphism-space map.

Every based loop in `C_A(H)` lies in `A⁰`, and every based homotopy does too. Thus its source is also `π₁(C_{A⁰}(H),1)`. On a hyperbolic type, the already proved contractibility of `A⁰` makes the target zero, and level 0 is exactly the vanishing of this source. For the plane, annulus, and torus, the targets are respectively the invariant subgroups of `Z`, `Z`, and `Z²`. Nonzero fundamental group of the actual centralizer alone does not establish failure in those three cases: the kernel of the displayed inclusion map is the required datum.

## 4. Exact relationship of the sphere and punctured comparisons

Let `R_α` be an irrational rotation of `S²` with poles `p_+,p_-`. Its identity centralizer is the same subgroup `C` inside each of

`A₂ = Homeo(S²,{p_+,p_-})`, `A₁ = Homeo(S²,{p_+})`, and `A₀ = Homeo(S²)`.

Indeed, a commuting homeomorphism preserves the set of fixed points `{p_+,p_-}`, and a path from the identity cannot permute this finite set. The angular/latitude parametrization of C.25 gives `C ≃ S¹`, with a full axis rotation as generator. Inclusions induced by forgetting marks yield the commutative diagram on based fundamental groups

```text
π₁C = Z  ──identity──>  π₁A₂ = Z  ──identity──>  π₁A₁ = Z  ──mod 2──>  π₁A₀ = Z/2.
```

Each identity assertion follows from the same rotation circle: it generates both marked homeomorphism groups by the plane and annulus comparisons proved in C.10. The last map is the inclusion of the axis-rotation circle into `SO(3)` under the established homeomorphism-group homotopy equivalence. The irrational rotation is joined to the identity through the same axis rotations in all three groups, so conjugation acts trivially on all these fundamental groups. The displayed groups are therefore the actual weak-transporter target groups as well.

There are no further finite invariant puncture sets to try for this action. A nonpolar point has an infinite orbit under `R_α`, whereas both poles are fixed. Thus deleting one or two poles gives admitted examples with zero kernel; deleting a finite invariant set cannot convert this particular action into a hyperbolic example.

## 5. The compact-open topology and the universal-lift reduction

For this finite-type setup, restriction

`r: Homeo(X,S) → Homeo(X\\S)`

is a topological-group isomorphism for the compact-open topologies. Every homeomorphism of `U=X\\S` extends uniquely to the finite end compactification `X`, so `r` is an algebraic bijection. Restriction is continuous. For completeness, continuity of its inverse can be checked directly. Choose disjoint connected target end neighborhoods `W_j` of diameter below a prescribed uniform tolerance in `X`, and let the complementary target core `C` be compact in `U`. Choose a compact source core `K` large enough that `f₀⁻¹(C)` lies in its interior, and connected source tails `V_i` outside `K` with `f₀(V_i)⊂W_{σ(i)}`. The compact-open condition `f⁻¹(C)⊂int K` is open because inversion is continuous in the homeomorphism group of the locally compact, locally connected surface `U`. Choose one point `z_i` in each `V_i` and impose the open conditions `f(z_i)∈W_{σ(i)}`. Connectedness now gives `f(V_i)⊂W_{σ(i)}`: the image avoids `C` and contains that prescribed point. This controls the extensions at all ends uniformly. Ordinary compact-open control on the complementary compact core controls the rest of `X`. Translating this neighborhood argument from `f₀` to any homeomorphism proves continuity of `r⁻¹`.

The canonical-lift map in the paragraph after C.12 is consequently a continuous injective homomorphism for the same topology and same ambient group used throughout Part C:

`j:A⁰→Homeo(Ũ)`, `p j(f)=f p`, `j(f)δ=δj(f)` for every deck transformation `δ∈π₁U`.

Centre-freeness gives uniqueness of the commuting lift, and proves the exact equality

`j(C_{A⁰}(H)) = j(A⁰) ∩ C_{Homeo(Ũ)}(H̃)`,

where `H̃` is the group of all lifts of elements of `H`. In particular, based centralizer loops lift to closed loops. This equality provides no null-homotopy in that intersection. For arbitrary `H`, metric geodesic interpolation need not commute with `H̃` and need not consist of homeomorphisms. Those are separate required properties, and neither has been established by the lift comparison.
