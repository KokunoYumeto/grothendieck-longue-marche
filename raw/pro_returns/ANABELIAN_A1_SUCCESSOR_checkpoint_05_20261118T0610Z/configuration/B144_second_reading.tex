\medskip\noindent\textbf{The explicit wild subgroup exists.}
The cyclotomic image of local inertia contains an
open principal-unit subgroup.  Its quotient by
the wild image is pro-prime-to-$p$, so that
principal pro-$p$ image lies in the wild image.
This justifies the lift of $1+p^s$, with the
stated $1+4\mathbb Z_2$ convention.  The explicit
values $1+p$ for odd $p$ and $5$ for $p=2$ are
available over $\mathbb Q_p$.

The closure of the cyclic subgroup is pro-$p$.
A nonzero kernel of $\mathbb Z_p\to C$ would
make it finite, contrary to the nonzero logarithm
of the prescribed cyclotomic value.  Thus it is
actually $\mathbb Z_p$, including at $p=2$.
Its fixed field contains the strict henselization
and is algebraic over the selected henselian
field.  The residue field is exactly the
algebraic closure of the finite residue field,
and algebraicity preserves the specified finite
transcendence degree.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The structural cell is well typed.}
For $c\in Z(G_F)$, its inverse acts naturally
and equivariantly on every continuous $G_F$-set.
The induced automorphism of $p^*$ has a
right-adjoint mate $\alpha_c:p_*\to p_*$.
CHW Notation~2.2 specifies precisely a cell
$q_*f_*\to p_*$ for a relative geometric
morphism, so the displayed pair with identity
underlying geometric morphism has the required
type.  Its topological map is the identity and
therefore preserves closed points.

The inverse in this construction is consistent
with the claimed actual point function.  For an
etale object over the affine line, applying
$c^{-1}$ to coordinates gives a map of fibre
functors from the fibre at $c(a)$ to the fibre
at $a$.  On objects pulled back from the base
this is the prescribed $c^{-1}$ action.  Its
mate supplies exactly the relative identification
with the composed point.  Thus the actual
point function is $a\mapsto c(a)$ with the
written convention.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{A relative isomorphism cannot erase the point function.}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Finite extensions and normal cores.}
Every finite extension of the fixed field has
absolute Galois group a nonzero open subgroup
of $\mathbb Z_p$.  It again has a nontrivial
center, so the same explicit relative topos
construction applies.  On the other hand, its
normal core in any open local Galois group is
abelian and normal.  B.143's group-theoretic
argument excludes every nontrivial such subgroup,
and the core is therefore trivial.  This is an
application of the proved normal-subgroup result,
not an inference that all nontrivial closed
subgroups have a nontrivial core.

The family in every finite transcendence degree
uses algebraically independent elements already
in $\mathbb Q_p$, whose generated field has
closure $\mathbb Q_p$.  No embedding of a
general henselian field is presumed.
