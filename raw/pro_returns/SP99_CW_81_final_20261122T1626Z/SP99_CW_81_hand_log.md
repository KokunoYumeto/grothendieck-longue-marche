## Cross-page comparison

The strongest recurring aids in this piece are the long low compression of prose, separate tall ascenders and descenders, dotted short i versus a longer hooked j, and the contrast between an angular Sigma and the upright added to form E. None licenses an automatic global substitution. The per-page rows specify where the habit was actually checked and where it failed or remained unclear.

Three distinctions need particular care in subsequent work on this hand:

1. **A letter's return versus another character.** A very short final e or s can disappear into an exit stroke. This affects de/des, contenu/contenue, choisi/choisie and several clipped or cancelled words. The direct reading of masculine un/contenu/tel on024 is secure; it does not settle every compressed ending elsewhere.
2. **A letter stroke versus an annotation.** The q descender may run through the next baseline; a capital E may have a detached upper bar; a curved insertion leader may look like a parenthesis. Their paths and attachment must be read before adding a cancellation, closure sign, word or bracket.
3. **A genuine earlier form versus retracing.** Some revised capitals and indices retain independently recognizable older forms; others contain only extra strokes. The I under K on020 and V under E on021 are identifiable. The curved spine and doubled bottom of E_beta on023 do not securely identify a prior Sigma. Heavy black patches on026 retain candidates without claiming a recoverable full earlier letter.

There is no uniform rule for the length of a plus's upper arm, the presence of abbreviation stops, or the shape and presence of accents. The later paired minus signs help read short crossing pluses, but a saturated patch can still conceal the distinction. The local contrast between def on024 and déf on026, and the unaccented first e in determinés on025, shows why accents were checked separately on each leaf.

The independent audit added a fourth concrete caution: an angular Sigma can consist of a detached horizontal cap above a nearly flat first diagonal, a second diagonal and a basal arm. Those four strokes can resemble a barred capital. On018, comparison with019 changed a provisional closure interpretation into an explicit plain/barred doubt. In contrast, the signed pair on020 has independently additional high closure lines. The final overwritten E on021 has its own top arm, without an independently established extra bar. These distinctions are local visual decisions, not a rule to delete high strokes everywhere.
