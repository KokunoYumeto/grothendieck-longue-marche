1. Primary planar inputs and the exact carrier homeomorphism

Primary source: D. B. A. Epstein, Curves on 2-manifolds and isotopies,
Acta Mathematica 115 (1966), 83--107, the retained verified PDF
work/part_b/research_data/epstein1966_isotopies.pdf.
Theorem 1.7 on printed p.85 states that a nullhomotopic simple closed
curve in a surface bounds a disk. Its proof explicitly treats the
sphere case as Schoenflies. Corollary 1.8 on pp.85--86 states that
a noncompact simply connected surface without boundary is a plane,
and constructs its homeomorphism by nested disks and annuli.
Appendix A1 on p.105 supplies the topological-curve comparison and
explicitly uses the disk extension version of Schoenflies.
Printed pp.85,86,105 were actually viewed and the full relevant
statements/proofs read. The cited Newman p.169 was not inspected;
the present argument uses Epstein's stated Theorem 1.7 and Corollary 1.8.

Here is a map-and-inverse construction of the global carrier chart
required in section 3 of the preceding note. Compactify the original
Euclidean plane by infinity to S^2 and put C=boundary(D_v).
The preceding finite-circle construction gives exactly two complementary
components, the bounded one and the component containing infinity.
For any a not on C, S^2 minus {a} is an explicit plane by stereographic
coordinates, so C is nullhomotopic in that punctured sphere.

Apply Epstein 1.7 first with a=infinity. The disk it supplies has
interior equal to the bounded component: its interior is nonempty and
relatively open and closed in S^2 minus C, and its compact closure
misses infinity. Thus the supplied disk is exactly D_v.
Apply the theorem with a in int(D_v). The same component argument
gives a closed disk E=S^2 minus int(D_v), containing infinity in its
interior. Choose disk homeomorphisms f:D_v->closed unit disk and
j:E->closed unit disk, each with its inverse.

Let a0=j(infinity), which lies in the open unit disk. The explicit
disk automorphism m_a0(z)=(z-a0)/(1-conjugate(a0)*z) has inverse
m_a0^{-1}(z)=(z+a0)/(1+conjugate(a0)*z). Its denominators do not
vanish on the closed unit disk because |a0|<1.
Put j0=m_a0 composed with j, so j0(infinity)=0.
The circle homeomorphism c=f|C composed with (j0|C)^{-1} extends
radially by C0(r*zeta)=r*c(zeta), C0(0)=0.
Its inverse is the same radial formula with c^{-1}.
Set e=C0 composed with j0. Then e(infinity)=0 and e|C=f|C.

The global plane homeomorphism s and its inverse are
s(z)=f(z) for z in D_v;
s(z)=e(z)/|e(z)|^2 for z outside D_v;
s^{-1}(a)=f^{-1}(a) for |a|<=1;
s^{-1}(a)=e^{-1}(a/|a|^2) for |a|>=1.
The two formulas agree on the common boundary. Equivalently they
paste to inverse continuous maps of the two compact disks in S^2,
sending infinity to infinity, and hence restrict to inverse plane
homeomorphisms. This proves s(D_v)=closed unit disk with the exact
global chart needed for the earlier r_v formula. No metric control
of s is used: the carrier radius remains measured in the original e_Q.

2. The actual target component and its universal covering

Let M be the component of O minus F_X containing v. Both maps
F(u,t)=W_{u,t}(v) and f(u,t)=w_{u,t} have image in M:
their domain P x I is connected, they avoid F_X, and both take v
on P x {0}. The target M is an open connected subspace of the
original plane O, and is therefore a surface without boundary.
It is noncompact: a nonempty compact open subspace of the plane
would be closed as well, contrary to connectedness and noncompactness
of the plane.

Take its actual universal covering c_M:tilde(M)->M, with a specified
point vtilde above v. This is a simply connected surface without
boundary. It cannot be compact because its continuous surjection
onto M would then make M compact. Its ordinary countable manifold
structure can also be seen directly: loops in an open planar set
are homotopic to finite rational polygons by sufficiently close
straight interpolation, so pi1(M) is countable; the covering has
countably many sheets over a countable disk-chart cover.
Epstein Corollary 1.8 applies to tilde(M), giving a specified
homeomorphism psi:tilde(M)->R^2 and its inverse psi^{-1}.

3. Both lifted tracks and a homotopy relative to the entire boundary

Let Z=P x I and
A=(P x {0,1}) union ({u0} x I).
This A is connected. The homotopy (u,t,s)->(u,(1-s)t)
retracts Z to P x {0}, where both F and f are constant at v.
Thus both induced homomorphisms on pi1(Z,(u0,0)) are trivial.
Each map has a unique lift Ftilde or ftilde to tilde(M), taking
(u0,0) to vtilde. Explicitly, choose any path in Z from (u0,0)
to a point z, lift its F-image or f-image from vtilde, and take
the endpoint. Triviality of the induced homomorphism makes the
endpoint independent of that path. Local covering sheets prove
continuity of these maps.

The original maps F and f agree on A. Their lifted restrictions
agree there as well: they are lifts of the same map from the
connected A, with the same value at (u0,0), and uniqueness in
overlapping local sheets gives equality throughout A.

The following is therefore an actual continuous homotopy in M:
T(u,t,s)=c_M(psi^{-1}((1-s)*psi(Ftilde(u,t))
                                  +s*psi(ftilde(u,t)))).
Its s=0 value is F, its s=1 value is f, and its value on A is the
common original value at every s. Equal lifted values stay equal
under the displayed straight interpolation. In particular
T(u,0,s)=v, T(u,1,s)=h_u(v), T(u0,t,s)=v.
This proves the required relative comparison in the actual obstacle
complement, with no alteration of F_X and no chosen new point tracks.
The use of psi carries no original metric estimate or circle claim.

4. Finite local extension of the two-parameter comparison

The projected compact family pi(T(u,t,s)) stays in N minus X.
Choose a relatively compact open neighborhood U of its entire
image, with closure disjoint from X. Apply the already explicit
finite local-extension product from the initial-fixed-region note
to embeddings of the one-point polyhedron {y} in N minus X.
Its compact parameter space is now P x I, and its time coordinate
is s. For each parameter (u,t), the initial embedding is the point
B_{u,t}(y)=pi(F(u,t)).

The product provides L_{u,t,s}, supported in U, with
L_{u,t,0}=identity and
L_{u,t,s}(pi(F(u,t)))=pi(T(u,t,s)).
For completeness the product at each subdivided s-interval uses
the actual successive point embeddings e_i and increments
phi(e_i) composed with phi(e_{i-1})^{-1}, in the order proved
in IFR.4--6. The finite partition of unity on the parameter space
gives the intermediate times. Every factor is identity when its
track is constant. This exact feature gives
L_{u,0,s}=L_{u,1,s}=L_{u0,t,s}=identity.
Extension by identity across X is continuous because every support
is contained in the single compact closure(U), disjoint from X.

Define A_{u,t,s}=L_{u,t,s} composed with B_{u,t}, with inverse
B_{u,t}^{-1} composed with L_{u,t,s}^{-1}.
These are jointly continuous maps in the ordinary homeomorphism
group topology, including their inverses, by the compact-open
product/inverse comparison already proved in the initial-region note.
They fix X; at s=0 they equal B, and on t=0, t=1 and u=u0
they equal the original B for every s.
Put B'_{u,t}=A_{u,t,1}. Then
B'_{u,0}=identity, B'_{u,1}=b_u, B'_{u0,t}=identity, and
B'_{u,t}(y)=pi(w_{u,t}).

The pointed lifts are exact as well. Lift A while fixing the
original point x above p. For fixed (u,t), its value at v is a
lift of the s-track pi(T(u,t,s)), starting at W_{u,t}(v).
Uniqueness of that path lift gives lambda(A_{u,t,s})(v)=T(u,t,s).
Hence lambda(B'_{u,t})(v)=w_{u,t}; there is no unidentified deck
translation at any parameter value. Each lift fixes all of F_X
by the original constant-track argument.

5. The residual path fixing the new entire orbit

The C81/C88 compact-support comparison supplies the original

Its inverse is (B'_{u,t})^{-1} composed with g_{u,t}.
It fixes X and y at every time, starts at identity, is identity at

Thus it is the explicit relative-(X union {y}) component witness.
