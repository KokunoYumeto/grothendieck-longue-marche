2. Exact filling map, all translates, and diameter equality
----------------------------------------------------------
For a nonempty compact connected A in O, Fill(A) adds the relatively
compact complementary components. An outer Euclidean annulus lies in
one complementary component; all other components have compact closure
in a smaller disk. Thus Fill(A) is compact. Each added component has
nonempty boundary in A, which proves connectedness of the filled union.

A separating ray sends every point outside conv(iota A) into the outer
component. Hence iota Fill(A) is contained in that convex hull. The
diameter of a convex hull equals the diameter of the original set by
the displayed convex-combination calculation. This gives the exact
e-diameter equality, including its reverse inclusion argument.

Every original operator q carries complement components bijectively,
and q and q^(-1) preserve compactness of their closures. The exact
maps therefore give q Fill(A)=Fill(qA). Taking the supremum of the
Euclidean diameters over every q proves
  diam_(e_Q) Fill(A)=diam_(e_Q) A.
No circle extension of q is used.

For an actual arc A with a common fixed endpoint, the compact union
A_P=A union union_u h_u(A) is connected even when P is disconnected.
Its translated union is exactly qA union union_u h_u(qA), by commutation.
The triangle inequality through the two corresponding original points
and the filling equality yield
  diam_(e_Q) Fill(A_P)
     <= diam_(e_Q) A + 2 sup_(pi A) a^+.
The estimate concerns every actual translated carrier in the original
disk. It makes no claim that these different filled sets are disjoint.

5. Actual deck-step example and its nullhomotopy
-----------------------------------------------
The example uses the original Euclidean closed disk, the exact radial
coordinate j(z)=z/(1+|z|), its displayed inverse, and the operator
q=j T_(1,0) j^(-1). The conjugated plane translations form an actual
transitive boundary-fixed centralizer subgroup. Freeness, properness
and discreteness follow from the literal integer translations.

The explicit maps F_(n,s)(a,y)=(a+s beta(y-n),y), with
beta(v)=max(0,1-4|v|), have inverses with the opposite horizontal
increment. Their radial conjugates fix x, commute with q, and extend
by the identity to the circle. The subtraction estimate for j gives
  D_e(H_(n,s),id) <= 4/(n+3/4)
for the map and inverse sum. Pasting the triangular amplitudes on the
specified dyadic intervals gives a continuous based loop. Replacing
each amplitude by (1-r) times itself is an explicit based nullhomotopy;
the same uniform bound proves continuity at the accumulation endpoint.
The common quotient tail for each displacement is 2/(N+7/4).

At a midpoint H(v_n)=qv_n. A single paste on a Q-invariant family
of disk interiors with each boundary fixed preserves every disk. A
left correction fixing v_n would have to send qv_n to v_n inside
one disk. That disk and its q-translate then meet in their interiors.
They cannot be the same compact disk, since the complete integer
orbit would lie in it, contradicting properness. The right correction
has the same argument with q^(-1). Thus the specified one-stage paste
cannot make this correction. The example already has an explicit
nullhomotopy, so this is exactly a limitation of the specified operation.
