DISJOINT ACTUAL TRACES: ONE CONTROLLED AMBIENT CARRIER
====================================================
Unnumbered continuation. Earlier accepted files remain unchanged.
This note gives a controlled ambient correction on the exact actual-
trace domain below, with the relative-component witness retained.

1. The original family and its precise trace domain
----------------------------------------------------
Retain C81, C92, CFT, FCE, IFR and the accepted orbit-track/component-
witness notes. Thus O=int Dbar is the original disk, Q is the actual
closed discrete free proper operator group, pi:O->N is its covering,
and e is the fixed original Euclidean coordinate metric. Use
  e_Q(z,w)=sup_{q in Q}e(qz,qw).
This metric gives the original interior topology and is Q-invariant.
Every later comparison is in these coordinates and with these Q labels.

Let P be a compact connected based finite CW complex, with basepoint
u0. Retain the original continuous family u->h_u in F_X supplied
after IFR, where X is its compact subpolyhedron containing a disk
neighborhood of p=pi(x), F_X fixes pi^-1(X) pointwise, and h_u0=id.
Retain also the ordinary relative-X component witness from IFR.
The resulting normalized ordinary contraction gives B_(u,t) from id
to b_u=desc(h_u), based at id; its unique pointed interior lift is
W_(u,t). The W-family fixes F_Xset=pi^-1(X) pointwise. Its endpoint
is the original h_u by the accepted deck-label comparison.

Write
  a(y)=max_u e_Q(h_u z,z), y=pi(z),
and retain the continuous positive C_0 majorant eta>a from CFT/FCE.
For v in O minus F_Xset put
  A={h_u(v):u in P}, K=Fill(A), a_v=a(pi(v)).             (DT.1)
A is compact and connected, contains v, and misses F_Xset. The
accepted Fill map gives K compact and connected, boundary K subset A,
qK=Fill(qA), and
  e_Q(z,v)<=a_v for every z in K.                        (DT.2)

The domain considered here is the following exact condition on the
given family:
  A intersect qA=empty for every q in Q minus {1}.       (DT.3)
It has the original-family equivalent description
  h_(u')^-1 h_u(v) is not in Qv minus {v}
                for every u,u' in P.                   (DT.4)
Indeed h_u(v)=q h_(u')(v) is equivalent, by commutation and applying
h_(u')^-1, to h_(u')^-1 h_u(v)=qv. This is an equality in the original
cover, so (DT.3) retains every deck label.

For a directly checkable sufficient range, let
  r_v=inf_{q!=1} e_Q(v,qv)>0                            (DT.5)
when Q is nontrivial, using C81's uniform discreteness of that fiber.
The inequality 2a_v<r_v implies (DT.3): an intersection point gives
e_Q(v,qv)<=a_v+a_v. For Q={1}, (DT.3) has no nonidentity labels.
The construction below uses (DT.3) itself. No uniform lower bound for
r_v over different quotient points is needed or asserted.

2. Filling preserves disjointness for these actual translates
--------------------------------------------------------------
Here is the planar comparison with its properness step in full.
For two disjoint compact connected sets A and A' in O, their filled
sets K and K' are either disjoint or one lies in the interior of the
other. To prove it, connectedness of A' and its disjointness from A
place it in a single component of O minus A.

When this is a bounded component, A' lies in int K. The connected
exterior U_K=O minus K lies in the exterior component of O minus A',
since it reaches the original circle. Hence K' subset K. Also
boundary K' subset A' subset int K. A point of K' on boundary K would
therefore be an interior point of K', hence of K, a contradiction.
Thus K' subset int K.

In the other case A' lies in the exterior of K. The connected set K
misses A' and is contained in one component of O minus A'. The
exterior component makes K disjoint from K'; a bounded component
puts K inside int K'. This proves all of the stated alternatives.

Apply this to A'=qA under (DT.3). A nesting qK subset int K makes
q^n K intersect K for every n>=0. Properness of the original action
on the compact K forces the set of powers of q to be finite. A
positive power is then the identity, and iteration gives the
impossible inclusion K subset int K. The latter is impossible for
a nonempty compact subset of the plane: it would be both open and
closed while the plane is connected and noncompact. The reverse
nesting is handled by q^-1. Consequently
  K intersect qK=empty for every q!=1.                 (DT.6)
This argument does not require a separate assertion about torsion in Q.

There is an open neighborhood U of K with compact closure and with
pairwise disjoint Q-translates of its closure. Here are the actual
finite choices. Take a compact neighborhood C of K. Properness makes
  S_C={q:qC intersect C is nonempty}
finite. For each nonidentity q in this finite set, the compact sets
K and qK are disjoint. Choose neighborhoods of these two compact
sets with disjoint closures, and shrink a neighborhood U_q of K so
that its closure lies in the first and q closure(U_q) in the second.
Intersect the finitely many U_q and int C, and shrink once more to
have compact closure in that intersection. The resulting U has the
required property for q in S_C; for the other q it follows from
closure U subset C. These are neighborhoods in the original O.

3. Two nested small Jordan carriers and the actual track
---------------------------------------------------------
The case a_v=0 already fixes Qv for the entire given family; take
the correction to be id. Its descents are a based continuous map
into Homeo_(X union {pi(v)})(N). Path connectedness of P puts this
image in the identity component. Yagasaki's contraction applies
with this compact fixed subpolyhedron, still containing the disk
in X; normalize it by C_t(b)C_t(id)^-1, directed from id to b,
exactly as in IFR. This gives the relative-component witness in
this case. The following construction treats a_v>0. Choose
  epsilon=(eta(pi(v))-a_v)/2>0.
Shrink the U above by intersecting with the open e_Q epsilon-
neighborhood of K. The radius estimate (DT.2) gives
  e_Q(z,v)<a_v+epsilon<eta(pi(v)) for z in U.            (DT.7)
Disjointness of its translated closures is preserved.

The boundary of K misses the closed F_Xset. Apply the accepted
finite-Jordan-neighborhood construction to
  U intersect (int K union (O minus F_Xset)).
This is an open neighborhood of all of K. It gives a closed Jordan
disk D0 with K subset int D0 and D0 inside this neighborhood.
Its boundary misses F_Xset: the boundary is outside K, where the
displayed neighborhood lies in O minus F_Xset.

Apply the same Jordan-neighborhood construction to the filled disk
D0 inside U, obtaining a second closed Jordan disk D1 with
  K subset int D0, D0 subset int D1, D1 subset U.        (DT.8)
Both entire families of Q-translates are pairwise disjoint. Moreover
  diam_(e_Q)(D1)<=2(a_v+epsilon)<2 eta(pi(v)).           (DT.9)
Old fixed points may lie inside these disks. They will be fixed by
every ambient factor below; no deletion of these obstacles is used.

Retain the exact global planar Schoenflies chart s for D0 from the
accepted primary-source comparison and its inverse. In that chart
use the literal retraction
  r(z)=s^-1(s(z)/max(1,|s(z)|)).                         (DT.10)
The fixed original disk coordinate is understood on both sides.
It fixes D0 and sends its exterior to boundary D0. Thus it maps
O minus F_Xset into D0 minus F_Xset. Put
  w_(u,t)=r(W_(u,t)(v)).                                (DT.11)
This is the retracted actual track, with
  w_(u,0)=v, w_(u,1)=h_u(v), w_(u0,t)=v.
Its image T is a compact subset of int D1 minus F_Xset. It lies in
the same component of O minus F_Xset as v because each t-track
starts there. The original endpoint and all Q-orbit labels are kept.

4. Explicit ambient maps on one disk, including their inverses
---------------------------------------------------------------
Use the original Euclidean coordinate on Dbar. The set
  Z=(Dbar minus int D1) union F_Xset
is closed in Dbar: any possible circle accumulation from F_Xset is
already in the first term. It is disjoint from the compact T. Set
  r0=dist_e(T,Z)/4>0.
For a,b in T with |b-a|<r0/2 define on the whole coordinate plane
  chi_a(z)=max(0,1-|z-a|/r0),
  L_(a,b)(z)=z+chi_a(z)(b-a).                           (DT.12)
The support lies in the closed r0-ball about a, which is in
int D1 minus F_Xset. The center is sent exactly from a to b.

For all z,z',
  |L_(a,b)(z)-L_(a,b)(z')|
      >=(1-|b-a|/r0)|z-z'| > |z-z'|/2.                (DT.13)
Here chi_a is 1/r0-Lipschitz. The exact inverse at y is the limit
of the explicit iteration
  z_0=y, z_(n+1)=y-chi_a(z_n)(b-a).                    (DT.14)
Successive differences are bounded by 2^-n |b-a|, so the sequence
is uniformly Cauchy and its limit satisfies L_(a,b)(z)=y. The
same contraction estimate makes this solution unique. This proves
both inverse compositions directly and gives surjectivity as well
as injectivity. Each iterate depends continuously on a,b,y; the
uniform geometric error gives joint continuity of the inverse.
The maps are identity off their supporting ball. Injectivity then
implies that each supporting ball is preserved as a set. In
particular these maps and inverses preserve D1 and fix F_Xset.
They are id when a=b.

Uniform continuity of (u,t)->w_(u,t) on compact P x I gives a finite
time partition 0=t0<t1<...<tn=1 such that
  |w_(u,t)-w_(u,tj)|<r0/2
for every u and t in [tj,t(j+1)]. Define homeomorphisms F_(u,t)
of the original coordinate plane recursively by
  F_(u,0)=id,
  F_(u,t)=L_(w_(u,tj),w_(u,t)) F_(u,tj)
                         for tj<=t<=t(j+1).             (DT.15)
At a partition endpoint the next new factor is id. Consequently
the piecewise formulas fit continuously. The inverse on that
piece is exactly
  F_(u,t)^-1=F_(u,tj)^-1 L_(w_(u,tj),w_(u,t))^-1.
The preceding inverse formula and finite recursion prove joint
continuity of maps and inverses. Induction gives
  F_(u,t)(v)=w_(u,t), F_(u0,t)=id.                       (DT.16)
Every factor fixes F_Xset and is supported in the one compact
neighborhood of T contained in int D1 minus F_Xset. Thus the
whole product fixes F_Xset and preserves D1. Its number of time
pieces will not enter the displacement bound.

5. The exact equivariant paste and its quantitative bound
----------------------------------------------------------
On the actual translated carriers set
  G_(u,t)(qz)=q F_(u,t)(z) for z in D1, q in Q,
and set G_(u,t)=id elsewhere in O.                      (DT.17)
Equation (DT.6) and the construction of U make the labels unique.
The boundary values agree with id. Properness makes the translates
of the compact D1 locally finite in O, so maps and inverses paste
jointly continuously. The inverse is the identical paste using
F_(u,t)^-1. Reindexing by each original q proves Q-equivariance.
The family fixes F_Xset, and its quotient support lies in the
fixed compact pi(D1). C81 therefore gives its joint extension by
id to the original circle, for both maps and inverses.

Each map preserves every qD1. Thus with
  B_v=diam_(e_Q)(D1)<2 eta(pi(v))
the actual uniform estimates are
  sup_z e(G_(u,t)z,z)<=B_v,
  sup_z e(G_(u,t)^-1 z,z)<=B_v.                         (DT.18)
Each moved point and its image lie in the same qD1, of original
e-diameter at most B_v. This proves the estimate for the complete
finite product, independently of its number of factors. It also
explains why the nested outer carrier D1 was retained when the
retraction lands on the boundary of D0.

Define the actual correction of the given family by
  H_(u,t)=G_(u,t)^-1 h_u,
  H_(u,t)^-1=h_u^-1 G_(u,t).                            (DT.19)
These are exact continuous inverse maps in the original topology.
They fix F_Xset and the original circle, commute with Q, start at
h_u and remain id for u=u0. At t=1, (DT.11) and (DT.16) give
H_(u,1)(v)=v, hence H_(u,1) fixes the entire actual orbit Qv.

For the original compact family's inverse modulus
  omega_P^-(r)=max{e(h_u^-1 z,h_u^-1 z'):
                    u in P, z,z' in Dbar, e(z,z')<=r}
one has omega_P^-(r)->0 as r->0, by compactness and joint inverse
evaluation. Substitution through the onto map h_u and use of
(DT.19) give
  sup_z e(H_(u,t)z,h_u z)<=B_v,
  sup_z e(H_(u,t)^-1 z,h_u^-1 z)<=omega_P^-(B_v).        (DT.20)
Thus D_e(H_(u,t),h_u)<=B_v+omega_P^-(B_v). Applying the exact
FCE comparison function omega_(e,d) to the two separate terms
gives the corresponding original d estimate. The modulus is that
of the actual input family and every deck label is unchanged.

6. Relative-component witness and the compact change region
------------------------------------------------------------
The accepted component-witness construction applies to the literal
retraction (DT.10). To identify its exact maps here, W(v) and w
are maps P x I into the same component M of O minus F_Xset,
agreeing on P x {0,1} union {u0} x I. Their normalized lifts to
the simply connected surface cover of M agree on that relative
set. A global plane chart in that cover gives the interpolation
  psi^-1((1-s)psi(Wtilde)+s psi(wtilde)),
followed by the original covering map. This is the accepted
relative track homotopy T_(u,t,s), with all endpoints fixed.

The finite local extension products from the component-witness
note give the ordinary quotient family B'_(u,t), relative to X,
with the same endpoints id,b_u and with
  B'_(u,t)(pi(v))=pi(w_(u,t)).
Let g_(u,t) be the descent of the actual (DT.17). The exact path
  k_(u,t)=g_(u,t)^-1 B'_(u,t),
  k_(u,t)^-1=B'_(u,t)^-1 g_(u,t)                       (DT.21)
fixes X and pi(v) throughout, is based at id, starts at id, and
ends at the descent of H_(u,1). This retains the required ordinary
relative-(X union {pi(v)}) identity-component witness for further
finite steps. Its role is a quotient witness; the original-circle
and uniform estimates for H were proved separately above.

The change from h to H also has one explicit compact quotient
region. Put Dbar1=pi(D1) and
  L=Dbar1 union {b_u^-1(y):u in P,y in Dbar1}.           (DT.22)
This set is compact by joint inverse evaluation on the compact
P x Dbar1. Outside pi^-1(L), the forward maps H and h agree:
h_u sends such a point outside pi^-1(Dbar1), where G^-1 is id.
Their inverse maps agree outside pi^-1(Dbar1), hence also
outside pi^-1(L), directly from (DT.19). Thus the construction
leaves the actual input tail exactly unchanged outside this
specified compact change region, in both directions.

7. Retained scope
------------------
The domain is the original family after IFR with the exact trace
condition (DT.3), equivalently (DT.4). On that domain the displayed
maps give a based controlled homotopy to the actual orbit stabilizer,
with the original inverse modulus, circle topology, all Q labels,
old fixed points and the next relative-component witness retained.
The sufficient test (DT.5) is point-specific and is not needed by
the main construction. The previously accepted cyclic-carrier note
supplies its separate explicit domain with intersecting deck traces.

The arbitrary-family task includes finite overlap sets
{q:A intersect qA is nonempty} that may generate a noncyclic subgroup.
This note supplies no bound for the entire connected union of those
overlapping translates and no global infinite deformation. That
remaining domain requires its actual overlap-component maps and
an ambient bound beyond the disjoint-carrier argument above.
