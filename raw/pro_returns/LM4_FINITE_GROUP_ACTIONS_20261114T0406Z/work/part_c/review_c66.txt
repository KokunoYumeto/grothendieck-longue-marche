1. Finite Jordan covers.
The construction supplies genuinely disjoint closed disks, not only
small connected neighborhoods. A finite clopen partition of the compact
zero-dimensional set has positive separation between its nonempty
pieces. A sufficiently fine finite grid neighborhood, with corner
contacts separated, has each component in an arbitrarily small
neighborhood of one such piece. Its outer Jordan disk lies in the convex
hull of that component. Filling its bounded holes therefore does not
increase its coordinate diameter. Outer curves of distinct components
are disjoint; the corresponding disks are disjoint or nested. Keeping
maximal disks gives pairwise disjoint closures without losing any point
of the deleted set. Small mesh and the positive distance of the compact
set from the complement of the prescribed neighborhood give the stated
containment. No local connectedness of Z is used.

The same finite-disk construction proves path connectedness of the
complement, and of the part inside any Jordan disk whose boundary avoids
Z. This is exactly what is required later to identify complementary
components of a compact core.

2. Exact end compactification and algebraic extension.
The nested disk families have child closures inside parent interiors.
Consequently K_n is compact, lies in M, and is contained in the interior
of K_(n+1). Every compact subset of M eventually lies in one K_n: the
remaining disks meet Z and have mesh tending to zero. The components of
M minus K_n are the specified connected punctured disks, and each is
non-relatively-compact because its disk contains a deleted point.
Compatible nested choices identify exactly one point of Z, and every
point of Z gives exactly one choice. The displayed map Xi and its
inverse carry the actual basic end neighborhoods to the corresponding
disk neighborhoods.

A homeomorphism of M is proper. Applying it to components beyond a
compact core defines its end map; applying the same construction to its
inverse proves invertibility. Density of M in the sphere proves the
uniqueness of extension and the displayed group laws. The full sphere
group in this comparison preserves Z setwise, as correctly stated.

3. Topology of extension.
The key compactness claim (C.66.5) is valid. The convergent sequence of
inverse maps together with its limit is a compact parameter space in
the actual homeomorphism group. Evaluation on K_m has compact image in
M, hence that whole image lies in one larger K_N. Every image of a
component beyond K_N consequently avoids K_m. A single reference point
in each of the finitely many source components pins down its target
parent component for every sufficiently late map. The extended images
of the ends lie in that same small parent disk. This gives a uniform
sphere bound off K_N, while compact-open convergence gives the bound on
K_N. Repeating the argument for inverses proves the required group
topology comparison. Both homeomorphism groups are metrizable, so this
sequential continuity at the identity proves continuity of the inverse
homomorphism everywhere.

It follows that compact parameter families and their inverses extend
continuously. For a connected family containing the identity, evaluation
at each deleted point is a continuous connected subset of the
zero-dimensional set Z and therefore a singleton. This justifies the
pointwise conclusion for the filling without replacing the setwise
group in (C.66.1) by a different group.

4. Application to the plane loop.
Every finite word in the loop values and their inverses is joined to
identity by scaling its finitely many time parameters. Thus each G-orbit
is path connected, and every orbit inside the zero-dimensional nonopen
locus is a point. The one-point compactification of the closed locally
compact zero-dimensional Y is zero-dimensional: compact clopen
neighborhoods give a countable compact clopen exhaustion and a clopen
basis at infinity. This also covers Y empty.

The complement O is connected, so the disjoint open G-orbits form a
single orbit. Every original commuting operator preserves it. Passing
to the closure of the actual operator restriction image is legitimate:
C.30 makes it a closed locally compact subgroup acting freely and
properly; the original loop commutes with its closure. C.32 gives the
ambient-null restriction, and C.33 applies to the connected noncompact
surface O with precisely this proper group. Its disk filling extends
through the proved topological isomorphism. The disk family fixes all
ends, including infinity, and hence yields plane homeomorphisms. Both
the operator commutation equations and the original boundary equations
hold on the dense O and therefore on the whole plane. No restriction
kernel or operator label is discarded.

5. Surface lift and descent.
The ambient disk family composed with the universal covering has an
actual based lift on D^2 times the plane. Lifting the inverse family and
using uniqueness proves that every lifted map is a homeomorphism with
the prescribed inverse. The boundary is the closed, ambient-null based
plane loop. For each chosen lift of each surface operator, including
each deck transformation, the two possible compositions lift the same
surface family and agree at the based parameter, so the boundary loop
commutes with every such lift. The plane filling therefore descends by
the exact equation (C.66.8). Openness of the covering quotient gives
joint continuity of descended maps and inverses. Finite puncture
extension uses the same compact-core argument with the named coordinate
disks and fixes every puncture in the connected family.

6. Primary source and scope.

arXiv PDF. The metadata is correct: Beguin--Crovisier--Le Roux,
Fixed point sets of isotopies on surfaces, JEMS 22(6) (2020), 1971--2046,

of isotopy information for a totally discontinuous deleted set; the
end discussion is Appendix A.1 (printed p.71). The stronger exact
topological group comparison used here is proved in C.66, rather than
attributed to those source statements.
URLs: https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/16819 and
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.00686 .

The claim and metadata correctly retain the zero-dimensional nonopen
