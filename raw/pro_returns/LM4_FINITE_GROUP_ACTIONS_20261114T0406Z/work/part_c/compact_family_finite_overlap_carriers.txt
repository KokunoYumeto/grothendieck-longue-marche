1. Original trace, finite labels and actual parameter paths
-----------------------------------------------------------
Retain the original C81/C92/CFT/FCE/IFR data used in the accepted orbit-
track and component-witness notes. In particular, P is a compact connected
based finite CW complex, h_(u0)=id is the given original continuous family
fixing F_Xset=pi^-1(X), and its ordinary relative-X component witness
gives B_(u,t) from id to desc(h_u), with pointed interior lift W_(u,t).
All maps retain the original disk, its original Euclidean coordinate e,
the metric e_Q=sup_q e(qz,qw), and every actual Q label.

Fix v outside F_Xset and put
  A={h_u(v):u in P}, K=Fill(A), a=a(pi(v)),
  S={q in Q:A intersect qA is nonempty}, H=<S>.          (FOC.1)
Here a(y)=max_u e_Q(h_u z,z) is the actual C_0 displacement function,
and eta>a is its retained continuous positive C_0 majorant.
The compact A is path connected, contains v and misses F_Xset.
It is path connected because P is a connected finite CW complex.
Its original radius obeys e_Q(z,v)<=a for z in A and, by FCE's
filling argument, for all z in K.

Properness of Q and compactness of A make S finite. It contains 1
and is symmetric: an intersection A cap qA is transported by q^-1
to q^-1 A cap A. Thus H is a specific finitely generated subgroup
of the original Q, defined by these exact intersections.

For each s in S choose a point
  z_s=h_(u_s)(v)=s h_(u'_s)(v)
in that intersection. Choose parameter paths gamma_s,gamma'_s in P
from u0 to u_s,u'_s, respectively. Their literal evaluation paths
  alpha_s(t)=h_(gamma_s(t))(v),
  beta_s(t)=h_(gamma'_s(t))(v)
are in A. The path alpha_s followed by the reverse of s beta_s
goes from v to sv and lies in A union sA. Its projection is a loop
based at y=pi(v) with exactly the original deck label s.

These paths define a based map from a finite rose, with one named
edge for each chosen nonidentity s, to pi(A). Its fundamental-group
image in Q is exactly H. We use the loop convention of following
the first loop and then the second; the lift of the second loop
from sv is its s-translate, so the product label is st. This fixes
the homomorphism convention and every generator label explicitly.

The same intersection point gives the exact small-edge estimate
  e_Q(v,sv)<=e_Q(v,z_s)+e_Q(z_s,sv)<=2a.
Also diam_(e_Q)(A union sA)<=4a, since each set has diameter at most
2a and they meet. For a word h=s1...sk the triangle inequality and
Q-invariance give e_Q(v,hv)<=2ak. The word length is retained in
this estimate; it is not bounded by the number of generators.

2. The overlap graph and the exact connected components
--------------------------------------------------------
The translated compact sets {qA:q in Q} form a locally finite family
in O. Indeed a compact neighborhood meets only finitely many labels
of this family by properness. The following graph uses those actual
q labels as its vertices throughout.

Use a graph with vertex labels q in Q and an edge between q and r
exactly when qA intersects rA. Its edge equation is
  q^-1 r in S.                                         (FOC.2)
Thus the graph component at 1 has the vertex set H, and its components
are the left cosets qH. Put
  C=HA=union_(h in H) hA.                               (FOC.3)
Finite graph paths show C is path connected: the path-connected sets
at consecutive vertices meet. Local finiteness shows C is closed
in O. The analogous union over any other coset is also closed.
Distinct coset unions are disjoint, because an intersection between
hA and qh'A would put h^-1 qh' in S and hence q in H.

The family {qC:qH in Q/H} is itself locally finite: a compact set
meeting one of its members meets at least one of the original qA,
and only finitely many such labels meet that compact set. Therefore
each qC is relatively open as well as closed in QA. They are exactly
the connected components of QA. The component label map
  QA -> Q/H, z in qC -> qH                              (FOC.4)
is continuous for the discrete coset topology. Its definition is
independent of a representation z=qh a by (FOC.2). The stabilizer
of C in the actual Q is exactly H: qC=C implies qC intersects C,
hence q in H, and the reverse inclusion is immediate.

The original quotient gives the exact continuous map
  C/H -> pi(A), [z]_H -> pi(z).                         (FOC.5)
It is onto because A subset C. For injectivity, pi(z)=pi(z') gives
z'=qz for a unique original q; the two components then intersect,
so q in H. The source is compact: the compact A maps onto C/H.
The target is a compact subspace of the Hausdorff surface N. Thus
(FOC.5) and its inverse are continuous. The inverse sends y' to
the H-orbit of any original lift of y' lying in C; the preceding
injectivity proves independence of that choice.

The restriction QA->pi(A) of the original covering is a covering
in the subspace topologies. Since C is relatively open and H acts
freely and properly on C, (FOC.5) identifies its H-orbit covering
with the restriction C->pi(A). Lifting a loop from v therefore
has endpoint in Hv. Conversely the actual parameter loops above
realize every generator in S. Consequently
  image(pi_1(pi(A),y)->pi_1(N,y)=Q)=H.                  (FOC.6)
The identification with Q uses the original endpoint labels and
the loop multiplication convention just specified.

3. Filling preserves the entire finite overlap set
---------------------------------------------------
For disjoint compact connected planar sets E,E', their fills are
disjoint or one is contained in the interior of the other. Here
is the needed argument. The connected E' lies in a single component
of O minus E. In a bounded component it lies inside int Fill(E).
The exterior of Fill(E) then lies in the exterior of E', giving
Fill(E') subset Fill(E). Since boundary Fill(E') subset E', any
point of Fill(E') on the outer filled boundary would be interior
to Fill(E'), hence interior to Fill(E), a contradiction. Thus the
inclusion is into the interior. In the exterior case, the connected
Fill(E) misses E' and lies in one component of its complement,
which gives either disjointness or the reverse interior inclusion.

For each q outside S apply this argument to A and qA. An intersection
of K and qK would give strict nesting. The inclusion qK subset int K
makes q^n K meet K for every nonnegative integer n. Properness on
the compact K makes the set of powers finite; a positive power is
then the identity, contradicting K subset int K. The reverse nesting
is the same argument with q^-1. This works separately for every q
outside S, without any assumption on intersections for labels in S.
The inclusion A subset K proves the other direction, so
  {q:K intersect qK is nonempty}=S.                    (FOC.7)

It follows that HK and its Q-translates have precisely the same
coset component labels and stabilizers as C. No additional deck
generator is introduced by the filling operation.

For epsilon>0 one can retain this entire overlap set in a compact
neighborhood of K. Take a compact neighborhood C0 of K. Only
finitely many q have qC0 intersect C0 nonempty. For the labels in
this finite set but outside S, the two compact sets K and qK are
disjoint. Shrink an open neighborhood U of K with compact closure
inside int C0 so that closure U and q closure U are disjoint for
each of those finitely many labels. The other labels outside S
were already excluded by C0. Intersect U also with the open
e_Q epsilon-neighborhood of K. Then
  K subset U, e_Q(z,v)<a+epsilon for z in U,
  closure U intersect q closure U=empty for q notin S.  (FOC.8)

Using boundary K subset A disjoint from F_Xset, the accepted Jordan
neighborhood construction gives nested closed Jordan disks
  K subset int D0, D0 subset int D1, D1 subset U,
  boundary D0 intersect F_Xset=empty.                  (FOC.9)
For D0 use U intersect (int K union (O minus F_Xset)); for D1 use
a neighborhood of the filled D0 inside U. Every s in S has an
intersection point of K and sK inside int D0 and s int D0. Hence
both closed disks and their interiors have exactly the original
overlap set S. In particular
  diam_(e_Q)(D1)<=2(a+epsilon).                         (FOC.10)
From now on choose epsilon=(eta(pi(v))-a)/2. The resulting diameter
bound is strictly less than 2 eta(pi(v)).
All statements apply to the original qD0 and qD1 without changing
the operator action or its boundary compactification.

4. The local covering and the finitely generated carrier
---------------------------------------------------------
Put V=H int D1 and U_D=pi(int D1). The edge equation for these
open disks is still (FOC.2); their components in Q int D1 are
exactly qV, indexed by Q/H. They are disjoint open sets, V is
connected, and its stabilizer is H. Its orbit quotient map
  V/H -> U_D, [z]_H -> pi(z)                            (FOC.11)
is a homeomorphism. The map is bijective by the same component
test as (FOC.5), and it is open because the original covering
and the orbit quotient are open. Its inverse is the unique
H-orbit of lifts inside V. The closed carrier H D1 is contained
in the corresponding closed component of QD1 and has compact
quotient pi(D1).

The original subgroup covering is
  pi_H:O->N_H=O/H,  r_H:N_H->N,
  r_H([z]_H)=pi(z),  pi=r_H pi_H.                      (FOC.12)
The local coordinates of the original covering prove that r_H
is a covering map. Equation (FOC.11) gives its continuous section
over U_D: choose the unique H-orbit of lifts in V. This section
is an open embedding, and r_H restricted to its image is its
continuous inverse. On pi(A) it is exactly (FOC.5) followed by
the inclusion C/H->N_H.

Every loop in U_D lifts from v inside V and ends at an Hv point.
Every h in H is realized by a path v->hv inside the connected
open V. Thus image(pi_1(U_D,y)->Q)=H. The earlier finite parameter
loops already prove the same lower inclusion through pi(A).
This is a local finitely generated subgroup construction. The
ambient Q and the full N retain their original unrestricted scope.

5. One explicit finite ambient extension with its actual count
---------------------------------------------------------------
Use the same literal Schoenflies retraction r0 of O onto D0 as
in the accepted orbit-track note, and put
  w_(u,t)=r0(W_(u,t)(v)).                               (FOC.13)
It lies in D0 minus F_Xset, has endpoints v,h_u(v), and is v for
u=u0. Its compact image T lies in int D1 minus F_Xset. This is
the retracted original track; it retains the original endpoint
family and actual Q-orbit embedding at every parameter.

There is one positive Euclidean radius r, valid for every a0 in T,
such that each closed ball B(a0,r) lies in int D1 minus F_Xset
and its distinct Q-translates are disjoint. To prove the uniform
choice, start with a compact neighborhood C1 of T contained in
int D1 minus F_Xset. Only finitely many q have qC1 intersect C1.
For every nonidentity one, the continuous positive function
e(a0,qa0) has a positive minimum on the compact T, by freeness.
Uniform continuity of those finitely many q on C1 then makes
B(a0,r) and qB(a0,r) disjoint for all a0 in T when r is sufficiently
small. Choose also r below the distance from T to the complement
of int C1. All other q were excluded by C1. These are original
Euclidean balls; all their translates use the original q maps.

For a0,b0 in T with e(a0,b0)<=r/3, put
  L_(a0,b0)(z)=z+max(0,1-|z-a0|/r)(b0-a0).             (FOC.14)
The displacement term has Lipschitz constant at most 1/3. Its
inverse is the limit of z_0=y and
  z_(n+1)=y-max(0,1-|z_n-a0|/r)(b0-a0).
The error decreases geometrically by at most 1/3, uniformly in
all parameters and y. The limit is the unique inverse solution,
proving both compositions and joint inverse continuity. The map
is id outside B(a0,r); injectivity then shows that it preserves
that ball. It sends a0 exactly to b0 and is id for a0=b0.

Paste this single map over the disjoint translates of its own ball:
  E_(a0,b0)(qz)=q L_(a0,b0)(z), z in B(a0,r),
  E_(a0,b0)=id outside Q B(a0,r).                      (FOC.15)
The inverse uses the displayed inverse of L. For parameters in
the compact set {a0,b0 in T:e(a0,b0)<=r/3}, local finiteness of
the translates of C1 and the identity boundary values prove joint
interior continuity, including the moving balls. Their quotient
support lies in the fixed compact pi(C1). C81 gives joint original-
circle continuity for maps and inverses. They fix F_Xset and commute
with every original q. Each complete factor moves a point within
one qD1, so both uniform e-displacements are at most
  B_D=diam_(e_Q)(D1).                                  (FOC.16)

Define the actual time modulus
  mu(delta)=max{e(w_(u,t),w_(u,t')):
                   u in P, |t-t'|<=delta}.
It tends to zero with delta. Choose a positive integer n with
mu(1/n)<r/3, put t_j=j/n, and define recursively
  G_(u,0)=id,
  G_(u,t)=E_(w_(u,t_j),w_(u,t)) G_(u,t_j)
                     for t_j<=t<=t_(j+1).             (FOC.17)
Its inverse is G_(u,t_j)^-1 E_(w_(u,t_j),w_(u,t))^-1.
At every partition boundary the new factor is id, so these are
joint continuous inverse families in the original topology.
Induction proves G_(u,t)(v)=w_(u,t) and G_(u0,t)=id.

At most n displayed factors act at any time. The triangle inequality
for the displacement of a composition, and the reversed product for
its inverse, give the explicit finite estimates
  sup e(G_(u,t)z,z), sup e(G_(u,t)^-1 z,z)<=n B_D.      (FOC.18)
The integer n is part of this actual construction and is retained
in (FOC.18). No uniform bound on n across escaping quotient labels
is established in this note.

There is also an exact account of the particle labels in that finite
product. At a nontrivial factor, its input and output lie in the same
qD1. Between consecutive nontrivial factors, the unchanged intermediate
point lies in both old and new translated disks. Their labels differ
by an element of S, by (FOC.2). Thus the trajectory follows a walk
in the actual overlap graph, remains in one qH carrier component,
and uses at most n disk moves. The reversed inverse walk has the
same property since S=S^-1. This proves the component preservation
and the finite bound from the actual maps, rather than replacing
the original family by an arbitrary collection of labeled tracks.

8. Precise remaining quantitative target
-----------------------------------------
The finite set S, the subgroup H, its actual coset components,
both orbit-quotient inverses, the local subgroup-cover section,
the small Jordan tiles, and the finite ambient maps above are proved.
H is finitely generated because it is generated by this displayed
finite S; this statement makes no finite-generation assertion about Q.

For the full original C_0 deformation, the remaining work is a
quantitative ambient construction whose displacement tends to zero
with the actual common tail for the arbitrary overlap domain.
Estimate (FOC.18) retains the integer n from its explicit inverse-
controlled finite product. The small overlap edges by themselves
give neither a bound for n nor a vanishing component diameter,
as the literal example shows. Compatible edge and cycle stages
and the infinite deformation remain active. No full C.12 result
is asserted by this note.
