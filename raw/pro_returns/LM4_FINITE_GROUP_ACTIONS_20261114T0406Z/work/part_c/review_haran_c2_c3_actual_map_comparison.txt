3. COMPACT MINIMALITY WITH THE ORIGINAL AMBIENT CONJUGATORS
-----------------------------------------------------------
For a closed H in L, X_H and Y_H are closed subsets of the compact M:
they are finite intersections of inverse images of H under continuous
conjugation evaluations. They are nonempty by the definition of L.
In a descending chain, every finite subcollection has an inclusion-
minimal member, so each of the two corresponding finite intersections
is nonempty. Compactness gives a conjugator in the full intersection
of the X_H and independently in that of the Y_H. Their conjugated
finite factors lie in the closed intersection of the chain. This
proves the lower-bound condition for Zorn's lemma in the reversed
inclusion order and hence produces a minimal H* in L.

Choose its two actual ambient conjugators u,v and let a*=u tau u^-1,
b*=v xi v^-1. The closed subgroup generated by these two elements
lies in H* and still belongs to L, so minimality makes it H*.
The full coproduct therefore gives an epimorphism e:M->H* with
these exact generator images. Its image is closed by compactness;
there is no distinction between a dense generated image and H* here.
The finite factor embeddings are retained by their exact orders.

5. THE S3 QUOTIENT, BOTH COSET MAPS, AND THE OPEN-IMAGE ARGUMENT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Let e be any of the specified conjugate-factor generator maps onto H.
The composite q e sends tau to a transposition and xi to a three-cycle,
with the latter generator orientation allowed to be either sign.
They generate S3. Their product is an odd permutation of S3, hence
a transposition, so the relation (AB)^2=1 is satisfied. The named
S3 presentation therefore yields a unique surjective endomorphism
a of S3 with q e=a q. Since S3 is finite, a is an automorphism; its
inverse is the inverse permutation of the six elements.

It follows for every original g in M that e(g) is in F exactly when
g is in F. In particular H intersect F=e(F), proving both inclusions.
Also q(H)=S3, so FH=HF=M. The map of coset spaces
  F/(H intersect F) -> M/H, f(H intersect F) -> fH
is injective because equality of the right-hand cosets puts f2^-1 f1
in both F and H. To recover the inverse at mH, choose h in H with
q(h)=q(m), and send mH to (m h^-1)(H intersect F). Changing h or
the representative m gives the same F-coset. The forward map is
continuous from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, so this bijection
and its inverse have the asserted quotient topologies. No normality
of H is being assumed in this coset-space comparison.

The image e(F) is topologically generated by the two literal images
of x=(tau xi)^-2 and y=xi(tau xi)^-2 xi^-1. An open H gives an open
L0=H intersect F with index d=[M:H]=[F:L0]. Intersecting with the
dense named discrete free group Delta gives a degree-d subgroup.
Its covering graph has d vertices and 2d edges; collapsing a spanning
tree leaves d+1 free generators. Its completion is exactly L0:
every finite-index subgroup in the discrete degree-d subgroup
contains a finite-index normal subgroup of Delta, obtained by the
finite coset core, so the induced profinite topology is the full one.
The closure is L0 because Delta is dense and L0 is open.

Thus L0 is free profinite of rank d+1. Its continuous F2-valued
characters form F2^(d+1) by the free universal property. On the other
hand any such character of e(F) is determined by its two displayed
generator values, giving an injection into F2^2. Hence d+1<=2,
so d=1 and H=M. This proves that a proper image in this specified
class has infinite index. For the original H3 this is exactly the
proved alternative H3=M or infinite index; it supplies no finite
index or full-image conclusion for that original subgroup.
