C.91 — CONTRACTION OF THE WHOLE COCOMPACT ORIGINAL CENTRALIZER

Exact scope
Retain every C81 hypothesis: the actual closed disk, its original boundary,
Q closed/discrete/free/proper on the interior, a transitive subgroup of the
actual boundary-fixed centralizer, and the literal quotient covering. Add
C90's compactness of N=O/Q. The entire original centralizer C_Q is
contractible by one continuous identity-fixed homotopy in the original
map-and-inverse topology. The full point stabilizer F=C_Q(x) is isomorphic
as a topological group to H_p=Homeo(N rel {p})^0, and C_Q is homeomorphic as
a space to O x F through the exact evaluation-bundle coordinates.
This strengthens C90's positive-homotopy assertion in the compact-quotient
case. It does not provide a contraction for a noncompact quotient.

1. Arbitrary pointed descent and the missing component argument
For every h in F, b(pi z)=pi(hz), with inverse pi(h^{-1}z), is the actual
pointed quotient homeomorphism. The lift of a p-based loop starts at x and
ends at an actual qx. Applying h preserves both endpoints because h fixes
x and commutes with that same q. Hence the induced based pi_1 map is the
identity, with each original label retained.
The source also constructs a based homotopy explicitly. It lifts a finite
quotient triangulation, prescribes h-to-identity paths at representative
vertices (constant at x), extends over edge squares and triangle prisms by
coning their boundary maps in a displayed plane coordinate, and transports
every extension by literal Q. Trivial simplex stabilizers make the labels
unique; compatibility and local finiteness prove continuity. Descent gives
a homotopy b -> id fixing p. Its domain N x I is compact, so it is proper.

2. Exact Epstein input and exceptions
Theorem 6.3 is read directly in the original source, printed pp. 99-101
(PDF pages 17-19). Its input is a proper homotopy of the boundary pair;
its last sentence preserves a basepoint fixed by that homotopy. N has no
boundary, and properness has just been established. Printed p. 101
explicitly treats a non-PL homotopy by a basepoint-preserving isotopy and
PL approximation, referring to the Appendix. Appendix Theorem A4 on
printed p. 107 (PDF page 25) was also read. The conclusion supplies an
isotopy rel p, so every actual b above lies in H_p. No arbitrary descent
was assumed to lie in H_p. The distinct Theorem 6.4(b) is unnecessary.
The original pages 99 and 101 were also rendered and actually viewed.

3. Exact Yagasaki input
Lemma 2.1(i), printed pp. 2-3, applies to (N,{p}). The disk, annulus and
Mobius-band exceptions have boundary. The sphere and projective plane
are excluded by pi_2(N)=pi_2(O)=0. The exceptional torus and Klein-bottle
pairs have empty fixed set. Thus the indicated pointed identity
component contracts for every compact quotient here, including all
nonorientable cases. The source uses the explicit normalization
J_r(b)=C_{1-r}(b)C_{1-r}(1)^{-1}; J_0=1, J_1=b and J_r(1)=1.

4. Actual inverse lift and topology
Lift J_r(b)pi from identity. Lift its inverse path separately; uniqueness
makes the two lifted families inverse. The same uniqueness proves Hq=qH
for each original q. Discreteness of the fiber over p keeps x fixed.
C88 with the compact parameter interval and K=N gives the original-circle
identity extensions, including continuous inverses. The endpoint defines
L(b). D L=id and L D=id follow from uniqueness of the lift fixing x.
Composition uniqueness also proves L(bc)=L(b)L(c).
For the descent, the quotient metric inequality and C81's exact isometry
give d_H(Dh,Dk) <= d_C(h,k) for the two sum metrics on maps and inverses.
For L, any convergent sequence b_n -> b defines a compact metric parameter
space {0} union {1/n}. Lift the joint J-family on I times this parameter
space and apply C88 with K=N. The endpoints converge in the original
closed-disk topology. Both groups are metrizable, so L is continuous.
A_t(k)=L(J_{1-t}(Dk)) therefore contracts the entire F and fixes identity.

5. Constructed global evaluation section
Use C81's actual local sections sigma_i over O. Start with the unit-grid
triangulation of R^2, with zero a vertex, and the displayed inverse plane
coordinate psi^{-1}:R^2 -> O. Haver Lemma 1, p. 458, applied with k=0 and
this actual map, gives a locally finite subdivision whose closed stars
land in local-section domains. This is the same source and use already
verified for C82. The original publisher PDF was reopened and its
closed-simplex-neighborhood notation and lemma were read; the paper
states this elementary lemma without proof, and no source proof is
claimed to have been read.
At each vertex choose an actual point-moving map, choosing identity at x.
Suppose s is defined on a simplex boundary. In one local chart write
alpha(u)=sigma(u)^{-1}s(u) in F. At y=(1-r)c+r u define
s(y)=sigma(y) A_{1-r}(alpha(u)); set s(c)=sigma(c). It is the previous s
at the boundary and still sends x to y. Compactness of alpha(boundary)
and the continuous contraction establish continuity at the cone vertex.
Edges first and then faces give compatible values. Local finiteness
proves global continuity in the original group topology and preserves
s(x)=1. Thus a global section has been constructed, with no unstated
principal-bundle-triviality step.

6. Product and full contraction
Phi(y,k)=s(y)k and Psi(h)=(hx,s(hx)^{-1}h) are exact inverse continuous
maps. With the explicit base contraction R_t(y)=psi^{-1}((1-t)psi(y)),
write y=hx and k=s(y)^{-1}h. Then
    C_t(h) = s(R_t(y)) A_t(k),
    C_t(h)^{-1} = A_t(k)^{-1} s(R_t(y))^{-1}.
C_0(h)=h, C_1(h)=1 and C_t(1)=1. Every factor belongs to the original
C_Q. Thus every value fixes the actual boundary, retains all Q-labels,
and the map and inverse vary jointly continuously.
