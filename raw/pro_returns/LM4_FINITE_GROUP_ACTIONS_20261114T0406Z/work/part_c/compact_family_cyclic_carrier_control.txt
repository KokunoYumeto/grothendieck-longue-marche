ACTUAL CYCLIC CARRIERS: A WINDING-INDEPENDENT ORIGINAL-METRIC BOUND
================================================================
Unnumbered continuation. New source; earlier accepted files unchanged.
This note constructs an actual controlled correction on the precise
family subspace described below, and its simultaneous disjoint-band
assembly. It supplies neither a general core-straightening operation
for arbitrary families nor a full noncompact contraction.

1. Original data and an exact band cover
----------------------------------------
Retain C81, C92 and CFT with the original closed disk Dbar, O=int Dbar,
the original Q action, pi:O->N=O/Q, x in O and p=pi(x).
Use the fixed original Euclidean coordinate metric e and
e_Q(z,w)=sup_{q in Q}e(qz,qw) from FCE. The topology of e_Q on O
is the original topology, Q acts by e_Q-isometries, and compact
quotient-support families extend jointly by the identity on the
original circle, with maps and inverses.

Let X be a compact subset of N containing p. The given family
  P -> F_X,  u -> h_u,   h_{u0}=id,                         (CC.1)
is continuous in the original closed-disk map-and-inverse topology.
Here P is a nonempty compact connected metric space and
F_X is the original Q-centralizer fixing pi^-1(X) pointwise.
Its actual positive displacement function is
  a(y)=max_{u in P} e_Q(h_u z,z),  y=pi(z).
CFT and FCE give a in C_0(N) and a continuous positive
eta in C_0(N) with eta>a. These are the actual given family
and its original lift displacements.

For sigma in {1,-1}, put
  T_sigma(t,b)=(t+1,sigma b),
  B_sigma=(R x [-1,1])/<T_sigma>.
The first model is an annulus and the second is a Mobius band.
Retain an actual embedding
  j:B_sigma -> N\X                                      (CC.2)
whose core generator has infinite-order image q0 in Q.
Choose its lift
  Theta:R x [-1,1] -> O,   Theta(0,0)=v,
  pi Theta=j kappa,   Theta T_sigma=q0 Theta,             (CC.3)
where kappa is the displayed quotient map.
This is the original covering lift; q0 is its actual deck label.

Here are the precise cover and inverse assertions used subsequently.
The strip is simply connected, so its map lifts after choosing v.
Two strip points with the same Theta-image project to the same
B_sigma point, since j is injective. They differ by T_sigma^k.
Their lift images then differ by q0^k; freeness and the infinite
order of q0 force k=0. Thus Theta is injective. Covering charts,
also at the two model boundary lines, give its continuous local
inverse. Path lifting in j(B_sigma) proves that its image V is
the complete component of pi^-1(j(B_sigma)) containing v.
The image is closed, since a component of this lifted compact
band is closed in O. Alternatively, the compact fundamental
rectangle Theta([0,1]x[-1,1]) and properness of Q prove that
Theta is proper, which also proves closedness and inverse continuity.
Indeed a compact target set meets only finitely many q0^k translates
of that rectangle, bounding the integer strip coordinate of its
preimage; that preimage is then a closed subset of a compact rectangle.

The stabilizer of V is exactly <q0>. Indeed qV=V supplies a path
in V from v to qv. Its projected loop in j(B_sigma) has an
integer core coordinate k; uniqueness of its original lift gives
qv=q0^k v and hence q=q0^k. The converse follows from (CC.3).
Distinct translates of V are therefore disjoint, with the only
multiple strip descriptions coming from powers of T_sigma.
Every component of pi^-1(j(B_sigma)) contains a lift of the chosen
core basepoint, so pi^-1(j(B_sigma))=QV by the original deck action.
The compact fundamental rectangles and all their Q-translates
are locally finite in O by properness of Q. Every later paste
uses these exact sets and coordinates, including their boundary.

2. The exact family subspace and its real coordinate
----------------------------------------------------
Retain the lifted core line
  L=Theta(R x {0}).
The family treated in this note takes its evaluation values in L:
  h_u(v)=Theta(lambda(u),0),  u in P.                     (CC.4)
This describes its exact domain in F_X by the evaluation map
h->h(v), with the subspace condition h(v) in L.
The coordinate lambda is recovered by the continuous inverse of
Theta on L. It is unique, continuous on P and lambda(u0)=0.
In particular it is a real lift coordinate, not its residue in R/Z.
All integer deck steps are retained.

Since P is compact and connected,
  lambda(P)=[m,M],  m<=0<=M,
  K=Theta([m,M]x{0})={h_u(v):u in P},
  A=max_{z in K}e_Q(z,v)=a(pi v).                         (CC.5)
This equality uses surjectivity onto the complete interval; choosing
an arbitrary path set in place of K would not give it.
Put ell=M-m. Every scaled value s lambda(u), 0<=s<=1, still
belongs to [m,M]. Also diam_{e_Q}(K)<=2A.
When A=0, (CC.4) already fixes v and all Qv. Take every correction
below to be id. In the rest of the construction choose epsilon>0.

We construct continuous maps and inverses G_c for c in [m,M],
with G_0=id, G_c(v)=Theta(c,0), fixing pi^-1(X), commuting with
every original q, and supported over one compact subband of (CC.2).
They will satisfy
  sup e(G_c z,z)=sup e(G_c^-1 z,z)
       <= 4A+2epsilon.                                  (CC.6)
The two equal suprema range over the original closed disk.
The construction for ell<1 gives the stronger 2A+2epsilon bound.
There is no factor depending on M, -m, an integer winding number
or a number of disk-paste stages in (CC.6).

3. Thin collars in the original invariant metric
-------------------------------------------------
For every epsilon>0 there is a delta in (0,1) with
  e_Q(Theta(t,b),Theta(t,0))<epsilon
            (t in R, |b|<=delta).                       (CC.7)
To see this first restrict t to [0,1]. Continuity into the
e_Q topology on that compact rectangle gives the asserted
uniform bound for small delta. For general t write t=k+r,
0<=r<=1. Equation (CC.3) expresses both points as q0^k images
of the corresponding pair at (r,sigma^k b); invariance of e_Q
gives the same estimate. This keeps the original action unchanged.

Set
  beta_delta(b)=max(0,1-2|b|/delta).
This is even, equals one at zero and is zero on a neighborhood
of each boundary of [-delta,delta].
Write V_delta=Theta(R x [-delta,delta]).
The smaller band j(kappa(R x [-delta,delta])) is compact and
still disjoint from X.

4. Range at least one: one explicit shear
-----------------------------------------
For ell>=1 define on the strip
  S_c(t,b)=(t+c beta_delta(b),b),
  S_c^-1(t,b)=(t-c beta_delta(b),b).                      (CC.8)
These formulas are continuous with c, and are inverse on every
point. The even beta makes S_c commute with T_sigma for both
sigma signs. They fix a neighborhood of the strip boundary.
On QV_delta define
  G_c(q Theta(t,b))=q Theta(S_c(t,b)),
and put G_c=id on its complement.                       (CC.9)
Two descriptions differ by a power of T_sigma, so its commutation
with S_c proves that (CC.9) is well defined. The analogous formula
using S_c^-1 is its actual inverse. The boundary values paste to
the identity; the locally finite fundamental rectangles give
joint interior continuity of both maps.
It commutes with every q by reindexing the same actual Q labels.
Its quotient support lies in the single compact subband. C81 gives
joint extension by the identity to the original circle, for c in
the compact interval. Thus it is a family in the original F_X.

Here is the winding-independent estimate. The core sets K and
q0 K have coordinate intervals [m,M] and [m+1,M+1]. Since ell>=1,
they meet, and their union is Theta([m,M+1]x{0}). Hence
  diam_{e_Q}(K union q0 K)
       <=2 diam_{e_Q}(K)<=4A.                           (CC.10)
For any strip input, use a deck power to put its horizontal
coordinate t in [0,1]. Both t and t+c beta_delta(b) then belong
to [m,M+1], since c beta_delta(b) is in [m,M].
The two corresponding core points belong to the one union in
(CC.10). Adding the two transverse errors from (CC.7) proves
  e_Q(Theta(S_c(t,b)),Theta(t,b))<=4A+2epsilon.
The bound is invariant under every q used in (CC.9).

Because G_c is onto and e is symmetric, substitution z=G_c^-1 w
gives
  sup_z e(G_c^-1 z,z)=sup_z e(G_c z,z).
Thus the same bound controls the inverse, without a separate
and generally incorrect claim that -c belongs to [m,M].
For an equivariant G, its original uniform displacement also
equals sup_{z in O}e_Q(Gz,z), by taking the two suprema over z,q.
These observations prove (CC.6).

The algebraic flow identities S_c S_d=S_(c+d) hold wherever the
parameters are defined; the ambient strip formula itself defines
them for all real parameters. The bounds in this note concern
only the retained interval of the given family.
In particular c=k in Z sends v to q0^k v literally. The bound
does not increase with |k| when that k lies in [m,M], because
all intervening core points already lie in the actual K.

5. Range less than one: an actual disjoint rectangle
---------------------------------------------------
For 0<ell<1 choose numbers
  a0<m<=0<=M<b0,  b0-a0<1.
Choose them sufficiently close to m,M and then choose delta>0
so that every point of the compact rectangle
  R0=Theta([a0,b0]x[-delta,delta])
has e_Q-distance less than epsilon from K. This follows first
by continuity on a compact core interval when a0,b0 approach
m,M, and then by compact uniform continuity in the transverse
coordinate. The two errors can each be chosen below epsilon/2.
Consequently
  diam_{e_Q}(R0)<=diam_{e_Q}(K)+2epsilon<=2A+2epsilon.    (CC.11)
Its Q-translates are disjoint: within one V they lie in the
disjoint horizontal intervals [a0+k,b0+k], and distinct V
translates were identified in Section 1. These are actual
closed disk carriers.

For d in [m,M], define on [a0,b0]
  f_d(t)=a0+(d-a0)(t-a0)/(-a0)  for a0<=t<=0,
  f_d(t)=d+(b0-d)t/b0           for 0<=t<=b0.             (CC.12)
It sends a0,0,b0 to a0,d,b0, respectively. Its inverse is
  f_d^-1(s)=a0+(-a0)(s-a0)/(d-a0) for a0<=s<=d,
  f_d^-1(s)=b0(s-d)/(b0-d)        for d<=s<=b0.          (CC.13)
Both composites are identities on the stated pieces. The
denominators d-a0 and b0-d have positive uniform lower bounds
m-a0 and b0-M. Thus maps and inverses are jointly continuous.

Extend these maps to R by the rule f_d(t+k)=f_d(t)+k on the
integer translates of [a0,b0], and by the identity elsewhere.
The disjointness of those intervals makes the rule unambiguous.
The endpoints match the identity, and f_d is an increasing
homeomorphism of R with the displayed inverse. Also f_0=id.
Now use
  S_c(t,b)=(f_(c beta_delta(b))(t),b),                   (CC.14)
with the inverse obtained from (CC.13) at that same b.
The parameter c beta_delta(b) belongs to [m,M].
Periodicity and the even beta prove commutation with T_sigma,
including the Mobius sign. Paste as in (CC.9).
The support lies in QR0, and both maps preserve each rectangle.
Hence (CC.11) bounds every map and inverse displacement by
2A+2epsilon. All topology, equivariance and boundary assertions
follow from the same literal pasting and compact-support argument.
This completes the construction of G_c in both interval ranges.
For ell=0 take G_0=id as already specified.

6. The controlled correction of the given family
------------------------------------------------
Define, for u in P and s in [0,1],
  H_(u,s)=G_(s lambda(u))^-1 h_u,
  H_(u,s)^-1=h_u^-1 G_(s lambda(u)).                    (CC.15)
This is a continuous original-topology family, by the exact
composition and inversion comparisons in FCE. It starts at h_u,
is id for u=u0, fixes pi^-1(X), and commutes with the original Q.
At s=1 it fixes v and therefore every Qv, by (CC.4) and
G_(lambda(u))(v)=h_u(v).

For the explicit inverse modulus of the actual compact family put
  omega_P^-(r)=max{ e(h_u^-1 z,h_u^-1 w):
                    u in P, z,w in Dbar, e(z,w)<=r }.
This maximum exists. Compactness and joint evaluation continuity
give omega_P^-(r)->0 as r->0; otherwise a convergent sequence of
triples with z,w having the same limit contradicts continuity.
With B=4A+2epsilon, the two exact comparison estimates are
  sup_z e(H_(u,s)z,h_u z)<=B,
  sup_z e(H_(u,s)^-1 z,h_u^-1 z)<=omega_P^-(B).          (CC.16)
For the first, substitute w=h_u z and use the inverse correction
bound. For the second use the displayed inverse in (CC.15).
Thus D_e(H_(u,s),h_u)<=B+omega_P^-(B).
The original d bound is
  D_d(H_(u,s),h_u)
      <=omega_(e,d)(B)+omega_(e,d)(omega_P^-(B)),         (CC.17)
with the exact metric comparison function of FCE.
For the correction itself D_e(G_c,id)<=2B, including both
directions. These estimates use the given family, not an
uncontrolled ordinary quotient isotopy.

Choose epsilon=(eta(pi v)-A)/2>0. Then
  sup e(G_c z,z)=sup e(G_c^-1 z,z)<4 eta(pi v),          (CC.18)
and in the small-range construction the bound is <2 eta(pi v).
This transfers the actual common C_0 envelope to the ambient
correction on the exact family subspace (CC.4).

7. Countably many disjoint actual bands
---------------------------------------
Here is a simultaneous assembly with an explicit uniform inverse.
Retain countably many embedded bands j_i as in (CC.2), pairwise
disjoint in N, with chosen lifts v_i, cores L_i, and actual
equations h_u(v_i)=Theta_i(lambda_i(u),0) for the same family.
Retain pi(v_i) escaping every compact subset of N. Each band is
disjoint from X. These are the precise data for this operation.
The C_0 envelope gives eta(pi v_i)->0.

Construct G_(i,c) by Sections 3--5 with
epsilon_i=(eta(pi v_i)-a(pi v_i))/2. Its support lies over
a compact subband of j_i(B_sigma_i), and it preserves that band.
The supports for different i are disjoint. For n>=1 set
  J_(n,u,s)=product_(i=1)^n G_(i,s lambda_i(u)).          (CC.19)
The factors commute by their disjoint invariant supports.
Its inverse is the product of their explicit inverses.
For n>r the entire difference is supported in the bands i>r,
and on each one only its own factor acts. Thus
  D_e(J_(n,u,s),J_(r,u,s))
          <=8 sup_(i>r) eta(pi v_i),                    (CC.20)
uniformly over the actual compact parameter space P x [0,1].
This is a maximum bound, with no sum of the number of bands.

The maps and inverses therefore converge uniformly on the original
closed disk and uniformly in (u,s), to J_(u,s) and I_(u,s).
Both limits are continuous. To verify the inverse maps, use
uniform convergence in
J_n J_n^-1=id and J_n^-1 J_n=id: replace one factor at a time,
and use uniform continuity of the limiting continuous map on the
compact disk. This proves J I=I J=id. The limits fix the circle,
fix pi^-1(X), and commute with each original q by interior
continuity at qz and z. They are exactly the pointwise pastes
of the individual band maps, with the identity off their union.
This also proves continuous dependence in the original
map-and-inverse topology. No separate local finiteness assertion
about the full closed bands is needed for this uniform-limit proof.

Now put
  H_(u,s)=J_(u,s)^-1 h_u,
  H_(u,s)^-1=h_u^-1 J_(u,s).                            (CC.21)
These are exact continuous inverse maps and form a based
deformation of the given family through the original F_X.
At s=1, every Qv_i is fixed, because all other band factors
fix that band's points and its own factor matches h_u(v_i).
For an exact comparison to the finite stages write
H_r=J_r^-1 h and B_r=4 sup_(i>r) eta(pi v_i).
The tail T_r=J J_r^-1 has both displacement norms at most B_r
and commutes with J_r, because their band supports are disjoint.
Then H=T_r^-1 H_r and
H^-1=h^-1 T_r J_r, H_r^-1=h^-1 J_r.
Substitution through the onto maps H_r and J_r gives
  D_e(H,H_r)<=B_r+omega_P^-(B_r),                       (CC.22)
uniformly in u,s. The same original inverse modulus is used at
every finite stage; it is not a modulus of an accumulated
corrected family. Applying omega_(e,d) to each of these two
bounds gives the exact original d comparison as in (CC.17).

8. Precisely what is still to be constructed
-------------------------------------------
The input subspace is specified by (CC.4), or by all its displayed
band-core equations in Section 7. Its inclusion into the original
centralizer is the literal inclusion, and the real coordinates
are recovered by the actual lifted core inverses. The produced
family (CC.15), or (CC.21), is an explicit homotopy of this given
family to the indicated orbit stabilizer. The large-range estimate
works even for arbitrarily many deck steps, with the original
deck labels and both metric directions retained.

For a general original family the existing orbit-track note
provides small actual tracks in a filled disk, not equations
(CC.4) on a single essential lifted core. No controlled
transformation from those arbitrary tracks to this domain is
proved here. Disjoint compatible bands, control over the
remaining edges and cycles, and the global initial-to-final
cutoff for an arbitrary original family remain active tasks.
The exact infinite-evaluation obstruction of C83 remains in
force; this construction uses the given uniform original family
and the explicit additional core equations throughout.
