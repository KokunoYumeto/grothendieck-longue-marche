Exact elementary metric comparison relevant to these leads
-----------------------------------------------------------
Retain C81 notation: d is the original metric on the compact closed
disk, d_Q(z,w)=sup_{q in Q} d(qz,qw) is its invariant metric on O,
and rho(pi(z),pi(w))=inf_{q in Q} d_Q(z,qw) is the quotient metric.
Put M=diam_d(Dbar)<infinity. Then d_Q<=M and rho<=M.
On any subset A of the actual quotient,
an L-Lipschitz map into a metric space has image diameter at most LM.
An unbounded Euclidean end has infinite diameter: fix one point and
take points on an outward ray. Consequently there is no surjective
bi-Lipschitz comparison from such an A, with its actual restricted
rho metric, onto an unbounded Euclidean end. This proves the precise
failure of that proposed comparison by its distance inequality.

Uniform changes of metric require a further actual group comparison.
For example on R the identity maps between |x-y| and min(1,|x-y|) are
uniformly continuous both ways: for every epsilon<1 their epsilon
balls coincide. The same small-epsilon observation gives exactly the
same uniform map-and-inverse topology on their common uniform
homeomorphism group. Nevertheless the bounded-displacement subgroup
for the first metric has a proper inclusion in the one for the second.
The homomorphism is the identity inclusion; h(x)=2x and h^(-1)(x)=x/2
are uniformly continuous in both metrics, while sup|h(x)-x| is infinite
and sup min(1,|h(x)-x|)=1. Thus a theorem about that specified bounded
subgroup cannot cover this omitted element through the identity map.
This is an explicit comparison and counterexample to equality of the
two bounded-displacement subgroups, not a claim that their topologies
or the relevant theories have no further relation.

The exact remaining C88 task is to deform a given compact family of
the original labelled lifts while maintaining its uniform quotient
C0 tail for maps and inverses. The earlier exhaustion counterexample
concerns the diameter of an entire stabilizer; it does not refute such
a family-dependent deformation. No general cutoff has been obtained
from the two sources above. Their hypotheses and the actual maps are
retained for continued work rather than replaced by an unproved transfer.
