# Independent audit of the two-point section comparison

## Result and exact setup

The proposed assertion \(H^1(C_2,B)=1\) passes the audit with the actual maps described below. This conclusion concerns the two-point pushing kernel \(B\). It does not identify the correction element with an element of the original image of \(\widehat\phi\).

Let \(M\) be the sphere with the six retained labels deleted. The specified orientation-preserving involution \(r\) interchanges those labels in three pairs and has two unmarked fixed points \(a,b\). Put
\[
 X=\operatorname{Conf}_2(M),\quad
 x_*=(x_0,rx_0),\quad
 R(x,y)=(ry,rx),\quad J=\pi_1(X,x_*).
\]
Both \(x_*\) and the actual basepoint of the completed point-pushing maps are retained. Write
\[
 \theta:J\longrightarrow
 Q=\pi_1(M,x_0)\times\pi_1(M,rx_0)
\]
for the map consisting of the two coordinate tracks. Then
\[
 R_*(u,v)=(r_*v,r_*u).
\]
The two occurrences of \(r_*\) here have their actual opposite source and target basepoints; their composites are the identity because \(r^2=1\).

## 1. The discrete extension and unique section class

The actual point-pushing identification embeds \(J\) as the kernel of forgetting the two distinguished labels in the pure eight-point sphere mapping class group. The involution of the eight-point configuration supplies a specified splitting of
\[
 E=J\rtimes\langle R\rangle.
\]
In the ambient eight-point mapping class group, this is the subgroup generated by that exact \(J\) and that exact involution. In particular, the permutation of the distinguished pair is the same exponent as the six-point quotient involution. Allowing the distinguished-pair permutation independently would give a larger group and is not the group used here.

An order-two subgroup of \(E\) mapping isomorphically onto \(C_2\) is represented by an orientation-preserving sphere involution with the same four labelled two-cycles as \(R\). Sphere half-turn classification and equivariant point motion give one conjugacy class under the pure eight-point mapping class group. To retain the label issue explicitly, the space of four ordered free-orbit representatives for a sphere half-turn is
\[
 \{(z_1,\ldots,z_4):
 z_i\notin\{a,b\},\ z_i\ne z_j,\ z_i\ne rz_j\ (i\ne j)\}.
\]
It is path connected: move one representative at a time through the sphere minus the finitely many prohibited points, using temporary positions when necessary. The paired representatives move by \(r\). Equivariant isotopy extension, equivalently ordinary point motion on the actual two-fold quotient followed by its unique prescribed lift, realizes the path. This provides the pure conjugator, including the choices within each labelled pair.

The exact forgetting map
\[
 C_{P\Gamma_{0,8}}(R)\longrightarrow C_{P\Gamma_{0,6}}(r)
\]
is surjective. Indeed the established cyclic-cover normalizer comparison represents a target centralizer class by a homeomorphism commuting with \(r\). Join its image of \(x_0\) to \(x_0\) by a path in \(M\setminus\{a,b\}\). Move the paired point by \(r\), and lift the resulting point-motion isotopy on the quotient. This isotopy is relative to the six retained labels and the branch set. The resulting homeomorphism fixes both distinguished labels, still commutes with \(r\), and has the original six-point mapping class.

If a pure conjugator between a section and \(R\) has six-point image \(c\), then \(c\) centralizes \(r\). Multiplying that conjugator by the inverse of the centralizer lift just constructed removes its six-point image without changing its conjugation of \(R\). The adjusted conjugator belongs to \(J\). Thus the discrete sections have exactly one \(J\)-conjugacy class.

## 2. The fixed subgroup: the actual double-cover calculation

The fixed configurations are
\[
 X^R=\{(x,rx):x\in M\setminus\{a,b\}\}.
\]
The inclusion alone is not the justification for identifying its fundamental group with the fixed subgroup. That identification follows from the following exact quotient point-pushing diagram.

The quotient sphere has three labelled ordinary punctures and the two branch points. Adding the distinguished free orbit adds one ordinary marked point. Forgetting this added point has point-pushing kernel
\[
 \pi_1\bigl(S^2\setminus\{\text{three ordinary points, two branch points}\}\bigr)
 \cong F_4.
\]
The source pure subgroup requires that the two distinguished lifts be individually fixed. Its intersection with this \(F_4\) is exactly the kernel of the double-cover monodromy: each branch meridian maps to \(1\in C_2\), and each ordinary meridian maps to \(0\). It is the index-two subgroup
\[
 \pi_1(M\setminus\{a,b\},x_0)\cong F_7.
\]
The cyclic-cover normalizer map is injective on the pure subgroup, because its nontrivial deck transformation permutes the six retained labels. The Birman point-pushing map downstairs is injective. These two facts prove both injectivity of the fixed-configuration loop map and
\[
 C_J(R)=\pi_1(M\setminus\{a,b\},x_0)\cong F_7
\]
with precisely the displayed loop map.

Its image under \(\theta\) is
\[
 u\longmapsto \bigl(\iota_*u,r_*\iota_*u\bigr),
\]
where \(\iota:M\setminus\{a,b\}\to M\) fills the two specified unmarked fixed points. Since this filling induces a surjection \(F_7\to F_5\), the image is all of \(Q^R\).

Full induced profinite topology on this \(F_7\) can also be retained. The downstairs \(F_4\) point-pushing subgroup has full topology in the corresponding quotient mapping class group by the completed Birman argument. Its index-two subgroup \(F_7\) has the full induced topology there. The pure centralizer is a finite-index subgroup of the cyclic normalizer, and the latter has the full topology in the original mapping class group by the established prime-order normalizer comparison. Passing along these actual embeddings gives the full topology on \(F_7\) in \(J\). The final cocycle-removal argument below only needs the closure of its actual image, so it does not depend on this stronger identification.

## 3. The completed two-track quotient

The Fadell--Neuwirth projection gives the actual extension
\[
 1\longrightarrow F_6\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow F_5
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
It splits as a group extension because the quotient is free. The kernel is finitely generated. Characteristic finite-index subgroups of \(F_6\), together with the finite action on their quotients, prove its full induced profinite topology in the split extension. Thus its profinite completion is exact.

The actual forgetful maps in F.14 identify the closure of \(J\) with the kernel of the composite two-point forgetting map. One can see the equality directly from the two successive completed Birman sequences: the closure contains their first kernel \(\widehat F_6\), and its image is the second kernel \(\widehat F_5\). Comparison with the just established completed Fadell--Neuwirth sequence identifies this closure with \(\widehat J\). This records the embedding, rather than identifying a completion with a closure without proof.

The map \(\theta\) is surjective. Its first coordinate is the Fadell--Neuwirth projection. On that projection's kernel, the second track is the filling map
\[
 \pi_1(M\setminus\{x_0\},rx_0)\longrightarrow\pi_1(M,rx_0),
\]
which is onto; it corrects the second coordinate after choosing the first. Consequently
\[
 \widehat\theta:\widehat J\twoheadrightarrow
 \widehat Q=\widehat F_5\times\widehat F_5

is the actual completed two-track quotient. The two track kernels are the restrictions of the two one-point forgetting kernels. Their intersection is therefore precisely
\[

All intersections here occur inside the same embedded \(\widehat J\) and the same eight-point completion.

## 4. The exact input to Boggi--Zalesskii

Comparison of the two Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre spectral sequences therefore proves goodness of \(J\). The corresponding comparison for
\[
 1\to J\to E\to C_2\to1,\qquad
 1\to\widehat J\to\widehat E\to C_2\to1

proves goodness of \(E\). Here \(\widehat E=\widehat J\rtimes C_2\), by the full topology of a finite-index subgroup.

These comparisons also apply to the discrete \(\mathbb F_2[[\widehat E]]\)-modules appearing in the exact wording of Theorem A. Each such module is the filtered union of its finite submodules. ## 5. From total-group conjugacy to the exact B-coboundary

Let \(z\in B\) satisfy \(zR(z)=1\). Then \(\langle zR\rangle\) is an order-two section subgroup of \(\widehat E\). Theorem A(i) conjugates it to a discrete order-two subgroup of \(E\). Its image in \(C_2\) is nontrivial, since conjugation preserves that image. It is therefore a discrete section, already shown to be \(J\)-conjugate to \(\langle R\rangle\).

A conjugator in \(\widehat E=\widehat J\rtimes\langle R\rangle\) can be replaced by its \(\widehat J\) factor because its \(R\) factor centralizes \(\langle R\rangle\). Choosing the inverse of that conjugator when necessary gives the exact equation
\[
 z=j^{-1}R(j)\qquad(j\in\widehat J).
\]
Applying \(\widehat\theta\) shows that \(q=\widehat\theta(j)\) belongs to \(\widehat Q^R\). This fixed group is exactly the graph
\[
 \widehat Q^R=\{(u,r_*u):u\in\widehat F_5\}.
\]

The compact closure of the actual \(F_7=C_J(R)\) maps onto this graph: the actual map is the puncture-filling surjection \(F_7\to F_5\) followed by its graph inclusion, and completion preserves its surjectivity. Choose
\[
 c\in\overline{C_J(R)},\qquad \widehat\theta(c)=q.
\]
It satisfies \(R(c)=c\). Then
\[
 b=c^{-1}j\in B,\qquad
 b^{-1}R(b)=j^{-1}c\,R(c)^{-1}R(j)=z.
\]
This is the asserted \(B\)-coboundary, with its exact map to \(Q\) removed.
