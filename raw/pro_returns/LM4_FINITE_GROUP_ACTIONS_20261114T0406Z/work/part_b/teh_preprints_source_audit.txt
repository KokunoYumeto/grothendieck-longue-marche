1. EXACT CONFIGURATION SPACE AND TOPOLOGY

Fix a literal enumeration of labels by positive integers. A configuration
z=(z_i) is an infinite sequence of distinct complex numbers, with only
finitely many z_i in each compact planar set. Its counting measure is
mu_z=sum_i delta_(z_i). Write

 d_prod(z,w) = sum_(i>=1) 2^(-i) min(1, |z_i-w_i|).

For a countable compactly supported test family (phi_j) detecting vague
convergence, put

 d_V(mu,nu) = sum_(j>=1) 2^(-j)
              |integral phi_j d(mu-nu)| /
              (1+|integral phi_j d(mu-nu)|),
 d_Sigma(z,w) = d_prod(z,w)+d_V(mu_z,mu_w).

Thus the topology simultaneously requires coordinate convergence and
vague counting-measure convergence. These are the definitions at the
locators above. The point zero is allowed. The positive-integer sequence
(1,2,3,...) is a chosen base configuration for the fundamental groups,
not a restriction on every point of the configuration space. Neither
collar lemma requires that base configuration. The explicit collar
example below is even nonzero and has a fixed positive-integer tail.

2. COLLAR ASSERTIONS: COMPLETE DATA USED IN THE AUDIT

T25 Lemma 5.10:
R>0; epsilon>0; R/2+epsilon<R and 2R<3R-epsilon;
A={R<=|z|<=2R}, B={R/2<=|z|<=3R};
K compact metric; F:K->unordered configurations vague-continuous.
Its functions are

 u_in(x)=sup{|z|: z in F(x), R/2-epsilon<=|z|<R/2},
          with empty value R/2-epsilon;
 v_out(x)=inf{|z|: z in F(x), 3R<|z|<=3R+epsilon},
          with empty value 3R+epsilon.

The assertions are USC(u_in), LSC(v_out), and continuous separators
u_in<r_in<R/2 and 3R<r_out<v_out. The stated radial isotopy starts at
identity, fixes A and radii <=R/2-epsilon or >=3R+epsilon, is supported
in the two closed epsilon-collars, and sends radii r_in,r_out to R/2,3R
at time one, making the resulting configuration avoid boundary(B).

T26 Lemma 6.7:
K compact metric; F vague-continuous; R,epsilon>0;
u(x)=sup{|z|:z in F(x), R-epsilon<=|z|<R}, empty value R-epsilon.
It asserts USC(u). For closed L with F(L) avoiding the closed
epsilon-collar, it asserts continuous r with u<r<R on K\L and r=R
on L, and a radial isotopy supported in that collar, stationary on L,
starting at identity, whose time-one inverse takes radius R to r(x).

COUNTEREXAMPLE AND DIRECT SUPREMUM CALCULATION

Set K=[0,1/8] and

 F(t)={1-t} union {10,11,12,...}.

This is infinite, simple, locally finite, nonzero and labelled by
(1-t,10,11,...). Every compactly supported test function phi gives

 integral phi d(mu_(F(t))) = phi(1-t)+sum_(m>=10) phi(m),

where the displayed tail sum is fixed and finite. Hence F is
vague-continuous. Its only varying ordered coordinate is the first,
so it is also continuous in the exact ordered sum topology.

For T25 take R=2 and epsilon=1/4. The required inequalities are
1+1/4<2 and 4<6-1/4. The inner collar is [3/4,1) in radius. At t=0
it is empty; at every 0<t<=1/8 it contains exactly the point 1-t.
Consequently

 u_in(0)=3/4,
 u_in(t)=1-t  (0<t<=1/8),
 limsup_(t downarrow 0) u_in(t)=1>3/4.

For T26 take R=1, epsilon=1/4, and the allowed closed set L empty.
Its u is exactly the same function.

Moreover, any continuous r with u(t)<r(t)<1 would have r(0)>=1,
because the positive-t inequalities force liminf_(t downarrow 0)r(t)>=1.
This contradicts r(0)<1. Thus both semicontinuity and the required
continuous-separator assertion actually fail on these admissible data.

The printed proofs express {u<c} as a countably infinite intersection
of open compact-avoidance sets and then declare it open (T25 lines
1816-1828; T26 lines 2194-2206). Here c=7/8 gives
{t:u(t)<c}={0} on [0,1/8], which is not open. This computes the
specific failed openness step rather than merely questioning an
application of an insertion theorem.

3. TAIL-REPRESENTATIVE ASSERTION AND PAIR-WINDING COUNTEREXAMPLE

T26 Lemma 6.8 asserts:
for n>=1 and g in V_n=ker(p_n), a representative gamma exists with
gamma_k(t)=k for k<=n and gamma_m(t) outside K_(n-1) for m>n,
where K_r is the closed disk of radius r.

Use n=3. At the base configuration (1,2,3,4,5,...), fix all strands
except strand 4. For strand 4 use the following rational polygonal
lasso; pairs list (real part, imaginary part):

 tail:
 (4,0), (4,1), (5/4,1), (5/4,1/4);
 square:
 (5/4,1/4), (3/4,1/4), (3/4,-1/4), (5/4,-1/4), (5/4,1/4);
 reversed tail back to (4,0).

All intermediate points avoid the other positive integers. The square
is positively oriented and encloses 1 but not 2 or 3. The tail and
its reverse cancel in every winding calculation. Thus the continuous
ordered-pair maps

 (z_i)_i |-> z_4-z_1,       (z_i)_i |-> z_4-z_2

to C\{0} give windings 1 and 0. Only one coordinate moves in a fixed
compact set, so the lasso is continuous in d_Sigma. Its first three
coordinates are constant and its class g belongs to V_3.

Any representative demanded by Lemma 6.8 would have its first two
coordinates equal to 1,2 and its fourth coordinate z(t) outside
the closed radius-two disk. Then

 (s,t) |-> z(t)-(1+s),       0<=s,t<=1,

is a homotopy of loops in C\{0}, since |z(t)|>2 and 1+s lies in
[1,2]. The two windings would therefore be equal. They are invariant
under every based configuration homotopy through the two displayed
continuous maps, contradicting their values 1 and 0. This refutes
the actual representative assertion with its prescribed finite labels.

The rational polygon is independently checked in
code/teh_configuration_audit_certificate.py. No numerical winding or
floating-point approximation enters the proof.

4. AN INDEPENDENT INFINITE REALIZATION AND COMPLETENESS FAILURE

The target assertion is T26 Theorem 6.14:
g_(n+1) in g_n V_n for all n implies intersection_n(g_n V_n) nonempty.
Its displayed metric in Definition 6.17 records the largest matching
finite truncation by d(g,h)=2^(-(N(g,h)+1)) for distinct g,h;
Theorem 6.18 asserts completeness for that metric.

For every m>=3, let a_m be a based configuration loop moving only
strand m along the preceding type of lasso once around 1, zero
around 2, with all other positive-integer strands fixed. The
same tail construction with (m,0),(m,1) in place of (4,0),(4,1)
works. Set g_1=g_2=1 and g_n=a_3 a_4 ... a_n for n>=3.
Then g_(n+1)=g_n a_(n+1) and a_(n+1) lies in V_n. Every g_n is
an actual class represented by a finite concatenation of continuous
locally finite configuration loops.

We prove directly the needed restriction on any actual continuous
configuration loop z(t), 0<=t<=1:

 For each compact planar K, only finitely many labels meet K
 at any time of the loop.

Otherwise take labels m_i tending to infinity, parameters t_i
converging to t_*, and z_(m_i)(t_i) converging to a point p of K.
Local finiteness at t_* allows a nonnegative compactly supported
function equal to one near p whose support contains at most the
single limiting configuration point p. If p is absent at t_*,
its limiting integral is zero but the integrals along t_i are
at least one. If p is present with label j, coordinate continuity
and the extra labels m_i give integrals at least two while the
limiting integral is one. Each conclusion contradicts vague
continuity. This proves the restriction without importing T26.

Choose a closed disk containing both first-strand and second-strand
tracks of z, and enlarge it slightly. All sufficiently late
strands avoid this disk uniformly. For such an m, the formula

 (s,t) |-> z_m(t)-((1-s)z_1(t)+s z_2(t))

is nonzero throughout the square. It proves equality of the two
pair windings of strand m with strands 1 and 2.

A class in every g_n V_n would instead have winding 1 with strand 1
and winding 0 with strand 2 for every m>=3, by choosing n>=m.
This contradicts the restriction just proved. Thus the asserted
intersection is empty. In the displayed finite-truncation formula,
(g_n) is Cauchy because g_n^(-1)g_m lies in V_n for m>=n.
Convergence in that formula would force the impossible common
finite-truncation data. Consequently its claimed completeness fails.
This argument does not require injectivity of the map to the
finite-truncation inverse limit.

The same winding data also appear in T26 Construction 4.6 and
Proposition 4.9. The direct argument above establishes the conflict
without relying on either source proof. It does not assert that the
quotient topology on the topological fundamental group lacks some
other complete compatible metric.

5. FURTHER PROOF-CHECK BOUNDARY AND USABLE FINITE MAPS

The infinite straightening in T26 Proposition 5.9 was also inspected.
In its compact-support induction, for a fixed target disk the text
first controls the supports S_k up to L, obtains an escape index M_R,
and starts the induction at n_start=max{L,M_R,j+2}+1. The induction
then assumes S_k avoids the target disk for every L<k<n.
At n=n_start this already includes the intermediate earlier stages
L+1,...,n_start-1, whose avoidance has not been established by those
choices. This is an unproved induction input in the written argument,
not an explicit refutation of its injectivity conclusion. A single
globally consistent construction for all target disks would also
have to be retained.

The finite-truncation local constructions in T26 Section 3 use
compactly supported finite-point ambient maps and give explicit
finite-configuration bundle charts. The local formulas were read;
they do not themselves display a lift continuous in the original
closed-disk map-and-inverse topology of C81.

C83 addresses that exact next map. Its actual evaluation source,
operator labels, target subspace topology and explicit nonliftable
path are all specified and proved independently. The source
counterexamples above are not used to infer C83.
