THE ACTUAL GT0 NEGATIVE TRANSPOSITION: FULL CENTRALIZER AND TRANSPORTERS
=====================================================================
Separate unnumbered proved continuation. No frozen source is modified.
The original D74.40 solution set remains the one defined using the
same unique h. No realization or nonemptiness conclusion is asserted.

1. The specified actual automorphism and its extension
-----------------------------------------------------
Retain the actual nonidentity involution alpha in GT0, its original
F=Fhat(x,y), z=(xy)^(-1), and its unique h in F' from D74. Put
  Y=h y h^(-1),  psi=Inn(h) theta,  J=alpha psi,
where theta(x)=y, theta(y)=x. The original identities give
  psi^2=1,  alpha psi=psi alpha,  J^2=1,
  psi(x)=Y, psi(Y)=x, J(x)=Y^(-1), J(Y)=x^(-1).             (1)
For the commutation, theta alpha theta=Inn(f)alpha and
alpha(h)=hf give
  psi alpha=Inn(hf)alpha theta=alpha psi.
The square of psi uses h theta(h)=1. All these are actual
automorphisms of the original F. On its named abelian basis,
  alpha_*=-I,  psi_*=[[0,1],[1,0]],
  J_*=[[0,-1],[-1,0]].                                   (2)
Thus J's outer class exchanges the original x and y cusps, fixes
the z cusp, and has the full global multiplier -1. This last value
uses the original alpha multiplier, not only its 2-adic coordinate.

Form the specified extension
  E_J=F semidirect_J <j | j^2=1>,
  u:E_J->C2, u(j)=1.                                     (3)
Let L_x=<x>, L_y=<y>, L_z=<z>, with full procyclic closures.
There is an actual a_z in F with J(z)=a_z z^(-1)a_z^(-1).
Set v=a_z^(-1)j. Then v normalizes L_z and inverts z.
Its square is in F and centralizes z, so v^2=z^a for one full
exponent a, by A.7. Conjugating this equation by v gives a=-a;
the full Zhat has no element killed by two, so v^2=1.

The two actual boundary-orbit stabilizers are
  D_x=N_(E_J)(L_x)=L_x,
  D_z=N_(E_J)(L_z)=L_z semidirect_-1 <v>.                 (4)
For D_x, an odd-parity normalizer would preserve the x cusp class,
contrary to the stated exchange of distinct x and y classes.
Its even-parity normalizer is L_x by A.7. For D_z the kernel in F
is L_z and v supplies the other coset. The inverse continuous maps
for the second equality in (4) are
  (a,epsilon)->z^a v^epsilon,
  d->(a,u(d)), where d v^(-u(d))=z^a.                    (5)

2. One full section class, without identifying its fixed subgroup
----------------------------------------------------------------
On H^1(F,F2), (2) is the nontrivial two-dimensional regular C2
module. Its positive C2 cohomology is zero. The split extension
(3) and its two-row Hochschild--Serre sequence consequently give
  dim H^n(E_J,F2)=1 for n>=3.                            (6)
This is the literal regular-module computation used in D37, now
with the original x,y labels; no relabelling of the cusps is needed.
Every nontrivial finite subgroup injects into E_J/F=C2, since F
is torsion-free. The full Brown restriction theorem used in D37
therefore applies. Each section class contributes at least one
dimension to (6), and the section v already exists. Thus there is
exactly one E_J-conjugacy class of nontrivial sections.

Conjugacy is also by F: for a section S, E_J=F S, and its S factor
centralizes itself. Hence both v and zv, and also j, are F-conjugate.
At this point (6) supplies only zero mod-two H1 for their fixed
subgroup. The full assertion below will use finite permutation
coefficients and the original boundary maps.

3. Exact three-cusp restriction of a two-orbit boundary module
--------------------------------------------------------------
Choose b in F so that
  v x v^(-1)=b y^(-1)b^(-1).
This is permitted by the original multiplier -1 and cusp exchange.
With k=F2 form
  P_boundary=k[[E_J/D_x]] direct_sum k[[E_J/D_z]],
  0->Delta->P_boundary--augmentation-->k->0.              (7)
There are explicit F-equivariant homeomorphisms of the three
original coset spaces onto the indicated parity orbits:
  F/L_x -> E_J/D_x,     aL_x -> aD_x,
  F/L_y -> E_J/D_x,     aL_y -> a b^(-1)vD_x,
  F/L_z -> E_J/D_z,     aL_z -> aD_z.                     (8)
The first two images are disjoint and together exhaust E_J/D_x.
The stabilizer of b^(-1)vD_x in F is L_y, by the displayed equation
for v x v^(-1); the other stabilizers are L_x and L_z. These facts
prove bijectivity. Compactness and the Hausdorff coset topologies
give inverse continuity. Thus Delta restricted to F is exactly
the original full three-cusp augmentation module.

For completeness, left multiplication by v in these coordinates is
  aL_x -> (v a v^(-1)b)L_y,
  aL_y -> (v a b^(-1)v^(-1))L_x,
  aL_z -> (v a v^(-1))L_z.                               (9)
Together with ordinary left F multiplication these are the transported
E_J action. All representatives on the right lie in F. The maps
come from the coset action in (8), which verifies independence of
representatives and the group law. The exchanged cusp pair has not
been replaced by a fixed cusp or omitted.

4. The exact finite-coefficient half-rank calculation
----------------------------------------------------
For a finite continuous k[E_J]-module M define
  H_M=Hom_(k,continuous)(Delta,M),
  (e phi)(d)=e phi(e^(-1)d).
It is the actual discrete coefficient module used for the relative
groups R^n(E_J,M)=H^(n-1)(E_J,H_M), n>=1. Applying continuous Hom
to (7) and Shapiro gives the actual subgroup-restriction sequence
  ... -> H^n(E_J,M)
      -> H^n(D_x,M) direct_sum H^n(D_z,M)
      -> R^(n+1)(E_J,M) -> H^(n+1)(E_J,M) -> ... .         (10)
All compact k-vector spaces in (7) are projective over k, so its
underlying vector-space split gives the required exact Hom sequence.

The coefficient evaluation pairing is
  M tensor H_(M^vee) -> H_k,
       m tensor phi -> [d->phi(d)(m)].                   (11)
It is E_J-equivariant. Through the explicit maps (8), the surface
pair is the original completed pair of pants with x,y,z. Its perfect
absolute-relative cup pairings give, for
  A=H^0(F,M), B=H^1(F,M),
the equivariant identifications
  H^0(F,H_(M^vee))=B^vee,
  H^1(F,H_(M^vee))=A^vee.                               (12)
These are precisely the cup comparisons of D74.13--17 applied to
the module (7) via (8). The target orientation group is one-dimensional
over F2, so its E_J/F action is trivial even though (9) exchanges two
boundary summands. Equivariance follows from (11), not from a
geometric realization of J.

The ordinary Hochschild--Serre sequences for M and H_(M^vee) have
only rows zero and one. In positive quotient columns their entries
are, respectively, Abar,Bbar and (B^vee)bar,(A^vee)bar, where
  Vbar=ker(1+sigma)/im(1+sigma).
The evaluation pairing between Vbar and (V^vee)bar is perfect;
the annihilator identities are those explicitly proved in D74.19.
Multiplication by the actual quotient class u is the identity in
these cyclic positive-column coordinates.

The filtered cup product from (11) has the same derivation identity
as D74.23. The product of an absolute fiber-row-one element with a
shifted-relative fiber-row-one element is zero in
H^2(F,H_k). Thus the two d2 maps are transposes for the perfect
positive-column pairings, and have the same rank. There are no later
differentials. Consequently, for self-dual M, the stable absolute
and relative dimensions are equal. Multiplication by u identifies
successive sufficiently high groups, as in D74.24--25.

The procyclic D_x has cd_2=1, so its terms in (10) vanish in these
high degrees. Its u-localization is zero; no positive-periodicity
assertion for a nonzero D_x group is needed. D_z has the usual
two-row sequence from L_z and C2, so its high groups are likewise
identified by u. Define degree-zero localizations
  A_M=H^bullet(E_J,M)[u^(-1)]_0,
  B_M=H^bullet(D_z,M)[u^(-1)]_0,
  R_M=R^bullet(E_J,M)[u^(-1)]_0.
They are finite-dimensional and (10) becomes the exact cyclic
sequence A_M -> B_M -> R_M -> A_M, with its connecting map divided
by u. Since dim A_M=dim R_M, the elementary three-rank calculation
of D74.27 gives, for the literal boundary restriction,
  rank(r_boundary)=one_half dim B_M.                     (13)

5. Two occurrences of the one section force its full centralizer
----------------------------------------------------------------
Put S=<v>, C=C_F(v). Before proving anything about C, the exact
normalizer maps are
  C x S -> N_(E_J)(S),        (c,v^epsilon)->c v^epsilon,
  n -> (n v^(-u(n)),v^u(n)).                             (14)
They are continuous inverses, since an order-two subgroup has no
nontrivial automorphism. In D_z the two internal section classes
are S and S'=<zv>. Their normalizers in D_z are precisely the
sections themselves: a rotation commuting with a reflection has
exponent a with 2a=0. Formula (5) supplies all such elements.

Apply the same full finite-coefficient Brown restriction theorem
as D74.29--30. The open groups F and L_z have cd_2=1, there is no
C2 x C2 subgroup, and the high cohomology with finite M is finite
by the two-row sequences. Hence the theorem's dense restriction
maps are onto their Hausdorff targets. With
  U_M=H^bullet(N_(E_J)(S),M)[u^(-1)]_0,
  V_M=H^bullet(S,M)[u^(-1)]_0,
the first gives A_M=U_M, because Section 2 proved one full class.
The second gives B_M=V_M direct_sum V_M after the following exact
coefficient transport for the second local occurrence.

Choose an actual a in F with zv=a v a^(-1), whose existence was
proved in Section 2. On cochains the transport is
  B(s1,...,sn) -> a^(-1)B(a s1 a^(-1),...,a sn a^(-1)).
Canonical inner conjugation with this coefficient transport acts
identically on ambient group cohomology, by the explicit bar prism
in D74.32--33. Consequently the actual boundary restriction is
  U_M -> V_M direct_sum V_M,       w->(r_S(w),r_S(w)),     (15)
where r_S is restriction from N_(E_J)(S) to S. No coefficient
automorphism has been suppressed in (15). Equations (13) and (15)
give rank r_S=dim V_M; thus r_S is onto for every finite self-dual M.

Suppose c in C is nonidentity. Choose an open normal N of E_J
contained in F with c not in N. Put W=E_J/N, Q=F/N, and Sbar=SN/N.
The actual finite permutation coefficient
  M=k[W/Sbar]
has its invariant perfect basis pairing, and therefore qualifies
for the preceding calculation. Positive cyclic cohomology of S
on M is the vector space on S-fixed cosets: a two-element orbit
contributes zero and a singleton contributes its basis vector.
This identification also respects the u-localization.

The coset Sbar is fixed. The nontrivial element cbar in Q centralizes
Sbar, so cbar Sbar is a distinct fixed coset. Thus c moves the class
of the basis vector at Sbar to the different class at cbar Sbar.
On the other hand every class in the restriction image of r_S is
C-invariant. Indeed conjugation by c is inner on N_(E_J)(S), while
on S its group arguments are unchanged and its coefficient action
is exactly c on M. Naturality of restriction and the same bar prism
prove this assertion before and after localization. The moved basis
class contradicts surjectivity of r_S. Hence
  C_F(v)=1, N_(E_J)(S)=S, and C_F(J)=1.                  (16)
The last equality uses the original j section and its F-conjugacy
to v from Section 2. It is a full original-group equality.

Every involution in E_J now has its own C2 as full centralizer.
The nonidentity involution set is closed: it is the intersection
of {g:g^2=1} with the clopen set E_J\F. The actual F is an open
projective subgroup of index two. These verify the same hypotheses
of Haran Proposition 2.2 used in D74, so E_J is real projective.
This is its stated weak finite embedding-problem property, with
no assertion that solutions are onto.

6. Unique original cusp transporters and their full coordinates
----------------------------------------------------------------
There is now a unique g in F with
  g v g^(-1)=zv, equivalently [g,v]=z.                   (17)
Existence is Section 2 and uniqueness is C_F(v)=1. On the named
full abelian basis write [g]=(a,b). Since v induces the same matrix
as J in (2), (17) gives
  (I-J_*)[g]=[z]=(-1,-1), hence a+b=-1.                 (18)
In particular the exact character
  chi:F->Zhat,  chi(x)=chi(y)=-1
satisfies chi(g)=1. It is not asserted that g belongs to a free
basis or that a-b is a full unit.

This character also gives the exact centralizer coordinates of g:
  C_F(g)=(C_F(g) cap ker chi) x <g>.
The maps send c to (c g^(-chi(c)),g^chi(c)) and multiply in the
other direction. The factor <g> is the full Zhat, since chi splits
its exponent map. A nontrivial first factor has a nontrivial pro-p
Sylow subgroup for some p. Because F is torsion-free, any nontrivial
element in that subgroup generates a closed Z_p. Its direct product
with the central Z_p in <g> is a closed Z_p x Z_p inside F, which
has nonzero H2 over F_p, contrary to cd_p(F)=1. Thus that first
factor is trivial. A normalizer of <g> acts by a unit exponent;
applying chi forces that exponent to be one. Therefore
  C_F(g)=N_F(<g>)=<g> isomorphic to Zhat.                (19)
These are the same explicit primitive-character coordinates used
in the accepted original-tau cusp note, now for this actual g.

For every nu in Zhat, let epsilon be its residue in {0,1} modulo
two. The entire original transporter set is the singleton
  {a in F:a v a^(-1)=z^nu v}
       ={z^((nu-epsilon)/2) g^epsilon}.                (20)
The half-exponent uses the continuous inverse of multiplication by
two on 2 Zhat. Direct conjugation by the displayed element gives
z^(nu-epsilon+epsilon)v; uniqueness again uses (16). The dependence
on nu is continuous on its two clopen parity pieces. Its inverse
on its compact image is the specified character chi,
because chi(z) = 2 and chi(g)=1 give chi(a)=nu.

In particular for every full unit mu the exact transporter is
  a_mu=z^((mu-1)/2)g.                                    (21)
The group, the original cusp z, the section v, and every exponent
in (17)--(21) are retained. These formulas do not assert that
g and J(g) generate F or align the other two original cusps.

7. Retained scope
------------------
The new conclusion is the full trivial fixed subgroup of the actual
negative transposition J in (1), together with its marked extension,
real-projectivity statement and unique original cusp transporters.
Its boundary calculation uses (7)--(9), with the paired x/y cusp
orbit kept through exact maps. The D74 two-row cup/transgression
proof, finite-coefficient Brown theorem and Haran characterization
are the already checked sources used here; no new literature
recognition theorem is imported.

The separate original norm branches still use the unique original h,
and T has not been proved nonempty. No additional local pro-p H_r
has been substituted for h. No numbered claim is assigned by this
note, and no free-product realization of E or E_J is concluded.
