3. Haran's Example 5.5 specialized inside this very full coproduct
----------------------------------------------------------------
Example 5.5, printed p.283, supplies a direct obstruction to a
general injectivity assertion for a map defined by conjugate factors.
Here is the specialized proof with all group maps specified.

Set A0=<tau>, B0=<xi> in M. Let L be the set of closed subgroups
H of M that contain a conjugate of A0 and a conjugate of B0.
For H in L define compact nonempty subsets of M by

  X_H={u in M : u A0 u^-1 subset H},
  Y_H={v in M : v B0 v^-1 subset H}.

They are closed because the finite-factor conjugation maps are
continuous and H is closed. For a descending chain of such H's,
the X_H's and Y_H's have the finite intersection property. Their
intersections give a pair of conjugators in the intersection of
the chain. Thus L is closed under descending chains and has a
minimal member H*. For some u,v in M, putting

  a*=u tau u^-1, b*=v xi v^-1,

minimality gives H*=<a*,b*>. The full coproduct gives an epimorphism

  e:M -> H*, tau |-> a*, xi |-> b*.

This epimorphism has nontrivial kernel. To prove that assertion,
suppose its kernel were trivial. A continuous bijection of compact
Hausdorff groups is an isomorphism, so e^-1 would be continuous.
Use the explicit epimorphism

  Q:M -> S4, tau |-> (12), xi |-> (134).

Permutation products act on the left, (ab)(i)=a(b(i)). Then
Q(xi tau)=(1234). The homomorphism Q e^-1:H*->S4 is onto, and
with r*=e(xi tau) the proper subgroup

  H**=<a*, r* b* (r*)^-1> <= H*

has image <(12),(241)>, the order-six stabilizer of 3 in S4.
Both its named generators are still M-conjugates of the original
factors, so H** belongs to L, contradicting minimality of H*.
Consequently ker(e) is nontrivial. This proves directly that the
conjugate-factor generator map need not be a marked embedding.

The same finite map already exhibits a proper closed subgroup
H1=<tau, r xi r^-1> of M, with the explicit word r=xi tau.
Its Q-image is the same point stabilizer. Therefore M is not
minimal in L and H* is proper in M. This conclusion does not
identify H* or H1 with the original H3.

4. Exact finite-index comparison for every such generator map
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let e:M->H<=M be a homomorphism onto a subgroup generated by an
M-conjugate of tau and an M-conjugate of xi or xi^-1. The composite
q e sends the two factors to a transposition and a three-cycle in
S3. These generate S3 and satisfy (AB)^2=1, so

  q e = a q

for an automorphism a:S3->S3. Its inverse is the inverse permutation
of the six elements. In particular e(F) is contained in F, and
for h=e(g) one has h in F exactly when q(g)=1. Thus

  H intersect F=e(F),       HF=M.

The subgroup e(F) is topologically generated by e(x),e(y), where
x=(tau xi)^-2 and y=xi(tau xi)^-2 xi^-1. These equalities give the
actual maps behind the index comparison. The coset bijection

  F/(H intersect F) -> M/H,   f(H intersect F) |-> fH

is well defined, injective, and surjective because M=FH.

There is no proper open H in this class. Indeed an open H would
give an open L0=H intersect F of finite index d=[M:H] in F.
Finite-coset Schreier rewriting gives L0 free profinite of rank
1+d, so dim H^1(L0,F2)=1+d. On the other hand its two displayed
generators show dim H^1(L0,F2)<=2. Hence d=1 and H=M.
The Schreier computation is the same actual open-subgroup
comparison used in D.32: intersect L0 with the dense discrete
F(x,y), use its degree-d finite covering graph with d vertices
and 2d edges, and take the completion of its free group of rank
2d-d+1. A finite-index subgroup has the full induced profinite
topology, so that completion is precisely L0.

It follows that the proper H* constructed above has infinite
index. For the actual H3, this proves the exact equality of sets
of alternatives: H3=M or [M:H3] is infinite. Its finite index has
not been established. No rank or congruence calculation supplies it.
