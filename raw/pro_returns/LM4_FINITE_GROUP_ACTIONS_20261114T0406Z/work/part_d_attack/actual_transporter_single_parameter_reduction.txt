2. Exact elimination of the conjugator variable, in both directions
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Put u0(mu)=x^k u1^(-1), where k=k(mu). For every mu in Zhat^times,
  u0 r u0^(-1)=x^k t1 x^(-k)=x^mu s.                    (4)

  z^mu r=z^k(zr)z^(-k),
  Y^(-mu)s=Y^(-k-1)t2 Y^(k+1).                           (5)
These use only the exact inversion actions of r and s.

Take an actual (mu,u) in S. Write B=x^mu Y^mu and R=x^mu s.
The involution R inverts B, by direct multiplication using alpha(x)
and alpha(Y). Since B=u z^(-mu)u^(-1) and mu is a unit, u^(-1)Ru
normalizes the whole original <z>. Its parity is odd, so D74's exact
normalizer description gives a unique a in Zhat with
  u^(-1)Ru=z^a r.
The left side is in the t1 section class. The two reflection classes
of the original D_z remain the different global labels t1 and t2;
therefore a belongs to 2 Zhat. Set b=a/2 and u'=u z^b. Then
  u'^(-1)Ru'=r.
Both u' and u0 in (4) conjugate r to R. Their ratio centralizes r in
F, so u'=u0. Right multiplication by z^b preserves the original
boundary equation; hence
  u0(z^mu r)u0^(-1)=B^(-1)R=Y^(-mu)s.
By (1) and (5), the two F-elements u0 z^k u2 and Y^(-k-1) conjugate
t2 to the same involution. Their equality follows from C_F(t2)=1.
Consequently
  u0 z^k u2=Y^(-k-1),
which is precisely D(k)=1.

Conversely, take mu in T. The equality D(k)=1 says
u0 z^k u2=Y^(-k-1). Equations (1), (4) and (5) give
  u0(z^mu r)u0^(-1)=Y^(-mu)s,
  u0 r u0^(-1)=x^mu s.
Multiply these equalities in the displayed order. Since r^2=1,
  u0 z^mu u0^(-1)
     =(Y^(-mu)s)(x^mu s)
     =Y^(-mu)x^(-mu)=B^(-1).
Taking inverses proves the original equation for (mu,u0). Thus the
two directions use the literal original z, its two labelled endpoints,
and both proved full transporter centralizers.

The complete solution-space comparison is the homeomorphism
  Phi:T x Zhat -> S,
      (mu,lambda) -> (mu, x^k u1^(-1) z^lambda).            (6)
The centralizer of (xy)^mu in F is <xy>=<z>, by A.7 and the unit
condition. Thus these are all the conjugators, and the exponent is
unique. For an explicit inverse, let chi_x:F->Zhat be the original
x-coordinate character. Since chi_x(z)=-1, the inverse is
  (mu,u) -> (mu, -chi_x(u0(mu)^(-1)u)).                    (7)
On S the element inside chi_x belongs to <z>. Substitution in (6)
and (7) proves both composites are identities. All displayed maps
are continuous, including the full exponent maps.

5. Finite reductions: the same-level arrow and an actual refinement
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Let N be any open normal subgroup of E contained in F. Write
  A_N=E/N, H_N=F/N, e_N=exp(H_N),
and retain the images of the actual x,Y,z,u1,u2 in H_N. For

       x_N^m Y_N^m=u(x_N y_N)^m u^(-1)}.
There is an explicit same-level injection
  T_N -> B_N,
  m -> (m, x_N^k_m u1_N^(-1)).                             (14)
The sufficiency calculation in Section 2 uses only endpoint identities,
so it proves (14) directly in the finite group. Reduction to a coarser
actual quotient respects T_N and this injection. The sign identity
(9) likewise descends to m -> -m on every T_N.

In the other direction the proved statement is the following actual
refinement inclusion: for every N there is an open normal N'<=N in E,
contained in F, such that reduction of the unit coordinate gives
  B_N' -> T_N, (m,u) -> m mod 2e_N.                       (15)
Here is the full proof, including the two uses of compactness. These
compactness arguments refine neighborhoods of already known exact
centralizers and inverters; they assert no nonempty solution set.

First choose an open normal M<=N in E so that
  [g,t_i] in M, g in F  ==>  g in N,       i=1,2.          (16)
For each i, the compact subset F minus N has no commuter with t_i.
At each of its points choose an open normal subgroup of E excluding
the nonidentity commutator; continuity supplies a neighborhood on
which it is still excluded. A finite subcover and intersection give
(16). Intersect M further with the kernel of the actual mod-two
abelianization E->C2^3. In that abelianization the three section
images are (0,0,1),(1,0,1),(0,1,1), and [z]=(1,1,0).

The exact set of outside inverters of z in E is <z>r, by the original
full normalizer. Choose N'<=M so that every v in E minus F satisfying
  v z v^(-1)=z^(-1) modulo N'
has its image modulo M in the image of <z>r. This refinement exists
by another finite-subcover argument on the compact set
  (E minus F) minus (<z>r M).
Every point in this set fails the exact inverter equation; exclude its
nontrivial error in an open normal quotient and intersect finitely
many of those quotients. The product <z>r M is a finite union of
M-cosets, so the complement used here is compact.

Take any (m,u_N') in B_N'. Lift m to mu in Zhat^times and u_N' to
u in F, and put k=(mu-1)/2, B=x^muY^mu, R=x^mu s. The equation modulo

  u'=u z^(a/2).
Then u' r u'^(-1)=R modulo M. Equivalently
x^(-k)u'u1 centralizes t1 modulo M. Property (16) gives
  u'=x^k u1^(-1) modulo N.                                 (17)

The boundary equation still holds modulo M after this right rotation.
Together with the normalized reflection it gives
  u'(z^mu r)u'^(-1)=Y^(-mu)s modulo M.
Using (1) and (5), the element Y^(k+1)u'z^k u2 centralizes t2 modulo
M. A second application of (16) gives
  u' z^k u2=Y^(-k-1) modulo N.
Insert (17). The resulting equation is D_N(k)=1, proving (15).
The conclusion is independent of all chosen lifts because k modulo
e_N is determined by m modulo 2e_N. Quotient exponents divide the
exponents at a refinement, so the displayed unit reduction is defined.
