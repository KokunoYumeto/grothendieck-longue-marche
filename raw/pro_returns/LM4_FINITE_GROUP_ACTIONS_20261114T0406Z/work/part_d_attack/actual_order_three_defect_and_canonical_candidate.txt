1. An actual positive order-three overgroup and full inverse maps
----------------------------------------------------------------
The accepted GT0 construction gives the actual automorphism Omega
of E with
  Omega|_F=omega, Omega(s)=y^(-1)f s, Omega^3=1.
The original Theta has square one. Direct substitution on x,y gives
  (theta omega)^2=Inn(x^(-1)).
Restriction to F is injective for the automorphisms of E under
consideration. Indeed an automorphism fixing F pointwise sends s
to another lift of its conjugation action; their quotient centralizes
F. Here C_E(F)=1: its even part is Z(F)=1, and an odd element would
make alpha inner, contrary to its abelian matrix -I. Therefore
  (Theta Omega)^2=Inn(x^(-1)) on the actual E.           (OT.3)

Identify E with its inner automorphisms (its center is trivial),
and in Aut(E) let xi=Omega and retain tau=Theta. Use the topology
of action on the characteristic finite quotients of the finitely
generated E; these quotients are cofinal among its finite quotients.
Put
  M=<F,tau,xi>,  M_all=<E,tau,xi>.
These are profinite groups: the displayed relations put M in at
most six cosets of the compact F, and M_all in at most six cosets
of the compact E. The actual quotient M/F is S3. On the named
abelianization modulo two, theta and omega have matrices
[[0,1],[1,0]] and [[0,1],[1,1]], respectively. Their six products
are the six different invertible two-by-two matrices over F2,
so all six quotient elements are distinct. In particular
  tau^2=1, xi^3=1, (tau xi)^2=x^(-1),
  xi x xi^(-1)=y, xi y xi^(-1)=z.                     (OT.4)
The original G embeds as E<tau> by these identifications: Theta
cannot be inner in E, since its matrix on the original F_ab is
the coordinate swap, whereas elements of E act there by I or -I.

Here is a full coproduct identification, with its inverse checked.
From C2(A) amalg C3(B) map A to tau and B to xi. In that coproduct
write
  X=(AB)^(-2)=B^2 A B^2 A,
  Y=B X B^(-1)=A B^2 A B^2,
  Z=B^2 X B^(-2)=B A B^2 A B=(XY)^(-1).
Reduction using A^2=B^3=1 gives
  A X A=Y, A Y A=X,
  B X B^(-1)=Y, B Y B^(-1)=Z, B Z B^(-1)=X.             (OT.5)
Thus H=<X,Y> is normal. Its quotient has A^2=B^3=(AB)^2=1
and is exactly S3, by the standard transposition/three-cycle map.
The map Fhat(X_0,Y_0)->H sending its basis to X,Y is onto. Its
composite with H->F in M sends X_0,Y_0 to the original free basis
x,y and is an isomorphism. Hence both component maps are inverse
isomorphisms on these kernels. The quotient S3 map is also an
isomorphism, so the full map C2 amalg C3->M is an isomorphism.
Its inverse is explicitly
  tau->A, xi->B, x->X, y->Y.                            (OT.6)
Both composites fix the indicated generators and are continuous.
In particular the actual word identities also include
  x=(tau xi)^(-2), y=(tau xi^(-1))^2,
  p=xi^(-1)tau xi^(-1).                                (OT.7)
The already used subgroup F<xi> is the original F semidirect_omega
C3: the maps (d,i)->d xi^i and the coset-coordinate inverse identify
it with the cyclic extension in the frozen norm note.

The actual s normalizes M. The original relations give
  s tau s=f^(-1)tau, s xi s=f^(-1)y xi.                 (OT.8)
This identifies its conjugation action with the given alpha on F.
For comparison, the standard delta has the explicit extension to M
  delta_M(tau)=tau, delta_M(xi)=tau xi^(-1)tau=y xi.
These images respect the factor orders and square to the identity;

5. Transport from an extension to the original pants equation
-------------------------------------------------------------
We use the following elementary consequence for the given actual
alpha. An alpha-inverted element A that is F-conjugate to x^nu,
for a full unit nu, equals x^nu itself. To verify this precise
assertion, write A=l x^nu l^(-1). The inversion equation gives
l^(-1)alpha(l) in C_F(x^nu)=<x>, so l^(-1)alpha(l)=x^e.
Then l^(-1)s l=x^e s. The latter is in the original s section
class, so e is in 2 Zhat: the two internal <x,s> classes are the
distinct full classes s and xs from D74. Therefore l x^(e/2)
is alpha-fixed, and the full C_F(alpha)=1 makes it 1. This proves
the stated equality. The identical argument with Y replaces
<x,s> by <Y,s>, whose two classes are s and Ys. Thus an
alpha-inverted element conjugate to Y^nu equals Y^nu.

For an extension (OT.14), the literal source identities
xi z xi^(-1)=x and xi x xi^(-1)=y give
  A_nu=kappa Z_nu kappa^(-1),
  B_nu=kappa A_nu kappa^(-1).
Since Z_nu is conjugate to the original z^nu, epsilon=+1 makes
A_nu conjugate to x^nu and B_nu conjugate to y^nu. Epsilon=-1
makes A_nu conjugate to y^nu and B_nu conjugate to x^nu.
Equation (OT.11) inverts both A_nu,B_nu under alpha. The preceding
normalizer argument, using Y conjugate to y, therefore gives
  epsilon=+1: A_nu=x^nu, B_nu=Y^nu,
  epsilon=-1: A_nu=Y^nu, B_nu=x^nu.                    (OT.18)
The product equation (OT.10) now proves nu belongs to the original
T, in the second case by conjugating Y^nu x^nu to x^nu Y^nu.
This transport does not first assume gamma_nu is onto.

---------------------------------------------------

Choose an original pants conjugator u with

centralizer is <p>; squaring in that full Zhat then shows

    g_nu=z^e g^(-1)z^e,

Every full unit is in exactly one displayed clopen branch. The

v_nu=w_nu j w_nu^(-1). These statements are also checked directly;

7. The exact canonical candidate and the full remaining word
------------------------------------------------------------
Projection (nu,epsilon,c)->nu maps Z_ext onto T by Sections 5--6.
It is one-to-one. Indeed C_M(beta(F))=1: modulo F, an element
centralizing beta(F) acts trivially on its spanning original
mod-two abelian image, so is 1 in the faithful S3=GL2(F2) action.
Inside F, (OT.2) reduces the centralizer to that of <z,g>.
An element there is z^d by C_F(z)=<z>. A nontrivial such element
centralizing g puts a nontrivial element in <z> cap g<z>g^(-1).
The original full free factor <z> is malnormal in F; hence g
would belong to <z>, contradicting its mod-two abelian vector
(1,0), since z has vector (1,1). Thus d=0. The same centralizer
calculation applies to gamma_nu(F), by (OT.11).
Two possible xi images induce the same conjugation on this image
of F, so their ratio centralizes it and is 1. Formula (OT.23)
is the explicit inverse of the projection. It is continuous on
the two clopen mod-four branches. Thus projection is a homeomorphism.

Define the following one full original word, even before knowing
that m is a unit:
  W=w^(-1)z^b g w x^(-m) in F'.                        (OT.24)
Its abelian vector is zero because [z^b g]=(a-b,0)=(m,0).
The exact solution-set identity is
  T={epsilon m:epsilon in {+1,-1}, m in Zhat^*, W=1}.   (OT.25)
Here is its full verification. By (OT.17), all original solutions
are among m,-m, and their existence puts m in the full unit group.
Also T is stable under negation: inversion of the original product
and conjugation by x^(-nu) carries a conjugator u for nu to

4, (OT.22) gives A_m=x^m, which is exactly W=1 by (OT.10) and
(m+1)/2=-b.
Conversely m in Zhat^* and W=1 give A_m=x^m. The exact psi/theta
intertwining then gives B_m=Y^m. Their retained product in (OT.10)
is x^mY^m=w^(-1)(xy)^m w. Negating gives the other original
solution. These are the required full equations, not conclusions
drawn from their abelian images.

The corresponding complete original conjugator fibers are
  (m, w^(-1) z^lambda),
  (-m, x^(-m)w^(-1)z^lambda), lambda in Zhat,           (OT.26)
on the exact domain m in Zhat^*, W=1. Full cyclic centralizers

Equivalently, the same solution set is retained through the full

Only candidates in the full unit domain that satisfy all three
