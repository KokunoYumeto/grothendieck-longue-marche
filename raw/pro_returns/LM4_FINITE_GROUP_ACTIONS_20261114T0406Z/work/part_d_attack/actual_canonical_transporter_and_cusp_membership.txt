1. Original groups, signs, and the unique order-three conjugator
----------------------------------------------------------------
Use the accepted M=C2(tau)*C3(xi) inside M_all, with original
F=Fhat(x,y), z=(xy)^-1, x=(tau xi)^-2,
p=xi^-1 tau xi^-1=x tau, and p^2=z^-1.
Write pi3:M->S3 with pi3(tau)=A3 and pi3(xi)=B3.
The two independent signs of kappa in the four-element K are
  epsilon: pi3(kappa)=B3^epsilon,
  sigma: kappa is M-conjugate to xi^sigma.
The four pairs of signs occur exactly once. In particular there
is a unique kappa+ with (sigma,epsilon)=(+1,+1). We give an
exact construction of that element from the original input.

The original involution satisfies
  s tau s=f^-1 tau,  s xi s=f^-1 y xi,
  tau xi^-1 tau=y xi.
There is a unique a in F such that
  s xi s=a(tau xi^-1 tau)a^-1.                         (CT.1)
Indeed the full finite-factor conjugacy theorem makes s xi s
M-conjugate to xi^-1: its order is three and its M_ab orientation
is negative. A conjugator to xi^-1 has a transposition as its
S3 coordinate. Right multiplication by a unique power of xi
makes that coordinate A3, without changing the conjugated element.
Multiplying that conjugator by tau on the right gives a in F
with (CT.1). Two choices of a differ by an element of
  F intersect C_M(tau xi^-1 tau)=1,
using the exact conjugate finite-factor centralizer.

In fact a belongs to F'. Comparing the original F components
in (CT.1) gives
  f^-1 y=a y omega(a^-1).
In the original abelianization, f has vector zero. Hence
  (I-Omega)[a]=0,   Omega=[[0,-1],[1,-1]].
The determinant is three. Multiplication by three is injective
on Zhat, so [a]=0. No inverse of three is used.

Set, in this precise order,
  d=tau a^-1 s.                                       (CT.2)
It inverts xi. Its square belongs to C_M(xi)=<xi>. Conjugation
by d both fixes d^2 and inverts <xi>, so d^2=(d^2)^-1; the
order-three cyclic group has no nontrivial element of order two.
Thus d is an involution, with quotient (sigma0,A3) in
M_all/F=C2(sigma0) x S3. The complete normalizer is
  N_M_all(<xi>)=<xi,d>=S3.
Of its three negative involutions, d is the unique one with
that specific quotient coordinate.

The accepted original J theorem gives a unique L in F with
  L d L^-1=j,  where j=st=h tau s.                     (CT.3)
Existence uses the one F-conjugacy class in the original Fj.
Uniqueness uses C_F(j)=1. Define
  kappa+=L xi L^-1.                                   (CT.4)
It is inverted by j, has order three, and has both signs positive,
so it is the specified unique point of K. Conversely its unique
F-conjugator to xi is L: C_M(xi) intersect F=1. The recovered
element L^-1 j L is the unique d in the indicated normalizer
coset, and then a is recovered from d=tau a^-1s. These are
typed inverse descriptions, with no change of the original h.

5. Both full cusp-word equivalences
------------------------------------
Let kappa-=t kappa+ t. Its signs are (sigma,epsilon)=(+1,-1)
and its coefficient is still nu0. For either sign put
  Q_kappa=kappa^-1 t kappa^-1,
  C_kappa=Q_kappa w^-1 p^(-nu0) w in F.
These definitions do not require nu0 to be a full unit.
Since j inverts kappa and tj=s, one has exactly
  Q_kappa j=kappa^-1 s kappa.
Conjugating by w and using (CT.7) yields
  (w Q_kappa w^-1)v
    =(w kappa^-1 xi) r (w kappa^-1 xi)^-1.
Also p^nu0 v=p^(nu0+1)r=z^(-k-1)r. Consequently
  C_kappa=1 iff
  (w kappa^-1 xi) r (w kappa^-1 xi)^-1=z^(-k-1)r.     (CT.11)

For kappa+, the conjugator in (CT.11) is precisely A in F.
The distinct F-classes of r and zr force k+1 to be even
whenever (CT.11) holds: if k+1=2e+1, the right side is
z^-e(z^-1r)z^e, and z^-1r=z^-1(zr)z has the other class.
Write e=(k+1)/2 in the even case. Its right side is
z^-e r z^e. The trivial full centralizer C_F(r) makes the
F-transporter unique, so A=z^-e. Conversely A in <z> has
the form z^a0 with 2a0=-(k+1), by (CT.9). Inversion by r
then proves (CT.11). This gives the complete equivalence
  C_kappa+=1 iff A in <z>.                            (CT.12)

For kappa-, put Araw=w kappa-^-1 xi. Exact multiplication gives
  B=Araw t_r p^-1,  Araw=B p t_r.
Indeed xi t_r=t xi and kappa-=t kappa+ t, so the first
word equals w t kappa+^-1 xi p^-1=t' A p^-1.
Since t_r centralizes r and p r p^-1=z^-1r, (CT.11) becomes
  B(z^-1r)B^-1=z^(-k-1)r.                             (CT.13)
Now B is in F and z^-1r has the zr section class. The two
distinct classes force k to be even. For e=k/2, the right
side is z^-e(z^-1r)z^e. Triviality of C_F(z^-1r), which is
the conjugate of C_F(zr), forces B=z^-e. Conversely (CT.9)
forces precisely that exponent when B lies in <z>, and
substitution proves (CT.13). Thus
  C_kappa-=1 iff B in <z>.                            (CT.14)
Both arguments use the full original centralizers and full
section classes; no pro-p fixed subgroup is substituted.

6. One forced two-branch word and the original T
------------------------------------------------

  B in <z> iff A in g^-1<z>.

every element of <z> has equal coordinates modulo two.

For all k in Zhat define the continuous, explicitly branched map
  R(k)=z^(-(k+1)/2)       for k in 1+2 Zhat,
  R(k)=g^-1 z^(-k/2)      for k in 2 Zhat.              (CT.15)
The two divisions are only on the displayed even ideals.

The two remaining points of K are the inverses of kappa+ and
kappa-. Their coefficients are -nu0 and their Q elements are
Q_kappa^-1. Hence their cusp words vanish exactly with the
corresponding positive-orientation word. The accepted four-point

The original unit gate and the full equality in (CT.17) are
