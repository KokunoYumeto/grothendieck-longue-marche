  F = Fhat(x,y), z=(xy)^(-1), E=F semidirect_alpha <s>,
  alpha(x)=x^(-1), alpha(y)=f^(-1)y^(-1)f, f in F', alpha^2=1.
D74 proves that all three full involution classes have centralizer C2,
that E is real projective, and that the exact equation
  f=h^(-1)alpha(h)
has its unique solution h in F'. In particular alpha(f)=f^(-1).
Put Y=h y h^(-1). The actual involutions
  t0=s, t1=xs, t2=Ys
represent the three classes. Write Ti=<ti>.
Their images in A0=H_1(E,F2), with coordinates (x,y,s), are respectively
  (0,0,1), (1,0,1), (0,1,1).
They are pairwise distinct. A0 is the actual elementary abelian quotient
of E, not a substituted free-product model.

The original peripheral subgroups and normalizers are
  Lx=<x>, Ly=<y>, Lz=<z>, Dj=N_E(Lj).
Thus Dx=<s,xs> and Dy=<fs,yfs> are the actual full dihedral groups,

A. A stronger, proved lifting property of the three actual sections
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For every finite surjection pi:B->A, continuous phi:E->A, and specified
homomorphisms sigma_i:Ti->B satisfying pi sigma_i=phi|Ti, there are
  gamma:E->B and k_i in ker(pi)
such that
  pi gamma=phi,
  gamma(t_i)=k_i sigma_i(t_i) k_i^(-1), i=0,1,2.                (A1)
Neither gamma nor the sigma_i are required to be onto. A sigma_i may
be trivial. This is an unconditional property of the actual D74 E.

Here are all finite refinements and maps proving it. Replace A by phi(E)
and B by its inverse image; this preserves ker(pi) and the local maps.
For each i the clopen compact set
  Wi=E \ (Ti ker(phi))
contains no element centralizing t_i, because C_E(t_i)=Ti. For each g
in Wi, choose an open normal subgroup N_i,g of E contained in
ker(phi) cap F cap ker(E->A0), with [g,t_i] not in N_i,g. The coset
g N_i,g still has this property. Finitely many such cosets cover Wi.
Intersect these finitely many open normal subgroups for all three i;
when a Wi is empty it contributes no conditions. The resulting N gives
  q:E->J=E/N, lambda:J->A, lambda q=phi,
and satisfies
  lambda(C_J(q(t_i))) <= phi(Ti).                             (A2)
Indeed a lift g of a finite centralizer element cannot belong to Wi.
Also q(t_i) are nonidentity and pairwise nonconjugate: their images in
the actual quotient A0 remain the three distinct displayed vectors.

Form the finite pullback
  Bhat = {(b,j) in B x J : pi(b)=lambda(j)},
  p:Bhat->J, p(b,j)=j,
  beta:Bhat->B, beta(b,j)=b.
Both coordinate maps are continuous homomorphisms; p is onto. Set
  b_i=(sigma_i(t_i),q(t_i)) in Bhat,
  C=union_i {v b_i v^(-1):v in Bhat}.
Each b_i has order exactly two, even when sigma_i is trivial. Every
involution t in E is g t_i g^(-1) for some i,g. Lift q(g) through p;
conjugating b_i by that lift shows q(t) lies in p(C). Moreover no
q(t) is trivial. These are exactly the data of Haran 1990 Definition
1.1(4), and D74 real projectivity plus Proposition 2.3 supplies
  Gamma:E->Bhat, p Gamma=q, Gamma(Inv(E)) subset C.             (A3)

Since the three q(t_i) are nonconjugate, for each i there is v_i in
Bhat with Gamma(t_i)=v_i b_i v_i^(-1); the factor index must be i.
Then p(v_i) centralizes q(t_i). By (A2), choose epsilon_i in {0,1}
with lambda(p(v_i))=phi(t_i)^epsilon_i. Define
  gamma=beta Gamma,
  k_i=beta(v_i) sigma_i(t_i)^(-epsilon_i).
We have pi(k_i)=1. Multiplication by this power does not change the
conjugation of sigma_i(t_i), so (A1) follows. This also handles
phi(t_i)=1 and sigma_i(t_i)=1 without changing any domain.

The same property holds for a surjection of arbitrary profinite groups
pi:B->A with the same specified maps. For each open normal V of B use
the finite problem
  B/V -> A/pi(V), phi_V, (sigma_i)_V.
Its kernel is (ker(pi)V)/V. The sets of solutions, including the three
kernel conjugators, are finite and nonempty: E is generated by x,y,s.
Reduction carries solutions at finer V to solutions at coarser V.
A common refinement solves every finite collection of compatibility
requirements. Compactness gives compatible solutions at all V, whose
inverse limits are the required continuous gamma and k_i in ker(pi).
The sets pi(V) form a neighbourhood basis in A, so pi gamma=phi
as actual profinite maps. This supplies the inverse-limit step directly.

Consequently (E,{T0,T1,T2}) has Wilkes's projective-pair lifting property
(Definitions 6.1--6.2 of the retained 2209.06492v1). The proof just given
uses the actual centralizers and Haran's restricted involution lifts;
it does not infer full pair projectivity from cohomological vanishing.

B. The precise cohomological comparison
---------------------------------------
D76.15 proves H^2(E,{Ti};M)=0 for every finite F2[E]-module M. For an
odd prime p and finite Fp[E]-module M, the actual index-two extension
has
  H^n(E,M)=H^n(F,M)^{C2}=0 for n>=2,
since averaging by 1/2 kills the positive C2 rows and cd_p(F)=1.
Also H^n(Ti,M)=0 for n>=1. The actual relative long exact sequence
therefore gives H^2(E,{Ti};M)=0 at every odd p. A filtration of a
finite abelian E-module by its primary and elementary-abelian layers,
and exactness in the coefficient module, gives the same vanishing for
every finite abelian E-module.

Wilkes Proposition 6.3 identifies this vanishing with the solvability
of the specified relative problems having finite abelian kernel.
Corollary 6.4 records that equivalence. His Proposition 6.6 promotes
the property through solvable kernels in its prosolvable setting.
Proposition 6.8 constructs its free-product map for a pro-p group and
uses its H_1 isomorphism to prove surjectivity; Theorem 6.9 retains that
pro-p hypothesis. Section A above gives a stronger finite lifting fact
for this E, but supplies no such surjectivity argument for the actual
full free-product comparison below.

C. An actual labelled retract of four involutions and a map to three
------------------------------------------------------------------
Let P4 be the full free profinite product of the four specified groups
C2(a), C2(b), C2(c), C2(d). The continuous homomorphism
  q4:P4->E,
  (a,b,c,d) |-> (s,xs,fs,yfs)                                 (C1)
is onto: ba maps to x, dc maps to y, and a maps to s. All four target
elements are involutions; this follows directly from the alpha formulas
and alpha(f)=f^(-1).

Its parity kernel P4+ is the full free profinite group on
  X=ba, U=dc, V=ca.
For an explicit proof, let R=Fhat(X,U,V) and define the involution
  delta(X)=X^(-1), delta(V)=V^(-1),
  delta(U)=V^(-1)U^(-1)V.
The maps
  P4 -> R semidirect_delta <a>,
      (a,b,c,d) |-> (a,Xa,Va,UVa),
  R semidirect_delta <a> -> P4,
      (X,U,V,a) |-> (ba,dc,ca,a)
are inverse continuous homomorphisms. Each named generator maps back
to itself, delta^2=1, and the three products Xa,Va,UVa have square one.
Thus these maps prove the kernel and basis assertion without changing
from the full profinite category.

On this exact kernel, q4 has the maps
  q4|P4+:Fhat(X,U,V)->Fhat(x,y),
  X|->x, U|->y, V|->f.
Its kernel is exactly the closed normal closure in P4+ of
  V f(X,U)^(-1).                                              (C2)
Indeed quotienting by this element eliminates V and gives the inverse
map Fhat(x,y)->P4+/ker(q4), x|->X, y|->U. The kernel is invariant
under delta because q4 intertwines delta with alpha.

Put H=h(X,U), so q4(H)=h. For the three Ti in section A choose the
specified lifts
  t0|->a, t1|->b, t2|->H d H^(-1).
For the last equality, in E one has
  h(yfs)h^(-1)=h y f alpha(h)^(-1)s=h y h^(-1)s=Ys.
Apply section A's profinite lifting property to pi=q4 and phi=id_E.
It gives a continuous section
  i:E->P4, q4 i=id_E,
and k_i in ker(q4) which conjugate these three specified local lifts
to i(t_i). In particular i is injective. Conjugating i by an element
of ker(q4) arranges i(s)=a, while retaining q4 i=id_E and the other
two labelled conjugacy conclusions.

Now define the actual full free product P3=C2(a)*C2(b)*C2(d) and fold
  rho:P4->P3, (a,b,c,d)|->(a,b,a,d).
It preserves parity. Its restriction to the parity kernels is
  Fhat(X,U,V)->Fhat(X0=ba,U0=da),
  (X,U,V)|->(X0,U0,1).
The continuous map
  tau=rho i:E->P3                                             (C3)
sends the actual F into P3+=Fhat(X0,U0), maps s to a, and satisfies
  tau|F composed with alpha = delta0 composed with tau|F,
where delta0 inverts X0 and U0. It sends the three full involution
classes to their respective a,b,d classes, by the displayed lifts.

On full abelianizations identify F_ab with Zhat^2 via x,y and P3+_ab
with Zhat^2 via X0,U0. The maps (q4|P4+)_ab and (rho|P4+)_ab have
the same matrix (X,U,V)|->(x,y,0), because f lies in F'. Hence
  (tau|F)_ab = identity on Zhat^2.                             (C4)
This also proves that the induced map on each maximal pro-p quotient
is an automorphism, by the Frattini criterion and finite-generation
Hopficity for the two free pro-p groups. Formula (C4) is a statement
about the actual homomorphism (C3), before taking any pro-p quotient.

The unproved points in this comparison are precise. We have not proved
that tau|F is injective or onto, or that tau maps the original x,y,z
to conjugates of a common unit power of X0,U0,(X0 U0)^(-1).
For example, after i(s)=a the first image has the actual form
  tau(x)=rho(k_1) b rho(k_1)^(-1) a,
for a kernel conjugator k_1. Section A supplies no equality of this
element with a conjugate of X0^mu. The maps (C1)--(C4) therefore do
not establish a solution of D74.40. D77 only addresses its specified
arbitrary representatives; its failed y-cusp alignment is retained.

D. Executed finite tests of the full peripheral lifting step
------------------------------------------------------------
D.1. A finite abelian kernel, with incompatible shared endpoints.
The distinction needed here is expressed by one actual commuting
diagram and a proved obstruction, not by omitting the peripheral maps.
Set
  A=A0=F2^3, coordinates (x,y,s),
  B=F2^4, basis (a,b,c,d), written additively,
  pi(a)=pi(c)=sbar,
  pi(b)=xbar+sbar, pi(d)=ybar+sbar.
This is an onto finite homomorphism with
  ker(pi)=<a+c> of order two.
Let phi:E->A be its actual mod-two abelianization.

Choose b_z in Dz in the t1 class; D34's original triangle supplies
this endpoint. Thus phi(b_z)=xbar+sbar. On the three full dihedral
groups specify the continuous homomorphisms

  source       rotation image in B      reflection image in B
  Dx           x |-> a+b                s |-> a
  Dy           y |-> c+d               fs |-> c
  Dz           z |-> b+d              b_z |-> b.

Each map factors through the actual quotient of Zhat semidirect C2
which reduces the rotation exponent modulo two; its target is an
elementary abelian group. The involution, inversion and square
relations therefore hold, and pi sigma_j=phi|Dj on both generators.
Their local endpoint classes use precisely the original triangle.

There is no continuous gamma:E->B with pi gamma=phi which agrees
with all these sigma_j, even allowing ker(pi)-conjugation. The latter
conjugations are trivial since B is abelian. The actual relation
xyz=1 would give
  0=gamma(x)+gamma(y)+gamma(z)
   =(a+b)+(c+d)+(b+d)=a+c,
contradicting the nonzero kernel generator. Already the first two
local maps conflict: fs=h^(-1)sh in E, whereas their prescribed
abelian images are respectively c and a.

For comparison, the three individual sections t0,t1,t2 with images
a,b,d do have a global lift. It is the homomorphism through A with
  gamma(s)=a, gamma(x)=a+b, gamma(y)=a+d.                     (D1)
Since h is a commutator, gamma(t2)=gamma(Ys)=d. Thus the three
section assignments commute with pi and are simultaneously realized,
while the stated additional original-y dihedral map is not.

This is a deliberately specified incompatible relative lift problem.
It is not a counterexample to D74.40, and gives no counterexample to
the existence of compatible peripheral choices. It proves exactly
that section A's universal relative lifting statement cannot be
promoted to arbitrary specified lifts of all three original Dj.

Executed verification:

  code/pants_peripheral_lift_obstruction_output.json
  code/pants_peripheral_lift_obstruction_stdout.txt
The code checks all 256 addition pairs for pi, each local quotient
map, the original xyz obstruction, and all 4096 linear maps A->B.
Exactly eight of the linear maps lift id_A. None realizes all three
peripheral maps; precisely one realizes the three prescribed section
images, namely (D1). All nineteen assertions for this first comparison
passed.

D.2. An A5 kernel, compatible endpoint classes, and injective local
projections.

The following stronger obstruction retains compatibility of all endpoint
conjugacy classes and makes each local image project isomorphically to
its specified finite peripheral image.

Let A=(Z/6)^2 semidirect_{-1} C2, with the fully specified operation
  (v,e)(w,d)=(v+(-1)^e w,e+d).
The actual homomorphism
  phi:E->A,
  x|->((1,0),0), y|->((0,1),0), s|->((0,0),1)               (D2)
exists because alpha induces inversion on the full F_ab and f is a
commutator. It is onto. In particular phi(f)=phi(h)=1. Put
  B=A5 x A, pi:B->A the second projection, K=ker(pi)=A5 x {1}.
Thus |A|=72, |B|=4320 and |K|=60.

Permutations below act on {1,2,3,4,5}; multiplication is composition,
with the right factor applied first. Define
  a=(12)(34), b=(13)(24), c=(12)(35),
  R=ba=(14)(23), Z=ca=(345).
Specify three continuous homomorphisms to A5 by the actual dihedral
generators:
  rho_x(x)=R, rho_x(s)=a;
  rho_y(y)=R, rho_y(fs)=a;
  rho_z(z)=Z, rho_z(b_z)=a,                                  (D3)
where b_z is the same actual t1-class endpoint chosen in Dz above.
In each case a has order two and conjugates the stated rotation to
its inverse. The rotation orders are respectively 2,2,3. These are
therefore homomorphisms of the full Dj, not just assignments to a
pro-p or discrete quotient. Define the precise local lift maps
  sigma_j:Dj->B, d|->(rho_j(d),phi(d)).                        (D4)
Then pi sigma_j=phi|Dj.

Each rotation phi(x),phi(y),phi(z) has order six, and the chosen
reflection has nontrivial C2-coordinate. Therefore
  ker(phi|Dj)=<c_j^6>, where (c_x,c_y,c_z)=(x,y,z).
Every rho_j kills this subgroup because its rotation order divides
six. It follows that pi maps sigma_j(Dj) isomorphically onto phi(Dj),
both of order twelve. Equivalently sigma_j(Dj) is the graph of a
specified homomorphism phi(Dj)->A5. This verifies the local-injectivity
condition also used in the Haran--Jarden embedding-problem convention.

All six canonical endpoint images under the first components are
nontrivial involutions of A5. In the e_2 representatives they are
  Dx: (a,b), Dy: (a,b), Dz: (a,a).
For the last pair e_2 is zero modulo three, so rho_z(z^{e_2})=1;
this finite fusion is retained explicitly. All fifteen involutions
of A5 are conjugate. In fact g=(234) satisfies g a g^(-1)=b.
These give explicit K-conjugators at every endpoint. To compare
different occurrences of one actual Ti, first transport along the
actual F-conjugators from D74. Since the map
  gamma0:E->B, gamma0(e)=(a^{u(e)},phi(e))                    (D5)
is a continuous global lift of phi and u is zero on those F-conjugators,
this transport has first component one. Its second component makes
the source labels agree by the actual equality in E. The preceding
identity or g in K then aligns each endpoint image with gamma0(t_i).
Thus all three full section classes admit simultaneously compatible
local lifts up to K-conjugacy in this precise finite problem.

Nevertheless no continuous gamma:E->B can satisfy pi gamma=phi and
  gamma|Dj = k_j sigma_j(-) k_j^(-1), with k_j in K,
for all three j. Suppose such gamma exists and let beta=pr_A5 gamma.
The images X=beta(x), Y=beta(y) have order two. The original equality
z=(xy)^(-1) and (D3) force XY to have order three. The group
  Q=<X,Y>=beta(F)
has order six. It is normal in beta(E), since F is normal in E.
Consequently S=beta(s) belongs to N_A5(Q). Moreover alpha(x)=x^(-1)
implies that S centralizes X. For every such ordered pair (X,Y),
  N_A5(Q)=Q and C_{N_A5(Q)}(X)={1,X}.                         (D6)
These are exact finite equalities, verified exhaustively below. They
also follow from the usual faithful action of Q on its three-point
orbit and its complementary two-point orbit: a normalizer preserves
those distinct orbit sizes, whose set stabilizer in A5 is Q; the
centralizer of a reflection in Q of order six is that reflection's
order-two subgroup.

On the other hand the Dx condition requires (X,S) to be simultaneously
conjugate in A5 to (R,a). Here a is nonidentity and a is different from
R. Thus S is neither 1 nor X, contradicting (D6).

This proof uses the actual original generators x,y,z, actual quotient
(D2), actual normal subgroup F, and actual alpha(x)=x^(-1). It does
not insert a hypothetical standard presentation of E. It proves that
the particular class-compatible local maps (D4) fail to have a global
lift even though all their local projections are injective. Its role
is to rule out promoting the proved section lifting property to this
stronger unrestricted peripheral lifting property. No assertion is
made that the specific peripheral choices needed in D74.40 encounter
this obstruction. The same proof applies at f=h=1, where the explicit
standard isomorphism E=C2(s)*C2(xs)*C2(ys) is available. Its obstruction
therefore concerns the stated universal local-lifting requirement,
including in that standard model.
