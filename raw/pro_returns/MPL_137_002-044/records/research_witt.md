## 2. The supplied 1983 correction and new binomial question

Searches: `Witt|binomial|119|120|Lazard|Lazare|commutat` in `context/ps_chapters_VI_VII/ps6.tex`; read lines 2870–3060, 3120–3215 and 4975–5010 in full.

In §119, beginning before the original-page marker 501, Grothendieck reports Illusie's rejection of his proposal that integral homology of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces is computed by derived functors of the divided-power algebra functor. The text supplies a specific degree-two illustration: the polynomial group scheme of truncated power series `1+at+bt²` has a nonsplit extension of the additive group by itself over the integers, while the corresponding extension on integer points splits because the integer-valued polynomial `a(a−1)/2` is available. The splitting equation is `P(a+a′)=P(a)+P(a′)+aa′`; its rational-polynomial solutions have the form `t(t−1)/2+ct`, none lying in `Z[t]`. This contrast concerns polynomial schematic cochains versus all discrete cochains, not a counterexample to every possible enriched integral-cochain theory.

On original pp. 502–503, he replaces integral-coefficient polynomial functions by integer-valued rational polynomials and asks whether a binomial symmetric-algebra construction maps quasi-isomorphically to all integral-valued cochains on suitable semisimplicial free abelian groups. The stated connective assumptions are tentative: zero H0, perhaps also zero H1; he expects dévissage toward K(Z,n). This is a **new question in the supplied source**, not a theorem to report as established by the 1970s pages. It supports B05's heading independently.

Two source cautions: the typed text says the binomial polynomials form a basis of “all integer-valued functions,” where the mathematically valid polynomial-basis claim is for integer-valued **polynomial** functions; do not silently repeat the unrestricted claim as mathematics. Its parenthetical uncertainty about prime fields admitting binomial structures is also a historical uncertainty, not a modern theorem or definition.

In §120, original pp. 506–507, his proposed analyzer consists of an operation ring, a composition law, and coaddition/comultiplication maps controlling the action on sums and products. He puts divided powers on an ideal, lambda operations, Adams-type operations, and binomial operations in a common discussion but does not identify these structures with each other. The 137 discussion of multiplication and divided powers can therefore be compared to this later program without asserting that it already specifies the binomial theory.

In §128, around original pp. 540–541, the supplied text explicitly identifies semisimplicial affine schemes with cosemisimplicial commutative algebras and discusses forgetting their algebra structure for linearization. This is a useful *later* comparison supporting B06; it does not by itself prove what the frozen 137 pages mean by every multiplicative structure.
