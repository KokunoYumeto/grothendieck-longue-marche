## Conditional counting argument

Fix a pair (T,T') and write K=N(T)∩N(T'). Suppose the pairs in question form one G-orbit, each torus lies in |W| Borel subgroups, N(T)∩B=T for a Borel B containing T, T∩T'=e, and |K|=hzφ(h). The first and last of these are substantive hypotheses from the surrounding assertions; they are not proved by the count.

K acts on the |W|² choices of (B,B'). An element stabilizing both belongs to N(T)∩B and N(T')∩B', hence to T∩T'=e. Thus the action is free and the number of quadruple orbits is |W|²/(hzφ(h)). If the data also include a generator of the distinguished cyclic group of order h, there are φ(h) generators. Provided K acts on that distinguished cyclic subgroup, the action on the enlarged data remains free, so the count becomes |W|²/(hz). This checks the implication between the stated order/stabilizer assumptions and both counts; it does not prove the classification or the general order formula. In type A the identity N(T)∩B=T is immediate because an invertible monomial upper triangular matrix must be diagonal. ## Exact rank-one example

In PGL₂, take the diagonal torus T and T'=F₂TF₂⁻¹, where F₂ has rows (1,1) and (1,−1). A diagonal matrix in T' is scalar, so T∩T'=e projectively. The common normalizer consists projectively of I, X, Z and XZ, with X swapping the two coordinates and Z=diag(1,−1). To see completeness, a matrix normalizing T is diagonal or antidiagonal; preserving the unordered pair of T' eigenlines (1,1),(1,−1) forces its nonzero entry ratio to be ±1. The four classes form a Klein four group and normalize both tori. Thus h=2, z=2 and φ(h)=1 give the stated order 4 in this model.

The two Borels containing T stabilize the coordinate lines; the two containing T' stabilize the two Fourier lines. Every coordinate line differs from every Fourier line, so all four Borel pairs are opposite. Their Lie-algebra intersections have dimension one in the exact certificate. The common-normalizer action on the four pairs is free, hence gives one orbit. This is a concrete check of the counts and an affirmative rank-one instance of the question; it is not a proof in all types.

## A rank-three Fourier comparison

Over Q(i), let F have entries F_rc=i^(rc), r,c=0,1,2,3. Let T be the diagonal torus in PGL₄ and T'=FTF⁻¹. The regular diagonal D=diag(1,i,−1,−i) conjugates the columns of F cyclically, so it normalizes T' with a four-cycle Weyl action. Its eigenvalues are distinct. A direct linear kernel calculation shows that the only diagonal matrices in the Fourier diagonal algebra are scalar, so T∩T'=e projectively. These are concrete regular/Coxeter-normalization features of the apposition discussion. Identifying this model with the precise terminology of Kostant's cited papers is a background input, not an externally checked definition here.

For the ordinary coordinate order and ordinary Fourier-column order, all complementary flag intersections vanish; the associated two Borel Lie algebras intersect in dimension three. For both orders (0,2,1,3), the two-dimensional steps meet in the line spanned by (2,0,2,0)=F₀+F₂. Their Borel Lie-algebra intersection has dimension four. A maximal torus in PGL₄ has dimension three, so this latter intersection is not a torus. The exact certificate records both computations.

Consequently the universal opposite-Borel conclusion is false for these explicit regular/Coxeter-normalized tori, while some pairs are opposite.
