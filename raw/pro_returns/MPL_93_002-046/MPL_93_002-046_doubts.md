# Legibility and scope of confidence

The printed offprint and most of the elliptic formulary are relatively clear. Typescript is less uniform: broken or overtyped characters, crowded subscripts and superscripts, cut-off right margins, and cancellations by repeated x's create real uncertainty even in otherwise readable sentences. The English reused sheets were inspected upright as well as in their supplied orientation.

The least secure material is the small free handwriting: the moduli glyph and small corrections on 004; the local-j notes on 018; the brief corollary on 021; the heavily cancelled part of 031; the looped sheaf signs on 031/033; the rotated, stained calculation and callout on 036; the faint pencil footnote on 041; the deleted passage and added hypotheses on 042; and the sloping marginal programme and overwritten formulas on 044–046. These locations retain explicit doubts, sometimes with several words in one doubtful phrase. An alternative is a visual candidate, not a proposed mathematical repair.

The only `\illegible{n}` uses are within the cancelled prose on 031, after enlarged inspection; no formula or diagram symbol is replaced by that macro. The ellipsis on 021 is in the manuscript. The last sentence on 046 is physically unfinished and has not been completed. Blank cross-reference slots in the programme remain blank.

Apparent mathematical errors are transcribed literally when the ink is clear. This includes unusual formulas, unexpected source or target subscripts, positive cohomological indices, duplicated punctuation, irregular agreements, and the absence of an arrowhead or parenthesis. A high-confidence entry in the doubt register may document a surprising but visually clear form; it is not an endorsement of the mathematical assertion.

Every whole image and its supplied halves was read directly by the same transcriber in the first pass, followed by a second image audit after all page files were saved. No OCR was used and no image reading was delegated. Mechanical checks verify page coverage, macro syntax, brace/environment balance, inventory fields, concatenation and hashes; they cannot certify that every visual reading is correct. TikZ reproductions encode the written shapes and relations, not physical paper texture, stains, holes, show-through, or printed ruling used merely as a writing background.

The LaTeX is the requested fragment with no preamble. The host document must supply the agreed diplomatic macros and the standard mathematics/TikZ packages. Full compilation could not be tested because the available TeX binaries lack the necessary format and package data; structural validation was performed instead. The diagrams and drawings should receive a typesetting review when incorporated into a host edition.
