# Typed-sheet census: independent review of frozen records

Source scope: the frozen `.tex` page files and `MPL_93_002-046_inventory.csv` only. No scan image, external bibliography, or final notes table was consulted for this census. Thus “recorded” below describes the diplomatic encoding; it is not an independent palaeographic authentication of handwriting.

- **D — Dieudonné/crystals programme (9 sheets):** opening title `Programme de la théorie de Dieudonné sur une base ... où p est localement nilpotent`; later heading `Théorie de déformations`. The cover at scan 037 reads `V ... Programme de la Théorie (Cristaux)`. These are the folder’s own programme sheets according to the frozen inventory, not a named-author attribution inferred from typography.

## Attribution and classification checks

1. **No per-scan `sheet_kind` mismatch was found.** The inventory’s selected set is exactly 003, 005–017, 019–020, 022, 024–025, 028–030, 032, 034, 038–046. The nine other retained scans are seven `text` sheets (004, 018, 021, 023, 031, 033, 036) and two `cover` sheets (027, 037).
2. **Two annotation-status qualifications matter:** scans **016** and **034** should not be described as unqualified “foliation only,” because their records contain additional nonverbal marks. These marks do not establish substantive commentary, and the mark on 034 is explicitly doubtful.
3. **Internal pagination must remain separate from foliation.** Scan 003 has no encoded internal page number; calling it p. 1 is an inference. The nine programme sheets likewise have no encoded internal numbers; calling them pp. 1–9 would impose a sequence absent from these frozen records. Their folios are 37–45.
4. **The hand’s identity is not established by the annotation macros.** The rubric on 003 explicitly names Grothendieck for the correction-marker convention, which is relevant evidence for the marked corrections. It does not by itself authenticate the rubric’s own hand, all other annotations, or the foliation. Likewise, the printed attribution to Tate/Deligne and the Wanderley byline identify texts, not annotators. Only the principal transcriber’s image audit can add a palaeographic attribution.
5. **Correction medium is not uniform or fully encoded.** An insertion or deletion can establish an added/cancelled state without proving whether the change was made in pen or by typing; inventory 044.28, for example, is part of an explicitly overtyped correction. For a final “with his hand” column, prefer “annotations/corrections recorded; attribution not independently authenticated” unless the principal transcriber has direct visual grounds for a stronger statement.
6. **Minor inventory convention:** early foliation elements are sometimes classified as `text` although their `.tex` encoding uses `\margin`; later foliation often has kind `margin note`. Both were counted here. This is an element-label convention, not a disagreement in `sheet_kind`, and no inventory or transcription file was changed.
