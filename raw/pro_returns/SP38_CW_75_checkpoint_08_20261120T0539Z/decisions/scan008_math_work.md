## Orientation data and the successor

Assume the incidence convention of scans004–005: every edge has two distinct endpoints and every contour vertex has two distinct incident edges. A chosen origin map σ:A→S with incident σ(a), and exactly one edge with origin s for every s, is a bijection. Thus each vertex has one outgoing and one incoming edge. In a nonempty connected contour, directing one edge forces each adjacent edge, and then all edges. Propagation closes consistently on a finite cycle; on a two-sided path it continues both ways. Reversal gives the other choice, so there are exactly two orientations, including the two-parallel-edge contour.

Define u_S(s) as the other endpoint of σ⁻¹(s), and u_A(a)=σ⁻¹(u_S(σ(a))). The incoming edge supplies the inverse of u_S. The endpoints of a are σ(a),u_Sσ(a); their images are the endpoints of u_A(a). This proves the automorphism and u_(-ω)=u_ω⁻¹. The words “arc de S,” if confirmed, remain a sort mismatch: an edge belongs to A, whereas S contains vertices.

## Individual adjacency tests fail to be equivalent

The exact certificate `scan008_adjacency_certificate.json`, generated by `scripts/math008.py`, checks four explicit incidence automorphisms. On C4, edges are e_i={i,i+1}. The reflection g_S(i)=1-i sends every vertex to a neighbor, but g_A(i)=-i fixes e_0,e_2. The other reflection g_S(i)=-i fixes vertices, but g_A(i)=-i-1 sends every edge to a distinct adjacent edge. Separate vertex/edge swaps give the analogous failures on the two-edge contour. Thus the written tests 1 and 2 are not separately equivalent under distinct adjacency.

Their conjunction selects the two rotations u,u⁻¹ on a connected cycle of length at least three or on the two-sided path; at length two these coincide. To check this refinement, label a connected contour cyclically (or by Z). A graph automorphism is a shift or reversal. A shift satisfies both conditions exactly for a unit step ±1. A reversal cannot satisfy both: on C4 the two counterexamples above show the alternatives; on an odd cycle a reversal fixes a vertex; on an even cycle greater than four a reversal sends some vertex more than one edge away; on the infinite path displacement c−2i is not always ±1. For length two, the conjunction requires both the vertex swap and the edge swap.

## Order, group, torsors, and finite circular order

On a finite connected n-vertex contour, the successor is an n-cycle on vertices and, by σ, on edges. Its order is n. On the two-edge contour the two orientations are distinct but both successors swap vertices and edges, so u=u⁻¹ and the orientation-to-automorphism map is two-to-one. This is a boundary check, not a claim that the manuscript omits its possible n≥3 qualification.

An orientation-preserving automorphism commutes with u_S; on its transitive orbit it is determined by one vertex image, hence a unique successor power. The edge action is then forced by σ. The group is cyclic and both S and A are torsors; it is finite cyclic in the finite case and infinite cyclic on the bilateral path. The ordinary finite Z/nZ notation should not be silently used for an infinite cardinal.

For finite n≥1, a Z/nZ-torsor is the same data as a transitive n-cycle permutation (the action of 1). With the usual degenerate conventions for n=1,2, that is an oriented finite circular order. No reading of the raised mark after ordre is decided by this observation.
