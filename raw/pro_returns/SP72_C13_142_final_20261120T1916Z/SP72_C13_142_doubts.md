## Legibility and comparison of witnesses

The large main-text lines are usually readable, but this is not a uniformly easy hand. Small lower indices, detached upper strokes, abbreviated words, and long loops can remain ambiguous even in the enlarged crops. A “high” confidence records a clear local reading; it is not a numerical error probability or a guarantee for the whole page. “Medium” and “low” are retained where particular strokes, endings, attachment points, or deletion states do not settle the choice.

The least secure material is the densely crossed marginal writing on 094 and 096, the cancelled and interlinear layers on 098, and the diagonal note on 100. On 096, the purported fixed-point insertion near the first parenthesis was withdrawn after the full second reading and root review: its strokes do not form an independent inscription apart from the intersecting body and marginal text. The unreadable extent in the following marginal note is explicitly uncounted. On 100, main-text strokes intersect the marginal writing and can falsely suggest extra marginal words. The final text marks the surviving uncertainty instead of carrying a complete phrase over merely because a witness supplied one. The isolated signs and tiny subscript on 099 are also left doubtful. The single joined u-mark in the name on 100 retains a circumflex/umlaut alternative; conventional spelling has not been used to make it certain. Some individual early-page annotations remain uncertain even though the surrounding formulas are clear.

Witness C is usually more complete in margins, insertions and cancellations, and often supplies the better continuous prose. Its unmarked readings nevertheless include errors in capitals, ornate letters, short words and entire phrases; the corrections on 094, 097, 099 and 100 make that limitation especially visible. H sometimes gives the better lexical reading, including “sent” and “rondelles” on 099 and the opening of the scan-100 marginal note. It also leaves readable text as illegible, expands abbreviations, changes quote shapes, misplaces additions, and sometimes mistakes an underline for cancellation. Their agreement is treated as a prompt to inspect the ink, never as corroboration from an independent source.

The notation register records recurring forms and their exceptions. Small cap-and-legs signs generally support capital Pi for the groups, but the locally defined component functor on 092 and 097–098 requires a separate reading. Hats, indices, primes and stars remain occurrence-specific. Doubled letterforms have not been imposed uniformly. Clear but surprising letters, repeated equation numbers, incomplete clauses, and nonstandard accents remain in the transcription; the mathematical implications and possible slips are discussed separately in the notes.

The totals in the changes log count recorded places that differ from at least one witness. They include explicitly retained disagreements and do not measure errors per manuscript token. Certainty-only decisions, macro-only differences and source foliation encoded with equivalent macros are excluded from those totals. All remaining doubts are localized in the page text and listed above; uncertainty is not used as a reason to omit visible writing.
