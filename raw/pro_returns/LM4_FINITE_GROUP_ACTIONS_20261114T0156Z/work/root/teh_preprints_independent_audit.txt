Counterexample 1 (one-sided collar supremum and continuous separator).
Take K=[0,1/8] and F(t)={1-t} union {10,11,12,...}. This is a countably infinite, locally finite, simple configuration in the complex plane at every t. For every compactly supported continuous test function phi, the sum over F(t) equals phi(1-t) plus a fixed finite sum, hence is continuous. With the order (1-t,10,11,...), the coordinate metric is also continuous. These facts check the actual stated vague and ordered sum topologies.
For Lemma 5.10 set R=2 and epsilon=1/4. Both numerical inequalities in its hypotheses hold. Its inner collar is [3/4,1) in radius. At t=0 it contains no point of F(0), so u_in(0)=3/4 by the stated empty-set convention. For 0<t<=1/8 it contains exactly 1-t, so u_in(t)=1-t. Thus limsup as t decreases to zero is 1>3/4=u_in(0), contradicting the asserted upper semicontinuity. A continuous r with u_in(t)<r(t)<1 would have r(0)>=1 by the values for t>0, contrary to its strict upper bound at zero. The continuous separator conclusion itself therefore fails.
The same F and epsilon with R=1 give precisely the same u in Lemma 6.7. Choose its allowed closed subset L empty. Its semicontinuity and strict-separator conclusions fail identically. In both written proofs the proposed openness step passes from open compact-avoidance sets to their countably infinite intersection. The explicit configuration above demonstrates the failure of that step in the source topology; the diagnosis does not depend on a merely formal objection.

Counterexample 2 (Lemma 6.8, fixed finite strands and radial avoidance).
Use n=3 at the ordered base configuration (1,2,3,4,5,...). Fix every label except 4. Move label 4 along a based lasso winding once positively around 1 and zero times around 2 and 3, avoiding all positive integers, and return to 4. Such a lasso consists of an arc from 4 to a sufficiently small circle around 1, that circle, and the reversed arc; it can be chosen in the upper half-plane away from its endpoint. Only finitely many coordinates move, and their paths lie in one compact set, so the loop is continuous for the actual ordered sum metric. Its first three coordinates are constant, placing its class in V_3. Pairwise difference maps to C minus zero give windings w_(1,4)=1 and w_(2,4)=0.
Lemma 6.8 requires a representative in the same based class fixing labels 1,2,3 and keeping label 4 outside the closed disk K_2 of radius 2. Write its fourth coordinate z(t). Then z(t)-(1+s), for 0<=s<=1, is a homotopy of nonzero loops: |z(t)|>2 and 1+s lies in [1,2]. Hence the two winding numbers around 1 and 2 are equal. Each is invariant under an arbitrary based configuration homotopy by the corresponding continuous ordered-pair difference map. This contradicts the retained values 1 and 0. Thus Lemma 6.8 itself, not only its supplied proof, is refuted.
