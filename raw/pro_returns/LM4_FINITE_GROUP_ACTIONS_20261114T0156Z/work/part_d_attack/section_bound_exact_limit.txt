An elementary subdirect-product fact, with the required maps
-----------------------------------------------------------
For a subgroup J of G^r, either some coordinate projection is proper,
or all coordinate projections are onto. In the latter case, a proper J
has indices i!=j and an automorphism sigma:G->G with
  J <= {(g_1,...,g_r): g_j=sigma(g_i)}.                       (1)
Here is a proof retaining the comparison. Induct on r. A proper
projection to the first r-1 factors supplies the desired relation by
induction. Otherwise that projection is onto. Its kernel inside the
last factor is a normal subgroup of the simple G. A full kernel gives
J=G^r. A trivial kernel makes J the graph of an onto homomorphism
theta:G^(r-1)->G. The images of its factors are normal and pairwise
commute. At least one is G, so every other image lies in Z(G)=1.
The one nontrivial restriction is an automorphism of G. This gives (1).
The r=2 case is the same graph argument. Thus all uses below have an
actual coordinate or graph map.

The comparison group and its three labelled classes
---------------------------------------------------

  C_i=I^20 x {e_i}, i=0,1,2,

Each C_i is one actual conjugacy class of involutions. The V-coordinates
keep the three labels distinct. Write K*=ker(epsilon)=G^20 x W0,
where W0 has basis e1+e0,e2+e0.

Every choice of one c_i from each C_i normally generates A*. Indeed,
the normal closure of c_i intersects each simple coordinate factor G_j
nontrivially: choose an element of that factor not commuting with the
noncentral jth coordinate of c_i and take its commutator with c_i.
This intersection is normal in G_j, hence is G_j. Therefore the normal
closure of c_i is exactly G^20 x <e_i>. The three normal closures generate
G^20 x V. These are literal normal-closure and projection maps.

The ordinary generator number of A* is exactly three. For a construction,
take the fixed generating pair a=(132), b=(354) in G and 20 distinct
elements r_1,...,r_20 of G. Define three elements
  A=(a,...,a;e0), B=(b,...,b;e1), R=(r_1,...,r_20;e2).
Their projection J to G^20 is onto every coordinate. A relation (1)
would force sigma(a)=a and sigma(b)=b, hence sigma=id because a,b
generate G. It would then force r_i=r_j, contradicting the choices.
Thus J=G^20. The commutator subgroup of <A,B,R> projects onto the
perfect G^20 and has V-coordinate zero, so it contains G^20 x {0}.
The three basis vectors in V finish the generation proof. The quotient
A*->V proves that fewer than three ordinary generators do not suffice.

Nevertheless no tuple in C_0 x C_1 x C_2 generates A*. In each coordinate

The bound holds for every transitive A*-set
------------------------------------------
Let M<=A*, Omega=A*/M, n=|Omega|. For any t in C_i the exact coset count is
  |Fix_Omega(t)|/n = |C_i cap M|/|C_i|.                      (2)
This follows by counting the elements g with g^(-1)t g in M: each
element of C_i cap M has |C_A*(t)| preimages, and passing to cosets
divides by |M|.

Let J be the projection of M to G^20. If J is proper, the subdirect
fact above has two alternatives. A proper coordinate projection
contains at most five of the fifteen involutions. Consequently
  |I^20 cap J|/|I^20| <= 5/15=1/3.
In the graph case (1), for each of the fifteen possible ith involutions
the jth is uniquely prescribed by sigma; automorphisms preserve I.
That ratio is at most 1/15. Projection from C_i cap M to I^20 cap J
is injective, so (2) gives |Fix_Omega(t_i)|<=n/3 for every i.
In this case their sum is at most n, a stronger bound than n+2.

If J=G^20, the commutator projection argument gives G^20 x {0}<=M.
Thus M=G^20 x W for an actual subspace W<=V. Put k=number of basis
vectors e_i lying in W. The quotient action is the translation action
on V/W, so each C_i fixes every point when e_i lies in W and fixes
none otherwise. Hence S=n k, with n=8/|W|. For dimensions 0,1,2,3
the respective bounds on k are 0,1,2,3 and n is 8,4,2,1.
Each gives S<=n+2; the checker also lists all 16 subspaces individually.
Together these exact subgroup cases prove
  sum_i |Fix_Omega(t_i)| <= |Omega|+2
for EVERY transitive finite A*-set, despite the proved empty relative
generating-triple set.

Full primary tests and the actual quotient exclusion
----------------------------------------------------
Since G^20 is perfect, every homomorphism from it to a finite p-group

maximal pro-p quotient for odd p is trivial. For any choices c_i in C_i,

vectors of W0. Their images therefore generate every maximal pro-p
quotient of the specified parity kernel. This is an explicit quotient

In fact K* is not two-generated. Its quotient G^20 is not two-generated:
there are only 19 S5-automorphism orbits of generating pairs in G, so
the same coordinate pigeonhole argument forces any proposed two
generators into a proper graph subgroup. Therefore no surjection from
the actual F=Fhat(x,y) to K* exists.

would satisfy epsilon q=u, the original E/F character. This is checked

every element in K* has an E-lift with u=0. The resulting onto map
Fhat(x,y)->K* contradicts the proved generator obstruction. Hence A*
with these labels cannot occur among the actual finite quotients in
the D74 task. No original peripheral map or value of tau is assigned
to this comparison group.
