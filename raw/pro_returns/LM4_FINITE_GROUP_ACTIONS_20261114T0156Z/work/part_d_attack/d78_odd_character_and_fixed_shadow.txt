1. An exact odd-prime criterion inside the actual fixed subgroup
---------------------------------------------------------------
Retain the original D.78 groups and maps, without replacing any group:

  F = Fhat(x,y), E = F semidirect <v>, T=<w>, B=C_F(w),
  chi:B -> Zhat, A=<a> with chi(a)=1, K=ker chi,
  B=K semidirect A, with K pro-odd.

The following two assertions are equivalent for these actual groups:

  K=1;
  dim_Fp H^1(B0,F_p)=1 for every open B0<=B and every odd prime p.

The coefficients in this display are trivial finite continuous modules,
so H^1 is exactly the vector space of continuous characters. No maximal
pro-p fixed subgroup is substituted for B or B0.

Here are all comparison maps in the nontrivial-kernel direction. Choose
a nontrivial Sylow pro-p subgroup of K. Such a prime exists because a
nontrivial profinite group has a nontrivial finite quotient. It is odd
by D.78. Let A_p=<a^(e_p)> and choose a Sylow pro-p subgroup P of B
containing A_p. Normality of K makes P intersect K in a Sylow pro-p
subgroup of K; hence P intersect K is nontrivial. The restriction

  chi_p:P -> Z_p

is onto, since its restriction to A_p is the specified isomorphism.
The image of P under the full chi is in the p-primary factor of Zhat,
so its kernel is exactly P intersect K.

The group P cannot be procyclic. It is torsion-free as a subgroup of F.
A torsion-free procyclic pro-p group surjecting onto Z_p is Z_p, and a
surjective endomorphism of Z_p is multiplication by a unit and has zero
kernel. This would contradict the exhibited P intersect K.

Every nonprocyclic pro-p group has at least two independent characters
to F_p. One elementary proof is that a finite p-group with only one
independent character has a unique maximal subgroup and is cyclic; if
all finite quotients are cyclic, compactness of their nonempty sets of
compatible generators makes the pro-p inverse limit procyclic. Thus
choose a continuous character ell:P -> F_p independent of chi_p mod p.
Subtract its value on a^(e_p) times chi_p mod p. The resulting character
ell' is zero on A_p and is nonzero on P intersect K: a character zero on
that kernel would factor through P/(P intersect K)=Z_p and would be a
multiple of chi_p mod p.

Choose an open normal subgroup N_B of B with

  N_B intersect P <= ker ell'.

Such a subgroup exists by the induced profinite topology on the closed
subgroup P. Put B0=P N_B. This is an actual open subgroup of B. The
quotient isomorphism

  P/(P intersect N_B) -> B0/N_B,   p(P intersect N_B) -> p N_B

transports ell' to a continuous character psi:B0 -> F_p. It remains
nonzero on B0 intersect K and vanishes on A_p.

The image chi(B0) is an open subgroup d Zhat, where d is a positive
integer. Since B0 contains A_p, p does not divide d. The actual character

  eta:B0 --chi--> d Zhat --divide by d--> Zhat --> F_p

is nonzero on A_p. Therefore psi and eta are independent, proving
dim H^1(B0,F_p)>=2. This contradicts the second displayed assertion.

Conversely, K=1 makes chi:B -> Zhat the original isomorphism, with inverse
t -> a^t. It sends each open subgroup B0 isomorphically onto d Zhat.
Division by d identifies its character space with Hom_cont(Zhat,F_p),
which is one-dimensional. This proves the equivalence.

2. The extra character occurs in an actual finite cover and E-set
---------------------------------------------------------------
For the B0 and psi above, choose an open normal subgroup N_E of E,
contained in F, such that N_E intersect B <= ker psi. The latter kernel
is open in B. Define the actual open subgroup

  U = B0 T N_E <= E.

The product is a subgroup: B0 centralizes T and N_E is normal. A member
of U intersect B has the form b0 t n. Its original parity is zero, so
t=1, and n=b0^(-1)b belongs to N_E intersect B <= B0. Hence

  U intersect B = B0.

The original finite E-set X=E/U has its identity coset fixed by T, with
B-stabilizer precisely B0. The character psi also extends to the actual
continuous character

  psi_U:U -> F_p,   b0 t n -> psi(b0).

To verify its definition, pass to U/N_E. The map from B0 x T onto that
quotient has kernel (B0 x T) intersect N_E, whose T-coordinate is trivial
and whose B0-coordinate lies in ker psi. The homomorphism psi on B0,
zero on T, therefore factors through this quotient exactly as claimed.

The open free group F_U=U intersect F=B0 N_E is the full completion of
the corresponding finite-index subgroup of the original discrete F(x,y).
Conjugation by the specified w preserves F_U. The restriction psi_U|F_U
is w-invariant and nonzero on B0 intersect K; its kernel defines a
specified degree-p covering of that actual finite cover. Thus an odd
character of K supplies an actual additional invariant cyclic-cover

The high-degree localization used by D.78 consequently supplies no
