1. Completely enumerated base data
----------------------------------
Let G=GL(3,F2), acting on the seven nonzero binary vectors numbered 1--7.
The checker constructs G from all 512 binary matrices and their actual
permutations. It proves by finite enumeration:
  |G|=168; G is nonabelian simple and perfect;
  its involutions form one class I of size 21;
  all 179 subgroups are enumerated;
  every proper subgroup is soluble, of derived length at most three;
  every proper subgroup contains at most nine involutions.
There are 19,152 ordered generating pairs, with 57 automorphism orbits,
and 2,352 generating involution triples, with seven automorphism orbits.
There are 3,696 generating pairs (x,y) for which x,y,xy each belong to
I I, and these form eleven automorphism orbits.

The complete automorphism group, of order 336, is computed without a
classification theorem. Fix one generating pair and a spanning tree in
its two-generator labelled Cayley graph. For every ordered generating
target pair, the tree uniquely prescribes a possible map G->G. The
checker verifies every edge labelled by either generator. Passing that
test proves the map respects right multiplication by every word, hence
every group element. Its image contains the target generators, so it
is onto and therefore an automorphism. Conversely every automorphism
occurs from its images of the fixed generating pair. Thus the finite
list is complete. All tuple orbits have size 336, as independently
verified in the enumeration.

Select the first eight representatives of the eleven three-cusp pair

subdirect subgroup of G^r has a relation g_j=sigma(g_i) for two

first r-1 projection is full, its kernel in the last simple factor is
either G or trivial. In the trivial case the subgroup is the graph of
an onto map G^(r-1)->G. The images of the factors commute and are normal;
only one can be nontrivial, and its map is an automorphism.
Applied to <(x_j),(y_j)>, such a graph relation would identify two
selected pair orbits. Hence its projection to G^8 is full. Since G^8
is perfect, the commutator subgroup of <X,Y> contains G^8 x {0}.
The two displayed W0 basis coordinates finish the assertion.
This proves an actual surjection
  phi0:F=Fhat(x,y)->K*, x->X, y->Y.                         (4)
The source here may be the original named free F in D74. Its kernel

4. The transitive-set bound holds for every A*-set
-------------------------------------------------
Let M<=A*, Omega=A*/M, n=|Omega| and f_i=|Fix_Omega(t_i)| for t_i in C_i.
The exact class/coset count is
  f_i/n=|C_i cap M|/|C_i|.                                 (8)
It follows by counting g with g^(-1)t_i g in M, then dividing by |M|.
Consider the eight G-coordinate projections of M.

Two or more proper projections each contain at most nine involutions;
since C_i has the independent uniform product I^8 in those coordinates,
(8) is at most (9/21)^2=9/49<1/3. Thus S=sum f_i<n.
If the projections are all onto but their joint image in G^8 is proper,
the subdirect graph map above gives f_i/n<=1/21, again S<n.

If exactly one projection is proper, write it H<G. A proper joint
projection to the other seven full factors again gives a graph bound.
Otherwise that projection is G^7. The projection of M to H x V is
a soluble group B. After at most three derived subgroups its B-image
is trivial, while its G^7-image is still G^7. Therefore M contains
G^7 x {1} and has the exact form G^7 x B. The coset action, including
the three classes, is now the action of G x V on (G x V)/B.

This last group has a generating triple from I x {e_i}: the output
exhibits a generating involution triple of G, and adjoining the three
V labels still generates by perfectness of G. In any transitive action,
the three involutions define a connected multigraph on n vertices,
with one edge for each two-point orbit of each involution. It has
(3n-S)/2 edges, at least n-1 by connectivity. Hence S<=n+2.
This proves the bound for the one-proper-coordinate case by the exact
reduced coset action, with no enumerated list of A*-subgroups assumed.

Finally, a full joint projection to G^8 implies M contains G^8 x {0}
by the commutator argument. Thus M=G^8 x W with W<=V. The quotient
action is V/W. With k of the three basis vectors in W, S=n k and
n=8/|W|. Dimensions 0,1,2,3 give bounds k<=0,1,2,3 and degrees
8,4,2,1, respectively, all satisfying S<=n+2. These cases prove
  sum_i |Fix_Omega(t_i)| <= |Omega|+2
for every transitive finite A*-set.

5. No relative generating triple, and an exact finite unit equation
-------------------------------------------------------------------
No choice of one element from each C_i generates A*. Every coordinate
would have to be one of the generating involution triples of G, but
there are only seven automorphism orbits and eight coordinates. Two
coordinate triples are carried to one another by an actual automorphism
sigma. Their generated subgroup lies in the proper graph g_j=sigma(g_i).
Thus the whole A*-image is proper. The relative generating-triple set
for these three classes is empty, despite the properties above.

The concrete h* also has solutions of the finite unit equation
  X^m h* Y^m h*^(-1)=U(XY)^m U^(-1), U in K*.              (9)
The exponent of K* is 84. The checker tests every unit residue modulo
168 and every conjugator in each of the eight coordinate G groups.
Exactly 24 unit residues work simultaneously; they are the units with
m=5 or 7 modulo 12. In particular m=5 works. Its eight actual
conjugators u_j are recorded, and U=((u_j),0) proves (9) in A*.
This finite unit residue lifts to Zhat^times; the integer 5 itself
is not being asserted to be a full profinite unit.

For example coordinate j=4 has
  x_4=(23)(4657), y_4=(14)(2673),
  h_4=(124)(365), u_4=(147)(365).
Its named x_4,y_4 generate all 168 elements, while
x_4^5,h_4 y_4^5 h_4^(-1) generate only 24 elements. The exact
equation (9) still holds there. In the whole product, the three
distinguished involutions at every unit m belong to C_0,C_1,C_2,
so their properness also follows from the preceding graph argument.
Equation (9) is computed with h*, not with an asserted image of the
original h; it is therefore not presented as a computation of an
actual D74 set B_N.
