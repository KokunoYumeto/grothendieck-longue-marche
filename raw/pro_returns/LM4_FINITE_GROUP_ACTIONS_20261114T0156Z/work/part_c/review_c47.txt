1. The full group and its actual topology

The common compatible tuple group contains all three target lifts and acts transitively on the covering plane. Its image acts through the stated full group W, so the latter is transitive without an independent homogeneity assumption. Each target map is unique because its F_i is onto.

The global F_i-sections from C.43 give both b_i=F_i a sigma_i and b_i inverse=F_i a inverse sigma_i. Uniform convergence on compact sets of a and its inverse therefore controls both target maps. In passing composition identities to limits, the compact image bounds supply the needed common compact sets. The limiting target maps remain inverse homeomorphisms and are increasing. Hence W is closed in the actual homeomorphism topology. The same formulas prove the exact topology of its coordinate-triple realization.

The signs of each partial restriction of w are locally constant and therefore constant on the connected source domain. A small coordinate rectangle supplies the fixed two test arguments needed for that local assertion. No differentiability of w or of the action is required.

2. The interior-mean stabilizer lemma

The recursive subdivision proof uses a compact set bounded away from the diagonal. Both child displacements have a positive minimum there. Along a chain of intervals of length at least epsilon, every generation shortens the interval by that minimum; a sufficiently deep subdivision therefore has all lengths below epsilon. This proves density of the fixed subdivision points and fixes the full initial interval.

The fixed-interval propagation is valid in both proper domains. At a finite right endpoint, choose the other fixed endpoint sufficiently close that the pair remains inside the domain; continuity keeps the mean in the fixed interior. Injectivity in its second argument forces all slightly larger endpoints to be fixed. The left endpoint uses the first argument. This establishes identity on the entire line from a single fixed ordered pair.

3. One proper pair and two full pairs

The full pair maps make each w-level a global graph r_z and give joint continuity of its inverse coordinate parametrization. The inequality r_z(x)>x excludes a decreasing onto homeomorphism. After the one allowable target reversal, r_z is strictly increasing in z as well.

For fixed a, r_s squared(a) increases strictly and continuously. The limits at the lower endpoint and, in the band case, at the upper endpoint follow by bracketing r_s(a) between two fixed input points. The limits are a and a+2 in the band, so every b in (a,a+1) is covered. In the half-plane the upper limit is infinite. Thus the equation r_s squared(a)=b has one solution, and fixed bracketing proves its continuous dependence.

The middle value M=r_s(a) is strictly interior. Its strict increase in the first argument is correctly proved by comparing the two parameters: for a1<a2 the parameters satisfy s1>s2, and a reversed order of the middle values would give the strict contradiction b>b after the second application. Conjugacy of the r_s family proves the precise equivariance of M. This makes every source stabilizer trivial.

4. A second proper pair

A finite endpoint homeomorphism gives the actual intertwining map f g=k f. Conjugating the third target by f inverse makes all three actions identical, including the decreasing-f case. Equalities between any two coordinate values are invariant under a transitive source action; therefore they are either global or absent. Independence excludes the global alternative, and connectedness fixes their relative order.

In the half-plane, the diagonal line action is transitive on ordered pairs. A point stabilizer is consequently transitive on each side of that point, so its only common fixed point is the point itself. This proves that the centralizer in all line homeomorphisms is trivial. Each finite endpoint of the other pair domains is thus the identity.

The two inverse-mean formulas have the claimed full domains. In the w>y case, the (x,w) interval has lower endpoint x and cannot have a finite upper endpoint, since that would also be x. The partial map is increasing because w(a,c)>c as c tends to infinity. Inverting it gives a<c<b. The w<x case is its exact other-coordinate counterpart. Both separate injectivity and continuity follow from the already proved pair homeomorphisms.

In the band, the forbidden equalities w=x+n and w=y+n are invariant because every g commutes with unit translation. Connectedness therefore supplies one integer n with x<w-n<x+1 and one strict side of y. In the upper-side case, (u,v)=(y,x+1) maps the band onto itself, with inverse (x,y)=(v-1,u), and the displayed M lies strictly between u and v. The integer shifts commute with the action, so equivariance survives. These cases exhaust all proper pair domains.

5. The global ordered loop and uniform estimates

When every pair image is the plane, the explicit separate coordinates based at (a,b) make m have identity zero and strictly increasing onto partial maps. The division maps are jointly continuous by bracketing. The point stabilizer is exactly Aut-positive(m) in the inherited topology: substituting each zero input forces all three target maps to coincide.

D(x)=m(x,x) is an increasing onto homeomorphism with its unique fixed point at zero. Its inverse iterates v_n of a positive a decrease to zero. The left inverses u_n also converge to zero by division continuity and are negative. Automorphisms commute with these constructions. Evaluation bounds A<=f(a)<=B therefore give f(v_n) and f(u_n) tending uniformly to zero.

Each translation T_c for positive c has positive displacement everywhere and is onto, so its integer iterates of zero exhaust both ends. The identity f(T_a^j(0))=T_f(a)^j(0), including negative j, bounds every compact source interval by a common compact target interval. Negative iterate continuity is justified by the division maps; no associativity is used.

For equicontinuity, the two gaps m(u_n,x)<x<m(v_n,x) have positive minima on a fixed compact interval. A single delta_n therefore brackets every nearby y. After applying f, both bounds approach f(x) uniformly because the f(x) have already been confined to a common compact set and f(u_n), f(v_n) converge uniformly to zero. This supplies equicontinuity with the quantifiers in the required order: choose n for the target error, then delta_n independently of f and x.

The inverse estimate is independent and correct. A sufficiently small v_n gives f(v_n)<a and hence f inverse(a)>v_n. A sufficiently large positive iterate T_a^N(0) has image at least T_A^N(0)>a, yielding a uniform finite upper bound for f inverse(a). The same forward argument applied to these fixed positive inverse-evaluation bounds supplies compact control and equicontinuity for every inverse.

Arzela--Ascoli on a countable exhaustion then yields a common subsequence of maps and inverses. The compact bounds permit passage to both composition identities; hence the limits are genuine inverse homeomorphisms. The multiplication identity and closed evaluation interval also pass to the limit. This proves compactness of every evaluation strip in the actual homeomorphism-group topology.

Evaluation on a positive a is injective: an automorphism fixing a fixes all v_n and all their translation iterates. The first iterate at or to the right of a prescribed x lies between x and T_v_n(x), and therefore converges to x. The fixed set is closed, so every x is fixed. Compact evaluation strips then make this injective evaluation a proper embedding into the positive real line, not merely a set-theoretic determination map.

6. Local compactness of the full group

The stabilizer S is now locally compact, and its faithful action on one coordinate of R3 permits Pardon. Hence it is Lie. Its established embedding in a line gives dimension at most one.

The proof does not yet assume that W is locally compact. Choose an actual W-identity neighborhood whose intersection with S has compact closure, then a smaller V with V inverse V inside that neighborhood. For the free right action, its transporter is contained in V inverse V intersect S and hence relatively compact in S. Left translates provide the same Cartan condition at every W-point. The metrizable W is completely regular.

The inspected Palais theorem therefore gives actual local products for W to W/S. Effros identifies this quotient with the original covering plane, including its topology. The product of S with a sufficiently small open subset of that plane is locally compact, proving local compactness of W. Only then is Pardon applied to W's faithful action on X times R. The local product also gives dimension two or three.

In the proper-pair cases, the already proved simple transitivity makes evaluation W to X a homeomorphism directly by Effros. This supplies the asserted dimension-two Lie conclusion there.

The final closure statement is exact because W is closed in Homeo(X), so the compatible loop-group closure is a closed subgroup of this locally compact group. No replacement of the global tuple coordinate by its possibly nonfaithful restriction is made. No ambient frontier continuity, operator normalization of an arbitrarily selected triple, or global C12 contraction is inferred.
