This audit checks the exact spherical quotient, the topology of both
restriction comparisons, the actual first-kind extension input, and the
operator-labelled filling. No numerical certificate is needed for these
arguments.

1. Spherical Caratheodory quotient and boundary rigidity.

The nondegenerate locally connected frontier gives the stated continuous
closed-disk quotient q; interior points have singleton fibres and the
interior is homeomorphic to O. The proof that no boundary arc is constant
is local in a finite complex coordinate near its alleged image point.
Continuity gives the required smaller source arc and target chart;
Schwarz reflection and the identity theorem then contradict interior
injectivity. This works for a spherical domain even when the original
coordinate of the conformal map is meromorphic.

The unlinked-fibre argument is exact. A closed source crosscut joining
two distinct boundary points with the same image has embedded interior
in O, and only its endpoints are identified. Its image is consequently a
Jordan curve J whose frontier intersection is that one endpoint image.
The two connected source components have images disjoint from J.
The local homeomorphism at an interior crosscut point puts them on
opposite Jordan sides. Alternating endpoints from a different fibre
would approach both sides and put their common image on J, contradicting
the stated intersection. This does not assume injectivity of q on the
circle.

For an orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism u with q u=q, a
moved point and a nearby point have alternating pairs with their images.
The nearby point can be chosen in a different q-fibre precisely because
q is constant on no nonempty boundary arc. The four strict inequalities
in (C.68.10) are compatible with an increasing lift, and contradict the
proved unlinked-fibre property. Thus u is the identity. The explicit
orientation-preserving hypothesis on the spherical relative group is
retained; no assumption that the fixed complement has interior is used.

2. Actual relative group and its topology.

The prime-end functor is the actual null-crosscut-chain functor from
C.67. Uniform continuity of an ambient sphere homeomorphism and its
inverse preserves null chains, containment, and equivalence. Its
interior restriction is phi^{-1} h phi and its continuous extension
satisfies q hhat=h q. For a relative positive homeomorphism, the
preceding circle rigidity gives a boundary-fixed lift.

Conversely, q a is constant on every fibre of the compact quotient q
when a fixes the boundary. The inverse constructed with a^{-1} proves
that the descended map is a homeomorphism of closure(O). Closed pasting
with the identity on the complement is valid and gives the exact
inverse of the prime-end construction. Composition and injectivity
are checked on the dense interior of closure(O), with the exterior
fixed pointwise. Orientation is determined by the nonempty open domain.

Uniform continuity of q controls the descended maps and their inverses,
so the group homomorphism is continuous in both compact uniform
coordinates. The source and target are closed Polish subgroups of their
homeomorphism groups. The continuous transitive action through this
bijective homomorphism satisfies the already verified Polish open
orbit-map theorem, hence its inverse is continuous. This proves the
topological group isomorphism, not just its set-theoretic bijection.
The Alexander contraction is used in the full boundary-fixed disk
group and transferred by that actual isomorphism.

For every retained operator, the conjugation equation (C.68.12) follows
on O from its actual interior coordinate and follows off O from the
identity. Reversing operators are permitted. The prime-end kernel is
exactly the original restriction kernel because the open disk is
dense in its prime-end compactification. No equivariance of the
Alexander contraction under arbitrary operators is invoked.

4. Centralizer restriction, parameters, and fillings.

The specified closed-disk homeomorphism k is used in both directions.
If g commutes with Q, then k g k^{-1} commutes with the actual
subgroup Lambda. Tukia extends it with identity boundary; conjugating
by the given closed-disk k^{-1} gives the unique boundary-fixed
extension of g. For each original a in A, commutation on the open
disk extends over the closed disk by continuity. Conversely,
commutation with every restriction of A implies commutation with
its actual closure Q by continuity of the group operations.

Thus restriction between the two specified centralizers is an exact
continuous bijective homomorphism. Both centralizers are closed
Polish groups, so the same open orbit-map theorem proves its inverse
continuous. This is sufficient to extend an entire compact family
and its inverse family; the proof does not replace this step with
pointwise extension or with an unproved restriction fibration.

A based loop in the left-hand group is ambient-null in the full
boundary-fixed disk group by the Alexander contraction. Its interior
restriction is therefore ambient-null. The precise proper free
locally compact Q action meets the hypotheses of C.33, which supplies
the centralizer filling in the open disk. The continuous inverse
restriction map extends the whole filling. Transferring through T_q
preserves the original operators and their kernels by (C.68.12).
For the plane application, the specified infinity is in the fixed
complement; the one-end topology comparison from C.66 gives exactly
the required plane compact-open and inverse topology.
