# Audit note: the exact remaining centralizer restriction

This note records a limitation of the currently proved centralizer constraints. It does **not** construct an actual nonstandard pants involution or realize the comparison subgroup below as its fixed subgroup.

Let `alpha = (-1,f)` be an actual GT0 involution, `E = F ⋊ <s>`, and `C = C_F(s)`, with `F = Fhat(x,y)`. D.34 proves `H1(C,F2)=0`. Since alpha acts by -1 on the torsion-free abelianization `Zhat^2`, every fixed element has zero abelianization, so `C <= F′`.

D.44 supplies an involution `h tau` commuting with s, where tau acts by the geometric swap `theta(x)=y`, `theta(y)=x`, and `h=k^-1 theta(k)`. Its conjugation action on F is exactly

`sigma = Inn(h) theta = Inn(k^-1) theta Inn(k)`.

Thus sigma preserves C and has no nonidentity fixed point on C: its full fixed subgroup on F is conjugate to `C_F(theta)=1`, by the explicitly identified free product `F ⋊ <tau> = C2 ∐ Zhat`.

These individual properties permit nontrivial closed comparison subgroups even after imposing any specified finite collection of quotient kernels. To see this directly, let `pi_j:F -> Q_j` be that collection, let m be a positive common multiple of the exponents of the finite groups Q_j, fix an odd prime p, and put

`c=[x,y]`, `C0 = <c^(m e_p)>`,

where `e_p` is 1 in the p-coordinate of Zhat and 0 in every other coordinate.

The map `Zhat -> F`, `a -> c^a`, is injective. For every prime l and n>=1, map x and y to the two standard generators in the group of upper unitriangular 3-by-3 matrices over `Z/l^n`. Their commutator has central coordinate 1 and exact order `l^n`. Hence `c^a=1` forces `a=0 mod l^n` for every l,n, and therefore a=0 in Zhat. Restricting this injection to `m e_p Z_p` proves that C0 is isomorphic to Z_p and is nontrivial.

All remaining comparisons are exact:

- `C0 <= F′`, since c is a commutator.
- `pi_j(C0)=1` for every j, since m annihilates every cyclic subgroup in Q_j.
- `H1(C0,F2)=Hom_cont(Z_p,C2)=0`, since p is odd.
- `theta(c)=c^-1`, so theta preserves C0 and acts by inversion.
- Its only fixed element on C0 is the identity, since multiplication by 2 is injective on Z_p.

For the actual conjugate swap sigma, the comparison subgroup is `k^-1 C0 k`. It remains inside F′ and all the selected normal kernels, and sigma acts on it by inversion with no nonidentity fixed point.

This proves only that the listed subgroup properties do not force triviality. **No assertion is made that C0 or its conjugate equals `C_F(s)` for any pants involution.** The actual fixed-subgroup realization, together with the three full cusp normalizers, imposes conditions not discharged by this comparison.

## Exact coherent-triple map

For a D.35 solution `(mu,h,u)`, the three elements

`r0=s`, `r1=x^mu s`, `r2=s h y^mu h^-1`

are involutions. Their products are respectively

`r1 r0=x^mu`, `r0 r2=h y^mu h^-1`, and `r1 r2=u(xy)^mu u^-1`.

The product subgroup in F is generated by two elements and contains conjugates of all three full cusp Z2 subgroups. D.32 makes it all of F. Consequently the homomorphism from `C2 ∐ C2 ∐ C2` sending its three named generators to r0,r1,r2 is surjective onto E. It preserves the parity maps to C2, and its restriction between their rank-two free profinite kernels is surjective; the Hopfian property of Fhat2 makes that restriction injective. Its entire kernel is contained in that parity kernel, so the original homomorphism is an isomorphism.

The incidence triangle proved in D.34 supplies the three conjugacy classes and their cusp incidences. The displayed equality for `r1 r2` is exactly the third-cusp equation of D.35. Its existence has not been obtained from the incidence triangle alone in this audit.
