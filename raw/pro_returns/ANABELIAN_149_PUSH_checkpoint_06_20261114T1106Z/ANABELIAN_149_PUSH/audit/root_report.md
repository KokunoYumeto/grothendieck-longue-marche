## D: proved deductions and exact failures of proposed routes

The universal birational-lifting assertion D3 is equivalent to the section conjecture with the quantifiers in Bresciani's Theorems A and B. The extra transcendental extension in Theorem A is essential: ordinary birational liftability is not substituted for t-birational liftability. The paper proves the implication D2 + D4 => point-or-cusp for the tripod, including the geometric conjugacy and inertia-quotient maps.

R23 was checked directly. The completion of Q* consists of a sign and profinite valuations with finite support modulo each positive integer. Actual local representatives and a common finite exceptional set of nonunits force an ordinary finite-support integer vector. Compactness proves the completion of the finite unit ideles; the positive real factor disappears because it is divisible. This arithmetic assertion does not prove the required common finite exceptional set for a section.

The proposed evaluation formula in the D2 route needs a correction at zeros and poles. A tangential section at t=0 with Kummer class 2 evaluates t^m u(t) to 2^m u(0). In particular the Kummer pullbacks of t,t−1,t+1 are 2,−1,1. They are multiplicative leading-coefficient data and are not a field evaluation. The section still comes from the rational place 0, so this is a refutation of the proposed unqualified formula, not of D2.

Stix's finite-support result supplies a precise sufficient condition for a section to be rational. The density-one integrality theorem for birational sections does not supply it: an infinite exceptional set can have density zero. Stoll's Theorem 8.2 was read in the attached source. The author's three-page errata dated 29 March 2017 were then opened. They correct complex-place factors and assertions about connected torsors; they preserve the density-one finite-support use made here. Harari–Stix is Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2(1) in the attached source, versus Theorem 11 in the published numbering quoted by Stix. It concerns all torsors under finite group schemes, not only geometrically connected constant torsors.

D.20 gives an actual continuous cuspidal birational section with a profinite tangent that is represented by an ordinary local element at every prime and is a unit at a set of density one, yet has infinitely many nonzero valuation coordinates and is not rational. This makes the density-one/cofinite distinction concrete, while preserving the rationality of its underlying place. D.21 gives the relevant Fermat twist x^4+2y^4=3z^4 above the rational point t=1/3. Its three sign-quotient elliptic classes all have positive rank, proved by explicit points and exhaustive good-prime torsion bounds. Orthogonal Klein-four idempotents show that every elliptic target is isogenous to one of these classes. Hence a universal rank-zero elliptic-target shortcut for all such twists is false; the global section conjectures are not refuted.

For D3–D4 we checked the finite-embedding-problem route and the distinction between individual and compatible lifts. For D5, D.7 constructs an explicit family of discontinuous abstract semilinear actions. An abstract functional on (product F2)/(direct sum F2) twists the rational involution t↦1−t at the rational base point 1/2. The function t has nonopen stabilizer. The action normalizes the original deck group, and its cyclotomic-kernel action is unchanged, so it has exactly one fixed completed pro-point. The proof uses Ribet's actual torsion-finiteness theorem and supplies the divisible-point deduction. One residue reduction alone would not prove that deduction; all finite quotients of the local compact abelian group are used. A completed pro-point here is a compatible system of geometric closed points, not the generic scheme point.

Changing the action on the constant field by a nonidentity automorphism of G_Q violates the prescribed semilinearity. An individual field automorphism only gives continuous inner conjugacy. Those suggested shortcuts do not produce the required abstract cocycle.

D.10–D.11 subsequently settle the second demand of D5. The additive finite adeles act by explicit Puiseux-character substitutions on the full modular pro-cover. Rational translations preserve the tower by commensuration; integral adeles give cusp inertia. The chosen modular coordinate has stabilizer exactly the profinite integers. Independent Kummer cocycles give a surjective product of Z_l on G_Q°; a basis-defined Z_l-linear functional taking the ith coordinate vector to l^(−i) is unbounded and has dense kernel. Its twisted semilinear action has an infinite coordinate orbit, so it cannot normalize any finite hyperbolic quotient's deck group. Density reduces its fixed set to the original tangential action, giving exactly one cusp. The construction uses the axiom of choice and is not an effective cocycle algorithm. Its complete independent review is recorded in audit/F_D5_construction.md. This particular example satisfies the fixed-point conclusion. The subsequent D.12–D.14 construction refutes the universal assertion with an empty fixed set, as recorded next.

D.12–D.14 settle the universal abstract-action assertion negatively. An explicit 3-adic Liouville point is approximated by rational integers with independent square classes, using a diagonal valuation CRT construction. Pointwise ultralimits of their point sections give an abstract section whose local restriction is geometrically conjugate to the transcendental local point section. The Kummer character of z²=t is a noncontinuous ultrafilter functional, proving discontinuity without any section-conjecture input. If its cyclotomic kernel fixed a completed geometric closed pro-point, evaluation of a unit regular at that point and the local cyclotomic Kummer lemma would force the transcendental local coordinate to be algebraic. The fixed set is therefore empty. The crossed cocycle relative to the point action at 1/2 is written explicitly. The earlier D.U5 obligation is superseded by D.12–D.14; the earlier text remains in checkpoint 01.

## E: exact scope of the constructions

The Artin–Schreier equation z^p−z=tx proves failure of full fundamental-group invariance, with a reduced principal-part argument and a check that forgetting the chosen C_p deck labelling does not repair descent. The logical category conclusion is stated with its needed hypothesis: its isomorphism groupoids must retain the full moduli marking groupoids, as (Z.2) does. M_0,4 already fails invariance; the unordered stack has the same obstruction through its ordered finite cover. The words 'arrows include covers' alone, without faithful marking data, are insufficient to rule out artificial quotient categories. This qualification avoids overstating what the Artin–Schreier example proves.

There is a second obstruction independent of path transport. An unramified degree-p cover of a proper genus-two characteristic-zero curve has genus p+1. No arrow between the same two proper types can factor in characteristic p through a cover with prime-to-p Galois closure, since Riemann–Hurwitz forces its degree to be p. Thus the unrestricted specialization functor requested in E1 is false. A repaired functor must restrict its source covers as well. Restricting the covering group to prime-to-p does not itself specify the appropriate moduli paths; tame paths and maximal pro-prime-to-p paths are different notions.

For a fixed smooth marked curve, the prime-to-p covering equivalence and specialization comparison follow from the exact SGA 1 relative-group statements. The full 25,947-line source is present; the small sga1-smf.tex file is merely a driver. XIII 4.4 is 'Examples', not a corollary, and XIII 5.1–5.2 are the local Kummer/Abhyankar statements, not a theorem about arbitrary completion. Bertin–Romagny 6.9, 6.14 and 6.20 were opened and their order-invertibility and smooth-locus hypotheses retained. E.10–E.12 now supply the distinct all-types argument: proper DM descent, finite logarithmic root stacks, faithful ordering and Jacobi levels, normalized Hurwitz path lifting with the deck gerbe retained, and compatible composition on chains of covers. The resulting boundary-tame path groups retain interior p-monodromy. Algebraically closed base extension is an equivalence; the characteristic-zero comparison restricts the covering arrows and is surjective on every stated automorphism and closed-coset Hom component. E.U1 is superseded with these precise repairs.

## Additional numbered lead: local covering germs

G.1 proves the exact general profinite-group criterion: H→G is injective if and only if every transitive finite H-set is an orbit of a restricted finite G-set, equivalently is dominated by one. The proof chooses N normal open in G with N∩H contained in an arbitrary open V⊂H, then uses VN and H∩VN=V. It therefore proves the stronger exact-component meaning of 'sub-germ', not only domination. SGA 1 V, Corollary 6.8 is the corresponding Galois-category statement, and XIII 5.1–5.2 identify the local normal-crossings group in characteristic zero. G.2 records an injected torus example, the failure at infinity of A1⊂P1, and the diagonal kernel at a crossing of two lines in P2.
