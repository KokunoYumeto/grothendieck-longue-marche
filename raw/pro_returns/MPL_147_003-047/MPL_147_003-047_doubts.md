# Doubts, abbreviations, and limits of legibility

## Scope and conventions

This register covers all **274 occurrences of `\unsure` and both occurrences of `\illegible`** in the frozen transcription, in scan and reading order. Each occurrence has its own row, including repeated words and canceled primes; one additional row records an uncertain insertion attachment noted during visual transcription. The first reading is the one placed first in the transcription, not a mathematical correction. A dash in the alternatives column means that no second defensible reading was entered; it does not imply certainty.

Locations give an approximate vertical band and the line in the corresponding page's `.tex` fragment. These are **not claimed to be original manuscript line numbers**: the transcription preserves paragraphing but permits prose lines to reflow. Mathematical readings are printed as literal LaTeX so that primes, braces, alphabets, and alternatives can be compared without editorial rendering hiding distinctions. “Deleted” and “inserted” identify the reading's editorial enclosure, including nested revisions.

Confidence concerns the **ink reading**, not the truth of the mathematics: **high** means a strong but still qualified reading; **medium** means a plausible reading with unresolved strokes or spelling; **low** means a serious competing reading, heavy cancellation, overlap, or physical clipping. The two unreadable fragments on scan023 are counted as one word each; these are estimates, not claims that complete word boundaries survive.

The `\mspage` macro records every visible page numeral, including the lighter pencil sequence at the foot. The main handwritten sheet numbers and the pencil/archive sequence are therefore preserved separately in their positions rather than silently merged. The pencil numerals are transcribed without asserting Grothendieck's authorship.

## Legibility and reliability

The transcription is least secure where the manuscript itself supplies little recoverable ink. **Scan040 is the most difficult page**: its long canceled passages, overwritten graph/flag labels, diagonal marginal notes, and overlapping arrows make some earlier word forms and the order of revisions indeterminate. **Scan032** has similarly dense canceled definitions and competing tuple/arrow labels; **scan046** has an obstructed canceled sentence, A/B replacements, a retraced graph symbol, an ambiguous `I_(α)`/`I^(α)` placement, and a small blackened mark beside its definition sign. **Scan023** has two physically clipped lower-right fragments: these are not merely unfamiliar words, and the surviving images do not support a responsible reconstruction. The compressed **scan003 table of contents** remains less secure for several titles, the action/calcul reading, a numerical bound, and the attachment of the inserted “locales.” Further weak areas include the rotated note on008, heavily revised moduli indices on019, the small overlapping note on030, the compressed final parenthesis on038, and the canceled fourth condition on041. The overwritten subscripts and canceled fragments on047 also retain local uncertainty. Main prose and most displayed formulas are generally more legible, but apparent mathematical consistency must not be mistaken for evidence of a particular letter, prime, or accent. The detailed doubt-instance table should be consulted for the exact extent of each unresolved reading.

Several unusual readings are deliberately preserved because the ink supports them more strongly than an expected mathematical correction: the `Sing(X)` annotation beneath the tilde-decorated A on011; missing hats in012; the `semi-stable` phrase on022; malformed parentheses in028; the G/G′ order in036 and043; and the unprimed tilde-A term on047. Confidence in reproducing those visible forms is separate from confidence in the mathematical assertions. Likewise, proposed expansions in this supplement neither validate a theorem nor erase the author's cancellations.
