### G02 — The missing restriction affects the corollary's necessity

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 034, condition 1 and the final “il f et s.” corollary; compare scan 033, lower half. **Kind:** own idea / correction to a contextual diagnosis. **Context:** H68, “Caractérisation sans parties infinies”, his p. 33 / scan 034, especially the footnote beginning “Pris à la lettre”. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high — the logical distinction follows from strengthening a hypothesis, and the one-position example is explicit.

### E09 — A direct negative answer for the unrestricted formal-functor question

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scans 043–044, arbitrary module-valued functors and the NB on bijectivity. **Kind:** own idea / counterexample. **Context:** H68, “Foncteurs en modules”, his pp. 42–43 / scans 043–044, which leaves the formal question unanswered. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high — both distinct endomorphisms and their identical images are explicit.

Take K=Q and the semilinear module-valued functor M(A)=Ared=A/N(A), with the A-module structure induced by reduction. Its natural endomorphisms 0 and id are distinct, but every nilpotent λ in every test algebra acts as zero on M, including after restriction to any base algebra, so exp(λ·0)=exp(λ·id)=id. Thus the proposed map EndK(M)→Homgr(Ḡa,AutK(M)) need not even be injective in the unrestricted class described on scan 043. This adds a formal-side counterexample to H68's ordinary-side objection and isolates the failure of nilpotent test parameters to detect the endomorphism.

### E10 — Pointwise logarithms extend the intertwiner result

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scans 039–042, the nilpotence mismatch and the equivalence involving exp. **Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Context:** H68, “Opérations sur un module” and “Morphismes, et le passage à exp”, his pp. 38–41 / scans 039–042. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high — the logarithm computation is finite on each vector.

Hua's replacement of uniform nilpotence by local nilpotence suggests extending scan 040's equivalence f exp(u)=exp(v) f ⇔ fu=vf to locally nilpotent u and v. For each m, choose a truncation large enough for the logarithm identities on m and f(m), then use f(exp(u)−1)ʳ=(exp(v)−1)ʳf to obtain fu(m)=vf(m). This avoids requiring a single polynomial logarithm valid on the entire module and explains how the intertwiner argument can survive the correction to scan 039.

### E11 — “Loc nilpotent” must be interpreted on the sheaf, not just global functions

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 044, “Globalisation”. **Kind:** own idea / qualification of a contextual counterexample. **Context:** H68, “Globalisation”, his p. 43 / scan 044. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high for the localization objection; medium for the intended meaning of the manuscript abbreviation. **Supplementary primary check:** the unit-polynomial step is documented as `Polynomial.not_isUnit_of_natDegree_pos_of_isReduced` in [mathlib's polynomial-nilpotence documentation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/Nilpotent.html); this checks that lemma only, not the interpretation of the manuscript.

The page assumes a derivation of the sheaf OX and writes “Θ loc nilpotent”, whereas H68 chooses nilpotence uniformly on neighbourhoods and contrasts it with d/dx on Q[x]. That familiar ring derivation is not locally nilpotent on every open's sections: on Q[x,x⁻¹], its iterates on x⁻¹ never vanish, so it is not a counterexample to a sheafwise reading of the hypothesis. In fact, a locally nilpotent derivation of a reduced local Q-algebra is zero: exp(tD) sends a unit to a unit of A[t], which must be constant when A is reduced, and every element of a local ring is either a unit or differs from a unit by 1. Thus a stalkwise locally nilpotent reading can also force identity on the reduction, but gluing the exponentials and the exact scope of “loc” should be stated explicitly.

### O06 — A possible meaning of the unfinished I(ω,ω′)

A candidate is the orientation of V/U obtained from the exact sequence 0→W/U→V/U→V/W→0, ordering the quotient factors as (W/U,V/W). With ω on V/W and ω′ on W/U, that convention gives the orientation represented by ω′ followed by ω and makes Stω′Stω the corresponding composite boundary operation. Reversing the order introduces (−1)ʳˢ for r=dim(W/U) and s=dim(V/W), which fits the product-sign calculation below. This is a proposal for study, not a decipherment of the incomplete Ω-expression.

### O07 — The product signs suggest a corner-cancellation test

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 054, Stokes/product formulas and the square and quadrant drawings. **Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Context:** H68, “Produits, et composition des applications de Stokes”, his p. 53 / scan 054. **R:** none; beyond **68-orientation-stokes-axioms**. **Confidence:** high for the conditional sign identity; medium for identifying the purpose of the drawings.

For two independent codimension-one faces, the two orders of taking boundary orientations exchange two one-dimensional normal factors and therefore differ by −1 under the displayed Koszul rule. This is the local cancellation required for a boundary-of-boundary identity, and gives a concrete test of the two Stokes/product signs using the quadrant. H68 describes those drawings and derives the product signs, but the page does not itself write a chain complex or assert ∂²=0, so that interpretation remains an implication to investigate.

### O08 — q and a question mark must remain separate readings

### O09 — Orientations are a real sign quotient of determinant lines

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scans 054 and 056, Koszul signs and the graded determinant target. **Kind:** unpursued implication / contextual connection. **Context:** H68, “Les déterminants gradués”, his p. 55 / scan 056, final paragraph linking his p. 53 / scan 054. **R:** none; related to **68-orientation-stokes-axioms**, not part of its reported verification.

Hua's project already makes the unspoken connection: for a real vector space, the two orientations are the two components of the nonzero determinant line, and the graded commutativity constraint becomes the sign on scan 054. Equivalently, the action of a unit on that line is passed through R*→{±1}, a map that discards positive scaling while keeping parity of interchange. The neighbouring sheets support this connection, but neither their adjacency nor the common sign rule proves that the author intended a single continuous exposition.

## Graded determinants: exact scope and possible extensions

### D01 — The final sheet gives axioms, not a determinant theorem

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 056, category definitions and clauses a)–c). **Kind:** unproved claim / scope question. **Context:** H68, “Les déterminants gradués”, his p. 55 / scan 056. **R:** none.

The page calls V an “additive (graduée)” category but explicitly restricts its morphisms to “iso”, and its target consists of free rank-one modules with an integer grading and Koszul commutativity. H68 correctly interprets this as a symmetric monoidal groupoid under direct sum, not an additive category with all module homomorphisms. The sheet stops at complement independence and its equivalent formulation; it states no existence or uniqueness theorem, so the analogy with the orientation theorem remains a research direction rather than a completed claim.

### D02 — The complement diagram has several independent reading issues

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 056, lower diagram and the preceding λ line. **Kind:** reading question. **Context:** H68, “Les déterminants gradués”, his p. 55 / scan 056, displayed diagram and footnote. **R:** none.

The frozen diagram retains an overwritten central `W|V`, lower-left Δ(V₁), and right-hand subscript `W₂|W₁`, whereas H68 regularizes these objects and reports a different left-hand subscript slip, φW₁,W₁, where this reading has φW₁,W₂. Both the manuscript transcription and H68's displayed diagram show the bottom arrow pointing from the W₂′ side to the W₂ side, although the preceding λ is written W₂≃W₂′. A corrected mathematical diagram must specify whether λ or λ⁻¹ labels that direction, but none of these decisions may be fed back into the frozen text. H68 also describes a doubled-stem P; the transcription retains ordinary P, so the alphabet is another localized reading question.

### D03 — Complement independence is invariance under a change of splitting

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 056, c) and the final “[ Equivaut à … ]”. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** H68, “Les déterminants gradués”, his p. 55 / scan 056, complement diagram and transvection discussion. **R:** none.

The final sentence asserts equivalence with “u … l'identité sur W₁ et sur V/W₁” implying Δ(u)=id. H68 restates that equivalence and relates it to transvections; a proof needs to use a chosen free splitting to identify such u with a block shear, then compare the old complement with its image under u using naturality of φ. The exact direction of λ in the displayed diagram must be fixed in the mathematical argument, even though the transcription preserves the drawn direction.

### D04 — A precise conditional form of the SK₁ obstruction

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 056, a)–c), especially the unrestricted commutative base ring k. **Kind:** own idea / proposed obstruction. **Context:** H68, “Les déterminants gradués”, his p. 55 / scan 056, its paragraph mentioning SK₁(k). **R:** none. **Confidence:** high for the conditional construction; no ring with a required nontrivial character is asserted here.

Hua's project already points to SK₁(k) as an issue when elementary matrices and rank-one units do not determine every stable matrix class. More precisely, a nontrivial character χ:K₁(k)→k* that is trivial on rank-one units would permit twisting the ordinary determinant action by χ: block sum gives the tensor compatibility, and elementary shears are killed. Such a twist would satisfy the splitting-independence requirement and the natural rank-one normalization but would not be naturally isomorphic to the untwisted theory when χ acts nontrivially. This turns Hua's hint into a concrete conditional test; it does not claim that such a character exists for every ring or that the sheet proves a classification.

### D05 — Rank-one normalization must include naturality

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 056, b), “Si rg(V)=1, Δ(V)≃V, de degré 1”. **Kind:** own idea / ambiguity in the strength of the axioms. **Context:** H68, “Les déterminants gradués”, his p. 55 / scan 056, clauses a), b) and the field case. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high for the weakened-axiom example; the author's intended compatibility is not decided.

If b) is read as only an objectwise isomorphism, rather than a natural identification on the rank-one groupoid, it does not force the usual action of k* even over a field. For example, over Q, assigning (det V)⊗³ in degree dim V has coherent tensor maps, the same Koszul symmetry because 3 is odd, and trivial action of shears, while every rank-one value is abstractly isomorphic to V but its scalar action is a³ instead of a. Thus a uniqueness theorem needs the naturality and compatibility of b) made explicit, as H68's determinant interpretation implicitly expects. This is a warning about a possible weak reading, not a claim that Grothendieck intended those choices to be unrelated.

### D06 — Passing to projective modules changes the target and the grading

**Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 056, the repeated word “libres” in source, target and complements. **Kind:** own idea / proposed extension. **Context:** H68, “Les déterminants gradués”, his p. 55 / scan 056; search also found the contextual mention of Picard-category papers in `hua_project/162-1.modern.tex`, “Catégories tannakiennes”, without a scan-level provenance for that mention. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high for the necessary scope changes; the proposed extension is not proved here.

The free-module restrictions are part of the sheet and should remain visible when comparing it with determinant theories for finite projective modules. Extending the domain would require invertible modules in the target rather than only free lines, and locally constant rank data when the base is disconnected. The contextual loan-register mention of Picard-category notes gives no evidence that those notes contain this extension or that the present sheet was derived from them; it is only a bibliographical lead, not a manuscript cross-reference.
