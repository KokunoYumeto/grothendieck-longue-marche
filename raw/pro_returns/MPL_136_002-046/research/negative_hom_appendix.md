# Checks supporting NH06 and NH09–NH14

These are research calculations made after the transcription freeze. They do not change the diplomatic text. The intersection/divisibility correction and the p-local coherent-root interpretation are already identified by **Hua, modernised reading of cote 136**, “Homomorphismes homogènes entre tronqués” (its p. 44 = archive scan 045); the examples and explicit arguments here explain or test that reading, with no novelty claim.

## A. An element of every nA that is not in a divisible subgroup

Fix a prime p and define the abelian group

\[
A=\langle x,y_n\ (n\geq1)\mid px=0,\quad p^ny_n=x\ (n\geq1)\rangle.
\]

The map to \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) sending \(x\) to \(1/p+\mathbb Z\) and \(y_n\) to \(1/p^{n+1}+\mathbb Z\) respects all relations, so x is nonzero and has order p. Each generator \(y_n\) has order dividing \(p^{n+1}\), so A is p-primary; integers prime to p act invertibly on A. Modding out by x removes all interactions between the generators and gives

\[
A/\langle x\rangle\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq1}\mathbb Z/p^n.
\]

The intersection of all p-power images in this direct sum is zero: a nonzero element has a nonzero coordinate in some finite cyclic summand, and that coordinate vanishes in \(p^h\) times the summand for h large enough. Consequently \(\bigcap_h p^hA\subseteq\langle x\rangle\); the reverse inclusion holds because \(x=p^ny_n\) for every n. Thus

\[
\bigcap_{m\geq1}mA=\bigcap_{h\geq1}p^hA=\langle x\rangle\cong\mathbb Z/p.
\]

Every divisible subgroup of A would lie in this intersection; no nonzero subgroup of the cyclic group of order p is divisible. Therefore the maximal divisible subgroup is zero although the written intersection is nonzero. In particular, no coherent infinite p-root chain can start at x: such a chain would generate a nonzero p-divisible subgroup.

This directly refutes the identification of the two targets, not the separate assertion that a particular map factors through the intersection.

## B. Why the manuscript’s coherent recurrence gives p-divisibility

Work over a \(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\)-algebra k. If

\[
\xi_s=p\nu'_s\xi_{s+1},\qquad \nu'_s\in k^\times,
\]

set \(a_r=1\), \(a_{s+1}=a_s\nu'_s\), and \(y_s=a_s\xi_s\). Then \(y_s=py_{s+1}\). The k-submodule generated by \(y_r,y_{r+1},\ldots\) is p-divisible, because every finite linear combination is p times the same linear combination with indices advanced once. The units a_s show that it also contains all the original \(\xi_s\). Over a p-local base, every integer prime to p is already invertible, so p-divisibility is the same as divisibility by all positive integers. This is stronger than the conclusion \(\xi_s\in\bigcap_h p^hN\) alone and explains Hua’s corrected local proposition.

## C. The listed tail relations are not a complete S-module presentation

For \(k=\mathbb F_2\), let \(T_s=T^{(2^s)}\). The divided-power algebra has basis the squarefree monomials in the T_s: binary expansion gives the index, their multiplication has the required carry-zero coefficients, and \(T_s^2=0\). Hence

\[
S=\Gamma_{\mathbb F_2}(T)\cong\mathbb F_2[T_0,T_1,\ldots]/(T_s^2:s\geq0).
\]

The ideal \(I_r=\tau_{2^r}S\) is generated by \(T_s\), \(s\geq r\). If the relations on scan 043 were the whole presentation, the presenting module would be

\[
E_r=\bigoplus_{s\geq r}S/(T_s)e_s,
\]

because \(p=0\) and \(p-1=1\). The map \(E_r\to I_r\), \(e_s\mapsto T_s\), kills

\[
z=T_{r+1}e_r-T_re_{r+1}.
\]

But z is nonzero: its first component is the nonzero class of \(T_{r+1}\) in \(S/(T_r)\), and its second is the nonzero class of \(T_r\) in \(S/(T_{r+1})\). Thus the cross-generator relation is genuinely missing. This only challenges sufficiency of the written relation list; every actual homomorphism still satisfies those relations, so the necessity argument in B remains available.

## D. Global factorization through the intersection target

This is a proof route for the **literal** target \(N_\infty=\bigcap_{n\geq1}nN\), not an identification with the largest divisible submodule. Let k be a commutative unital ring, \(S=\Gamma_k(T)\), \(d>0\), \(\alpha\geq d\), and

\[
u:\tau_\alpha(M\otimes_kS)\longrightarrow N\otimes_kS
\]

be S-linear of degree \(-d\). We show that each output coefficient belongs to \(N_\infty\).

Fix m in M and an integer prime p. Localize all data at p. Restricting \(u\) along \(a\mapsto m\otimes a\) gives a map from \(\tau_\alpha S\) to \(N_{(p)}\otimes S_{(p)}\). Choose r with \(p^r\geq\alpha\) and \(p^r>d\). The images of the generators \(T_s=T^{(p^s)}\), \(s\geq r\), satisfy the necessary relations of scan 043 and hence the coherent recurrence in B, after expressing their output in the p-digit monomial basis; their coefficients belong to the largest p-divisible submodule \(D_p(N_{(p)})\).

Every divided-power monomial of weight at least \(p^r\) is an S-linear multiple of some \(T_s\), \(s\geq r\), by a unit-adjusted product of digit monomials. Therefore all output coefficients in that high tail lie in \(D_p(N_{(p)})\). This step uses generation of the ideal, not completeness of the relation presentation rejected in C.

For a remaining index \(j\geq\alpha\), write the coefficient of \(u(mT^{(j)})\) as \(b_j\), so its target degree is \(j-d\geq0\). Choose t large enough that

\[
\ell=p^t-1-(j-d)\geq0,\qquad j+\ell\geq p^r.
\]

S-linearity gives

\[
c_{\ell,j-d}b_j=c_{\ell,j}b_{j+\ell}.
\]

The left coefficient is a p-local unit because \((j-d)+\ell=p^t-1\) has all digits p−1, using the no-carry criterion of scans 020–034. The right coefficient multiplies an element already in the p-divisible submodule; hence \(b_j\in D_p(N_{(p)})\) as well.

Now fix a positive integer n. For every prime p, the localized coefficient \((b_j)_{(p)}\) belongs to \(nN_{(p)}\): p-power division follows from the preceding paragraph, and factors prime to p are units. Its class in \(N/nN\) therefore localizes to zero at every integer prime and is zero. (A nonzero element of an abelian group remains nonzero at some maximal ideal of \(\mathbb Z\), by applying this to its cyclic submodule.) Thus \(b_j\in nN\) for all n, which proves the desired factorization through \(N_\infty\otimes S\), coefficient by coefficient in the free graded basis of S.

No finite-generation hypothesis is needed: the argument starts with an actual map and each actual coefficient, rather than asserting that localization commutes with an infinite Hom set. It leaves open here whether one can replace \(N_\infty\) globally by the maximal divisible submodule through an additional argument; that strengthening is not required for the written intersection-target statement or its torsion-ring corollary.

## E. Two short checks

**Endpoint test (NH06).** In \(\Gamma_{\mathbb F_2}(T)\), multiplication by T sends \(T^{(j)}\) to \((j+1)T^{(j+1)}\). Restricting to j≥1 kills j=1, so the image lies in degrees ≥3, and the j=2 value is nonzero. This proves that the upper endpoint in the scan 041 list cannot be imposed in addition to the strict range written immediately below it.

**Torsion base (NH14).** If k is a unital torsion ring, some e>0 kills its unit, hence e kills every k-module. Therefore \(N_\infty\subseteq eN=0\), and the factorization in D forces the negative-degree Hom group to vanish. This uses the ring’s bounded exponent supplied by its unit, not an unsupported assertion that every torsion abelian group has zero divisible part.
