### N06 — Exact ideal formula and the exceptional completion at 2

**Source:** 136, scans 239–241; **kind:** own idea; **confidence:** high, by the displayed valuation calculation and the classification of ideals of \(\mathbb Z_p\).

For \(i\geq1\), the stated ideal is \(S^{[i]}=p^{a_p(i)}\mathbb Z_p\), where
\[
a_p(i)=\min_{n\geq i}\left(n-v_p(n!)\right)
      =\min_{n\geq i}\frac{(p-2)n+s_p(n)}{p-1}.
\]
For \(p>2\), one can compute this minimum in the finite interval \(i\leq n\leq\left\lfloor((p-1)(i-v_p(i!))-1)/(p-2)\right\rfloor\), since every larger \(n\) has valuation exceeding the candidate at \(i\). For \(p=2\), the arbitrarily large powers \(n=2^{r}\) have digit sum 1, giving \(S^{[i]}=2\mathbb Z_2\) for every \(i\geq1\) and hence inverse-limit completion \(\varprojlim_i\mathbb Z_2/S^{[i]}=\mathbb F_2\). This confirms the written exceptional behavior and shows why PD completion cannot here be substituted for ordinary 2-adic completion.

**Context:** C6 §117, pp. 493–495, distinguishes completed and uncompleted objects but contains no such valuation computation; adds a precise test case. **Register:** WG2 §2.4 #5 [unchecked plan; W2].

### N10 — A direct obstruction to the strongest ordinary integral PD-DGA reading

**Source:** 136, cancelled scan 247, “contient \(A^{+}\)” and the differential identity; **kind:** own idea; **confidence:** high under the stated axioms.

This does not refute a weighted construction, a PD structure on a smaller ideal, or a reconstruction of products after passing to another category, and it does not turn the cancelled definition into a surviving claim.

### N22 — The stronger unit needs the weighted rigidity calculation

**Source:** 136, scan 252, conditions a′–c′; scan 275, coefficient equalizer; **kind:** own idea; **confidence:** high for nonnegative graded modules over \(\Gamma_k(T)\), by the explicit presentation in the supporting memo.

For degree \(\alpha\geq0\), a tail map is encoded by \(h_n\) satisfying
\[
\binom{j}{n}h_j=\binom{j+\alpha}{n+\alpha}h_n,
\qquad j\geq n,
\]
and the desired extension has \(h_n=\binom{n+\alpha}{\alpha}x\). Choosing \(j+\alpha=\ell^r-1\) proves this description after localization at every prime, and the tail of the coefficients \(\binom{n+\alpha}{\alpha}\) has gcd 1, allowing a finite Bézout combination to recover \(x\) globally. This establishes \(M\otimes_kS\simeq\rho_{0N}\tau_{N,0}(M\otimes_kS)\) in nonnegative degrees for every \(k\)-module \(M\), and hence the finite-tail full faithfulness asserted in the register. It does not establish a derived statement or an infinite quotient solely from the underived unit, and the stronger unit is false if its right adjoint is instead taken in all Z-graded modules: over a characteristic-zero field, that right adjoint applied to the positive tail of k[T] gives k[T,T^{-1}], with new negative components.

### N25 — A concrete obstruction: elementwise negligibility need not be extension-closed

**Source:** 136, scan 256, elementwise definition, and scans 257–267, infinite-localization use; **kind:** own idea; **confidence:** high, by an explicit extension over \(\mathbb F_p\).

## F. A specified repair and the interpretation it permits

### N71 — A localizing Serre closure gives a precise replacement problem

**Source:** 136, scans 256–273; **kind:** own idea; **confidence:** high for the explicitly changed category, not for its historical identification.

For nonnegative modules over \(S=\Gamma_k(T)\), let \(\mathcal T\) be the localizing Serre subcategory generated by bounded-above modules, and take its genuine Gabriel quotient \(Q\) with section functor \(R\). The exact left adjoint \(L\) of N32 kills \(\mathcal T\), while \(i(I)\) for an injective \(k\)-module \(I\) is a \(\mathcal T\)-local injective, so an injective resolution proves \(\mathbf R R\,Qi(M)\simeq i(M)\). This provides a precise repaired reconstruction setting, with a full proof in *research/derived_functor_checks.md*. It changes the literal elementwise negligible class and therefore does not establish that the manuscript had already defined this quotient.

**Context:** C6 §115, pp. 483–486, supplies an example of changing the ambient category to obtain a universal construction, but not this quotient; adds an explicit repair. **Register:** Hua [unsearched finite-tail claim; R] is extended under stated new hypotheses; GALOIS E.11 [Open, return's claim; G] remains open in its bare-derivator generality.

### N72 — The weight pattern explains the transition to tail categories

**Source:** 136, scans 237–239 and 244–273; **kind:** own idea; **confidence:** medium as an interpretation, high for the stated conditional module calculation.

Suppose a weighted complex \(G\) has \(H^n(G)_r=M_n\) for \(r\geq n\), zero for \(r<n\), and \(T^{(s)}\) acts by \(\binom{r+s}{r}\). Then \(H^n(G)\simeq\tau_{\geq n}(M_n\otimes\Gamma(T))\), so a genuine exact quotient killing bounded weight differences identifies its cohomology with the image of \(M_n\). This gives a precise conditional reason for the manuscript's graded-module detour and, with an actual chain comparison and monoidal compatibility, could recover multiplication as a ring object in \(D(k)\). Those additional data are not supplied by cohomology isomorphisms alone and do not amount to strict PD or \(E_\infty\) equivalence.

**Context:** C6 §124, p. 521, frames homology as common to several approaches; adds a concrete route for comparing this tail construction with the weight pattern recorded in N54. **Register:** Hua [unsearched finite-tail claim; R]; GALOIS E.11 [Open, return's claim; G], whose general enhancement problem is not solved.

### N73 — Weighted products can recover a primitive square without strict divided powers on it

**Source:** 136, scans 237–239 and 273, interpreted under the explicit weight-product hypothesis of N72; **kind:** own idea; **confidence:** high for the arithmetic illustration, conditional for its manuscript application.

If cross-weight products are the usual cup product multiplied by \(\binom{r+s}{r}\), weights \((2,2)\) and \((3,4)\) give factors 6 and 35. After the specified additive identifications of the target weight groups, \(6\cdot6-35=1\) recovers an ordinary product from these two weighted products. Thus recovery of a primitive square is compatible with a weighted algebra even though a strict ordinary PD operation on a closed representative would force divisibility by 2 as in N10.

**Context:** no such numerical illustration occurs in C6/C7; adds an explicit explanation of the distinction between the PD and binomial/weighted comparisons. **Register:** WG2 §2.4 #5 [unchecked divisibility plan; W2], as a test of what “computes products” must mean.
