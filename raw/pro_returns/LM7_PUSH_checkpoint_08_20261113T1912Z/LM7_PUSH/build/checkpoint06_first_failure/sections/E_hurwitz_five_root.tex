\subsection{A five-point Hurwitz quotient and its complete correction source}

\begin{statement}{E.46}{Proved}{Every retained CM fibre survives the five-point alternating Hurwitz test}
There is a specified quotient of discrete groups, with its continuous
extension to the profinite completion,
\[
 q_5:\Gamma_{0,[5]}\twoheadrightarrow A_{96}.
 \tag{E.46.1}
\]
Its pure mapping-class image is also $A_{96}$.  The four standard
half twists have orders $30$, and the rotation
$\delta_5=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_4$ has image of order five.
The restriction along the actual four-strand map
$B_4\to\Gamma_{0,[5]}$, fixing the fifth marked point, is onto
$A_{96}$ as well.

Retaining all five pentagon evaluations and all three based
five-strand action evaluations gives an explicit induced source
\[
 \rho_5:B=C_2*C_3\twoheadrightarrow G_5,
       \qquad |G_5|=2^{18}3^{20}5^{15}7^2.
 \tag{E.46.2}
\]
Every one of the $864$ points of E.24 extends to the common source
and four-strand targets with this quotient, for every compatible
profinite unit multiplier $\lambda\equiv1\pmod{72}$.
The resulting maps satisfy both source hexagons, the complete
pentagon, and all five-strand braid and sphere relations.
Their five-strand image is $A_{96}$, and
\[
 \sigma'_1\sigma'_2\sigma'_3\sigma'_4
             \sim_{A_{96}} q_5(\delta_5)^\lambda.
 \tag{E.46.3}
\]
Here the primed generators are the actual based action values
defined below.  In particular all $24$ CM fibres, including the
$12$ extra fibres, survive this order-five test.

For multiplier one, the same correction preserves any prescribed
full boundary family of E.42 and any compatible class-three factor
of E.44.  Put $C=\rho_5(N')$, $K=\rho_5(K_6)$, $S=G_5/C$,
and $J_5=G_5/K$.  The exact common source maps and their corrected
images are
\[
 D_{23}\times_{J_5}G_5,
       \qquad |J_5|=216,
 \tag{E.46.4}
\]
and, for the cofinal indicated boundary sources retaining $P_{20}$,
\[
                 G_{\rm old}\times_S G_5,
                 \qquad |S|=5400.
 \tag{E.46.5}
\]
The actual common four-strand target and corrected image are
$T_{23}\times A_{96}$.
All common-quotient maps in these displays are induced by the
same literal generators of $B$ or $B_4$.
\end{statement}

\begin{proof}
\paragraph{The actual five-point action.}
Let $\Pi_5=\langle c_1,\ldots,c_5\mid c_1\cdots c_5=1\rangle$
be the discrete fundamental group of the sphere with five marked
points removed, with the chosen positively oriented peripheral
loops.  Let $\mathcal X$ be the set of epimorphisms
$\Pi_5\twoheadrightarrow A_5$ sending each $c_i$ to an involution,
modulo postcomposition by $\operatorname{Aut}(A_5)$.
Precomposition with the peripheral outer mapping-class action
defines a permutation of this set.  The ambiguity from a based
inner automorphism is absorbed by an inner automorphism of $A_5$.
Thus this construction defines an actual action of
$\Gamma_{0,[5]}$ on $\mathcal X$.

We use $\operatorname{Aut}(A_5)=S_5$ with its actual conjugation
action, proved through its faithful permutation of the five
Sylow $C_2^2$ subgroups in E.29.  Enumerating the fifteen
involutions gives $11820$ ordered product-one five-tuples.
Exactly $300$ generate a $C_2^2$ subgroup; the remaining $11520$
generate $A_5$.  The automorphism action on generating tuples
is free, because an automorphism fixing the five entries fixes
their generated group.  Hence
\[
                         |\mathcal X|=11520/120=96.
 \tag{E.46.6}
\]
Every tuple, orbit representative, and transporter is retained.
With right actions on the index set, the positive half twist is
\[
 (g_i,g_{i+1})\longmapsto
              (g_ig_{i+1}g_i^{-1},g_i).
 \tag{E.46.7}
\]
The constructed permutations satisfy all Artin relations and
the two literal sphere relations
\[
 \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_4^2\sigma_3\sigma_2\sigma_1=1,
       \qquad (\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_4)^5=1.
 \tag{E.46.8}
\]
Each generator has cycle type $2^6 3^9 5^{10}1^7$ and is even.
The image of the second word before taking its fifth power
has order exactly five.  These word checks accompany the
topological construction of the map; they fix its conventions.

We prove the full image order by a short exact permutation
certificate.  Put $u_i=\sigma_i^2$ for $1\le i\le4$.
The saved word $W$ of length $120$ in the signed $u_i$ has
\[
                          W^{410}=(17\ 26\ 43).
 \tag{E.46.9}
\]
The certificate gives $72$ explicit conjugates of this
three-cycle by signed $u_i$-words.  In the retained order,
each new support meets the preceding union, and their union
is the entire $96$-element set.  The deterministic verifier
multiplies every printed word and checks each support and
permutation.  Array composition $g[c[g^{-1}]]$ in that
certificate represents the word $g^{-1}cg$ under our right
action convention.

Here is the group-theoretic implication of the support check.
Start with the alternating group on the first three vertices.
A three-cycle with two vertices in an already covered set $U$
and one new vertex, together with $A_U$, generates
$A_{U\cup\{a\}}$: $A_U$ is transitive on unordered pairs
for $|U|\ge3$, and conjugation and inversion give every
three-cycle involving the new vertex and two old vertices.
These and the three-cycles on $U$ generate that alternating
group.  A new cycle meeting $U$ in one vertex $x$ has the
form $(x\ a\ b)$, after an orientation choice.
Choose distinct $y,z\in U\setminus\{x\}$.
Conjugating $(x\ y\ z)$ by a suitable orientation of the
new cycle gives $(a\ y\ z)$; the preceding argument first
adjoins $a$, and the original cycle then adjoins $b$.
Induction along the saved supports proves
$\langle u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4\rangle=A_{96}$.
All these squares are pure.  The evenness upper bound therefore
proves both image assertions in \textup{(E.46.1)}.
This proof uses the explicit three-cycles and does not rely
on the initially interrupted generic order calculation.

The reversed sphere word is checked on the same arrays:
\[
 y_4=\sigma_3\sigma_2\sigma_1^2\sigma_2\sigma_3,
                   \qquad y_4\sigma_4^2=1.
 \tag{E.46.10}
\]
Thus the subgroup generated by the first three half twists
contains all four $u_i$, and equals $A_{96}$.
It is precisely the image of the stated $B_4$ map.

\paragraph{All evaluation maps and the actual induced source.}
For $1\le i<j\le5$ let
\[
 A_{ij}=
 (\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_{i+1})\sigma_i^2
 (\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_{i+1})^{-1}
 \tag{E.46.11}
\]
in this finite target.  The first five pairs are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 (A_{12},A_{23}A_{24}),\quad (A_{13}A_{23},A_{34}),
       \quad (A_{23},A_{34}),\\
 (A_{12}A_{13},A_{24}A_{34}),\quad(A_{12},A_{23}).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{E.46.12}
\]
They are the five literal pentagon substitutions of E.17.
For $i=2,3,4$ put
\[
 y_i=\sigma_{i-1}\cdots\sigma_1^2\cdots\sigma_{i-1};
 \qquad\text{the last three pairs are }(y_i,\sigma_i^2).
 \tag{E.46.13}
\]
These are exactly the evaluations in the based action
\cite[(1.1)]{BCNakamuraSchneps2000}:
\[
 \sigma'_1=\sigma_1^\lambda,\qquad
 \sigma'_i=f(y_i,\sigma_i^2)^{-1}\sigma_i^\lambda
                     f(y_i,\sigma_i^2)\quad(2\le i\le4).
 \tag{E.46.14}
\]
The order of the two inputs and the conjugation orientation
are the exact comparison established in E.29 and E.38.

Each pair gives a homomorphism $\theta_i:F\to A_{96}$,
$1\le i\le8$, taking $x,y$ to its entries.
Choose the retained Schreier section $b_q$ of $B/F\simeq S_3$.
For $b\in B$, the induced map has permutation $q\mapsto q\bar b$
and coordinate
\[
          \theta_i(b_q b b_{q\bar b}^{-1})
                   \quad(q\in B/F,\ 1\le i\le8).
 \tag{E.46.15}
\]
Cancellation of adjacent section words proves multiplication,
so this is a homomorphism of discrete groups and extends
continuously to the profinite completion.  Its kernel lies
in $F$ and is normal in $B$.  On $F$, its coordinate at the
identity coset is the specified $\theta_i$.

The original action has degree $4608$.  Its $87$ transitive
orbits comprise nine isomorphism classes of $B$-sets, of sizes
\[
                    72,24,300,162,6,162,300,30,18.
 \tag{E.46.16}
\]
For every deleted orbit a saved bijection intertwines both
$s$ and $r$ pointwise with a retained orbit.  Deleting only
these repeated actions therefore gives a faithful action
of the same source image on $1074$ points.  The exact
permutation-group calculation gives \textup{(E.46.2)}.

\paragraph{The complete correction image and its modular map.}
Put $P=\rho_5(N)$, $E=\rho_5(F')$, and retain $C,K$ from
the statement.  The full subgroup computations give
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 |C|=2^{15}3^{17}5^{13}7^2,\qquad C'=C,
               \qquad \rho_5(K_6\cap F')=C,\\
 |G_5/P|=72,\quad |P/C|=75,\quad |K/C|=25,
          \quad |E/C|=4,\quad |G_5/C|=5400.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{E.46.17}
\]
All $97$ actual $N$ generators are evaluated.  The four
selected generators, with indices $1,7,14,27$, generate
their entire image.  Its full derived subgroup is $C$.
The $97$ sixth powers give $K$; the $95$ products prescribed
by the primitive integral kernel of
$N^{\rm ab}\to F^{\rm ab}$ give the entire
$\rho_5(K_6\cap F')$.  The rank and Smith diagonal
$(1^{95})$ of this kernel are checked, as is its zero
product with the actual two-row exponent-sum matrix.
All $95$ image products lie in $C$.
Normality under both $s,r$, the second derived image of $C$,
and the complete derived image of $\rho_5(F)$ are recomputed.
Thus \textup{(E.46.17)} concerns full subgroup images.

Map $B$ to $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z/6)$ by
\[
 s\longmapsto\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 r\longmapsto\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{E.46.18}
\]
Its actual image has $72$ elements.  Enumerating its Cayley
graph gives $144$ Schreier words generating the entire kernel.
Each of their $\rho_5$ images lies in $P$.
Both inverse images have index $72$ in $B$, proving equality
of the kernels.  Reduction modulo three therefore gives
the specified homomorphism $q_3:G_5\to A_4$, with
\[
 x\longmapsto\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 y\longmapsto\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{E.46.19}
\]
These generate $A_4$, so $q_3(E)=V_4$.  Since
$C\subset P\subset\ker q_3$ and $|E/C|=4$, we obtain
\[
                         \ker(q_3|_E)=C.
 \tag{E.46.20}
\]
The retained $24$ base words have the three matrices and
the exact index table of E.29.12--E.29.13.
Every old correction word and all six ternary and seven
binary fibre-basis words are checked to be identity under
\textup{(E.46.18)}.  Checking the actual cusp-gauged
reconstruction proves that all $864$ points have their
specified value $M_\kappa$ under $q_3$.

\paragraph{Arithmetic selection and finite generation.}
Fix such a point and its profinite unit $\lambda$.
The arithmetic construction in E.38 chooses an actual
$\sigma\in G_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\chi(\sigma)=\lambda$
and the required $\rho_2(\sigma)\bmod3$.
The intersection
$\mathbb Q(\zeta_3,\sqrt[3]{2})\cap\mathbb Q(\mu_\infty)
=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$ proves this freedom in the cyclotomic
kernel.  Applying Nakamura--Schneps, Theorem 2.5,
to this actual element gives $q_3(f_\sigma)=M_\kappa$,
with precisely the inputs \textup{(E.46.19)}.
For the retained word $f_0\in\widehat F'$,
\[
                    c=\rho_5(f_0)^{-1}\rho_5(f_\sigma)\in C.
 \tag{E.46.21}
\]
The joint image of $N'$ in $C$ and its solvable image
in $D_{23}$ is their direct product.  Indeed, Goursat's
common quotient would be both perfect and solvable,
and its derived series forces it to be trivial.
There is a discrete $k\in N'$ with these two images
equal to $c$ and $1$.  Thus $f=f_0k$ retains the entire
old point and satisfies $\rho_5(f)=\rho_5(f_\sigma)$.

This equality matches all $48$ induced coordinates.
It gives all five pentagon values and all three values
in \textup{(E.46.14)} of the actual Galois action.
The resulting $B_5\to A_{96}$ and $B_4\to A_{96}$
maps are onto: they are the original onto maps
precomposed with the corresponding profinite Galois
automorphisms.  Their relations, including both sphere
words, are therefore satisfied.  E.39 applied with
$n=5$ and then with $q_5$ proves \textup{(E.46.3)}.
This uses two actual maps from the full braid group;
it imposes no unproved invariance of a finite quotient kernel.

Every common quotient of $D_{23}$ and $G_5$ kills $C$
by perfectness, and then kills $K/C$ of order $25$
because $D_{23}$ has only two- and three-primary factors.
It factors through the actual $J_5$.
Conversely $B\to J_5$ kills $K_6$, so it factors through
$G_6$, which $D_{23}$ retains.  This proves the maximal
common-quotient assertion in \textup{(E.46.4)}.
The corrected maps agree there, since their values are
the same literal source formulas.  Their onto projections
and Goursat's lemma give the asserted fibre product.

The group $A_{96}$ is perfect: every three-cycle is a
commutator of two double transpositions, using two extra
letters, and three-cycles generate $A_{96}$.
Its common quotient with the solvable $T_{23}$ is therefore
trivial.  Both original four-strand projections are onto,
as are both corrected projections.  Goursat's lemma proves
the actual product $T_{23}\times A_{96}$ in both cases.

\paragraph{The exact solvable quotient and every retained boundary stage.}
The literal images of $x,y$ in $G_5$ have order $15$.
By \textup{(E.46.17)}, the image of $F$ in $S$ has
order $900$, its derived group has order four, and its
abelianization has order $225$.  The map from
$(\mathbb Z/15)^2$ taking its two basis vectors to $x,y$
in that abelianization is onto between groups of equal
order.  It is consequently the actual abelianization map.
Together with \textup{(E.46.20)} this gives an injection
of $\rho_5(F)/C$ into $A_4\times(\mathbb Z/15)^2$.
Its exact image is
\[
 \left\{(g,a,b):\kappa_3(g)=a+b\pmod3\right\},
 \tag{E.46.22}
\]
where $\kappa_3:A_4\to C_3$ takes both matrices in
\textup{(E.46.19)} to one.  The complete $900$-element
enumeration checks this equality with the generator maps.

For the actual Fermat subgroup
$H=\ker(F\to(\mathbb Z/3)^2)$, its ten Schreier words
give exactly
\[
                \rho_5(H)C/C=V_4\times C_5^2.
 \tag{E.46.23}
\]
Here $V_4$ is the zero-abelianization part of
\textup{(E.46.22)}, and the two central order-five
generators are the images of $x^3,y^3$.
All $100$ elements and all ten generator images are
retained; commutativity and exponent ten are checked
on the exact model.  In particular the literal source
map has the factorization
\[
 B\longrightarrow B/\bigl([H,H]H^{10}\bigr)
                     \longrightarrow S.
 \tag{E.46.24}
\]
The quotient $S$ is solvable, being an extension of
the solvable group in \textup{(E.46.22)} by $S_3$;
every solvable quotient of $G_5$ kills the perfect $C$.
Thus $S$ is its maximal solvable quotient.
Since $\gamma_3(H)H^{20}\subset[H,H]H^{10}$,
the same map factors through the retained $P_{20}$.

Let $R_H=\bigcap_r\widehat H^{[r]}$ be the closed
prosolvable residual used in E.43.  Perfectness gives
$\rho_5(\widehat N^{[r]})=C$ for every $r\ge1$.
The inclusions
$\widehat N^{[r+1]}\subset\widehat H^{[r]}$ and
compactness of the nested nonempty fibres over each
element of $C$ therefore prove the actual continuous
epimorphism
\[
                         \rho_5|_{R_H}:R_H\twoheadrightarrow C.
 \tag{E.46.25}
\]
Its image is contained in $C$ because the $H$ image
in $S$ is abelian.

Now fix any compatible multiplier-one boundary word
$f_0$ of E.42, with any E.44 factor included.
It has the same $D_{23}$ value as its retained point,
so the modular value used in \textup{(E.46.21)} is unchanged.
Choose a preimage $k\in R_H$ of $c$ by
\textup{(E.46.25)}.  Every finite geometric image in
the specified old family is solvable.  Hence this
single $k$ has identity in each of them and preserves
the full compatible family, including every full
pro-$p$ boundary component for $p\ge5$.
The corrected word still matches $f_\sigma$ in $G_5$.

Every finite old source is solvable, including the
class-three factor, since its geometric kernel is
solvable and $B/H$ has order $54$.
For old sources retaining $P_{20}$, formula
\textup{(E.46.24)} makes $S$ a specified common quotient.
Maximal solvability makes it the maximal common quotient.
The component source maps are onto by E.42 and E.44
on the old side and by the actual Galois automorphism
on the new side.  They agree on $S$ by their literal
source formulas.  This proves \textup{(E.46.5)} for
the original and corrected images.  Adjoining $P_{20}$
stays in the displayed boundary family, so these old
sources are cofinal within that family.

\end{proof}
