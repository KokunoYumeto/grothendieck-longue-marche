\subsection{The full \texorpdfstring{$A_6$}{A6} refinement through the entire congruence system}

\begin{statement}{E.57}{Proved}{One full old word retains the $A_6$, signed, boundary and uniform symplectic images}
Retain the exact sources and maps of E.52--E.55.  In
E.54's notation put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 J=G\times_{\mathcal Q_{30}}G_L,
 \qquad \mathcal A=A_5^3,\qquad
 C_J=C\times_{\mathcal A}C_L,\\
 \mathcal R_0=E\times_{\mathcal A}C_L,
 \qquad \mathcal R_1=P'\times_{\mathcal A}C_L,\\
 I=\gamma_5\widehat N\cap R_{\rm nil}(H),
 \qquad R=\prod_{p\ge5}\mathbf Z_p.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{E.57.1}
\]
Let $\rho_J=(\rho_G,\rho_L)$ be the specified joint source map.
Write $\eta:C_L\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_5)$
for the actual signed modular map of E.53: its source
composite on $R_{\rm nil}(H)$ is $\theta\rho_5$.
Use the same symbol for its composite with the $C_L$
projection of $\mathcal R_0$ or $\mathcal R_1$.
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 \rho_J(R_{\rm nil}(H))&=\mathcal R_0,\\
 \rho_R(I)&=\Phi_R(\operatorname{SL}_2(R),0),\\
 \operatorname{im}\bigl((\rho_J,\theta\rho_R)|_I\bigr)
   &=\mathcal R_1\times_{\eta,\,\mathrm{red}_5}
                                      \operatorname{SL}_2(R).
 \end{split}
 \tag{E.57.2}
\]
The last equality is an equality of actual closed source
images; the additive $V_3(R)$ coordinate is zero on $I$.
In particular the specified restriction is onto:
\[
 \rho_G:
 I\cap\ker\rho_L\cap\ker\rho_R
          \twoheadrightarrow\ker(P'\longrightarrow\mathcal A).
 \tag{E.57.3}
\]

The uniform correcting image of E.53 is unchanged after
retaining the entire joint Ferrari source:
\[
 \rho_R(R_{\rm nil}(H)\cap\ker\rho_J)
       =\mathcal K_R
       =\Phi_R\left(
          \Gamma_5(1)\times\prod_{p\ge7}\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z_p),
                                                   V_3(R)\right).
 \tag{E.57.4}
\]
Intersecting the source with $\ker\rho_{D_{23}}$ leaves
this image unchanged.  Intersecting additionally with
$\ker\rho_5\cap\ker\rho_7$ gives exactly
$\mathcal K_R^{35}$ of E.53.5.
The maximum common quotient of the specified maps
$\rho_J,\rho_R$ is the same $\mathcal J_5$ of E.53:
\[
        \operatorname{im}(\rho_J,\rho_R)
                    =J\times_{\mathcal J_5}G_R.
 \tag{E.57.5}
\]

Every full old value supplied by E.55, for any of its
$432$ points and every admitted coherent boundary
parameter, has a single extension retaining that entire
value and satisfying
\[
       \rho_G(f)=\rho_G(f_\sigma),\qquad
       \rho_L(f)=\rho_L(f_\sigma),\qquad
       \rho_R(f)=\rho_R(f_\sigma),\qquad \chi(\sigma)=1,
 \tag{E.57.6}
\]
with the same actual Galois element already used in E.55.
For a chosen old word $f_{55}$, its correcting word lies
in the exact source of \textup{(E.57.3)}.

Here is an explicit common-source presentation of the
whole enlarged family.  For $m$ prime to six put
$g=\gcd(m,5)$, use $\mathcal J_1=A_2$, and set
\[
 \mathcal Q_m^{\!*}
      =A_{2m}\times_{A_{2g}}\mathcal J_g.
 \tag{E.57.7}
\]
Let $\Gamma$ be any finite actual E.54 source retaining
$P_{4m}$ and the chosen boundary factors.  Its specified
map to \textup{(E.57.7)} uses its ordinary exponent
map and its $G_L$ projection.  The actual and corrected
common source images are both
\[
                         \Gamma\times_{\mathcal Q_m^{\!*}}G_m.
 \tag{E.57.8}
\]
These sources are cofinal.  With $\Gamma_\infty$ the
actual closed E.54 source image and
$\mathcal A_R=\varprojlim A_{2m}$, the inverse-limit
version is
\[
 \Gamma_\infty\times_{\mathcal Q_R^{\!*}}G_R,
 \qquad
 \mathcal Q_R^{\!*}=\mathcal A_R\times_{A_{10}}\mathcal J_5.
 \tag{E.57.9}
\]
It is the actual joint source of all E.55 factors with
the full $A_6$ factor, and the corrected map is onto.

Write $T_{L,A_6}$ for the actual joint target of E.54,
and $\mathcal P^{\rm rt}$ for the exact symplectic
stabilizer of E.56.  For $T=\mathbf Z/m$ or $R$, the
actual and corrected four-strand joint images are
\[
   \bigl(T_{23}\times_{S_L}T_{L,A_6}\bigr)
                       \times\mathcal P^{\rm rt}(T)\times A_{96}.
 \tag{E.57.10}
\]
The five-strand images remain
$\operatorname{Sp}_4(T,\Omega)\times A_{96}$.
Every pentagon, normalized cycle, full-twist and
periodic-conjugacy relation in the retained factors
is preserved.
\end{statement}

\begin{proof}
We determine the two closed correcting images, retain
the complete old word, and then prove generation through
the specified source and target quotient maps.

\paragraph{The full image of the nilpotent residual.}
The actual group $\mathcal R_0$ in \textup{(E.57.1)}
is contained in $\rho_J(H)$.  Indeed any compatible
pair in it is an actual $B$ image by E.54.2, has trivial
outer $S_3$ value, and has zero ordinary exponent values
modulo $30$.  Its source preimage therefore lies in
$F'F^3=H$.
Its quotient by $C_J$ is $E/C$, of order $2^6 3^9$.

In the exact nine-dimensional ternary layer of E.48,
the literal conjugation matrices give
\[
       (X^3-1)V_9+(Y^3-1)V_9=V_9.
 \tag{E.57.11}
\]
The certificate freshly evaluates all eighteen
commutators of $x^3,y^3$ with the nine actual $F'$
basis values in the full solvable collector.  Their
ternary columns are exactly $[X^3-1\mid Y^3-1]$ and
have rank nine.  Their complete values, including
the binary coordinate, are retained.  The nine
$F'$ words belong to $H$; their commutators with
the six actual binary words have rank six on the
whole binary layer.  These are the same specified
binary source words as in E.54, independently
evaluated again.

The perfect group $C_J$ belongs to both
$\rho_J(H)$ and $\mathcal R_0$, so it belongs to
$[\rho_J(H),\mathcal R_0]$.  The two full ranks
just proved fill both successive quotients.  Hence
\[
                    [\rho_J(H),\mathcal R_0]=\mathcal R_0.
 \tag{E.57.12}
\]
It follows that $\mathcal R_0$ lies in the image of
every lower-central term of $\widehat H$.
Nonempty nested compact fibres give its inclusion
in $\rho_J(R_{\rm nil}(H))$.
Conversely that residual lies in $\widehat F'$,
so its $G$ image lies in $E$; its $G_L$ image is
$C_L$ by E.43.  Compatibility over $\mathcal A$
gives the reverse containment.  This proves the
first equality in \textup{(E.57.2)}.

E.52 identifies $\rho_R(N)$ with
$\operatorname{SL}_2(R)\times R^5$ through $\Phi_R$.
The matrix factor is topologically perfect, so
$\rho_R(\gamma_5\widehat N)=\Phi_R(\operatorname{SL}_2(R),0)$.
Its commutator with $\rho_R(H)$ is again the whole
matrix factor, because $\rho_R(H)$ contains it.
Iterate commutators with $\widehat H$ starting
from $\gamma_5\widehat N$, exactly as in E.54.20.
The resulting descending subgroups have the same
matrix image and are contained in successively
deeper lower-central subgroups of $\widehat H$.
Compactness in the fibre of each prescribed
matrix proves the second equality in
\textup{(E.57.2)}, including the full product of
all primes and precisions.

\paragraph{The exact common image and the literal power relation.}
First apply Goursat to the actual image of
$R_{\rm nil}(H)$ in $\mathcal R_0\times C_R$.
The common quotient $D$ is finite, since
$\mathcal R_0$ is finite.  The abelianization
$D^{\rm ab}$ is a quotient of
$\mathcal R_0^{\rm ab}$, whose prime divisors
are only two and three because $C_J$ is perfect.
It is also a quotient of $C_R^{\rm ab}=V_3(R)$,
whose finite quotients have order prime to six.
Consequently $D^{\rm ab}=1$.

The central additive factor of $C_R$ now has
trivial image in $D$.  Indeed, in this perfect
quotient the image of $\operatorname{SL}_2(R)$
is the whole group: the commutator of its product
with a central group is its own commutator.
Every finite quotient of $\operatorname{SL}_2(R)$
has centre of exponent dividing two by E.53.8--10.
The image of $V_3(R)$ is central and has order
prime to six, so it is trivial.  Thus
\[
 \rho_R(R_{\rm nil}(H)\cap\ker\rho_J)
                         =\Phi_R(U,V_3(R))
 \tag{E.57.13}
\]
for a closed normal $U\triangleleft\operatorname{SL}_2(R)$.

The same argument on $I$ is simpler.  Its two
images are $\mathcal R_1$ by E.54 and the full
matrix factor by \textup{(E.57.2)}.  Write
\[
       \rho_R(I\cap\ker\rho_J)=\Phi_R(U_I,0).
 \tag{E.57.14}
\]
Here $U_I$ is again closed and normal in the whole
matrix product.  The next actual word determines
both $U$ and $U_I$.

In the full $G$ model, the literal $x,y$ have
order $120$; in all $36$ signed source coordinates
they have order $30$.  These assertions are
independently checked on the full permutation
and signed arrays.  Therefore
\[
                         x^{120},y^{120}\in\ker\rho_J.
 \tag{E.57.15}
\]
Choose $c\in I$ with $\theta\rho_R(c)=E_{12}(1)$,
using the proved onto map in \textup{(E.57.2)}.
The subgroup $I$ is normal in $\widehat B$, so
$[x^{120},c]\in I\cap\ker\rho_J$.
The canonical signed pure matrices in E.53 are
$x\mapsto E_{12}(2)$ and $y\mapsto E_{21}(-2)$;
conjugating by the displayed coordinate swap gives
$\theta\rho_R(x^{120})=E_{21}(240)$.
Thus the exact matrix coordinate of this word is
\[
 \begin{split}
 [E_{21}(t),E_{12}(1)]
       &=\begin{pmatrix}1-t&t\\-t^2&1+t+t^2\end{pmatrix},
                          \qquad t=240,\\
 [E_{21}(240),E_{12}(1)]\pmod{25}
       &=\begin{pmatrix}11&15\\0&16\end{pmatrix}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{E.57.16}
\]
For every $p\ge7$, its upper-right entry is nonzero
modulo $p$, because the only prime divisors of
$240$ are two, three and five.  The normal
projection of $U_I$ to $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z_p)$
therefore has noncentral reduction.  E.53's exact
normal-subgroup argument makes that projection
the whole group.  Commutating with elements
supported in this one prime factor then places
the pure factor inside $U_I$, since it is
topologically perfect.  Closedness gives their
whole product for $p\ge7$.

At five, the first-layer vector in
\textup{(E.57.16)} is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&3\\0&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Three literal conjugates have rank three in
$\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbf F_5)$.  Powers and
successive approximation, as in E.53.9, give
all of $\Gamma_5(1)$ in the five-projection.
Subtracting the already contained other prime
factors gives this pure five-factor as well.
The actual signed map $\eta$ forces the reverse
containment: every element killed by $\rho_J$
is killed by $\rho_L$, so its five-reduction
is the identity.  Consequently
\[
       U_I=U=\Gamma_5(1)\times
                         \prod_{p\ge7}\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z_p).
 \tag{E.57.17}
\]
For $U$, the lower inclusion follows from $U_I\subset U$,
and the same signed map gives the upper inclusion.
This proves \textup{(E.57.4)} and identifies the
kernel in \textup{(E.57.14)} exactly.

The two maps from $I$ agree under $\eta$ and
reduction modulo five, by the specified E.53
signed comparison.  They are onto their two
factors.  Their exact kernel just determined
therefore gives the full fibre product in
\textup{(E.57.2)}: lift its first coordinate,
then correct its second coordinate by
$\Gamma_5(1)\times\prod_{p\ge7}\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z_p)$.
For every $d\in\ker(P'\to\mathcal A)$, the
triple $(d,1,1)$ belongs to this exact fibre
product.  Its preimage proves
\textup{(E.57.3)}.

Every finite solvable quotient of $\mathcal K_R$
has order prime to six: its unrestricted matrix
factors are perfect, its first five-congruence
factor is pro-five, and its additive factors
have primes at least five.  The actual common
quotient with the $D_{23}$ image is therefore
trivial.  Goursat proves that imposing
$\ker\rho_{D_{23}}$ preserves
\textup{(E.57.4)}.  The level-five and level-seven
maps factor through $\rho_R$, so imposing their

$\mathcal K_R^{35}$, as asserted.

For \textup{(E.57.5)}, the $G_L$ projection
already supplies the agreed quotient
$\mathcal J_5$.  The image of $\ker\rho_J$ in
$G_R$ contains $\mathcal K_R$ by
\textup{(E.57.4)}.  After quotienting by that
subgroup, the remaining kernel of
$G_R\to\mathcal J_5$ injects into the two
even ordinary exponent coordinates and is
exactly their kernel of reduction modulo five,
as proved through the signed map in E.53.
The actual words $x^{120},y^{120}$ in
\textup{(E.57.15)} have those coordinates
$(60,0),(0,60)$.  Their closed spans are
$5R^2$, because twelve is a unit in $R$.
They therefore fill the entire remaining
kernel.  This proves the exact kernel equality
and hence the actual fibre product
\textup{(E.57.5)}.  Reduction gives the common
quotient $\mathcal J_{\gcd(m,5)}$ at every
finite modulus.

\paragraph{The whole common source after correction.}

 G_m\longrightarrow\mathcal Q_m^{\!*},

\paragraph{The full target product.}
The actual braid generators $a,b,c$ have

The remaining prime five has a direct

$T_{A_6}$ by its perfect free subgroup

The old solvable factor $T_{23}$ is
