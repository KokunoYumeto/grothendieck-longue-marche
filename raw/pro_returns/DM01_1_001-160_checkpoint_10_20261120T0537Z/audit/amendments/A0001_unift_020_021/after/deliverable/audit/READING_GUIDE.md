# Confirmed distinctions for subsequent readers

This short guide records tests supported by completed pages. It is a location and comparison aid, never permission to decide another occurrence without viewing its own ink. Different fragments can use different conventions. Keep every image context scoped to the assigned page and compare with clear examples on that page when available.

## Arrow versus wedge — confirmed on scan 005

M0035, M0036 and M0040 were changed to `\uparrow` after separate visual readings agreed. Each glyph has two sloping arrowhead arms **and a third central stroke** descending from the apex; M0040 also preserves a white gap beside that shaft. A plain `\wedge` has only the two sloping arms. Test for the central shaft in each new occurrence, and retain uncertainty if cancellation or a neighboring stroke hides it. Do not select the arrow merely because it makes mathematical sense.

## Crossings-out and compressed connections

On scan 003, M0008, M0010, M0012 and M0013 remain uncertain because deletion or superposition removes the distinguishing counters or numeral layers. Enlargement does not restore absent ink. In partly legible phrases, remove uncertainty from securely read words and keep the marker around the unclear residue (scan 004, M0021 and M0024). This principle must be applied to actual surviving strokes, not used as a shortcut for a difficult crop.

## Local phi/psi distinction on scan 006

M0056, M0057 and M0059 were independently confirmed as \varphi, correcting first candidate \psi: each has a closed right oval/loop and long descending stem. The same page supplies a contrasting open psi with separate arms/central prong. This is evidence for this fragment’s local hand; examine every later occurrence in its own ink before using the distinction.

## Repeated fblt abbreviation on scans 012 and 015

The same abbreviation was individually checked in M0197, M0202, M0203, M0256, M0258, M0262 and M0263. The evidence is a tall elongated f with a deep lower return, a compact raised middle component and a short final upright/hook. Cancellation can cover the small connecting bodies while leaving that silhouette. This is a comparison aid for locating the components; inspect each new occurrence, and keep uncertainty if a distinguishing component is absent or merged. Do not expand the abbreviation into a full word.

## Short unift abbreviation — re-proved on scans 020 and 021

M0334 and M0335 were corrected from uniformt to unift after an independent inspection: the descending f is immediately followed by a small raised terminal t, with no separate o loop or r/m shoulders between them. Scan021’s clear fully written uniforme has an additional low connected group after f, providing a positive local comparison. The compressed prefix limits both decisions to medium confidence. Preserve the written abbreviation. Inspect every later occurrence in its own ink; this comparison alone does not settle a similar obscured form (including scan022 M0364, which remains uncertain in its reviewed submission).
