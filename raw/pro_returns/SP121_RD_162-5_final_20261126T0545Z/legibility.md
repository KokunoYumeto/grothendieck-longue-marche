The handwritten mathematical leaves are substantially harder than the ordinary typewriting. Their most vulnerable passages are dense cancellations, superposed indices, small interlinear words, and looped letters whose alphabet is not uniquely determined by the surviving strokes. The typescript's ordinary baselines are usually legible, but its old impressions beneath keyed corrections require separate judgments about the visible letter and the order of typing. A recovered underletter is not necessarily evidence that its chronological order can be recovered. Whole-pair doubts preserve that distinction.
