## Legibility and witness comparison

The handwritten mathematical leaves are substantially harder than the ordinary typewriting. Their most vulnerable passages are dense cancellations, superposed indices, small interlinear words, and looped letters whose alphabet is not uniquely determined by the surviving strokes. The typescript's ordinary baselines are usually legible, but its old impressions beneath keyed corrections require separate judgments about the visible letter and the order of typing. A recovered underletter is not necessarily evidence that its chronological order can be recovered. Whole-pair doubts preserve that distinction.

The least secure readings are explicitly marked in the transcription and listed above. They include parts of the cancelled mathematical calculation on025, the looped hypercohomology grades and clipped parenthesis on031, the lower-edge marginal note on035, the cancelled handwritten marginal tail on039, the clipped fragment below the diagram on040, the corrected article and single l/a cell on045, and the crowded first H exponent, raised typed insertion, clause numeral and B wave on046. Enlarging the supplied pixels makes strokes easier to inspect but does not restore ink outside the scan or distinguish impressions that occupy exactly the same pixels. Confidence in a retained mathematical letter is therefore separate from confidence that the formula is mathematically intended.

Neither AI witness is a reliable authority on its own. H often supplies readable running prose but drops cancelled wording, flattens insertions, regularizes keyed o indices to zero, or replaces paired source quotation strokes with guillemets. C retains more material and more doubt marks, yet sometimes reads a cancelling x as an underlying letter, misses a surviving letter beneath it, or treats a leader as text. Both witnesses can agree on a plausible ordinary word while omitting its overtyped layer; such agreements were read against the image as well. These are observations about this supplied selection, not estimates of either witness's general error rate. Counts in the changes log describe local evidential places, and one row may differ from both witnesses.
