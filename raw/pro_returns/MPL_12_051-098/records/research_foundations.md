### F02 — The correspondence-ring connection is substantive but not an identification

**Source:** CT16/015–018 and /049; F12/056; **quote:** “anneau ... de correspondances Lefschetzien”, “category of motives which is semi-simple”, and “L'anneau des correspondances ... opérant en dim i”; **kind:** literature comparison; **context search:** `anneau|correspondances|semi-simple` matches CN S13–S16; **RS:** WG2 §1.1 #2, WG2 §2.4 #3, MPL_11_043-058+56 N031. The classical construction uses correspondences between all varieties, with a specified adequate equivalence relation, then projectors and twists, whereas a single Lefschetz correspondence ring describes operators and decompositions on a chosen realization; Jannsen [J92], pp. 447–448, gives the objects as triples `(X,p,m)`. Thus the ring formalism is compatible with an ingredient of the construction, but F12/056 is a commutant assertion in one cohomological degree, not the missing categorical construction.

### F03 — Semisimplicity has not changed its logical status in this packet

**Source:** F12/056 and /086; CT16/049; **quote:** “est un anneau semi-simple ... [via Hodge]” and “Nous ne considérons que des motifs ... simples ... purs de poids ρ”; **kind:** unproved claim / hypothesis distinction; **context search:** `semi-simple|numérique|homologique` matches CN S15–S16; **RS:** WG2 §2.3 #7, MPL_15_087-138 §127, MPL_14_087-132 M23. The new pages assert or assume semisimplicity rather than supplying the missing argument for homological correspondences; their precise addition is the double-commutant formulation and the positivity axioms.

### F04 — A nilpotent kernel and a tensor category require separate qualifications

**Source:** F12/056, injectivity clause; CT16/049; **quote:** “Le deuxième énoncé + injectivité ... entraîne tout”; **kind:** literature comparison; **context search:** `injectivité|radical|nilpotent|Künneth` matches CN S16 and RS WG2 §2.3 #7; **RS:** WG2 §2.3 #7, MPL_15_087-138 §127. ### F06 — A small example explains why tensor data matter

**Source:** F12/057–059; **quote:** “Comment caractériser l'image” and “structures tensorielles”; **kind:** own explanatory idea; **context search:** `image|enveloppe|tenseur` matches the transition from /057 to /059; **RS:** WG2 §2.4 #3, compared with MPL_15_087-138 §111 rather than redoing that calculation. Over a characteristic-zero field, the standard representations of `SL₂` and `GL₂` generate the same associative algebra `End(V)`, while the determinant tensor distinguishes the groups. **Confidence: high**, because elementary unipotents and diagonal elements generate the full matrix algebra, whereas preservation of `Λ²V → 1` is exactly the determinant-one constraint; the example explains the information missing from a single commutant without identifying either group with the manuscript's Galois group.

### F07 — The proposed Lefschetz subgroup inclusion is not supported

**Source:** F12/056–059 and CT16/015–018; **quote:** “commutant” and “L ∈ E^(2)”; **kind:** bounded comparison; **context search:** `Lefschetz|SL|sl|graduation|π_i` matches the graded operator relations in CT16/015–018 and CN S3, but no `sl₂ → G_mot` construction in the frozen current text; **RS:** MPL_14_087-132 M04 and MPL_15_087-138 §111, both first-pass claims of the obstruction, plus WG2 §2.4 #3. Motivic tensor automorphisms must respect motivic morphisms, while Lefschetz operators change cohomological degree and have to be read with their Tate twists; the full group can therefore act on their untwisted realization by a Tate character rather than literally commute with a degree-changing operator. The present contribution is the explicit commutant/image distinction in F12/057, not a fresh proof of the already registered counterexample.

### F08 — Weight grading and Lefschetz grading are different structures

**Source:** F12/056 and /086; CT16/016–017; **quote:** “est de poids 0”, “purs de poids ρ”, and “Il faut donc se donner en même temps L et les π_i”; **kind:** own interpretation checked against formal literature; **context search:** `poids|graduation|π_i` matches the projectors in CT16 and the existing pure-weight terminology in F12, not an identification of the two gradings; **RS:** WG2 §2.4 #3, MPL_15_087-138 L07 and §111. A tensor-compatible grading of a pure Tannakian category corresponds to a **central** cocharacter by [DM22], Definition 5.1, whereas the standard Lefschetz `sl₂` Cartan acts by `i−dim X` on `H^i(X)` and has nonzero bracket with raising/lowering operators. **Confidence: high**, because centrality and `[h,L]=2L` are incompatible for a nontrivial common image; the dimension shift and Tate twists also preclude identifying the two merely because both use degrees.

### F09 — Positivity assumes the pure semisimple setting

**Source:** F12/086–089; **quote:** “Nous ne considérons que” and “Le produit tensoriel de deux formes amples est ample”; **kind:** scope observation; **context search:** `positif|ample|forme|Hodge|index` matches CT16/049 and CN S13 but moves from an index pairing on cycle classes to axioms for all objects in a stipulated category; **RS:** WG2 §2.4 #3, MPL_60_002-035 F21, MPL_15_195-228+18_002-017 B03. The parity of forms, stability under restriction/direct sum/tensor product, and compatibility with Tate twists resemble the later formalism of graded polarizations ([DM22], §5), but the opening restricts to pure semisimple motives. These axioms cannot be cited as a proof that the category constructed from all homological correspondences is semisimple.

### F10 — Weight zero does not by itself mean a constant motive

**Source:** F12/088(vii) and /089, invariant pairing; **quote:** “Si ρ=0, de sorte que essentiellement M est défini par un vectoriel” and “Γ_k M”; **kind:** own scope diagnostic; **context search:** `poids 0|vectoriel|Tate|Γ` matches F12/086's weight-zero internal Hom, but no explicit restriction to trivial motives before /088(vii); **RS:** WG2 §2.4 #3 and MPL_15_087-138 L07. Under modern meanings, a pure weight-zero motive need not be a sum of the unit: for an elliptic curve, `h¹(E)∨ ⊗ h¹(E)` has weight zero and nontrivial Hodge types, so the implication in the quoted sentence requires an additional restriction or interpretation. **Confidence: high** for this modern distinction, since duality negates weights and tensor product adds them; this is a research qualification, not a proposed change to the frozen wording.

### F16 — S15: unconditional group construction and comparison

**Source:** F12/059 and /086; **quote:** “catégorie des motifs, munie de ses structures tensorielles”; **kind:** verified literature comparison; **context search:** `Nori|Ayoub|Aut|semi-simple` matches RS's earlier comparison leads, not a construction in the current source; **RS:** MPL_15_195-228+18_002-017 S15: and M07, MPL_18_018-062 MB10. ### F20 — Period torsor and period conjecture must be separated

**Source:** F12/059 as tensor-categorical comparison; external [H18], §§4.4–5.3; **quote:** “structures tensorielles”; **kind:** verified literature comparison; **context search:** `period|transcend|generic|torsor` matches RS MPL_18_018-062 §2, not F12's text; **RS:** MPL_15_087-138 T26, MPL_18_018-062 §2 and MB10. For a tensor-generated Nori category, the scheme of tensor isomorphisms between Betti and de Rham fibre functors is an unconditional torsor, with a comparison point over `C`; the period conjecture adds injectivity of evaluation of formal periods, equivalently the appropriate genericity/connectedness assertion and `trdeg_Q Q(periods(M)) = dim G(M)` ([H18], Proposition 5.11). An actual comparison isomorphism and a group exact sequence do not establish this genericity.

### F21 — The known evidence has a linear-versus-algebraic boundary

[BT25], Theorem 1.1, proves a functional transcendence result over complex function fields and explicitly distinguishes it from the numerical conjecture over `Q`, still described there as very open.
