# Scan 012: conditional regularity check

## Statement and source limits

The sentence beginning on scan011 and continuing on scan012 says that adjoining roots of some parameters of a regular local ring again gives a regular ring, without requiring the exponents to be prime to the residue characteristic. The diplomatic text has blank relation slots in its index ranges and before the numeral 1, and the displayed polynomial variables have no visible enclosing brackets. The following check is explicitly conditional on positive integer exponents and on the polynomial-quotient interpretation. It neither restores those signs nor establishes that the hypotheses were fully written.

**012-M01.** Let (A,m,k) be a Noetherian regular local ring of dimension d. Let x_1,...,x_r be part of a regular system of parameters, completed by x_{r+1},...,x_d. For positive integers n_1,...,n_r, put B=A[T_1,...,T_r]/(T_1^{n_1}-x_1,...,T_r^{n_r}-x_r). Then B is a regular local ring of dimension d. No n_i needs to be invertible in A or in k.

## Argument actually checked by the main reader

1. Successive division by the monic polynomial in each distinct variable leaves a unique remainder with each T_i degree below n_i. Thus the residue classes of the monomials T_1^{e_1}...T_r^{e_r}, with 0 <= e_i < n_i, form an A-basis. In particular A embeds in B, B is nonzero and finite over A, and every T_i is integral over A. This uses positivity of the exponents explicitly.

2. Modulo mB, the algebra is k[T_1,...,T_r]/(T_1^{n_1},...,T_r^{n_r}). Each variable is nilpotent. The quotient by all variables is k, and an element with nonzero constant term is invertible by a finite geometric series in a nilpotent element. Therefore this quotient has a unique maximal ideal generated by the variables. In a finite integral extension every maximal ideal contracts to a maximal ideal; because A is local, all maximal ideals of B lie over m. Consequently B has the unique maximal ideal n=(m,T_1,...,T_r).

3. In B, the equalities x_i=T_i^{n_i} for i<=r show that n is generated by T_1,...,T_r,x_{r+1},...,x_d: a list of d elements. Finite integral extensions with an injective base map have the same Krull dimension, so dim B=d. The minimal number of generators of the maximal ideal of a Noetherian local ring is at least its dimension. Here it is at most d, hence equals d. This is the regular-local-ring criterion.

The conclusion follows without a derivative, separability argument, or a residue-characteristic coprimality assumption. This proof does not verify the cited results 7.1, 7.2, 9.4, 10.2 or 10.3, does not prove the earlier inertia-specialization assertion, and supplies no missing text to either scan.

## Background explicitly distinguished

The argument rechecks the monic normal-form and nilpotent-quotient steps directly. It uses, without a fresh proof here, Noetherianity of a finite algebra over a Noetherian ring, contraction of maximal ideals under integral extension, equality of dimension under an injective integral extension, and the dimension/generator criterion for a regular local ring. These uses are separately recorded as background events. No external source, computation, or theorem-proving software was consulted.
