## PR-02 — Doubled rational line: admissibility and failure of unique hull

**Verdict: valid, high confidence, conditional on the reported tronçon definition.** On $D=\mathbb Q\times\{0,1\}$, put $(q,i)<(r,j)$ exactly when $q<r$, with equality added for the nonstrict order. This is a partial order, is directed upwards and downwards, and is dense; every nonempty strict upper cone is downward directed because a rational level can be chosen strictly between its vertex level and the smaller of two given upper levels, and the lower-cone condition follows dually.

Let $a=(0,0)$, $b=(0,1)$, and $T=\{(q,i):q<0\}$. Then the closed lower rays $C_a=D_{\le a}=T\cup\{a\}$ and $C_b=D_{\le b}=T\cup\{b\}$ are incomparable, and

\[
T=C_a\setminus\{a\}=C_b\setminus\{b\},\qquad C_a\cap C_b=T.
\]

Thus026's unique-presentation claim fails as a claim about subsets under these reported axioms, and027's definition of the boundary by choosing that presentation is ambiguous. A least closed moderate superset $Z$ of $T$, if it existed, would be contained in $C_a\cap C_b=T$, hence equal $T$. Every closed pseudo-tronçon contained in $T$ has a greatest endpoint at a negative rational level; finitely many such pieces miss points at levels closer to zero than all their endpoints, so $T$ is not a finite union of closed pseudo-tronçons. This proves the claimed failure of the least closed moderate superset, independently of any requirement of mutual compatibility on those finite pieces.

**Scope:** this is a counterexample to the general subset model as reported, not to dense total orders. It does not show that endpoint-decorated pieces cannot be used instead; it shows that decoration would be additional data.

## PR-03 — Strong-coordinate real plane is an admissible Hausdorff model

**Verdict: valid, high confidence; emphatically not the ordinary product order.** On $P=\mathbb R^2$, define

\[
p<q\iff p_1<q_1\text{ and }p_2<q_2,
\qquad p\le q\iff p=q\text{ or }p<q.
\]

Reflexivity, antisymmetry and transitivity hold. A common upper bound of two points is obtained by adding a positive margin to each coordinatewise maximum; a common lower bound is dual. Midpoints prove density. If $u,v>a$, choose each coordinate $w_i$ strictly between $a_i$ and $\min(u_i,v_i)$; then $a<w<u,v$, proving the downward directedness of the strict upper cone, with the dual argument for lower cones.

The strict order interval $(a,b)$, when $a<b$, is the ordinary open rectangle $(a_1,b_1)\times(a_2,b_2)$. Every ordinary bounded open rectangle is obtained this way, and all the one-sided strict cones are Euclidean-open quadrants. Therefore the canonical topology generated by these intervals and cones is exactly the Euclidean topology: it is Hausdorff and in particular $T_0$. None of the following failures can be repaired merely by imposing $T_0$.

## PR-04 — Pointwise precedence and inclusion need not imply compatibility

**Verdict: both counterexamples valid, high confidence.** In PR-03's order take $a=(0,0)$, $b=(0,1)$, $T=P_{<a}=(-\infty,0)^2$, and $T'=\{b\}$. Every $t\in T$ satisfies $t<b$, yet the assigned endpoints $\partial T=\{a\}$ and $\partial T'=\{b\}$ are incomparable because their first coordinates agree and the points are distinct.

This refutes028's claim that pointwise $T<T'$ itself entails moderate position. Also

\[
P_{<a}\subset P_{<b},
\]

but these two rays have the incomparable boundary points $a,b$, refuting the concrete inclusion-implies-moderateness claim of032/033. Each ray by itself is a moderate set arising from a one-piece flag, regardless of any further difficulty in the global separated-family construction. The endpoint presentation of each ray is unique in this strong-coordinate model, so this is independent of PR-02's doubled-point ambiguity.

## PR-05 — Even the strong-coordinate Hausdorff model lacks least closed hulls

**Verdict: valid, high confidence; stronger than the doubled-line example as a separation test.** With $a,b,T$ as in PR-04, set

\[
C_a=P_{\le a}=T\cup\{a\},\qquad
C_b=P_{\le b}=((-\infty,0)\times(-\infty,1))\cup\{b\}.
\]

Then $T\subset C_a,C_b$ and $C_a\cap C_b=T$; the point $a$ is excluded from $C_b$ because it is incomparable with $b$. If $T$ had a least closed moderate superset, it would therefore equal $T$.

Every closed pseudo-tronçon contained in $T$ has a greatest endpoint with both coordinates negative: a closed upper ray or all of $P$ cannot be contained in $T$, and the remaining closed interval, lower-ray or singleton cases have such an endpoint. Given finitely many endpoints $e^{(j)}$, let $m_i=\max_j e^{(j)}_i<0$. The point $(m_1/2,m_2/2)\in T$ has both coordinates above every endpoint and lies in none of the corresponding pieces. Thus $T$ is not a finite union of closed pseudo-tronçons and has no least closed moderate superset.

**Important distinction:** the unique presentation $T=C_a\setminus\{a\}$ does not make $C_a$ the least closed pseudo-tronçon containing $T$. Unique endpoint presentation and existence of a least hull are distinct assertions; the latter fails already in this Hausdorff example.

## PR-06 — The closed-square separator example needs a supremum qualification

**Verdict: the separator calculation is correct; using it to refute029 under its explicit supremum/infimum hypothesis would be incorrect.** In PR-03's order let $a=(0,0)$, $c=(1,1)$, $T=P_{<a}$, and $T'=P_{>c}$. A separator $x$ satisfying $t<x<t'$ for every $t\in T,t'\in T'$ is exactly an ordinary point of the closed square $[0,1]^2$: a negative coordinate fails against a lower-quadrant point nearer zero, a coordinate above one fails against an upper-quadrant point nearer one, and all coordinates between zero and one work.

The strong-order interval $[a,c]$ is only

\[
\{a,c\}\cup((0,1)\times(0,1)),
\]

which excludes the other edge points, so the two sets differ. **However, $T$ has no supremum in the strong order.** Its upper bounds include $a$ and $b=(0,1)$; if a least upper bound $s$ existed it would satisfy $s\le a$, while being an upper bound of the negative quadrant forces both its coordinates nonnegative. Hence $s=a$, but $a\not\le b$, a contradiction. Dually $T'$ has no infimum.

The square example therefore refutes an unrestricted extension from boundedness to endpoints-as-suprema, and exposes the unproved preceding inference; it does **not** satisfy029's inserted assumption “si $a=\operatorname{Sup}T,b=\operatorname{Inf}T'$”. The draft's author and root were notified, and agreed to qualify the claim.

## PR-07 — The separator formula with actual supremum and infimum is valid

**Verdict: valid in every poset, high confidence, with nonempty pieces and actual extrema as stated.** Suppose $T,T'$ are nonempty, every $t\in T$ is strictly below every $t'\in T'$, and $a=\sup T,b=\inf T'$ exist. Each $t'$ is an upper bound for $T$, so $a\le t'$ for every $t'$, hence $a\le b$. A strict separator $x$ is in particular an upper bound for $T$ and a lower bound for $T'$, so $a\le x\le b$; conversely every $x$ in that interval is a strict separator except that $x=a$ must be removed if $a\in T$, and $x=b$ must be removed if $b\in T'$.

Thus

\[
\{x:T<x<T'\}=[a,b]\setminus
\bigl((\{a\}\text{ if }a\in T)\cup(\{b\}\text{ if }b\in T')\bigr).
\]

If $a<b$, density makes this interval nonempty even when both endpoints are removed. If $a=b$, it is the singleton $\{a\}$ exactly when both pieces omit $a$, and is empty precisely in the raccordement case. This proves the order-theoretic separator formula behind029. Identifying its boundary as an intrinsic function of the subset still presupposes a coherent boundary definition; PR-02 shows why that separate issue cannot be ignored in the general reported class.

## PR-08 — Six-element horizontal-sum countermodel to clopen centrality

**Verdict: valid, high confidence, for the coherent intended axioms1–7 plus the marginal compatible-family Boolean condition.** Let

\[
M=\{0,1,a,a',b,b'\},\qquad
0<r<1\quad(r\in\{a,a',b,b'\}),
\]

with the four middle elements pairwise incomparable. Declare a pair compatible exactly when it lies in one of the four-element Boolean blocks $B_a=\{0,1,a,a'\}$ or $B_b=\{0,1,b,b'\}$. Complement exchanges $0,1$, $a,a'$, and $b,b'$, and take $M_f=M$, so closure is the identity.

- Axioms1–2 hold: bottom and top exist, and comparable elements always belong to a common block.
- Axiom3 and the marginal Boolean-family condition hold: every mutually compatible family lies in one block, whose meets, joins, complements and distributive laws agree with the corresponding operations in $M$. This includes arbitrary indexed compatible families, because the set of distinct values is finite.
- Axiom4 holds: for $a$, the compatible disjoint elements are $0,a'$, with largest $a'$, and likewise for the other middle elements; for $0$ the largest disjoint compatible element is $1$, and for $1$ it is $0$. Complement reverses order and preserves each compatibility block, including the mixed complement invariance.
- Axioms5–7 hold because every element is closed and closure is the identity; closure preserves every compatible join and all stipulated compatibilities.

Interior is also the identity, so every element is clopen. Nevertheless $a$ and $b$ are incompatible. Thus037's expectation that a clopen element is compatible with every $Y$ is not a consequence of these axioms. The subsequent sentence “S'il en est ainsi” remains valid as a conditional. Globally the six-element lattice is not Boolean, as witnessed by $a\wedge(b\vee b')=a\ne0=(a\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge b')$, without violating distribution inside compatible blocks.

**Qualification:** this is a countermodel to the coherent complement axiom $X'\wedge X=0$. The frozen doubtful alternative $X''\wedge X=0$, taken literally together with involutivity, forces $X=0$ for every $X$; that literal contradiction is a separate research reading issue and is not what this countermodel purports to satisfy. Axiom8's special concrete one-dimensional finiteness condition is not assumed:034 explicitly starts the abstraction with1–7.
