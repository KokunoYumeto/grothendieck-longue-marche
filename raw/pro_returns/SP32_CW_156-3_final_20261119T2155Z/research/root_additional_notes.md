### ROOT-02 — Scan 034, (a)–(c) — unproved claim checked in the total-order model

**Anchor:** “X est fini en tant que partie de L”. **Context/relation:** S3 §1.6 / SA21-R3-12 is confirmed with an explicit model-level proof; E 119/044 provides a contrast in dimension and scope. **RS:** MPL_150_044-084 Q9, thematic only. **Confidence:** high for a dense total order with more than one point, because each finite normal-form piece is either a singleton or has interior.

For the finite-interval algebra of a dense total order, every nondegenerate component contains an open subinterval, so an element with empty moderate interior has only singleton components and is finite. Conversely a finite union of singletons is closed and has empty interior, even when some singletons are ambient endpoints, and its moderate frontier is itself. Hence the three conditions on 034 are equivalent in this precise model, while ALG-03 explains why they do not follow from the abstract axioms alone.

### ROOT-03 — Scans 037–038, spectrum and finite qualification — own idea / hypothesis check

**Anchor:** “son spectre”; “Si l'alg. de Boole en question est finie”. **Context/relation:** S5 §4 reports the Stone-space issue at 156-5/017; the present qualification anticipates a distinction between spectrum points and elements of an algebra. **RS:** no exact entry; WG2 B15 is a possible later comparison, not a theorem used. **Confidence:** high for the elementary obstruction; no claim of historical priority.

The qualification “finie” is essential if components are to be actual minimal nonzero clopen elements: the Boolean algebra of finite unions of cylinder sets in `{0,1}^N` has no atoms, since every nonempty cylinder splits at a later binary coordinate. Its evaluations at infinite binary sequences nevertheless give distinct Boolean homomorphisms to `{0,1}`, hence spectrum points. Thus spectrum points cannot in general be replaced by minimal nonzero elements, even after universal compatibility of clopens is assumed.

### ROOT-06 — Scans 026–041, what is and is not an order-theoretic model — own methodological conclusion

**Anchor:** 034 “Considérons un ens ordonné M satisfaisant …”; 041 “Caractérisation”. **Context/relation:** E 119/034 calls for testing provisional axioms on examples; S3 §2 leaves general non-total validity open. **RS:** MPL_150_044-084 Q9 and WG2 B15, both retained at their supplied status. **Confidence:** high for this logical separation, because the counterexamples and conditional proofs test different hypotheses.

There are three distinct claims to retain: the finite-interval construction works on a dense total order; the proposed construction on every reported non-total tronçon has counterexamples; and the relative theorem works for an abstract structure that actually satisfies the strengthened axioms. The second failure does not refute the third theorem, while the third proof cannot establish that the manuscript's concrete model satisfies its assumptions. Keeping these layers separate is the main safeguard against turning a plausible formal calculation into an unsupported general result.
