# Main-reader local check: scan 015, p13 question

Scope: the ordinary category of small groupoids and functors. This check was developed from the definitions during reading; no outside source was consulted. It does not alter the wording of the question.

For a group G, write BG for its one-object groupoid. A subgroup inclusion H into G gives a monomorphism BH into BG: the induced functor is injective on objects and arrows, so equality after composition forces equality of the original functors.

Suppose that true: 1 into Omega were a subobject classifier. Let t be the selected object of Omega. A characteristic functor chi: BG into Omega for BH into BG must send the sole object of BG to t, since the pullback is nonempty. On arrows, chi is a homomorphism G into Aut_Omega(t). The strict categorical pullback of true along chi is the one-object groupoid of its kernel: an arrow g lies over the only arrow of 1 exactly when chi(g) is the identity. That kernel is a normal subgroup of G. An isomorphism of this pullback with BH over BG forces the included subgroup H to equal that kernel.

Take G=S_3 and H generated by the transposition (12). It is not normal: conjugating (12) by (123) yields (23), which is not in H. Therefore this monomorphism cannot be classified. The ordinary category of groupoids has no subobject classifier, a sufficient obstruction to being a topos. This supports the question's proposed explanation; it does not establish any larger claim about higher or homotopy categories of groupoids.

The proposed computation of pi_3(S^2) without the Hopf fibration was not performed in this check.
