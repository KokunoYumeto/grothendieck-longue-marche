## Legibility and limits

The late hand frequently reduces internal letters to a continuous wave. The least secure readings are the cancelled drafts and overlapping formula layers, particularly on156-4/076,079,081–082 and088, and the overwritten names, indices and old formula numbers on156-6/005. Clear uncancelled prose and the repeated ordinary order signs are generally more legible. A short reference or a covered final letter can remain doubtful even when its surrounding sentence is clear.

The current register contains 172 located departures fromC and 646 fromH; these counts overlap. They include alphabet, placement, cancellation and abbreviation readings, so they are not word-error rates or a measure of all tokens. H more often omits readable cancelled material, supplies expanded or regularized forms, and flattens vertical comparisons into horizontal typography. C preserves more of the manuscript layers but still has wrong words, symbols, attachments and cancellation states, including places it did not mark doubtful. The per-page apparatus identifies those locations.

Both witnesses are drafts from the same scans. Their agreement was not evidence: for example, the written empty-set glyph on156-4/075 and the literal unaccented definition/def forms on078 required departures from both. Conversely, an unexpected formula is preserved where the ink supports it; the notes explain the apparent mathematical intention separately. Unresolved readings remain marked, with the preferred reading first. No OCR, external mathematical source or TeX compilation was used.
