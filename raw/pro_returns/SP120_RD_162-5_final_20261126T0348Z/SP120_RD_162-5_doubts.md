## Legibility and witness comparison

The main typewritten lines on scans003–018 are generally legible. Their principal limitations are the copied right edge, worn or merged cells, x-mask cancellations, and small handwritten replacements. A plausible complete word is often less faithful than the visible clipped fragment. The bibliography on020 is comparatively clear; its apparent spelling errors are retained.

Cancelled underletters, overlapping correction layers, crowded additions, pale annotations, and rotated or clipped marginal writing require the most local qualification. The manuscript sheets022–024 add compressed words, detached accent/point marks, small indices and references crossing frames. The current report decisions identify which readings remain doubtful; a first-stage confidence statement does not override a later recorded adjudication.

C usually preserves more source-state detail than H, especially cancellations, native-edge fragments and unresolved layers. H sometimes supplies a better lexical or symbol candidate but also regularizes spelling, accents, abbreviations or incomplete wording. Agreement between the witnesses does not settle a reading. The records therefore distinguish selected-reading corrections, additions or retention of doubt, and differences of TeX representation. Confidence labels concern the local visible reading, not the truth of the mathematics.

The frozen first and second readings preserve the independent observations; the reports separately record root adjudication and audit status.
