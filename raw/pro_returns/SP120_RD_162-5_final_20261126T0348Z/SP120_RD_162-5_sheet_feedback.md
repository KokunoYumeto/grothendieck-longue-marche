## Recurrences and refinements for the next run

The following local patterns refine the supplied error categories and provide specific guidance for subsequent work.

- **A regular copy cutoff can look like a complete French ending.** The right edges of the typescript repeatedly remove final letters or divide a word across lines/pages (notably003–018). C and H sometimes supply those endings together. The native edge, not lexical probability, fixes what may be transcribed. Keep the clipped fragment even when the continuation is linguistically obvious.
- **A single typewriter cell may contain two ordered impressions.** Examples include the cell sequences on003–004,011,014,016–018. Treat a recoverable underletter as deleted before the standing letter. If the impression order cannot be seen, doubt the entire composite rather than only one proposed underletter. Adjacent doubled-looking live letters and x-mask cancellations are distinct cases.
- **Upper symbols corrected below the baseline need occurrence-by-occurrence reading.** Scans007–008 have cancelled superscripts and live replacement subscripts, while some upper o signs survive. A correction in one occurrence does not license changing every similar symbol.
- **The machine's baseline and raised dots carry distinct roles.** Scans013–018 use baseline covariant dots and raised contravariant/graded dots, with literal bang-like keyed forms in some combinations. Keep their height and do not infer a prime from expected notation. Keyed x and small round o must not create spurious macro-only correction counts.
- **Inserted words and their attachment are separate questions.** Curves beside chaines de (011), the annotations on014–015, the revisions on017, and the manuscript additions on023–024 are not parentheses. Record where the curve meets the base line independently of the words, and keep the words as ordinary TeX.
- **A source can use the wrong mathematical symbol clearly.** The displayed beta condition on023 and section X wedge Y on024 are preserved. Mathematical checking produces a note, not a repaired source formula. Repeated descending g on023–024 is a recognizable hand form; this differs from a clear source slip.
- **Retracing need not encode an earlier character.** The reinforced Theta and gamma contours on024 and uncertain typewriter-cell layers elsewhere do not justify an invented correction sequence. The first bottom frame's f-like corner remains locally doubtful.
- **Editorial apparatus is not manuscript writing.** H's explanatory notes can mention uncertainty or a graphic without transcribing it at the source locus. Such notes are not added to the page, and marked_in_H means an actual uncertainty/illegibility marker at that reading.

Correction totals count distinct recorded places, each assigned one principal kind. A place may contain several related letter or state corrections. Selected-reading corrections, uncertainty-only changes, placement or graphic changes, and representation differences must remain distinguishable for each witness. An uncertainty-only change for one witness does not erase a separate omission or placement difference in the other. Resolved original C marks remain separately identifiable in decisions.tsv and the changes log.

Audit boxes, unequal box strings and changes to a page are separate accounting units. Root may identify missing graphic strokes even where the two box texts agree, or find that a differing extraction did not require a change to the first-stage page. The blind-comparison files retain both independent readings in either case. Historical first-stage statements should be labelled or reconciled when a later adjudication changes the preferred reading, its source state or its uncertainty.
