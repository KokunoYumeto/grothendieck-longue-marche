\subsection{Lifting the remaining moving module through the entire two-adic homology tower}

\begin{statement}{D.50}{Proved}{The actual D.49 action lifts across its moving homology kernel}
Keep the actual groups, parameter and action of D.49. Thus
\[
 G=G_3,\quad |G|=n=576\cdot3^{97},\quad
 K_3=\ker(\widehat B\to G),\quad
 a=f_h\bmod G,\quad A=\langle a^G\rangle .
\]
The group \(A\) is the actual nonzero elementary abelian
three-subgroup of \(G\) defined there. Set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 U=K_3^{\rm ab,(2)}\simeq\mathbb Z_2^\rho,\qquad
 \rho=1+n/6,\qquad
 e_A=|A|^{-1}\sum_{b\in A}b,\\
 U^-=(1-e_A)U,\qquad
 w=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z_2}U^-
       ={n\over6}\left(1-{1\over|A|}\right)
       \ge {n\over9}>0 .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{D.50.1}
\]
The coefficient \(|A|^{-1}\) is a unit of \(\mathbb Z_2\).
For \(k\ge1\) define actual marked quotients
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathfrak K_k=\ker(K_3\to U/2^kU),\qquad
 \mathcal J_k=\widehat B/\mathfrak K_k,\qquad
 W_k=U^-/2^kU^-,\\
 \mathfrak K_\infty=\ker(K_3\to U),\qquad
 \mathcal J_\infty=\widehat B/\mathfrak K_\infty
                         =\varprojlim_k\mathcal J_k .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{D.50.2}
\]
In particular \(\mathcal J_1\) is D.49's
\(\mathcal J_{3,2}\), with \(W_1=W\). The exact images
of its remaining source kernel are
\[
 \operatorname{im}(\mathfrak R_h\to\mathcal J_\infty)=U^-,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{im}(\mathfrak R_h\to\mathcal J_k)=W_k.
 \tag{D.50.3}
\]
Consequently the full common marked quotients with
\(\mathcal W_h\) are \(\mathcal J_\infty/U^-\) and
\(\mathcal J_k/W_k\), respectively. The D.49 action
descends to them and is identity on the retained
finite base \(G\).

There is a continuous peripheral automorphism on
the strictly larger actual source
\[
 \mathcal Z_h
   :=\widehat B/(\mathfrak R_h\cap\mathfrak K_\infty)
    =\mathcal W_h
       \times_{\mathcal J_\infty/U^-}\mathcal J_\infty .
 \tag{D.50.4}
\]
It projects exactly to the complete D.49 action
and parameter. Its normalized conjugators are
compatible through every level \(2^k\), its multiplier
is one, and it fixes the original cusp \(t=sr\).
Every previously retained CM, Fermat, full metabelian
and full congruence comparison is therefore preserved.
The two projection kernels and full source kernel are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \ker(\mathcal Z_h\to\mathcal W_h)=U^-,\qquad
 \ker(\mathcal Z_h\to\mathcal J_\infty)
       =\mathfrak K_\infty\mathfrak R_h/\mathfrak R_h,\\
 \ker(\widehat B\to\mathcal Z_h)
       =\mathfrak R_h\cap\mathfrak K_\infty .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{D.50.5}
\]

The initial lift modulo two is an actual automorphism,
with generation proved. A first peripheral endomorphism
is obtained from the onto cusp map
\[
 W_1\oplus W_1\longrightarrow W_1,\qquad
 (b,c)\longmapsto(1-s)b+s(1-r)c.
 \tag{D.50.6}
\]
Its possible noninvertibility is corrected by the
explicit projective Fitting construction below.
After this first correction, every higher step has
exact common quotient
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal C_k
   =\mathcal J_k/(2^{k-1}W_k)
   =\mathcal J_{k-1}
       \times_{\mathcal J_{k-1}/W_{k-1}}
                 (\mathcal J_k/W_k),\qquad k\ge2,\\
 \ker(\mathcal J_k\to\mathcal C_k)
       =2^{k-1}W_k\simeq W_1 .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{D.50.7}
\]
This is the maximal common marked quotient of
\(\mathcal J_k\) and the already constructed joint
source at level \(k-1\). Cusp corrections in this
kernel are onto; the unchanged invertible reduction
modulo two proves generation at all higher levels.

The new full source kernel is still nonzero under
an explicit next finite map. Put \(V=U/2U\), and use
the actual class-two, exponent-four quotient
\[
 \mathcal T_2
   =\widehat B/
      \overline{\Gamma_3(K_3)(K_3')^2K_3^4}.
 \tag{D.50.8}
\]
Its kernel over \(G\) has abelianization
\((\mathbb Z/4)^\rho\) and commutator subgroup canonically
\(\bigwedge^2_{\mathbb F_2}V\). In that commutator subgroup,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 W_1\wedge V
 \ \subset\
 \operatorname{im}(\mathfrak R_h\cap\mathfrak K_\infty
                                  \to\mathcal T_2),\\
 \dim(W_1\wedge V)
     =w(\rho-w)+\binom w2>0 .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{D.50.9}
\]
The inclusion and its source elements are proved by
actual commutator maps below. Equality with the entire
remaining image, and lifting the retained action

Full profinite GT0 realization remains undecided.

The runnable certificate is a separate exact finite

The initial full kernel map has rank \(141\).

\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The actual common quotients and their actions.}
The averaging idempotent \(e_A\) is integral over
\(\mathbb Z_2\), is central in the \(G\)-action because
\(A\) is normal, and splits \(U\) into its invariant
and moving direct summands. Their reductions modulo
two are exactly the modules in D.49. The graph
calculation there gives the rank in (D.50.1).

Let \(H\) be the image of \(C_h\) in
\(\mathcal J_\infty\). It projects onto \(A\).
Commuting its lifts with the whole abelian kernel
\(U\) gives \([U,A]=(1-e_A)U=U^-\), so \(U^-\subset H\).
The quotient \(H/U^-\) is a central extension of
the finite odd group \(A\) by a pro-two group.
Finite coprime complements and compactness split
this extension; it is pronilpotent. Conversely
\([U^-,A]=U^-\), by averaging. In any pronilpotent
quotient of \(H\), the odd image of a complement
to its pro-two kernel commutes with the image of
\(U^-\), forcing the latter to vanish. Thus the
exact pronilpotent residual of \(H\) is \(U^-\).
D.49's residual-image argument proves (D.50.3);
reduction modulo \(2^k\) proves its finite version.
The products of the original two source kernels
give the asserted maximal common quotients.

The entire \(G\) quotient factors through
\(\mathcal P_{h,3}\), since the image of \(C_h\)
there is the three-group \(A\). It therefore has
identity action. The image of \(K_3\) in
\(\mathcal W_h\) is invariant. Taking its maximal
abelian pro-two quotient, or its abelian quotient
of exponent \(2^k\), is characteristic. Their
actual kernels are the images of
\(\mathfrak K_\infty\) and \(\mathfrak K_k\).
This proves action descent to the common quotients,
including their identity action on \(G\).

\emph{An actual peripheral endomorphism at the first level.}
Write \(J=\mathcal J_1\) and \(W=W_1\). Take the
images of the already retained normalized
conjugators in \(J/W\), and lift these elements
to \(A_J,\beta_J\) in \(J\). Their \(G\)-images
centralize \(r,s\), respectively, since the lower
action fixes \(G\). The two conjugates
\(\beta_Js\beta_J^{-1}\), \(A_JrA_J^{-1}\)
have the required orders; their product differs
from \(t\) by an element of \(W\).

Left corrections \(b,c\in W\) change that error
by exactly (D.50.6). This is an equality in the
abelian-kernel group, with no omitted quadratic
term. Its image is the augmentation image
\((1-s)W+(1-r)W\). Since \([W,A]=W\) and
\(A\subset G=\langle s,r\rangle\), the coinvariants
\(W_G\) vanish, and this map is onto. Choose a
correction killing the error. The resulting map
from \(\widehat B\) fixes the cusp exactly and
induces identity on \(G\). It sends \(K_3\) into
the abelian exponent-two kernel \(V\), hence kills
\(K_3'K_3^2\). It is therefore a well-defined
peripheral endomorphism \(\Phi:J\to J\), whose
quotient action on \(J/W\) is precisely the
retained automorphism. Generation is proved next.

\emph{The edge and vertex maps and projective Fitting part.}
Let \(S=\langle s\rangle\), \(R=\langle r\rangle\)
inside \(G\), and let \(\mathbb F_2[G]\) act on
the left. The actual Bass--Serre graph gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 0\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow C_1
 \xrightarrow{\partial} C_0
 \longrightarrow\mathbb F_2\longrightarrow0,\\
 C_1=\mathbb F_2[G],\qquad
 C_0=\mathbb F_2[G/S]\oplus\mathbb F_2[G/R],\qquad
 \partial e_g=e_{gS}+e_{gR}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{D.50.11}
\]
Choose discrete word representatives of the two
finite conjugators used for \(\Phi\). They define
a homomorphism of \(C_2*C_3\) inducing identity
on \(G\) and the specified map on \(J\).
If their \(G\)-images are \(b_0,c_0\), its vertex
maps are exactly
\[
       gS\longmapsto gb_0S,\qquad
       gR\longmapsto gc_0R.
 \tag{D.50.12}
\]
They are well-defined bijections: \(b_0\) centralizes
\(s\), and \(c_0\) centralizes \(r\). Their inverses
use \(b_0^{-1},c_0^{-1}\). These are \(G\)-module
automorphisms of both vertex modules.

For an explicit edge map, use the path cocycle
\(\mathfrak p\) with
\(\mathfrak p(s)=0\),
\(\mathfrak p(r)=e_1-e_r\).
The image of \(e_1\) is the path
\(\mathfrak p(A_J)-\mathfrak p(\beta_J)+e_{c_0}\),
from \(b_0S\) to \(c_0R\); translate it by \(g\)
for the image of \(e_g\). This defines a \(G\)-linear
endomorphism \(D:C_1\to C_1\) commuting with
\(\partial\) and the displayed vertex automorphism.
Writing \(\psi\) for the chosen word homomorphism,
the generator formulas give
\[
 D\mathfrak p(g)=\mathfrak p(\psi(g))
                         +(g-1)\mathfrak p(\beta_J).
\]
For \(g\in K_3\) the last term vanishes. Hence
the restriction to the actual cycle module \(V\)
is exactly the induced map \(T=\Phi|_V\), including
the basepoint transport.

Take the Fitting decomposition of the finite
edge module under \(D\):
\(C_1=P\oplus C_1^{\rm inv}\), with \(D\) nilpotent
on \(P\) and invertible on the other summand.
Both are \(G\)-submodules. If \(D^m v=0\), then
the invertible vertex map gives \(\partial v=0\).
Consequently \(P\subset V\), and it is exactly
the nilpotent Fitting part of \(T\). The quotient
map of \(T\) on \(V/W\) is invertible, so
\(P\subset W\). Most importantly, \(P\) is a
direct summand of the regular module \(C_1\),
and is therefore projective over \(\mathbb F_2[G]\).
Let
\[
                  e:C_1\longrightarrow P
 \tag{D.50.13}
\]
be the \(G\)-linear Fitting projection. Its
restriction to \(V\) is the exact projection
onto the possible noninvertible part of \(T\).

\emph{The explicit derivation and the cyclic norm correction.}
The path function \(\mathfrak p\) is a crossed
homomorphism to \(C_1\): its given values satisfy
the order-two and order-three relations, the
latter by telescoping. Its restriction to
\(K_3\), after abelianization modulo two, is
the actual inclusion \(V\hookrightarrow C_1\).
Thus
\[
          \delta_0=e\mathfrak p:\widehat B\longrightarrow P
 \tag{D.50.14}
\]
is an explicit derivation restricting to \(e|_V\).
It factors through \(J\), since its restriction
to \(K_3\) is additive of exponent two.

This also gives the full cohomological comparison.
Projective \(\mathbb F_2[G]\)-modules are summands
of finite sums of the regular coinduced module.
Its positive cohomology vanishes by the contracting
cochain resolution, and hence
\(H^1(G,P)=H^2(G,P)=0\).
The actual inflation--restriction sequence therefore
identifies
\[
                  H^1(\widehat B,P)
                     \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
                         \operatorname{Hom}_G(V,P).
 \tag{D.50.15}
\]
The concrete cocycle (D.50.14) realizes the
particular homomorphism needed here; existence
is not left as an unspecified cohomology class.

The order of the image of \(t\) in \(G=G_3\)
is \(m=36\), by D.26's exact cusp computation.
Since \(\Phi(t)=t\), the cycle
\(c=[t^m]\in V\) is fixed by \(T\), and hence
is killed by its nilpotent projection \(e\).
The crossed-homomorphism identity gives
\[
       (1+t+\cdots+t^{m-1})\,\delta_0(t)
                         =\delta_0(t^m)=e(c)=0.
 \tag{D.50.16}
\]
Restriction of \(P\) to \(\langle t\rangle\)
is still projective: the regular \(G\)-module
restricts to a free module for this subgroup.
For a free cyclic-group module the kernel of
the norm is the image of \(1-t\), directly
from its coefficient sums. This exactness
persists under direct summands. Consequently
there is \(b\in P\) with
\((1-t)b=\delta_0(t)\); no division by the
even cusp order is used.

Subtract the inner derivation \((1-g)b\), and
write \(\delta(g)=\delta_0(g)-(1-g)b\).
Then \(\delta(t)=0\), and, in characteristic two,
\[
 \delta(s)=(1-s)b,\qquad
 \delta(r)=(1-r)(e(e_1)+b),\qquad
 \delta|_V=e|_V.
 \tag{D.50.17}
\]
Define \(\Phi'(g)=\delta(g)\Phi(g)\) in the
abelian-kernel extension. It is a homomorphism
because \(\Phi\) is identity on \(G\), the
group controlling this module action.
The two displayed values change the conjugators
by left multiplication by \(b\) and \(e(e_1)+b\),
respectively. Thus the two torsion images remain
conjugates of the original torsion generators,
and the cusp stays exactly fixed. All corrections
lie in \(P\subset W\), preserving the complete
quotient action and conjugators modulo \(W\).

On \(V\) the new map is \(T+e\). It is \(T\)
on the invertible Fitting summand and \(T+1\)
on the nilpotent summand, so is invertible on
both. With identity on the finite quotient
\(G\), this proves \(\Phi'\) is an automorphism
of \(J\). This completes the initial generation
argument.

\emph{The exact higher common quotients and compatible lifts.}
Suppose the constructed level is \(k-1\).
Its joint source has full kernel
\(\mathfrak R_h\cap\mathfrak K_{k-1}\).
Because \(\mathfrak K_k\subset\mathfrak K_{k-1}\),
the image of that intersection in \(\mathcal J_k\)
is exactly
\[
  W_k\cap2^{k-1}(U/2^kU)=2^{k-1}W_k.
 \tag{D.50.18}
\]
Here equality of images follows from the actual
preimage property: an element of \(\mathfrak R_h\)
whose image in \(\mathcal J_k\) reduces to identity
already lies in \(\mathfrak K_{k-1}\).
The last equality uses the integral direct
summand \(U^-\subset U\).
This proves that the maximal common quotient
is the first description of \(\mathcal C_k\).
The two original quotient kernels in
\(\mathcal J_k\) have intersection \(2^{k-1}W_k\)
and sum \(W_k+2^{k-1}(U/2^kU)\).
Finite Goursat gives precisely the fibre product
in (D.50.7).

The already constructed automorphism on
\(\mathcal J_{k-1}\) and the D.49 action on
\(\mathcal J_k/W_k\) agree on the indicated
common quotient. Their conjugators agree there
as actual elements, not just conjugacy classes.
They therefore give an automorphism and
conjugators on \(\mathcal C_k\).
Lift those two elements to \(\mathcal J_k\).
The resulting cusp error is in
\(2^{k-1}W_k\simeq W_1\); the same onto map
(D.50.6) supplies left corrections in this
kernel. This preserves both previous conjugators
and their full D.49 common-coordinate values.

The corrected map sends \(K_3\) into the abelian
kernel of exponent \(2^k\), so defines an
endomorphism of \(\mathcal J_k\).
Its kernel matrix reduces to the already
invertible matrix of \(\Phi'\) modulo two.
Its determinant is therefore a unit modulo
\(2^k\), proving it is an automorphism.
This is the finite-module form of Nakayama's
criterion; no repeated generation assumption
enters the induction.

Choose these lifts successively. Their actual
conjugators, automorphisms and inverses are
compatible, so their limits give an automorphism
of \(\mathcal J_\infty\) and one pair of complete
conjugators. Closed Goursat and (D.50.3) identify
its joint source with (D.50.4). The two component
automorphisms agree on the complete common
quotient, so define an automorphism of that
actual fibre product.

The chosen conjugators form elements of the
fibre product because their reductions agree
at every level. Its parameter therefore projects
exactly to the D.49 parameter. That source
retains the entire loop abelianization, and
its full source kernel lies in \(\widehat F''\).
Every source preimage of the new parameter
thus belongs to \(\widehat F'\). This proves
the normalization, the two hexagons, and
retention of the prescribed CM data through
the original maps. The two fibre-product
projections give exactly (D.50.5).

\emph{The next class-two residual map is nonzero.}
The class-two exponent-four quotient of the
free group \(K_3\) has collected normal form
\[
       g_1^{a_1}\cdots g_\rho^{a_\rho}
          \prod_{i<j}[g_i,g_j]^{b_{ij}},
       \qquad a_i\in\mathbb Z/4,\quad b_{ij}\in\mathbb F_2.
 \tag{D.50.19}
\]
The commutator convention is
\([u,v]=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}\).
These coordinates are independent: multiplication
on \((\mathbb Z/4)^\rho\oplus
\mathbb F_2^{\binom\rho2}\) with the usual

all of them. It proves that the actual quotient
in (D.50.8) has the stated abelianization and
commutator module \(\bigwedge^2V\).
The defining subgroup is characteristic in
\(K_3\), hence normal in \(\widehat B\); this
is an actual finite source quotient.

Choose a basis \(u_i\) of the integral direct
summand \(U^-\). By (D.50.3), each has an
actual preimage \(z_i\in\mathfrak R_h\).
Its image in \(G\) is identity, so \(z_i\in K_3\).
For a basis vector \(v_j\) of \(U\), choose
an actual preimage \(x_j\in K_3\).
The commutator \([z_i,x_j]\) lies in
\(\mathfrak R_h\), by normality, and in

displayed finite commutator module is exactly
\(\bar u_i\wedge\bar v_j\).
These elements span \(W_1\wedge V\).
As \(W_1\) is a direct summand of \(V\),
its dimension is
\(w(\rho-w)+\binom w2\), proving (D.50.9)
and nontriviality of the remaining source
kernel by specified maps and elements.

\end{proof}
