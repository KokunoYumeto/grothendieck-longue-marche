# SP61_CW_75 — Hand and notation log

Every habit below is limited to the occurrences inspected on these ten sheets; exceptions are recorded rather than regularised.

## Established on scan 016

- **Wide cap on P.** The standalone torsor letter has a long horizontal head and a low loop, unlike the ordinary small superscript P. It holds at the product, map, key and hook on this page. A bar versus an extended letter stroke remains open; C proposed P/barred P/T, H used P. T is excluded by the loop. The later pages do not settle all attached caps; scan 017 has one securely detached bar. The remaining broad-cap forms keep their explicit doubt.
- **u can resemble a flattened x.** The NB has live u in `u.g=u` and the stabilizer line, while the first membership parenthesis has a clear crossed x. This is not a universal substitution: the two shapes coexist. C preferred x throughout; H read u but expanded the membership expression. Places: NB equation and `G_u (u∈B)`.
- **la resembles 2.** The tall joined l-a in “de la structure” has the silhouette of a numeral 2. It is a single occurrence here; H read “2 structures” and displaced the sentence.
- **Compressed words can retain only their ends.** In the foot addition, the middle of équivaut and the raised ending on applic overlap the corollary. This habit does not settle those readings. Both witnesses lose certainty here.

## Added on scan 017

- **Curled category C.** The first Isom subscript has a strongly inward upper curl and can look like e; the next two are open. Across these three occurrences the letter is C. The second reading qualifies the first pass: an inward curl does not establish a calligraphic alphabet. The first occurrence is `\unsure{C|\mathcal C}`, the next two plain C. C’s first candidate was e; H consistently supplied calligraphic C. This alphabet doubt also governs the category C family on scans 018–019 and 022.
- **P caps are not uniformly separable.** One bar above P is detached at the first Isom result; all other caps on scans 016–017 touch or nearly touch the letter. The one clear bar is retained explicitly. The P/barred-P ambiguity of the other forms is not claimed to be settled.
- **Cursive x can look like n.** In `(u,x)` and `(ug)(x)` the first crossing stroke is rounded; the clearer x in `u(x)` and `gx` fixes the repeated variable. This holds in the evaluation block only, not as a universal substitution. C marked `(ug)(x)` doubtful; both witnesses read x in the ordered pair.
- **Source bars versus arrowheads.** The leftward assignment to i_u has a straight transverse source bar at right, not a second arrowhead. H made it bidirectional. The evaluation connector has a small closing nub instead of a clear arrowhead and remains doubtful.

## Added on scan 018

- **Small G may look like C.** The initial in raised “G-ens.” and the initial of the interlinear automorphism identity have an open curved body with a downward right tail. The large G above is rounder and more closed. This favours G for the identity, while its C alternative is retained. C preferred C there; H supplied G. The G at the start of G-structure has the same short form.
- **Category alphabet remains uncertain.** Most category letters on this sheet are simple open C’s; the cursive “C étant” has a loop. Unlike the distinct double-stroke complex-field C, these shapes do not securely distinguish ordinary from calligraphic C. The second reading has qualified the earlier script interpretation on scan 017 accordingly: the first occurrence carries the alphabet doubt, and subsequent occurrences are plain C.
- **Two t’s abbreviate toutes.** The bracket ends ttes with compressed vowels absent; H expands it.
- **Connector variation on one line.** The tau row has a clear arrowhead. Both sigma-row connectors are blunt and bar-ended. C and H made both mapsto. This difference is visibly local; no general convention replaces all connectors by arrows.

## Added on scan 019

- **C and script E are distinct locally, while C versus calligraphic C is not.** The action-category names and Aut(X) superscript have a middle inward turn and lower lobe; nearby C’s are open curves. H repeatedly replaces the former by C or a circle. This does not settle the stylistic alphabet of the C family.
- **Colons can replace expected equalities.** After G in the subcategory paragraph, the marks are two vertical dots. The equality expected from context is not transcribed.
- **Source bars and blunt tips recur.** The lower solid diagonal begins with a transverse bar; the slanted margin element correspondence has a bar and a rounded, headless end. C and H turn these into a two-headed arrow and mapsto respectively. Other arrows on the same sheet have unmistakable pointed heads, so the distinction remains local.
- **Small zero can collapse to a short dark dash.** The margin’s C₀ has rounder zeros than the doubtful E₀ just below it. No global rule treats every low dot as an index.

## Added on scan 020

- **Tiny equals signs can have a curved upper stroke.** The sign in `D_n=Aut(P_n)` has two short extended strokes, unlike the two dots after G on scan 019. This is not a rule to read every pair of marks as an equality.
- **Raised abbreviation endings survive cancellations.** The old heading has Chang with raised mt; the NB direction probably has dr with raised te. H expands both. The second remains explicitly doubtful.
- **Overwriting preserves the earlier descender.** The g beneath the new d in the inserted “à dr” remains visible as a low loop. H reads the old direction as live.
- **Small product crosses may resemble wedges.** The two operators in the simplified contracted-product formula have a second crossing stroke. H writes wedge twice; the larger nearby products corroborate the local shape.

## Added on scan 021

- **Unit notation has two stems and a base.** The first 1 is double-stemmed; the next is written over an earlier G-like unit and separately underlined. H keeps only the earlier G in the proposition. This distinction is confined to the two unit signs on this page.
- **Source-barred connectors may end bluntly.** The raised g-to-minus-g label again has a transverse end; the underlying G-to-G shaft has a bead-like end. Pointed arrows elsewhere are still read separately.
- **Heavy cancellation does not imply every nearby word is cancelled.** The live section-7 insertion runs above the old sentence and continues below it. H treats much of the new example as cancelled. The overwritten X in the product shorthand was instead omitted by C.
- **Some blots cannot be recovered.** The word before que has no stable letter contour even after enlargement; it remains an illegible one-word deletion. This is not used as a model for the readable crossed disons or Soit.

## Added on scan 022

- **A vertical wavy equivalence mark is not a question mark.** The left-hand S-shaped stroke beside the downward arrow has no dot. C's tilde/question alternatives are replaced by wr; this observation concerns this one decoration.
- **A short final on can look like ve.** The page has involution, where C preferred involutive. The i dot and low rounded ending support on; no general replacement of other endings is made.
- **Category C varies in its loop.** The first prose C is more looped than the arrow-node C. One category is meant throughout this page; plain C is used with an explicit alphabet doubt at its first occurrence. This supplements the same caution on scans 018–019.
- **The wedge's group can be very low.** Here {±1} is a subscript, unlike H's superscript. Each actual vertical placement remains separately checked.

## Added on scan 023

- **Several interior letters flatten into one stroke.** Inversmt may be an abbreviation, whereas éléments may be full spelling; the flat medial strokes do not justify a general expansion rule. C marks these doubts; H expands inversement.
- **Arrow and relation signs must be read separately.** The first identification has a real left-pointing head. The last formula has crossed bars but no secure arrowhead. Their similar mathematical role is not used to standardize the ink.
- **Double diagonals mark Z here.** All three cyclic-group occurrences have doubled strokes, unlike the plain Z of the later abstract semidirect-product notation. H's bold alphabet does not reproduce this visible distinction.
- **Cancelled nouns are not replaced by a grammatical supply.** Tors is readable under cancellation, and no live ens follows it. H adds one.

## Added on scan 024

- **Abstract Z is single-stroke.** The semidirect-product group is written with a single diagonal at all live occurrences here; this is distinguished from the double-stroke cyclic group on scan 023. No automatic conversion to blackboard Z is made.
- **A tiny accented word can resemble punctuation.** Before celle de, the upper and lower marks are detached. The first pass preferred a colon, but the second reading compares the same reduced à forms on scans 018, 019 and 025 and prefers à, with colon retained as an alternative. This does not turn the clear colon after G on scan 019 into a word.
- **Overwriting keeps old factor names visible.** The live order Z, Γ is secure, with an earlier Z visible under the second factor. The first earlier capital is not secure enough to call a transposition without qualification.

## Added on scan 025

- **E may have a very short bottom arm.** The second ω_E initially resembles ω_F, but the crop shows a third arm. The upper ω_E has a more rounded form. Neither is replaced by F on grammatical expectation alone.
- **Punctuation on abbreviations is not automatic.** Dim, hom, ens, tors and the Aut qualifier quadr have no separate stop here; Isom's quadr. and bases orthon. do. H regularly adds stops. The same caution applies across the run.
- **Two detached ticks can stand for à.** The à before celle and before direction words is reduced almost to an accent and short lower stroke. This recurring form is relevant to the uncertainty on scan 024; it is not a rule for every pair of dots.
- **A smudge can imitate an earlier label.** The small dark group beneath det=s may be a cancelled sgn, but that is not certain. It is not used to infer a general cancellation habit.

## Cross-page audit conclusion

The second reading retained local distinctions in P caps, twists, product glyphs, alphabets and arrow decorations. It qualified the original calligraphic-C choice and used the recurring reduced à form to reorder the scan-024 doubt. No general handwriting rule was used to erase a clear local letter, prime, punctuation mark or cancellation. The notation table in the doubts file gives the final scope and exceptions.

The small pencilled numerals 15–24 are visually distinct from the body hand. Their attribution is left uncertain; they are treated as archive numbering, not as the author’s own page count.
