# Notation table

This table records the package-wide visual conventions used in the transcription. The whole-page and half-page readings were compared again before the transcription was frozen; differences between typescript and handwriting are retained where the ink differs. A notation convention is not an assertion that every doubtful instance has been resolved. The alternatives in the following doubts register remain part of the transcription.

| Double quotation marks | two backticks and two apostrophes, ` ``...'' ` | One encoding throughout both cotes, including the nested, unmatched quotation marks in 39/017. No guillemets have been substituted. |
| Exclamation-mark exponent | `^!` | 36/056: the small vertical stroke and dot after Hom are confirmed by the adjacent explicit words “l’exposant !”; not a prime. |
| Primes on maps, schemes and numbers | `j'`, `A'`, `X'`, `d'`, `u'_n` | Compared with exclamation marks and stars; primes in 36/054–058, 062–064, 074–084 and 39/004–020 remain primes. The first correspondence label on 36/077 is ξ′; later ξ₀ remains indexed. |

# Legibility

All 49 whole-page images and all 98 half-page images were read visually, followed by a second page-by-page comparison and a package-wide notation review before the transcription was frozen. No OCR was used. The reused typescripts are generally clearer than the handwriting, although their ornate mathematical fonts sometimes remain ambiguous. The handwritten pages have compressed endings, very small indices, faint blue ink, interlinear insertions and multiple layers of cancellation. The least secure passages are the cancelled divisibility proof on 36/067, the compressed marginal and draft arguments on 36/069–071, the marginal category/module notes on 39/011, the overwritten functor notation on 39/015, and the multiply cancelled preparation proof on 39/019–020. Several formulas and phrases remain surprising even where their strokes are clear; they were not corrected to make the mathematics work. Explicit doubt marks are a candid record of the remaining reading risk, not an excuse to omit a passage. Dense diagrams were transcribed object by object and arrow by arrow; very small heads, indices and decorations remain less secure than the main objects. Long descenders were compared against genuine underlining. Structural checks establish balanced LaTeX syntax and complete page/inventory coverage, but do not certify a reading or replace a real TeX compilation; the available TeX installation lacked its formats and required packages.
