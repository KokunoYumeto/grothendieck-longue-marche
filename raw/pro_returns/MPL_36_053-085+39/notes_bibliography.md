## Primary bibliography and comparison limits

These sources were checked after the freeze, only for research notes. They supply comparison targets and definitions; none was used to rewrite the transcription. The cited original SGA pages were also inspected visually. No copy of these sources is included in the deliverable.

**P1. Raynaud, Michel (1970). _Spécialisation du foncteur de Picard_. Publications Mathématiques de l’IHÉS, 38, 27–76.** [Original article and record](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1970__38__27_0/); [original PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1970__38__27_0.pdf). Relevant locators: §6.1.3, §6.1.11, Theorem 6.4.1, §8.1.2 and Theorem 8.1.4, printed pp. 48–50, 56, 64–66. These concern vertical divisors, Picard-group torsion and the curve/Néron comparison. The group involving $P^\tau$ is not automatically the kernel of the manuscript’s full Néron–Severi map. The two 1967 C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris announcements, 264, 941–943 and 1001–1004, are bibliographically attested but their original pages were not checked; the manuscript’s “th. 3” and “prop. 7(ii)” remain unidentified.

**P2. Grothendieck, Alexander, and Raynaud, Michel (1972, publisher attribution). _Modèles de Néron et monodromie_, SGA 7 I, Exposé IX, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 288, 313–523.** [Publisher chapter, DOI 10.1007/BFb0068694](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/BFb0068694); [original volume](https://library.slmath.org/nonmsri/sga/sga/pdf/sga7-1.pdf). Theorem 2.4 is on [printed p. 338](https://wstein.org/sga/sga/7-1/7-1t_338.html), and Remark 2.6(a) on [pp. 341](https://wstein.org/sga/sga/7-1/7-1t_341.html)–[342](https://wstein.org/sga/sga/7-1/7-1t_342.html). The theorem gives the toric part as the intersection of the fixed part with the orthogonal of the dual fixed part over a henselian trait, without a semistability hypothesis. The remark separately identifies the arithmetic weight argument for the inclusion.

**P3. Grothendieck, Alexander (1972). _Compléments sur les biextensions. Propriétés générales des biextensions des schémas en groupes_, SGA 7 I, Exposé VIII, LNM 288, 218–312.** [Publisher chapter, DOI 10.1007/BFb0068693](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/BFb0068693). Theorem 7.1(b), [printed p. 300](https://wstein.org/sga/sga/7-1/7-1t_300.html), gives the component-group obstruction to extending a generic biextension for smooth commutative groups over a trait when one component group has bounded torsion. Its vanishing is the extension criterion; perfectness is a separate issue. This is the precise comparison used for N57 and N97.

**P4. Bosch, Siegfried, and Lorenzini, Dino (2002). _Grothendieck’s pairing on component groups of Jacobians_. Inventiones Mathematicae, 148, 353–396. DOI 10.1007/s002220100195.** [Original published paper on the author’s university site](https://dinolorenzini.franklinresearch.uga.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/papers/LorenziniBosch.pdf). Section 1 treats the abstract symmetric-matrix pairing; Theorem 4.6, p. 379, identifies the geometric pairing for a smooth proper geometrically connected curve with a rational point and a proper flat regular model over a strictly henselian DVR. Corollary 4.7 distinguishes prime-to-characteristic perfectness and the stronger conclusion under geometrically reduced components. This later work is a comparison, not evidence that the manuscript had supplied its proof.

**P5. Chiu, Christopher, and Hauser, Herwig (2021 preprint; published 2022). _Isosingular loci of algebraic varieties_. Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra, 226, 107131. DOI 10.1016/j.jpaa.2022.107131.** [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12961). Lemma 1.2, printed p. 3, states the formal inverse criterion over an arbitrary coefficient ring for substitutions with zero constant terms. It is a comparison for the ambient formal inverse step, not a theorem asserting closure inside an arbitrary chosen subfamily $\mathcal A_*$.

**P6. The Stacks Project. Section 15.11, _Henselian pairs_, tag 09XD.** [Section and exact definition](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09XD); [equivalent criteria, tag 09XI](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09XI). Definition 15.11.1 requires the radical condition and lifting of monic coprime factorizations. Lemmas 15.11.2 and 15.11.4 give the locally nilpotent and adically complete cases. These supply a definition against which N75 and N78 can be checked; they do not say that every family in the manuscript is noetherian or complete.

**P7. Strickland, Neil P. _Formal schemes and formal groups_.** [Author’s text](https://strickland1.org/vietnam/formal.pdf), §5.2, Definition 5.13, Lemma 5.14 and Corollaries 5.15–5.16, printed p. 40 (PDF viewer p. 41). The nilpotent lower-coefficient version supplies division and preparation over a general commutative ring, with no noetherian or local assumption.

**P8. Strickland, Neil P. _Functorial Philosophy for Formal Phenomena_, preliminary draft.** [Original draft](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/Strickland-fpfp.pdf), Definition 5.1, printed p. 10, and Theorem 33.1–Corollary 33.2, printed pp. 54–55. The definition uses a complete linear topology, and the preparation result uses topologically nilpotent lower coefficients. This directly addresses the topological scope of 39/019; its draft status is retained.

**P9. Moret-Bailly, Laurent (2023). _A henselian preparation theorem_. Israel Journal of Mathematics, 257, 519–531. DOI 10.1007/s11856-023-2545-1.** [Author text, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.13552v4). This is a general henselian-pair preparation statement for the henselized series ring $R\{t\}$, which must be distinguished from all of $R[[t]]$. Section 2.2.2 restricts evaluation to the appropriate henselian ideal. The discussion of O’Malley’s formal theorem in §1.2 is a useful lead, but O’Malley’s original article was not independently checked here.

### What has and has not been identified

The arithmetic orthogonality method has a specific verified match in P2; the general biextension obstruction has one in P3; the preparation statement has close general-ring and topological comparisons in P7–P9. P1 supplies relevant Picard ingredients, but no independently verified printed statement of the manuscript’s **entire** finite invariant-cycle sequence, with all its amended hypotheses and $p$-primary terms, is claimed here. The reused Reynolds/quotient and Haar-measure typescripts have not been assigned an author or an edition from these leaves alone. Those are explicit bibliographic limits, not evidence that their statements are new.
