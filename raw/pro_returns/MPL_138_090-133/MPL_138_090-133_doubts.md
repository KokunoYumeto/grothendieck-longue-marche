## Legibility assessment

The ink is often clear at stroke level, but rapid cursive, overwritten symbols and abbreviated words create reading uncertainty. Explicit doubt marks record these limits; a plausible mathematical interpretation has not been used to remove a visual doubt. All 43 retained scans received a first transcription pass and a separate second visual comparison by the same primary reader; no OCR or parallel transcription was used. The earlier reading table is an authored selection of difficult places; the exhaustive locator supplement below covers every uncertainty macro in the final page files.

The least secure passages are the overlapped cancellations and margin on 93–100, the short heading on 97, the numerical and polynomial overwriting on 106–112, ink-obscured passports on 120, the marginal and final phrases on 122–125, and the stained revisions on 132. Scan 127 mixes densely placed calculations, graphs and pictorial doodles; the TikZ drawings are hand-built approximations of their visible geometry, and fine incidental pen strokes are less trustworthy than the graph connectivity and written labels. The typed sheets 106,110,133 are more legible, apart from transfer marks and signatures.

Local uncertainty remains after audit. Mathematical plausibility has not been treated as evidence for a reading: examples include the discriminant roots on 111, the rightward limit arrow on 123, S¹ on 125, the small denominator on 127, field labels on 130, and n≥2 on 131. Any later mathematical correction or context-suggested reading belongs only in the research notes. No claim of error-free transcription or pixel-exact drawing reproduction is made.
