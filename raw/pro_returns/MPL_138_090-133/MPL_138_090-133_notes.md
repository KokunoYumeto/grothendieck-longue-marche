**Note:** Oranges of order at least3 provide further failures of uniqueness, so the list is not complete. The words “il semble” matter: this is a tentative inference resting on the preceding false orange claim. The page itself does not retract it.
**Context/status:** Hua §15 already rejects the orange premise; OP33–OP34 give the bounded check.


### OP36 — The infinite conformal argument is expressly unfinished

**Place/kind:** 138, scan 131, lines 13–16; self-flagged.
**Source:** “tirer au clair entièrement … voir s'il s'applique aux cartes infinies”.
**Note:** Both the conformal argument and its extension to infinite maps are named as remaining work. The finite proof in OP31 uses a finite analytic boundary and cannot simply be extended without local finiteness, prime-end and completeness hypotheses. No infinite-map classification is claimed here.
**Context/status:** Hua §15 repeats the reservation; LM4 C.12 is a distinct later infinite-operator problem and remains open.


### OP37 — Additional centres remove the displayed continuous freedoms

**Place/kind:** 138, scan 131, lines 18–22; unproved claim.
**Source:** “centres des faces”; “II et III, mais non le cas I”.
**Note:** For the explicit oranges, face centres or edge midpoints lie on the unit circle and force a positive dilation to be trivial. For I, one marked edge midpoint still leaves positive dilations, while the vertex and two face centres give three fixed points and force a Möbius map to be the identity. This checks the displayed examples, not a general uniqueness theorem for arbitrary marked infinite maps.
**Context/status:** Hua §15 makes the same observation; proofP4; related to the normalization issue in 138-operations-belyi-extending.


### OP38 — The holomorphic-preservation conditions are not declared equivalent

**Place/kind:** 138, scan 132, lines 11–27; unproved claim.
**Source:** “Considérons les conditions”.
**Note:** Conditions(a), (b) and(c) distinguish existence of a compatible representative, isomorphism of the canonical surfaces, and a common complex structure also compatible with their supremum graph. The page lists them but does not state or prove their equivalence. In particular the simultaneous-graph condition is extra data and cannot be suppressed.
**Context/status:** Hua §15, “Opérations holomorphes”, likewise says the implications are unfinished; 138-operations-belyi-extending.


### OP39 — Equality of holomorphic automorphism groups is an extra demand

**Place/kind:** 138, scan 132, line 29; unproved claim.
**Source:** “a') b') c') … mêmes groupes d'autom. hol.”
**Note:** The primed variants add preservation of automorphism groups to preservation of the surface or realization. A new graph on the same Riemann surface can have a different symmetry subgroup, so those variants need separate arguments. No verification of all these variants is supplied on the sheet.
**Context/status:** Hua §15 lists the operator variants; compare MM's distinction between monodromy and map automorphisms; 138-operations-belyi-extending.


### OP40 — The special-map condition does not always fix 1

**Place/kind:** 138, scan 132, lines 31–40; reading question.
**Source:** “1 en {0,∞,1}”; “φ(1)=1 implique … étale en1”.
**Note:** The frozen condition allows1 to map to0 or∞ and imposes an étale condition only when it maps to1. Hua's summary says it fixes1, a materially narrower formulation. The doubtful exception in the index inequality and the allowed rounded endpoint must also be kept visible.
**Context/status:** Hua §15, last subsection; 138-operations-belyi-extending; proofP2 clarifies the local alternatives.


### OP41 — Strict preservation needs exact local indices

**Place/kind:** 138, scan 132, lines 33–38; own idea.
**Source:** “ramification ≤2”; “étale en1”.
**Note:** For strict input maps, composition is strict if φ sends0 and∞ away from1, is unramified at 1 when φ(1)=1, and has index exactly2 at every other point above1. The inequality at most2 alone permits unwanted unramified points unless the surrounding graph condition excludes them. This isolates a concrete condition to check in any proof of the proposed implications.
**Confidence/reason:** high for the sufficient criterion and the inequality caveat; proofP2 applies multiplicativity of ramification.
**Context/status:** Refines Hua §15 and 138-operations-belyi-extending without altering scan 132.


### OP42 — Wood supplies the requested later comparison

**Place/kind:** 138, scans 124–125 and128; unpursued implication.
**Source:** “(φf)⁻¹([0,1])”.
**Note:** Wood's Belyi-extending maps are Q-defined three-point sphere maps preserving {0,1,∞}; postcomposition gives Galois-equivariant operations. Her §3.1–3.3 gives the relevant comparison, including a combinatorial construction. The manuscript's extra index-two and endpoint requirements delimit a more specialized cartographic setting.
**Context/status:** Credits Hua's registered lead 138-operations-belyi-extending; fills the reference gap specifically requested in README and RESULTS_STATE; Wood, 2006, §3.


### OP43 — Arithmetic equivariance requires an arithmetic condition

**Place/kind:** 138, scans 124 and132; own idea.
**Source:** “application holomorphe … spéciale”.
**Note:** The identity σ(φ∘f)=(σφ)∘(σf) proves equivariance when φ is defined overQ, and guarantees G_K-equivariance when it is defined overK without excluding extra equivariance. The topological branch-point conditions on these pages do not supply that rationality. Thus “all operations commute with Γ_Q” is not justified by the manuscript's composition argument alone.
**Confidence/reason:** high; direct coefficient conjugation, proofP7.
**Context/status:** Wood §3.1, Proposition 2, supplies the later rationality hypothesis; bears on 138-operations-belyi-extending and EXIT_TOWER E.24.


### OP44 — The sextic family gives a concrete non-equivariant operation

**Place/kind:** 138, scans 107–112 and124; own idea.
**Source:** “P(c)=0”; “φf”.
**Note:** For the three certified sextics f_c, the clean outer maps β_c=4f_c(1−f_c) are distinct conjugate sphere maps preserving the branch set. Equality of their cleaned covers would force equality or a target swap of the original sextics; their unequal0/1 passports exclude the swap, and normalization fixes the remaining source Möbius map. Evaluating the corresponding operation on the reference identity cover shows that fixing a nonrational β_c need not commute with Galois.
**Confidence/reason:** high within the folded-edge reference category; proofP7 uses the exact AR normalization and a factored quadratic identity.
**Context/status:** Links Hua §13's sextic to §15's operations, a link not made there; refines 138-operations-belyi-extending rather than settling EXIT_TOWER E.24.


### OP45 — Operation invariants can separate orbits, with a limited conclusion

**Place/kind:** 138, scans 103 and124; own idea.
**Source:** “déterminer l'opération”; “φf”.
**Note:** If an operation Ω commutes with Galois and I is a Galois invariant, then I∘Ω is another invariant, so unequal values certify different orbits. Wood's Proposition 4 gives an explicit later example of this separation. No completeness of such invariants is proved here, and they cannot distinguish the three sextics as different orbits because those sextics form one orbit.
**Confidence/reason:** high for the formal implication; proofP8 gives it, while Wood's example is used as a cited result rather than recomputed here.
**Context/status:** Responds directly to RESULTS_STATE's operation/orbit question; Wood §3, Proposition 4; EXIT_TOWER E.24 remains open.


### OP46 — “Operation” is not a single commuting class in later literature

**Place/kind:** 138, scans 103 and121–132; own idea.
**Source:** “τz=z̄”; “‘Opérations’”.
**Note:** On the sextic S3 orbit, mirror reversal acts as a transposition and does not commute with a3-cycle from Galois. Jones, Example 7.7, explicitly makes this noncommutation comparison for the associated regular covers and the wider Out(F₂) operation group. This does not contradict rational Belyi-extending equivariance; it shows why the manuscript's spherical constructions must not be equated without proof with every later use of “operation”.
**Confidence/reason:** high for the finite-group distinction; the noncommuting permutations are elementary and the literature comparison is explicit.
**Context/status:** Hua §15 and 138-operations-belyi-extending concern a particular construction; Jones Example 7.7 supplies the later contrast, not a manuscript claim.


### SR12 — The classification theorem is an invoked input, with a finite-case meaning

**Place/kind:** 138, scan 129, lines 17–23; unproved claim using background classification.
**Source:** “Par le th. de classification”; “un unique isomorphisme de cartes, isotope à l'identité”.
**Note:** In the finite setting the relevant input is the classification of covers of the punctured sphere by monodromy, with the local fillings and cartographic incidence data retained. An isomorphism over the fixed complex sphere is holomorphic off the branch fibres and extends across them by the local power-map model; uniqueness in the identity component also uses the absence of a finite-order kernel, as in OP22. The source does not identify the theorem's full hypotheses here, and this explanation supplies no infinite-cover classification without extra assumptions.
**Context/status:** Esquisse §3, scans 119/13–14, explicitly states the finite cartographic/cover dictionary; Hua §15 invokes the same reduction; LM4 C.12 remains a different open comparison. The general classification is carried as background, not re-proved in this package.

## Reproduction, check boundaries and transcription integrity

The main exact calculations are reproducible by running, from the extracted
package directory, the following scripts:

~~~sh
python3 records/arithmetic_certificate.py
python3 records/arithmetic_supplement.py
python3 records/check_maps_moduli.py
python3 records/operations_certificate.py
~~~

The arithmetic scripts use SymPy; the finite-permutation map script uses
the Python standard library. The accompanying JSON files retain the
successful results even if a reader has no runtime installed. The longer
arguments are in records/maps_moduli_checks.md,
records/maps_moduli_supplement_checks.md and records/operations_proofs.md.
These scripts compute research checks; the historical page-writing scripts
are preserved as work records and should not be rerun over the frozen files.

Peer checks are documented in records/operations_peer_review.md and
records/b6_arithmetic_peer_review.md. The latter found an overly strong
“only G_K” phrase and a section-number slip in a draft research note;
both were corrected in the notes, with the source untouched. Coverage
review found several omitted research dispositions, now SR01–SR12.
These reviews are bounded checks, not an independent re-transcription.

The exact frozen hashes are in records/frozen_hashes.json and progress.log.
The final integrity confirmation is appended to progress.log after
assembly. Structural validation does not prove that every difficult word
or drawn curve has been read correctly; the doubt apparatus and the
explicit limits on catalogue identification remain part of the deliverable.
No source image or context file is included in this package. No compiled
PDF is claimed: the available TeX installation lacked the required format
and class/package files, so the saved source received structural checks
rather than a successful TeX rendering.
