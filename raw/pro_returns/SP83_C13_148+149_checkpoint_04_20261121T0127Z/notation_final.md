The table consolidates recurring forms after comparison of the page readings and their ink evidence. It does not turn a local ambiguity into a general rule. The hand log retains the fuller observations, occurrence-specific exceptions and witness errors. Abbreviation expansions appear separately below and are never substituted in the transcription.

|---|---|---|
| `\phi` | ϕ : l'ensemble vide. An oval with a long descending crossing stem. | 148: 049, 056, 058, 062, 064, 071. The clear occurrences use this source-shaped rendering consistently. |
| `\gamma` | A low loop with a descending return, sometimes with a long right tail; it can resemble a cursive y, o or sigma-like form. | 148: 048, 049, 053, 067. Formula-local indices and doubtful overwritten forms remain separately assessed. |
| `\sigma` | Small left loop joined to a long rightward arm. A prime is a separate raised stroke. | 148: 048, 049, 052, 059. The crowded sigma/star exponent on 049 and o/sigma downward-arrow label on 052 retain local doubts. The clear cancelled sigma on 059 does not resolve the overworked insertion on 048 by itself. |
| `\Pi` | Two tall uprights and an upper bar distinguish capital Pi. | 148: 065, with subscript 1; 149: 003–004, with field subscripts. The small indices are read separately. |
| `\mathbb Z` | Doubled diagonal Z, sometimes heavily retraced. | 148: 050, 057, 061, 067. The alphabet can be secure while its neighbouring divisor or index remains doubtful. |
| `\mathbb N` | The repeated natural-number target alphabet is kept consistent in the weighting and counting maps. | 148: 048–050, 052, 056, 067. Indices, map labels and handwritten finite numerals remain separate readings. |
| `\mathbb D_\infty` | Doubled upright in D; infinity below the baseline. | 148: 051. The cancelled D-alphabet candidate on 052 remains doubtful; unrelated live D letters are not doubled by this rule. |
| `l` / `\ell` | Looped lowercase l for a free-edge or free-circle index. These two TeX renderings denote the same handwritten letter. | 148: 051, 052, 055, 061. It is distinct from clear numeric 1; damaged local indices retain doubts. |
| `p` / `\rho`; live `r` over old ink | A bowl on a descending stem can leave p/rho unresolved. A short dark shoulder can identify a live r without identifying the old descending ink beneath it. | 148: 050 map names retain p/rho alternatives; 048 preserves doubtful cancelled underlying ink. |

No universal substitution is imposed. |
| `\pm` | Two horizontal bars with a short upright, sometimes resembling capital I. | 148: 051, 057, 063, 065, 071. Clear occurrences agree; the cramped target operator on 063 remains explicitly doubtful. |
| `\vec a`, `\vec A`, `\vec R`, `\vec{\underline R}` | A pointed right end distinguishes a vector arrow from a smooth tilde. A separate lower line is retained only where drawn. | 148: 050 has vector a/A; 051 has the additional R underline; 055 has vector R without silently importing that underline. |
| `\underline r` | Short centred bar below r, without an independent lower-right a. | 148: 061. This resolves the local r-a/plain-r disagreement only. |
| `\widetilde\Sigma`, `\widetilde R`, `\Gamma` | A separated upper stroke is an accent; a connected lower foot belongs to the letter. | 148: 050, 062, 064. The Sigma/tilde-Sigma ambiguities in the cancelled lower square on 050 and on 064 are retained. A retraced Gamma foot on 062 is not converted into an underline. |
| `S\mathcal T` and related category forms | S and calligraphic T can overlap. Raised prefixes, cancelled prefixes, D replacements and literal changes of letter order are preserved in position. | 148: 052, 062–065, 071. The final TS on 071 is not repaired to the earlier TD or STD. |
| `\subset`, `\in`, `\cup`, `\amalg` | Read the open curve, base and prongs at each occurrence. The disjoint-union form on 055 has two uprights on a long base. | 148: 050, 052, 055, 056, 071. The relation and second index in the crowded 056 formula remain doubtful. |
| `\unsure{\wedge|\cap}` | A sharply pointed connector is retained as wedge with intersection as an alternative; its set-theoretic role does not settle the graphic form. | 148: 055 containment condition. Other clear intersection signs keep their own readings; the torseur wedge on 050 is a separate construction. |
| `=` as a word abbreviation | Two horizontal bars stand for même in phrases such as de =. | 148: 055, 061, 063, 067, 071. The separate equality/cancelled-ou group on 061 and la/= cluster on 071 retain their own alternatives. |
| `\Vert` as vertical equality | Two short upright parallel strokes express equality between vertically arranged objects. | 148: 050, 062, 071. Their vertical position is preserved instead of flattening them into a baseline relation or a downward arrow. |
| Comparison and extended arrows | Shaft count, direction, hooks, intermediate heads and upper wavy strokes are independent visible features. | 148: 048 has a left-pointing comparison with a wavy upper stroke; 053 has an intermediate and terminal head on one extended shaft; 067 has a double-shaft equivalence. |
| Short map strokes | A blunt downturned nib is not by itself a separable arrowhead. | 149: 004 has blunt strokes in the Spec map and displays (1), (3), but a detached rightward head in display (2). The mathematical role does not supply a missing head. |
| Primes and raised indices | Separate short raised strokes remain distinct from bars, lower indices and cancellation marks. | 148: 053, 057, 059, 067; 149: 003–004. The six overworked target marks on 149:004 retain cancelled-prime/live-star alternatives; the actual layers are not claimed resolved. |
| `c_i^0`, `c_i^1`; literal `\pi(j)` | The open c is distinguished from the closed a and tall b elsewhere in the same drawing. The descending j remains j even when the argument suggests another index. | 148: 067. The eight boundary labels use the visible c forms; the possible index slip is discussed only in the notes. |
| `E_{\bar K/K}` and `\Pi_K` | Plain capital E; bar over the first K only in the displayed quotient index. | 149: 003–004. Repeated agreement of the witnesses was checked against each visible occurrence. |
| Handwritten numbers | A flagged 1 can have a long upper entry and lower foot; 7 may be crossed; a small 2 can have only a shallow upper curve and short return. | 148: 049, 067, 072. Folio numbers and overwritten formula numbers are read individually; these habits do not resolve the uncertain marks on 149:003–004. |
| Numeric labels in drawings | 0, 1, infinity and local branch weights retain their own positions and enclosures. | 148: 069, 072. Obscured labels on 069 remain doubtful. On 072 the separated lower 1 and 9 at the upper vertex are not combined into 19. |
| Printed zero and letter O | Fixed-width numeric-zero patterns and the O in PREDONTA are distinct printed forms. Spaces form part of the printed arrangement. | 148: 068. The isolated terminal N and original row offsets are preserved. |
| Final n, e and s | A long low n can end in a downward flick; a final hook need not be an extra e or s. A plural ending requires its own visible run. | Recurrent on 148: 048–052, 056–059, 063–065. Local de/des and other unresolved endings remain doubtful. Grammar does not settle them. |
| Accents and compact à | A detached short accent can make a compact a resemble punctuation or i.e. Accent slant alone does not distinguish acute from grave. | 148: 051, 052 and 055 retain local à/colon alternatives. 149: 003 has compact à twice and the written réconstitue; 004 has réconstituer. Other source spellings and visible missing points are preserved locally. |
| Short forms and category abbreviations | Points, spaces, raised endings and omitted letters belong to the written form. | Examples include Gra bipond; Gr.pndrep / Grpondrep / Gr.pond.rep. / Grbipond; gr.poïde-; Gr.pond top; p with raised ts; tte; ens. The differing occurrences on 148:048, 052–053, 062, 064 are not made uniform. |
| Raised quotation marks | Paired raised ticks are set as two backticks and two apostrophes; guillemets require drawn angular marks. | The quoted clauses and revised names are retained in their local source form, including 148:071 and 149:004. |
| Insertion leaders and crossing descenders | A curved leader attaches raised text to a gap. A live replacement's descender may cross an old cancellation without making the replacement struck. | Recurrent on 148:053, 057–059, 061, 064–065, 071 and 149:003–004. Text and attachment are decided separately. |
| Cancellation returns and boundaries | A returning strike can resemble punctuation; an open rule or bracket need not be a letter or a fully closed enclosure. | 148:055 retains doubtful terminal commas; 061 preserves open marginal boundaries; 063 preserves the source attachment bracket; 071 preserves open enclosure joins. |
| Drawing layers and retracing | Grey pencil, darker retracing, red contours, marked vertices and local gaps remain separate visible features. A doubled physical trace need not add an endpoint incidence. | 148:068–069, 072. The pale background under 071 is excluded as show-through. The graph and contour corrections are documented with their evidence crops. |
