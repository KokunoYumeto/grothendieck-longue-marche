## Legibility and limits

The ordinary prose and many large formulae are readable, but this is a working notebook, with rapid writing, overlapping cancellations, changed indices, thin arrowheads and very compressed margins. The least trustworthy passages are the opening corollary (002), portions of the cancelled exploratory sheets (003 and 027), the diagonal Lawvere-interval note (016), the small product/closure qualifications (010–014 and 020), the cancelled clause on 038, the overwritten coefficient and bars on 046, and especially the lower gutter/bottom notes on 049 and the damaged right margin and revised five-lemma discussion on 052. Long tentative phrases in those places must not be treated as secure quotations.

The printed material is generally more legible than the handwriting, but its machine identifiers, literal banner spacing, clipped columns and O/0 or I/1 distinctions introduce a different kind of uncertainty. Scan 037 ends with a physically clipped row; no missing identifier was reconstructed from another occurrence. Dates in printer output date the output, not automatically the manuscript written on the reused sheet. Show-through from the other face was not transcribed as an additional mirrored text layer.

Every supplied whole image and both supplied halves were viewed in the first pass; the completed page fragments were subsequently compared with their scans in a second reading, with rotated or enlarged crops used for specific difficulties. No OCR or delegated reading was used. The inventory records the retained elements, not a guarantee that every doubtful letter is correct. Diagrams preserve the apparent directions and unheaded strokes even where the mathematics might suggest a different diagram; the notebooks' revised and withdrawn claims remain in the transcription.

Confidence below concerns the visible reading, not the truth of the claim. Medium means a plausible but unresolved ink-based reading; low marks physically obscured words and especially tentative compressed phrases. No doubt has been promoted on the authority of the research context. Approximate lines are the line numbers of the individual LaTeX page fragments, whose paragraphs can span several manuscript lines.
