# Legibility and witness comparison

The principal text is often readable through the high-resolution halves and local crops, but its compression varies markedly. Small terminal returns, detached accents, short abbreviation stops, nested indices and letters under cancellation carry most of the remaining uncertainty. The tightly retraced groups on scans 054 and 056, the overwritten base letters on 074–075, and the long canceled formulas on 077 deserve particular caution. Oblique marginal writing can overlap darker text from the body, as at the end of scan 054's note. A dark overlapping stroke can obscure a letter or a stop even after rotation.

Confidence belongs to the exact place recorded. Clear surrounding words do not settle a doubtful subscript, revision sequence or final letter. The transcription retains those doubts in place; the table records their competing readings and the particular stroke that remains unresolved. Doubt marks under cancellations have been kept where the complete canceled text cannot be read. No overall accuracy percentage is claimed.

C and H were each useful for locating disagreements. The consolidated reading preserves substantial source detail absent from H, including abbreviation forms, local punctuation, source revisions and marginal elements. C also required corrections at clear words, indices, omitted pieces and revision states, including places it had not marked doubtful. The counts in the changes log are counts of material decision places. They also include resolved C doubts, and a single place can affect both witnesses; they therefore do not give a measured error probability for either witness. Wrapper-only correspondences and changes of certainty alone are kept outside those material totals.
