# A.45 working consequence: the full multiplier is an explicit dihedral value

**Status:** proved consequence of A.44, awaiting independent review and post-checkpoint formal insertion. Formal sources remain frozen for checkpoint04.

Let alpha=(-1,f) be a full peripheral involution and u its canonical R1 parameter. Consider the actual universal profinite dihedral quotient

    q:F -> D = Zhat semidirect C2,
    q(x)=a, q(y)=a*t, a^2=1, a*t*a=t^(-1).

This quotient is obtained by imposing x^2=y^2=1; equivalently it is the full profinite completion of the infinite dihedral group. Its rotation subgroup is the full Zhat, generated by t=q(xy). Its commutator subgroup is the rotation subgroup 2Zhat. Thus write q(u)=t^k with k in 2Zhat, and define

    d_alpha=2k+1.

## The induced involution is trivial and d_alpha is a full unit

The kernel of q is invariant under alpha because it is the closed normal closure of x^2,y^2 and alpha is peripheral negative. Writing q(f)=t^l, the induced alpha fixes a and sends t to t^(1+2l). Its square being one implies (1+2l)^2=1. At every odd prime the root is +1, because A.44 proved identity action already modulo p and the only p-adic roots of one are ±1. At two, l belongs to 2Z2 because f is a commutator; hence 1+2l is1mod4, and the only possible dyadic root is again +1. Therefore alpha acts identically on D and q(f)=1.

A.44 says that 2k+1 is nonzero modulo every odd prime. It is odd at two. Hence d_alpha is a full profinite unit. Moreover its dyadic component is1mod4, because k belongs to2Zhat.

## Any full multiplier is d_alpha or its global negative

For a full unit mu, the normalized endomorphism with prescribed parameter u sends the images of x,y in D to the two reflections

    a,  a*t^(2k+1)=a*t^d_alpha,

because unit powers of a reflection equal the same reflection. Its product has rotation t^d_alpha. The required third-cusp image is t^mu. Two rotations in this full dihedral group are conjugate if and only if their exponents are equal or globally opposite: the rotation subgroup is abelian, and the other coset acts by a single inversion. Consequently any full third-cusp solution has

    mu = d_alpha  or  mu = -d_alpha.

Conversely the full third-cusp equation for either sign is equivalent to that for the other: the products of the inverses are conjugate to the inverse product. By A.39, alpha is conjugate to c if and only if the single explicitly specified equation

    x^d_alpha * u^(-1) y^d_alpha u  is conjugate to  (xy)^d_alpha

holds in full F. The multiplier search is therefore eliminated; one need only test this particular unit, up to the harmless global sign. The finite-quotient compactness criterion of A.39 can accordingly use this fixed unit rather than a variable over the unit group.

For alpha in full GT, A.42 separately proves that D_alpha=1+24c2(u) is a full square unit. Full conjugacy necessarily implies

    d_alpha^2 = D_alpha.

That equality has **not** been proved here, and even if it holds the full third-cusp equation still has to be checked. The point is that the dihedral value provides a concrete candidate and a concrete cross-quotient compatibility test. It does not itself settle the existential full hexagon.

## Possible next calculation, not a proved assertion

A finite target H_p semidirect C2, with H_p the exponent-p Heisenberg group and the involution inverting its two degree-one generators while fixing the central commutator, sees both the odd/odd dihedral coordinate and the even/even coefficient c2(u). It may permit pentagon relations that couple d_alpha^2 to D_alpha. No such relation is currently established. Finite dihedral targets alone leave the c2 coordinate invisible.
