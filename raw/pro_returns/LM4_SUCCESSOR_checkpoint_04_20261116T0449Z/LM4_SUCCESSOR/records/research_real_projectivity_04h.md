We searched primary literature for a full profinite analogue of the virtually-free pro-p splitting theorem. Herfort–Zalesskii, arXiv0712.4244, Theorem1.1, is expressly pro-p. Section5, Lemma5.1 gives the full profinite counterexample S3 amalgamated overC2 withS3; it has finite torsion centralizers and no finite free factor. Pál–Quick, Real projective groups are formal, Math.Ann.392(2025), Theorem2.1, quotes Haran's criterion: an index-at-most2 subgroup of cohomological dimension<=1 together with self-centralizing involutions is equivalent to real projectivity. Its hypotheses appear to hold for the actual pants involution extension byC28. Before using this as a new formal statement, read Haran1993Proposition2.2 and its proof. The resulting real projectivity solves suitable embedding problems weakly; it does not automatically give the proper, prescribed-generator solution needed here.

Pál–Quick, Quasi-Boolean groups, author manuscript https://gereonq.folk.ntnu.no/boolean-group.pdf, Theorem6.8, quotes Haran–Jarden1985Theorem3.6: real projective groups embed as CLOSED subgroups of real-free profinite groups. Its reconstruction theorem applies to maximal pro-2 quotients, not the full group. A closed subgroup need not inherit a Kurosh decomposition in the full category. Possible exact bottleneck: if one could decompose E as the free profinite product of representatives of the threeC2classes and a projective remainder, then the index2 free subgroup and Hopficity could remove the remainder. Establishing that decomposition is the missing point; do not use closed-subgroup Kurosh without its additional hypotheses.
