Review through scan 030 (17 committed pages; to be extended when the remaining
pages are decided).

The clean typescript lines and uncrowded pen additions are generally the most
secure parts. Exact accents, short punctuation, repeated typewriter strikes
and the small decoration above or below a mathematical letter require more
care than the surrounding readable prose suggests. A clear sentence is not
evidence that its index or cancellation state is clear.

The least secure readings so far are mixed typewriter overprints, heavy local
obliterations, clipped letter beginnings, and the nested cancellations on scans
023–025. The cancelled diagonal argument on scan 025 preserves only fragments
at several places. Its explicit alternatives and estimated unreadable word
counts are intentional limits of the reading. The drawings reproduce observed
shape and relative placement; their coordinates are typesetting
approximations, not measurements of the author's intentions or revision order.

C has usually supplied the fuller and more reliable literal starting point,
including many cancellations and short forms. It nevertheless leaves some
recoverable ink doubtful and misses or misplaces individual accents, rules,
additions and overprint details. H sometimes supports the better reading,
including the 1.25 reference and the final accented ét on scan 025. On dense
pages H more often changes wording or order, expands abbreviations, or gives
graphics as descriptions. H explicitly abridges the large cancelled passage
on scan 025 and excludes some types of superposition elsewhere; the changes
log acknowledges those declared scope limits. H's description-only pages are
not treated as textual witnesses.

Agreement is not a guarantee: the final ét accent on scan 025 was missed by
all three independent readers and recovered in main crop adjudication. The
counts in the changes log are counts of recorded differing loci, including
state and doubt resolution, not estimates of either witness's word-error rate.
Confidence labels describe the local ink decision. Remaining alternatives
should be consulted before quoting a doubtful passage as settled text.

Scans 027 and 030 reinforce the distinction between readable components and
unknown correction order. A central pen shaft can settle a mathematical glyph
even when another isolated instance remains ambiguous; a clear margin note
can still contain a clipped initial and an uncertain short word. Thin detached
marks are preserved graphically or with local uncertainty, without assigning
them a mathematical or editorial meaning the ink does not establish.
