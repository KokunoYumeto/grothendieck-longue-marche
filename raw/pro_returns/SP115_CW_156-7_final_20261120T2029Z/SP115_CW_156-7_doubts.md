# Notation table and remaining doubts

## Notation table

| Source form | Transcription | Scope and decision |
|---|---|---|
| Paired high quotation ticks | <code>``...&#x27;&#x27;</code> | All eight pages; guillemets are used only if drawn. Opening and closing marks are recorded separately where one is missing. |
| Figure set, looped script capital | <code>\mathscr F</code> | Distinct from the plain figure variable F. Printer’s mathscr/mathcal mapping is uncertain at its first occurrence on scan004; later occurrences follow that choice. |
| Multistrates and lieux | <code>M; L</code> | Ordinary M and L are the consistent editorial mappings of the hand. Calligraphic alternatives are registered at their first occurrences (003 and004), rather than repeatedly changing the alphabet. |
| Inclusion / refinement | <code>\leq; \ll</code> | Two distinct source relations. At0 states inclusion implies refinement. No extra strictness is supplied. |
| Strata / incidence | <code>\widetilde F; X\triangleleft F</code> | The source defines tilde F as the multistrates X≤F. The triangle points left; it is distinct from the pair of refinement chevrons. |
| Compatibility | <code>\mathrel{\tikz{...}}</code> | Two opposed open chevrons cross to form a central diamond, reproduced by the same TikZ strokes. It is the source relation whose definition refers to a common upper bound. |
| Set of strata versus decorated figure family | <code>\widetilde F; \widetilde{\mathscr F}; \overline{\mathscr F}</code> | The first is the strata of one figure; the second is the represented family. A clearly straight overbar on scan005 b′ is retained, even though the family context suggests a tilde. |
| Large / small multiombre operators | <code>\mathrm{Mulomb}; \mathrm{mulomb}; \mathrm{Omb}; \mathrm{omb}</code> | No t is inserted into Mulomb or mulomb. Capitalization distinguishes the large construction on multistrates from the small construction on lieux (003–004). |
| Empty figure / empty set | <code>\varnothing_{\mathscr F}; \varnothing; \phi</code> | Slashed-oval and long-stemmed phi forms are distinguished by visible strokes. | Greek capital Phi | <code>\Phi</code> | Oval crossed by a central stem; capital in the family conditions on005. Small lowered Phi remains distinguished by placement and the whole formula. |
| Order-specific infima | <code>\mathrm{Inf}^{\leq}; \mathrm{Inf}^{\ll}</code> | The relation is raised on Inf. The lower dot in F·G on004 is reproduced close to the baseline. |
| Axiom labels and manuscript numbers | <code>At 0; At 1–At 7; manuscript pages 1–8</code> | At has no period. Revisions of a number are transcribed, not inferred from the sequence. |
| Same / même abbreviations | <code>\overline{\mathrm m}; =</code> | Overlined m and the equals sign in en = temps remain abbreviations. Expansions are confined to the list below. |

The table records one editorial notation across the cote. It does not resolve a local letter, numeral, cancellation, or decoration when the ink remains ambiguous. First-occurrence font alternatives are not an assertion that different symbols are intended.

## Legibility and confidence

The main horizontal prose is generally readable at the supplied resolution. Compressed endings, small alphabet choices, overwritten numerals, and cancelled letters require more caution. Diagonal margins, especially the long note on003 and crowded formulae later in the sequence, carry a larger share of the remaining doubt. Enlargement and sharpening cannot recover strokes absent from the supplied pixels.

Both witnesses can omit source state or normalize a plausible word. Agreement between them was not treated as evidence. H often regularizes quotations, abbreviations, grammatical endings, and mathematical letters; C is generally more explicit about cancellation and uncertainty, but also has unsupported punctuation, a wrong deletion boundary, and some apparently confident wrong symbols. The page-level counts are counts of recorded places, not a statistical estimate of either model’s general accuracy.

Pale background marks and show-through are excluded. Watermarks are paper features, not manuscript text, and are never given doubt tokens here.
