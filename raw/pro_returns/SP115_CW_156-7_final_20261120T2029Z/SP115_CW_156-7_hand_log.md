## Scan 002

- Strong horizontal compression of m, n, e, and terminal s — Body lines 2, 4–6, 11–12, 19–22. Many humps flatten almost into a line; small detached ticks or terminal down-hooks distinguish letters. The i dot in dominant and the two l loops in essentielles are decisive. C and H disagree at des/de, fines/finies, and several endings; no rule is assumed beyond this page.
- Looped d followed by a flat e can resemble a short two-loop sign — des in body lines 2 and 6, de in line 10. The final s in the two des forms appears as an additional long terminal stroke with down-hook. The de at the left of line 10 ends sooner. All three occurrences were compared on their own crops.
- Tall f with a looped descender and a broad right shoulder — figures, fines, formalisme, and facultatif. The shoulder of a final f can obscure whether a following s is present. The blind adjudication resolves this final form as singular facultatif; the large low initial-f sweep is not a cancellation or guillemet (B024).
- Terminal t often rises to a narrow triangular peak — dominant, moment, proprement. It helps distinguish the terminal sequence of dominant and the end of proprement; it is not a deletion stroke. The final-paragraph forms are not secure examples: the blind adjudication retains pourront/pourraient/pourrait and doute/donc/dans as doubts (B025, B027).
- Accent strokes vary in slant and size — densité, propriété, pièces. A short raised stroke is present in each relevant place. The supplied convention governs acute/grave rendering; pièces is not transcribed with an acute merely because the stroke tilts upward.
