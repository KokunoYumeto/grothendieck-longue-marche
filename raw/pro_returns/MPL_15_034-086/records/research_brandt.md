## B01 — The projective envelope is conditional on the generator and closure hypotheses

**Scans 060–061; kind: unpursued implication / own verification; quote:** “sous réserve d'existence des gén. stricts”; the comparison is Hua15, “Catégories A-linéaires et ⊗-enveloppes”, pp. 58–62, rather than CT16/049's semisimple-motive programme (**RS WG2 §2.4 #3**, unchecked plan). If an additive category is idempotent complete and $O=\operatorname{End}(X)$, a projective right module $P=eO^n$ gives $P\otimes_O X=\operatorname{im}(e:X^n\to X^n)$, and the tensor–dual-Hom identification follows by applying the same idempotent to the free-module identity. Full faithfulness follows from the matrix description of maps between these summands, but identifying an entire category with all finitely generated projectives additionally requires that every object lie in this additive idempotent closure; scan 060 explicitly reserves the generator hypothesis. **Confidence: high**, because this is an elementary idempotent-splitting argument; it does not establish the unrelated motivic semisimplicity or standard conjectures in CT16/049.

## B02 — The exact sequence has group terms followed by pointed sets and orbits

**Scans 066, 068; kind: own verification of an unproved assertion; quote:** “Le groupe de stabilité d'un élément X”; compare Hua15, “Le groupe de Brandt et la fibration sur les ordres”, pp. 65–69 (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate context, no settled result). For $C\simeq\mathbb P(O/A)$, the map $\operatorname{Aut}_A(O)\to\pi_0\operatorname{Eq}(C)$ sends an automorphism to the corresponding twisted regular bimodule, its kernel is $\operatorname{Inn}(O)$, and its image consists precisely of bimodule classes whose underlying right module is isomorphic to $O$. Thus the stabilizer is $\operatorname{Out}_A(O)$ without needing $\operatorname{Pic}(A)=0$, agreeing with Hua's explicit qualification of the manuscript; the maps onwards to $\operatorname{Is}(C)$ and $\operatorname{Is}(\mathcal O)$ describe a pointed-set fibre and an orbit quotient, not extra group homomorphisms. **Confidence: high**, because choosing a right-module trivialization identifies the left action with an automorphism and changing that trivialization conjugates it; [V18, §18.5.3] provides a compatible normalizer formulation.

## B03 — A precise homotopy-fibre construction supplies the stated analogy

**Scan 066; kind: own idea supplying the missing construction; quote:** “On a envie de dire que C est fibrée”; compare Hua15, pp. 65–69, which calls this heuristic, and the distinct fibre-functor distinction in `context/same_cote_15/MPL_15_195-228+18_002-017_notes.md`, A13, scans 15/207,209 (**RS WG2 §2.4 #3**, unchecked; **15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). Pass to the core $C^{\simeq}$ of isomorphisms and take the functor $Y\mapsto\operatorname{End}(Y)$ to the groupoid of apparentée algebras and algebra isomorphisms; its homotopy fibre over $O=\operatorname{End}(X)$ consists of pairs $(Y,\theta:O\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{End}(Y))$. Under the stated Deuring equivalence this fibre is equivalent to $\operatorname{Eq}(C)$, by evaluating an equivalence at $X$, and the usual groupoid fibre sequence gives the displayed kernel–stabilizer–orbit sequence after replacing the functor by an equivalent isofibration if necessary. **Confidence: high under those hypotheses**; using the full A-linear category's nerve instead would be incorrect, since its zero morphisms make a constant functor naturally connected to the identity, whereas the required invariants concern isomorphisms.

## B04 — The scalar quotient and the central Picard inclusion are different steps

**Scans 066, 068, 072; kind: reading question / own verification; quote:** “Pic A”; “biidéaux ... mod”; compare Hua15, pp. 65–77 (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). In the central-order case $Z(O)=A\subset K=Z(E)$, embedded invertible bimodules are identified by scalars in $K^\times$, so $\pi_0\operatorname{Eq}(C)=\mathcal I(O)/\{aO:a\in K^\times\}$, while the automorphisms of its unit are $A^\times$; scan 068's frozen mention of principal ideals and $A^\times$ is therefore a reading question, not a license to replace it. Separately, $L\mapsto L\otimes_A O$ from invertible $A$-modules is fully faithful because, locally after trivializing $L,M$, its bimodule Hom is $Z(O)=A$, and these local identities glue; this explains injectivity on Picard classes. **Confidence: high**, by generic extension and local trivialization; [V18, Lemma 18.4.4 and (18.4.6)] is a concise literature comparison for the scalar quotient.

## B05 — Ramification is the residual divisor quotient, not the whole local Brauer group

**Scans 066, 068, 070; kind: reading question / qualification; quote:** “Ram(C/A)”; the margin's “??”; compare Hua15, pp. 65–69, especially its local-Brauer footnote (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). For a maximal quaternion order over a global Dedekind ring, [V18, (18.4.9)] gives $0\to\operatorname{Pic}(A)\to\operatorname{Pic}_A(O)\to\bigoplus_{\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak D}\mathbb Z/2\to0$, supporting the manuscript's quaternion specialization without asserting a canonical splitting. The manuscript's general $e_{\mathfrak p}$ should be read with its written Deuring–Brauer scope: over a nonarchimedean local field the finite subgroup generated by the particular Brauer class must be distinguished from the entire Brauer group, and nothing here verifies the marginal residue formula over arbitrary imperfect residue fields. **Confidence: high for that distinction and the cited quaternion case**; the wider residue-field assertion is left unproved.

## B06 — Two mathematical reading alerts on the quaternion specialization

**Scan 070; kind: reading questions, frozen text unchanged; quotes:** “splitté”; “Z4 resp Z6”; compare Hua15, pp. 65–69, which replaces the former by ramification and explicitly reverses the latter (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate context). The standard Deuring algebra is ramified at $p$ and infinity [V42, Theorem 42.1.9], so the frozen uncertain word before those places is mathematically consequential. For characteristic other than 2, 3, the geometric automorphism orders are 6 at $j=0$ and 4 at $j=1728$, as [S, III.10.1] states; in short Weierstrass form the substitutions $(x,y)\mapsto(u^2x,u^3y)$ require $u^6=1$ in the first case and $u^4=1$ in the second. **Confidence: high for the mathematical comparison**, while deciding the written word remains a future source-reading task and no alteration to the frozen page follows.

## B07 — Frobenius orbits distinguish curve classes from order types

**Scan 070; kind: own verification / scope limit; quote:** “son j∈Fp”; compare Hua15, pp. 65–69, and its footnote separating class number and type number (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate context). Given the page's identification of the nontrivial Brandt class with $E\mapsto E^{(p)}$, B02 makes surjectivity $\operatorname{Aut}(O)\to\mathbb Z/2$ equivalent to $[E^{(p)}]=[E]$, which over an algebraic closure is precisely $j(E)^p=j(E)$. Thus $\pi_0C$ is the curve-class set and $\pi_0\mathcal O$ its Frobenius-orbit set, not two names for the same set; scan 068's “le quotient est fini??” should retain its question marks until the arithmetic hypotheses furnishing class-number finiteness are supplied. **Confidence: high for the orbit calculation**, conditional on the asserted Deuring/Frobenius identification, with no finiteness theorem claimed over every Dedekind base.

## B08 — Inner generic automorphisms are what make the embedded-ideal model available

**Scan 072; kind: own verification / hypothesis audit; quote:** “si tout autom de E est intérieur”; compare Hua15, “Les 2-catégories de Brandt et la catégorie de Picard B”, pp. 71–77 (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). If a special bimodule becomes free of rank 1 on both sides over $E$, a right-module basis writes its left action as an algebra automorphism of $E$, and an inner implementation changes that basis into an $E$-bimodule identification with the regular bimodule. Restricting this identification embeds the lattice in $E$, while any two identifications differ by multiplication by an element of $Z(E)^\times$; the relevant automorphisms must be the $K$-algebra automorphisms compatible with the base, not arbitrary semilinear automorphisms. **Confidence: high**, by this direct rank-one calculation; semilocality/generic freeness and the written strict-generator assumptions are retained rather than inferred from the name “Deuring”.

## B09 — The warning about natural quasi-inverses remains real

**Scan 072; kind: self-flagged / interpretive correction to the comparison; quote:** “n'ont pas de quasi-inverses ‘naturels’!”; compare Hua15, pp. 71–77, whose paraphrase says the functors are not natural equivalences (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). ## B11 — An explicit abelian two-term model proves strict commutativity under (C)

**Scans 072, 074; kind: own proof under explicit hypotheses; quote:** “les contraintes ... sont des identités”; compare Hua15, pp. 71–77 (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate now supported in this stated model). Let $\mathcal I(O)$ be the group of special invertible two-sided fractional ideals, assume all its products commute, and form the groupoid with objects $I\in\mathcal I(O)$ and arrows $I\to J$ the scalars $a\in Z(E)^\times$ satisfying $aI=J$; tensor products of objects and arrows are multiplication, so associativity, units, and commutativity are literal identities. This is the Picard groupoid of the abelian two-term complex $Z(E)^\times\xrightarrow{a\mapsto aO}\mathcal I(O)$, with $\pi_1=Z(O)^\times$ and $\pi_0=\mathcal I(O)/Z(E)^\times$, and every pentagon, hexagon, and self-commutor condition is an identity. **Confidence: high**, by the displayed construction; it is $B_2$'s ideal model that is literally strict, whereas $B_1$'s ordinary bimodule tensor product has its usual canonical associators, a distinction clarified by [D, §§1.4.1–2,1.4.11].

## B12 — The transported commutor is not an elementwise flip

**Scans 074–076; kind: own proof completing an asserted independence; quote:** “ne dépend pas du choix des plongements”; compare Hua15, pp. 71–77 (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). For invertible two-sided ideals choose $\sum_r a_rb_r=1$ with $a_r\in I$ and $b_r\in I^{-1}$: the inverse to multiplication $\mu_{I,J}:I\otimes_OJ\to IJ$ is $x\mapsto\sum_r a_r\otimes b_rx$, because $b_rx\in J$ and the balancing relation gives $\sum_r a_r\otimes b_rij=i\otimes j$; thus $c_{I,J}=\mu_{J,I}^{-1}\mu_{I,J}$ is defined using $IJ=JI$. Replacing the two embeddings by central multiples $a,b$ multiplies both multiplication maps by the same $ab=ba$, so $c$ is unchanged; the same calculation proves naturality, and the associativity and hexagon diagrams become equality of multiplication into the common product ideal, with $c_{I,I}=1$. **Confidence: high under B08 and (C)**, without any additional principal-ideal hypothesis; this formula is necessary because a formal $i\otimes j\mapsto j\otimes i$ is not generally balanced over a noncommutative order.

## B13 — The actual generator-change proof works and is composable

**Scan 076; kind: own proof expanding the written argument; quote:** “P.L.P⁻¹”; “OK”; compare Hua15, pp. 71–77 (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). For a special invertible $(O',O)$-ideal $P$ in the stipulated connecting-ideal model, put $T_P(I)=PIP^{-1}$ and leave central-scalar arrows unchanged: $P^{-1}P=O$ gives $T_P(I)T_P(J)=P(IJ)P^{-1}$, so the functor preserves the identity commutors of B11. For a composable special connecting ideal $Q$, $T_QT_P=T_{QP}$ as ideal functors; if $P'=PS$ with $S\in\mathcal I(O)$ a special invertible two-sided $O$-ideal, then (C) gives $T_{P'}=T_P$, since $SIS^{-1}=I$. **Confidence: high**, because these literal identities verify both the manuscript's generator independence and compatibility among successive changes, while transporting them back to arbitrary bimodules requires the multiplication isomorphisms of B12 rather than a choice of strict inverse to every equivalence.

## B14 — The bracketed universal autoisomorphism assertion is too broad literally

**Scan 076; kind: own counterexample to a literal generalization, not to the ideal-model proposition; quote:** “autoisomorphismes ... transportent ... en elle-même”; compare the actual $PLP^{-1}$ proof in Hua15, pp. 71–77 (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate; no earlier matching counterexample in the searched context). Define a strict monoidal groupoid with objects $V=(\mathbb F_2)^3$, no arrows between distinct objects, automorphism group $\mathbb F_2$ at each object, and addition as both composition and tensor product, so the identity arrow has label $0$; its commutor has label $b(x,y)=x_1y_2+x_2y_1$, whose bilinearity gives the hexagons, symmetry gives $c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y}=\operatorname{id}$, and $b(x,x)=0$ gives strict commutativity in Deligne's sense. The strict monoidal autoisomorphism $F(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(x_1,x_3,x_2)$, identity on arrow labels and with identity tensorator, does not preserve this commutor, because $F(c_{e_1,e_2})=1\in\mathbb F_2$ but $c_{F(e_1),F(e_2)}=0$. **Confidence: high**, by direct evaluation; the fixed monoidal functor fails to be symmetric, so the bracket cannot supply a theorem about every autoisomorphism with its given monoidal structure.

## B15 — Changing the tensorator repairs that example, without repairing the literal assertion

**Scan 076; kind: own qualification of B14; quote:** “une petite démonstration”; compare Hua15, pp. 71–77, which omits this bracketed problem (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). In B14 set the new tensorator to $\alpha(x,y)=x_1(y_2+y_3)$: bilinearity is precisely its monoidal cocycle equation, and $\alpha(x,y)+\alpha(y,x)=b(x,y)+b(Fx,Fy)$ is the symmetry-compatibility equation. Thus the same underlying functor can be made symmetric after changing its monoidal data; the example refutes automatic preservation by the **fixed strict monoidal functor**, not existence of an adjusted symmetric lift, and it does not challenge the particular conjugation functors in B13. **Confidence: high**, since both required equations follow by expanding the displayed bilinear formulas.

## B16 — The final torsor equivalence is formal for a connected 2-groupoid over a point

**Scan 078; kind: own proof under explicit interpretation; quote:** “une équivalence de 2-catégories”; compare Hua15, pp. 71–77, and CT47/003's different $C$-torsor pairing (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate; **WG2 §2.4 #3**, unchecked). Let $\mathcal G$ be the connected 2-groupoid of the specified Deuring categories, equivalences, and invertible natural transformations, fix $C\in\mathcal G$, and let $B=\operatorname{Eq}(C)$; define a $B$-torsor groupoid to be nonempty and to have, for each object $t$, an orbit functor $B\to T$, $b\mapsto t\cdot b$, that is an equivalence. Then $C'\mapsto\operatorname{Eq}(C,C')$ is locally an equivalence by evaluating equivariant functors at one chosen equivalence $C\to C'$, and is essentially surjective because any such torsor becomes equivalent to $B$ after choosing $t$; the quasi-inverse choices are not canonical, exactly as scan 072 warns. **Confidence: high for this ordinary categorical statement**; a version for torsor stacks over a site additionally requires local nonemptiness and effective descent/closure under twisting, which these leaves do not establish.

## B21 — The localization criterion has an elementary sufficient direction and a Dedekind converse

**Scan 074 margin; kind: own proof under explicit hypotheses; quote:** “Il suffit (et il faut si A est ... de dim 1)”; compare Hua15, pp. 71–77, and its discussion of local commutativity (**RS 15-brandt-picard-category**, candidate). If every maximal localization satisfies (C), then $(LM)_{\mathfrak m}=L_{\mathfrak m}M_{\mathfrak m}=M_{\mathfrak m}L_{\mathfrak m}=(ML)_{\mathfrak m}$ for every $\mathfrak m$, so $LM=ML$ by the local criterion for equality of submodules, with no Dedekind assumption needed in this direction. Conversely, for a Dedekind domain $A$ and a finite torsion-free $A$-order $O$, a special invertible ideal $L_{\mathfrak p}$ extends with value $O_{\mathfrak q}$ at all $\mathfrak q\ne\mathfrak p$: choose bounds $\mathfrak p^nO_{\mathfrak p}\subset L_{\mathfrak p}\subset\mathfrak p^{-n}O_{\mathfrak p}$ and take the preimage of its submodule in the $\mathfrak p$-primary quotient $\mathfrak p^{-n}O/\mathfrak p^nO$; extending its inverse the same way verifies invertibility by localization, and local rank-one freeness is built into the construction. **Confidence: high for this localization equivalence**, because the explicit lattice extension lets global (C) test any two local special ideals; the margin's further completion/henselization versions require a separate descent argument and are not asserted here.
