## R01 — The faithful-object hypothesis restricts the scope

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 084, opening; **kind:** unpursued implication; **quote:** “Soit E un motif ‘fidèle’”; **context / register:** CT 16/049, CN 16:49:4, N15a L01 at 15/135 and N15b M01 at 15/219 and 18/011; adds a finite faithful object to an already assumed category; WG2 §2.4 #3 [unchecked plan].

The page assumes a motivic category, a commutative band, and a faithful motive before it studies its endomorphism algebra; it does not construct the category from correspondences. A finite-dimensional faithful representation places the relevant group in a finite-dimensional general linear group, so applying this argument to an unrestricted pro-motivic group requires first passing to the subcategory generated by finitely many objects. Hua, modernised reading of cote 15, “Algèbres de Riemann et tores compacts,” likewise begins with an existing category, while adding semisimplicity to the opening formulation.

## R02 — A precise compactness argument

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 084, last paragraph; **kind:** own idea / proof completion; **quote:** “U° est un tore compact”; **confidence:** high, because the defining equation bounds a positive definite quadratic form; **context / register:** adds a proof detail to Hua, “Algèbres de Riemann et tores compacts,” and to CT 16/049's proposed use of positivity; WG2 §2.3 #7 and §2.4 #3 [unchecked plans].

For a positive involution on a finite-dimensional real algebra A, the equations aa′=1 define a closed subset of A and imply Tr(aa′)=Tr(1), so that subset lies on a bounded positive definite sphere and is compact. To pass from the written relation on U(Q) to U° as an algebraic subgroup, use Zariski density of rational points on the torus U°, rather than a claim that the rational points themselves form a compact topological group (Milne, Algebraic Groups, 2015 notes, Corollary 14.42 [AG]). This supplies the missing algebraic and topological steps under the page's faithful-action hypothesis, with no assertion about analytic density or about all disconnected components.

## R03 — Positivity explains the product of fields

**Witness:** cote 15, scans 084–086, “auto-adjointe” and “B=∏ L_i”; **kind:** unproved claim with later-result comparison; **context / register:** CT 16/049 and CN 16:49:4 ask for a positivity-to-semisimplicity argument; the present pages give its commutative endomorphism-algebra setting; WG2 §2.3 #7 [unchecked plan].

The bridge from a commutative adjoint-stable subalgebra to a product of number fields uses positivity, not commutativity alone: a commutative algebra can otherwise contain nilpotents. Milne, Complex Multiplication, Propositions 1.36–1.39 [CM], gives the relevant semisimplicity and classification statements, including the possibility of a totally real factor with identity involution. This supports the semisimple step in Hua, modernised reading of cote 15, “Algèbres de Riemann et tores compacts,” while leaving the construction of a motivic polarization itself as an assumption.

## R04 — Hua's totally real-factor caveat is supported

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 086, first paragraph; **kind:** reading question / checked contextual qualification; **quote:** “chaque L_i ext. quadratique”; **context / register:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 15, “Algèbres de Riemann et tores compacts,” explicitly adds totally real factors and a Tate-motive example; differs from the present wording; CT 16/049 and WG2 §2.4 #3 [unchecked plan].

Hua's qualification is mathematically necessary for the hypotheses written on these two pages: even the tensor category generated by the unit has E=1 and B=Q with identity involution, so no quadratic extension occurs. The positive-involution classification [CM, Proposition 1.39] confirms the two alternatives, CM with conjugation or totally real with identity. The qualification is credited to Hua's project, not claimed as a new finding here, and the frozen quadratic-only wording remains unchanged.

## R05 — What the real factors do to the unitary group

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 086, H^u and “tore ‘compact’”; **kind:** own idea / unpursued implication; **confidence:** high, by evaluating the group functor after extension to R; **context / register:** builds on Hua's real-factor caveat and N15a T01–T06 at 15/117–119; adds the component-group qualification relevant to the later real-gerbe obstruction; WG2 §2.4 #3 [unchecked plan].

A CM factor L/K contributes Res_{K/Q} Res^1_{L/K} G_m, whose real points form a product of circles, whereas a totally real factor F with identity involution contributes Res_{F/Q} μ_2 because the equation is a²=1. Thus the full unitary group need not be a torus after the omitted real factors are restored, but its identity component is still a compact real torus. This connects the earlier quadratic-field omission to the need to retain finite sign factors in the real-fibre questions reported by N15a, without identifying those factors with a particular motivic gerbe class.

## R06 — Rational points need not recover a disconnected band

**Witness:** cote 15, scans 084–086, “G(Q)” and “l'algèbre engendrée”; **kind:** own idea / counterexample to an unrestricted reconstruction; **confidence:** high, from the explicit norm-one group and its torsion; **context / register:** qualifies Hua's appeal to rational-point density in “Algèbres de Riemann et tores compacts”; N15a FR01 at 15/088–090 instead has an explicitly Zariski-generating Frobenius; WG2 §2.4 #3 [unchecked plan].

Let T=Res^1_{Q(i)/Q} G_m, let G=T[3], and let E=Q(i) with multiplication action and the positive form Tr_{Q(i)/Q}(x conjugate(y)). This is a faithful representation of a nontrivial finite group scheme, but G(Q)={1} since Q(i) has no nontrivial cube root of unity, so the algebra generated by its rational points is only Q. Consequently B detects the connected-torus argument through density but cannot, without an additional hypothesis, reconstruct the full disconnected G or all of its characters. The example concerns the formal representation and positivity assumptions on the page, not a claim that this particular category is the author's intended geometric motive category.

## R07 — The exact norm equation behind the final NB

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 086, final NB; **kind:** own idea / conditional completion; **quote:** “déterminer ε ... en termes des ... normes”; **confidence:** high, by composing field norms; **context / register:** adds an explicit calculation to Hua, “Algèbres de Riemann et tores compacts,” which says the determination is not done; compares N15a FR01 at 15/088–090 and its character/cocharacter conventions; WG2 §2.4 #3 [unchecked plan].

On a connected subgroup for which the algebraic representation factors through B*, write ρ_i for its projection to Res_{L_i/Q} G_m and retain the page's multiplier normalization aa′=ε(a). For a CM factor the stronger identity is N_{L_i/K_i}(ρ_i(g))=ε(g) as a scalar in K_i, and applying N_{K_i/Q} gives N_{L_i/Q}(ρ_i(g))=ε(g)^{[K_i:Q]}. An absolute norm therefore gives a power of the multiplier, while the relative norm and its scalar-diagonal condition retain the actual similitude relation. If the polarization has a different Tate-weight normalization, ε in these formulas must be replaced by its actual multiplier character, not silently raised or lowered in the transcription.

## R08 — Recovering a character requires integral divisibility

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 086, final NB; **kind:** own idea; **confidence:** high, because the character group of a torus is torsion-free; **quote:** “hom. normes”; **context / register:** adds an integral test to N15a FR01 at 15/088–090 and Hua's unfinished norm question; bears on WG2 §2.4 #3, with no update to LM6 A.22 [open return claim].

For the CM factors i, put χ_i=N_{L_i/Q}∘ρ_i and d_i=[K_i:Q] in X*(G°); the previous identity becomes χ_i=d_i ε, so the problem is to find one Galois-invariant integral character satisfying all these equations. If it exists it is unique, since multiplication by a positive integer is injective on this lattice, but the existence of a rational solution alone does not establish integral divisibility. When gcd(d_i)=1 and the equations are compatible, a Bézout combination recovers ε directly; otherwise compatibility and lattice divisibility must both be checked. A totally real factor F instead has ρ_F²=ε and hence 2χ_F=[F:Q]ε; on disconnected groups torsion characters can be killed by powers, so the uniqueness argument remains restricted to the torus.

## R09 — The cocharacter is also an integral problem

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 086, “et j ... de même”; **kind:** own idea / comparison of normalizations; **confidence:** high, by the explicit norm-torus example; **context / register:** N15a FR01 and FR22 at 15/090 report εj(t)=t², whereas N15b P14 at 18/011 reports εj=id in a Levi quotient; differs in normalization and ambient group; WG2 §2.4 #3 [unchecked plan].

For G=Res_{Q(i)/Q} G_m with ε=N_{Q(i)/Q}, every Q-cocharacter is t↦t^n in the scalar diagonal, and εj(t)=t^{2n}; thus a square-weight cocharacter exists but a Q-cocharacter section of ε does not. More generally, in this commutative setting, when ker(ε)° is compact over R, it has no nonzero Q-cocharacter, so a prescribed value of ε∘j determines at most one Q-cocharacter j, and its existence remains a lattice divisibility question. This explains why the square convention reported at 15/090 cannot be identified without comparison with the section convention reported in the different setting of 18/011. Neither contextual report supplies the determination explicitly requested on the current scan.

## R10 — Weight and polarization conventions remain visible hypotheses

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 084, adjoint equation; **kind:** reading question; **quote:** “φ(u)φ(u)′=ε(u)1”; **context / register:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 15, “Algèbres de Riemann et tores compacts,” inserts a weight exponent in its formula and explains the insertion in a footnote; differs from the source; N15a FR22 at 15/090 and N15b P14 at 18/011; WG2 §2.4 #3.

Hua writes ε(u)^w for a pure object of weight w, whereas the frozen page has ε(u) without that exponent and does not state the purity convention in its opening lines. The safe mathematical comparison is to name the multiplier character of the chosen pairing, then compare it with the chosen Tate character and the object's weight. A direct sum of objects whose pairings take values in different Tate powers need not have one common scalar multiplier, so the faithful-object and polarization assumptions must be made compatible before extending the argument to a larger category. This is a question about hypotheses and normalization, not permission to insert the exponent into scan 084.

## R11 — The field fixed by the involution

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 086, parenthesis after K_i; **kind:** reading question; **quote:** frozen “l'un des s/c tt. réels” within a doubt mark; **context / register:** Hua, “Algèbres de Riemann et tores compacts,” uses the maximal real subfield; N15a CM04–CM05 at 15/128–130 develop the totally real/CM-field comparison; adds a precise reading question; WG2 §2.4 #3.

## R12 — Compactness does not remove the finite real obstruction

**Witness:** cote 15, scans 052, 084 and 086; **kind:** unpursued implication; **quotes:** “non triviale” on 052; “tore compact” on 084; **context / register:** N15a T04 at 15/117–119 already calculates H² for a compact torus and a μ_2 factor; adds the earlier Deuring witness, without presenting that calculation as new; WG2 §2.4 #3 and N15b B03 [first-pass lead].

## R13 — The trace assertion has a representation-theoretic meaning

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 086, first paragraph; **kind:** own idea / conditional proof detail; **quote:** “la trace est proportionnelle ... à la trace ordinaire”; **confidence:** high under the stated realization-trace hypothesis; **context / register:** CT 16/048–049 uses rational traces and characteristic polynomials, while Hua's same subsection paraphrases this sentence using “normes”; the present text supports a trace statement instead; WG2 §2.4 #3.

If the trace on B=∏L_i is the trace of a finite-dimensional rational realization V, decompose V=⊕V_i and let m_i be the L_i-dimension of V_i, obtaining Tr_V(b)=Σ_i m_i Tr_{L_i/Q}(b_i). This proves proportionality in that setting and identifies the constants as multiplicities. For a general positive linear functional on a field, proportionality to the ordinary trace is additional structure, so positivity alone should not be cited as its proof. In particular, a field norm is nonlinear and is not the trace being discussed in the frozen sentence.

## R14 — A later Serre-torus comparison is a method, not an identification

**Witness:** cote 15, scan 086, norm maps and j; **kind:** unpursued implication; **context / register:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 15, “§ 1. Les tores T_K, V_K et le tore de Serre” and “§ 2. Structures CM et normes réflexes” concern later scans 141–152; N15a §125 records an earlier-context search; adds the present starting point; WG2 §2.4 #3 and LM6 A.22 [open return claim].

Milne, Abelian Varieties with Complex Multiplication (for Pedestrians), §4 [Serre], describes the Serre group by characters satisfying a conjugation/weight condition and gives norm-compatible maps. This is a concrete later method for organizing the character and cocharacter data requested here, but a general compact subtorus of the displayed H^u is not thereby identified with a Serre torus. An identification would need the actual character lattice, the motivic tensor relations, and the normalization of the weight map. It also would not construct the profinite procyclic subgroup required by LM6 A.22.
