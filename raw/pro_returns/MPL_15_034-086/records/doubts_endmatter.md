## Legibility and limits

The selection contains 23 handwritten subject sheets and 8 reused typed or printed sheets. All 93 supplied images (whole sheet and both halves for each scan) were inspected manually on the first pass; every page was then read against its saved transcription again. No OCR, published transcription or context source was used to make the diplomatic text, and no transcription work was delegated.

The most vulnerable readings are the compressed upper insertions and left margins of scan 34; overwritten material on scans 44, 50 and 52; cancelled names and left notes on scan 60; the top and margin of scan 72; the local criterion in scan 74; and small rewritten words on scans 84–86. The solitary \illegible{3} on scan 44 occurs inside a cancellation whose ink could not be disentangled on enlargement; it is not a formula placeholder. Elsewhere doubtful words or symbols have a proposed reading in place.

The reused typescripts are generally more legible, but their mathematical additions, subscripts and changes of type weight are less secure than their prose. Scans 79 and 85 have text close to the edge, and scan 85 has actual blank slots in the copy. Surprising factors, index ranges and spellings have been preserved and may be source errors; they must not be treated as editorial corrections.

The package is complete as a page-by-page transcription attempt, not a claim that every proposed reading is certain. Its dense early cancellations particularly need expert human checking against the supplied scans. Ends of pages that stop mid-sentence, the last question on scan 70 and unfinished labels on scan 77 remain unfinished. The selection is nonconsecutive: missing scans were excluded by the supplied task and have not been reconstructed.
