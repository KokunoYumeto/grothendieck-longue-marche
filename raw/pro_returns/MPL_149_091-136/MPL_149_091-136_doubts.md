## Scope and reading conventions

This register covers all 407 marked uncertainty occurrences in scans 091–136. Each item corresponds to one uncertainty macro in the transcription. Item numbers identify occurrences within a scan; they are not manuscript line numbers. Locations reproduce the visual audit’s approximate manuscript positions, including marginal and interlinear writing.

The first reading follows the visible ink. Alternatives are retained without correcting the manuscript toward an expected mathematical statement. Literal LaTeX in code spans preserves small decorations, uncertain connectors, and editorial notation. An em dash in the alternatives column means that no specific alternative was proposed. For an illegible span, the number is an estimated word count.

**Confidence:** high means the first reading is comparatively well supported by the visible strokes; medium means it is plausible but some strokes or boundaries remain uncertain; low means the reading is weak or the ink cannot be securely separated. A high-confidence item is still retained here because the transcription explicitly marks it as doubtful.

## Legibility and least secure readings

The larger main text and most displayed formulae are more legible than the small annotations. The least secure readings occur in faint strokes, crossings and overwriting, and compressed diagonal margins—particularly on scans 117, 120, 127, 130, 133, and 136. Tiny subscripts and superscripts, bars versus tildes, prime marks, and the presence or absence of arrowheads also remain recurrent sources of doubt. Several canceled words can be read only tentatively, and the two marked illegible prose spans have estimated extents. A smooth mathematical interpretation does not increase confidence in unclear ink. The table records the source-based readings fixed before contextual research; later interpretive or comparative questions belong in the research notes.
