## A. Scans 91–104: specialization, actions, and good reduction

### RN091-01 — The Kummer obstruction and the choice of a splitting [unproved claim; scan 091, manuscript p. 45, approximately lines 8–15]

The phrase “seul le facteur $I_{s^{\prime}}\simeq T$” isolates the inertia left before a residual section can be chosen. This restates the splitting problem in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 73 (“un torseur”), with scan 82 calling the peripheral extension trivial: split does not mean that its cyclotomic conjugation action is trivial. For an equicharacteristic-zero henselian trait, a uniformizer supplies a splitting, and its inertia-conjugacy classes form a torsor under $H^1(k,\widehat{\mathbf Z}(1))\simeq\varprojlim_n k^\times/(k^\times)^n$ (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 03PK). Thus the asserted second-species section is a choice in that torsor, rather than a canonical removal of the Kummer factor.

### RN091-02 — Cuspidal splittings and geometric tangent directions [own idea; scan 091, manuscript p. 45, final clause of the first paragraph; compare scan 095, first paragraph]

The conclusion “de deuxième espèce” should be compared with the later restriction to splittings arising from a uniformizer, rather than silently identifying those two families. This differs in scope from `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 79, which explicitly distinguishes the whole Kummer-cohomology torsor from a smaller “sous-famille” called geometric and parametrized by a $k^\times$-torsor. Our elementary test over $\mathbf Q$ is a compatible family of classes of $p^{a_n}$, with $(a_n)\in\widehat{\mathbf Z}$ not arising from an ordinary integer: its valuation at $p$ prevents it from being the Kummer class of any rational number (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 03PK). Confidence is high because rational valuations are integers, so this supplies explicit non-tangent classes in the local splitting torsor while making no claim to prove a global section conjecture.

### RN092-01 — What the closed-point family actually determines [unproved claim; scan 092, approximately lines 1–3]

The qualified assertion about “l’ens des pts fermés” is valid as a uniqueness statement for already existing morphisms from a reduced finite-type scheme over a field to a separated target, provided their restrictions as morphisms $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)\to Y$ agree, including the residue-field maps. Indeed, the equalizer is closed and contains every closed point, so density and reducedness force equality as morphisms; finite-type schemes over fields are Jacobson (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 01T9). The present passage adds this specialization family to `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 63 (“classes de scindages”), while scan 64 already marks the unrestricted point–section bijection “faux tel quel”. Recovering a rational section from an arbitrary compatible family of abstract group splittings is a stronger existence problem and does not follow from closed-point density.

### RN092-02 — Decomposition and inertia in arbitrary codimension [unproved claim; scan 092, approximately lines 11–19 and the final extension; continued on scan 093, lines 1–4]

The proposed generalization beyond “codim $1$” has a precise formulation for a normal local domain $A$: with compatible choices, $D=G_{\operatorname{Frac}(A^h)}$, $I=G_{\operatorname{Frac}(A^{sh})}$, and $D/I\simeq G_{\kappa(s)}$ (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 0BSD, Lemma 58.13.1). This adds a higher-dimensional local statement to `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 26, where the kernel is identified “par Kummer” for a punctured trait. The exact sequence survives this generalization, but the assertion $I\simeq\widehat{\mathbf Z}(1)$ belongs to the tame one-dimensional situation and must not be transferred to an arbitrary higher-dimensional local ring. Normality and the choices of a geometric point and compatible henselizations should therefore remain visible in any reformulation.

### RN092-03 — Competing normalizations and decorated fields [reading question; scan 092, second half of scan 092; opening paragraph of scan 093, manuscript p. 46]

The phrase “On introduit le normalisé $S^{\prime}$ de $S$” reuses $S^{\prime}$, already used on scan 091 for the domain of definition, and the next page gives competing single- and double-tilde labels for the strict and ordinary henselian fraction fields. This differs from the local organization in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 75 (“faire un raisonnement local général”), which first fixes the trait and its fraction field before adjoining roots. The mathematical comparison suggests $G_{\operatorname{Frac}(A^{sh})}$ for inertia and $G_{\operatorname{Frac}(A^h)}$ for decomposition, but it cannot decide which decoration the author intended to write (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 0BSD). The frozen readings, including the stabilizer’s ambient-group subscript on scan 092, should remain untouched while this notational ambiguity is recorded separately.

### RN093-01 — Killing inertia gives the residual section [unpursued implication; scan 093, manuscript p. 46, approximately lines 6–12]

The condition “s’annule sur $I_{s^{\prime}}$” gives a homomorphism from $D/I\simeq G_{k(s)}$ by the universal property of a quotient. This restates the punctured-trait-to-residue-point passage in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 26, now applied to the homomorphism produced by the rational point. Identifying the resulting extension with the special fiber also uses the good smooth-pair specialization hypotheses, as in SGA 1, Exposé XIII, §§2.8–2.10 (Grothendieck, 1971/2003). The parenthetical converse identifying this group condition with the domain of definition is consequently a claim about the given geometric section and its boundary behavior, not a formal consequence for every abstract section.

### RN093-02 — The pure-codimension-one exceptional locus [unproved claim; scan 093, manuscript p. 46, final paragraph; continued at the top of scan 094]

The assertion that “$D=S\setminus S^{\prime}$ est purement de codim $1$” follows if the rational map extends to the compactification and the pulled-back boundary equation remains a non-zero-divisor. In that situation the inverse image is an effective Cartier divisor, hence has pure codimension one; the local pullback condition is essential (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 01WQ). This adds a global divisor interpretation to the “indice de ramification” discussed in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 45. The parenthesis about $g\geq1$ or a one-dimensional base should therefore be kept as part of the author’s extension argument, rather than treating purity as a property of every indeterminacy locus on an arbitrary higher-dimensional base.

### RN093-03 — A normal-crossing test for intersecting exceptional components [own idea; scan 093, manuscript p. 46, question in approximately lines 12–14; compare scan 094]

The question “Et que se passe-t-il pour $s\notin S^{\prime}$ ?” suggests an editorial calculation at a regular point where the exceptional divisor has simple normal crossings. This adds local section data to the branch bookkeeping in `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 107, rather than assuming that a single rank-one inertia group captures every branch. If a boundary parameter pulls back as $u\prod_{j=1}^{r}t_j^{m_j}$, Kummer theory sends the $j$th tame inertia generator to $m_j$ times the target generator, while the unit supplies the residual Kummer class (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 03PK). Confidence is high for this explicit local calculation, but its globalization needs additional choices; Deligne’s higher-dimensional discussion in §15.2 is itself a proposed framework, whereas his subsequent detailed construction treats curves (Deligne, 1989).

### RN094-01 — Why the Kummer exponent is the boundary multiplicity [unproved claim; scan 094, approximately lines 1–2]

The identification with “sa multiplicité en $D_\alpha$” follows by writing $f^*q=u t^m$ in the discrete valuation ring at the generic point of $D_\alpha$. Pulling back $z^n=q$ makes the induced tame-inertia homomorphism multiplication by $m=v_{D_\alpha}(f^*q)$; the unit affects the splitting data but not that integer (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 03PK). This restates the positive ramification-index rule in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 45, and its Kummer-compatible morphism formula on scan 54. The argument verifies the asserted relationship without deciding whether the uncertain symbol on scan 093 should be read $\mu_\alpha$ or $n_\alpha$.

### RN094-02 — What is essentially canonical about the cusp subgroup [reading question; scan 094, last two manuscript lines]

The qualification “de façon ess. canonique” is naturally read at the level of a conjugacy class of peripheral subgroups, since a chosen geometric path is needed to place a particular $L_i$ inside a fixed based $\pi$. This restates the peripheral intersection $\mathcal E_i\cap\pi=L_i$ in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 53, while scan 69 adds the stronger transporter-reconstruction question. For hyperbolic curves in the relevant arithmetic setting, the peripheral decomposition subgroup is recovered as the normalizer of inertia (Tamagawa, 1997, Remark 7.5). That result explains the normalizer appearing here and on scan 099, but does not make every choice of a representative subgroup or of a cusp splitting canonical.

### RN095-01 — Dependence only on the first conormal class [unproved claim; scan 095, manuscript p. 47, approximately lines 4–9, including the insertion]

The inserted reference to “$\mathfrak m_{s_\alpha}/\mathfrak m_{s_\alpha}^{2}$” restates the explicit argument in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 77, where changing a uniformizer by $u\in 1+\mathfrak m$ leaves the associated section unchanged up to isomorphism. In residue characteristic zero, Hensel’s lemma gives unique $n$th roots in $1+\mathfrak m$, so the compatible Kummer covers for two equal first-order classes identify, as in the later tangent-base-point comparison (Deligne, 1989, §§15.23–15.25). `LM_140-3_text.tex`, scan 238, adds a sharper unresolved question within the supplied manuscript: whether a canonical normalizer section is associated with “l’uniformisante $z$, ou $-z$”, ending “Il faudrait y revenir !”. That contextual sign question does not alter the frozen insertion’s uncertain verb or conormal dual mark, and the Kummer class of a sign must be compared at the specified coefficient level rather than discarded.

### RN095-02 — Why a nonvanishing conormal section specializes [unpursued implication; scan 095, manuscript p. 47, approximately lines 9–13]

The condition “reste régulière, et inversible” means that the chosen rational conormal section supplies a nonzero conormal vector at each point under consideration. This adds variation along $D_\alpha$ to the pointwise torsor of “bases de $m/m^2$” in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 77. On a regular Cartier divisor, a local lift of that nonvanishing class is a transverse parameter, so the corresponding punctured formal-neighborhood construction can be specialized there; changes with the same first jet give the same tangential fiber functor (Deligne, 1989, §§15.23–15.25). This explains the stated open locus without extending the conclusion across zeros, poles, or singularities of the divisor.

### RN095-03 — Zeros and poles of the tangent datum [own idea; scan 095, manuscript p. 47, approximately lines 14–18]

The proposed study of what “se passe en les autres points” adds a specialization problem to `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 78, which already defers the functorial behavior of the distinguished tangent torsor. An editorial local test is to replace a nonzero conormal section $v$ by $h v$: on the common invertible locus, the change of splitting is the Kummer class of $h$. At a codimension-one zero or pole, its residue is the integer $v(h)$, so the failed nonvanishing condition leaves a calculable ramification contribution rather than a well-defined nonzero tangent vector (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 03PK). Confidence is high for this local statement because it is the same Kummer valuation computation as RN094-01; a global extension across singular strata is not asserted.

### RN096-01 — The formal pointed curve is the relevant local object [unproved claim; scan 096, approximately lines 1–7]

The reformulation in terms of “éléments de courbe” restates the local reduction in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 75, and adds the relative formal completion with its distinguished section. For a smooth curve, the punctured completion and its nonzero tangent line carry the covering-theoretic data used to define a tangential base point (Deligne, 1989, §§15.22–15.26). The statement should therefore be understood as a localization of the cusp-splitting problem, not as saying that the full global fundamental group is determined by one formal disk. The arithmetic descent data and the morphism from that local group into the global group still have to be retained.

### RN097-01 — A common language for subfields and residue fields [unpursued implication; scan 097, manuscript p. 48, approximately lines 9–16]

The desired “vue d’ensemble” can be organized by composing group homomorphisms over the arithmetic quotient, with residue-field maps supplied by decomposition groups rather than by nonexistent canonical inclusions $k(s)\subset K$. This adds a comparison between the local groupoid in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 26, and the conjugacy classification of splittings on scan 63 (“mod. autom. intérieurs”). For a fixed based extension, a splitting gives an actual conjugation action on its geometric kernel, whereas changing the base point or transport path changes the comparison by an inner automorphism. The latter dependence is explicit in SGA 1, Exposé XIII, §2.10, and explains why the author’s insistence on “effectives” requires compatible choices (Grothendieck, 1971/2003).

### RN098-01 — The finite residue extension does not canonically embed its Galois group [reading question; scan 098, opening margin and approximately lines 1–10]

The observation that the closed-point fields are “extensions finies de ce $k$” permits $G_{k(s)}$ to be identified with an open subgroup of $G_k$, after compatible embeddings of separable closures have been chosen. This restates the warning in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 73, that the residue algebraic closure is not canonically the previously chosen closure of the ground field. Taking the complete regular curve model organizes its places intrinsically, but does not remove the conjugacy choice of a lift $s^{\prime}$ or supply a preferred splitting of $D_{s^{\prime}}\to G_{k(s)}$ (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 0BSD). Consequently the current formulas should be compared as compatible based constructions or as conjugacy classes, according to which choices have been fixed.

### RN098-02 — Specializing a point and its actual action [unproved claim; scan 098, approximately lines 13–19, including the replacement of ext. by effective]

The assertion “est aussi de première espèce” is the functoriality of the rational-point section under a smooth-pair specialization for which $f$ extends to $s$. It adds this compatibility to the point-to-section construction of `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 63 (“une section”). Once the fundamental groups and paths are identified compatibly, the composed action is the action associated with $f(s)$; without those based identifications the invariant statement is equality up to inner conjugacy (Grothendieck, 1971/2003, Exposé XIII, §§2.8–2.10). The visible replacement of “ext.” by “effective” should be retained, while making these comparison choices explicit in the interpretation.

### RN099-01 — A boundary value produces a cuspidal action [unproved claim; scan 099, manuscript p. 49, approximately lines 1–4]

The phrase “normalisant un $L_i$” describes the action associated with a section whose extension to the compactification reaches a boundary point. This restates the peripheral compatibility in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 84, and connects the degeneration to the tangent-parametrized family on scan 79. The decomposition-group normalizer description supports the cuspidal interpretation (Tamagawa, 1997, Remark 7.5). The cusp can be determined up to conjugacy while the actual lift continues to depend on the tangent or Kummer splitting, so the passage does not select a unique actual action.

### RN099-02 — Good reduction must preserve the marked boundary [reading question; scan 099, manuscript p. 49, approximately lines 5–17; compare the margin of scan 103]

The acknowledgment that “bonne réduction” had been implicit needs the pointed-pair meaning: a proper smooth compactification together with a relatively étale boundary. This restates the finite étale marking condition in `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 5, and agrees with Tamagawa’s Definition 5.1 (1997). Smoothness of the compactification alone allows punctures to collide, and even a smooth nonproper model can lose punctures in its special fiber. The genus-zero case proposed in the scan 103 margin therefore tests a real missing boundary condition, as made explicit in RN103-01.

### RN099-03 — Computing the proposed modular cusp [unpursued implication; scan 099, manuscript p. 49, approximately lines 17–21; continued on scan 100, lines 1–7]

The proposed “courbe modulaire pour courbes elliptiques” can be tested at a split Tate cusp using $U=E_q\setminus\{O\}$ over $\mathbf Q((q))$. This adds a local degeneration calculation to the elliptic modular family in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 517. Tate’s torsion sequence $0\to\mu_n\to E_q[n]\to\mathbf Z/n\to 0$ has Kummer class $q$, so the induced inertia on $H_1(U_{\bar K},\mathbf Z_\ell)=T_\ell E_q$ is nontrivial unipotent monodromy (Tate, 1995, torsion discussion, manuscript p. 19). This verifies the author’s predicted nontriviality for the stated test case and supplies the more restrictive residual-action obstruction in RN103-05.

### RN100-01 — The nonabelian good-reduction conjecture [conjecture; scan 100, approximately lines 13–16; restated as a) and b) on scan 101, manuscript p. 50]

The conjecture “ssi il y a bonne réduction” has a precise later theorem for hyperbolic pointed curves over a discrete valuation field. Tamagawa proves that good reduction of the pair is equivalent to trivial outer inertia on one, equivalently every, prime-to-residue-characteristic pro-$\ell$ geometric fundamental group (Tamagawa, 1997, Theorem 5.3). This adds a group criterion to the marked-curve setting of `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 5, with its “fini étale” boundary. In the manuscript’s residue-characteristic-zero situation, this validates the nonabelian a)–b) comparison, without validating the proposed substitutions of $H_1$ in c) and d).

### RN100-02 — The scope of the marginal reference to VA [reading question; scan 100, lower-left diagonal margin]

The margin “dans le contexte des VA” plausibly alludes to the abelian-variety good-reduction criterion, but neither the abbreviation nor this autobiographical claim identifies a particular publication. Its terminology restates the use of “V.A.” and $\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}$ in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 64, but that passage concerns recognizing points and does not establish a curve-reduction theorem. The Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich–Serre–Tate criterion detects good reduction of an abelian variety from its prime-to-residue-characteristic Tate module; the precise statement was checked in Andreatta et al. (2015, Theorem 1.1), with Serre and Tate (1968) retained as the original bibliographic reference. For a proper curve this controls its Jacobian, and the compact-type example below prevents transferring the criterion to the curve itself.

### RN100-03 — A separating node invisible to the Jacobian [own idea; scan 100, lower-left diagonal margin; compare scan 102 c)–d) and scan 103, manuscript p. 51, opening lines]

The expectation “être connu sur $H_1$” invites the following editorial counterexample: join two elliptic curves over $\mathbf Q$ at rational points and smooth their single node over $\mathbf Q[[t]]$, locally by $xy=t$. This adds a reduction test to `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 12, whose formula $g=\sum g_\alpha+h_1$ separates the component genera from cycles in the dual graph. The graph is a tree, so the degree-zero Picard scheme has no toric part and the Jacobian has good reduction, whereas uniqueness of the stable model leaves the genus-two curve with its singular special fiber; its inertia on $H_1$ is therefore trivial despite bad curve reduction (Deligne & Mumford, 1969, Proposition 1.5, Theorem 1.11, Lemma 1.17, and Theorem 2.5; Grothendieck, 1971/2003, Exposé X, Corollary 3.9). Confidence is high because independent node smoothing, the graph–torus computation, and stable-model uniqueness give each step; removing one section through a smooth rational point leaves $H_1$ unchanged and gives an affine hyperbolic counterexample as well.

### RN101-01 — The missing geometric special fiber is not a realization obstruction [unproved claim; scan 101, manuscript p. 50, approximately lines 1–6]

The answer “Sans doute pas” to “est-elle admissible ?” is supported here only by asking which curve could realize the action if the expected smooth reduction does not exist. This restates the realization meaning of “action ext. admissible” in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 410, but does not force a realizing curve to be the literal special fiber of the given model. The ramified quadratic-twist calculation in RN103-03–RN103-04 supplies a different realizing curve over the residue field and therefore refutes that inference in the stated generality. The Tate-cusp calculation in RN103-05 gives a separate case where geometric realization really is obstructed, so bad reduction alone does not decide this question.

### RN101-02 — An arithmetization versus this prescribed action [reading question; scan 101, left diagonal margin]

The margin’s “arithmétisation” should not be treated as a definition of the uncertain adjective following it. In `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 403, an arithmetization is additional structure on a potential curve, accompanied by admissible-lift data, and scan 405 separately asks how to recover that structure. ### RN101-03 — Composing the splittings gives a cocycle-sensitive actual action [unpursued implication; scan 101, manuscript p. 50, approximately lines 7–10]

The question “qu’attendre d’une telle action ?” can first be separated into its formal and geometric parts. It restates the splitting framework of `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 63, while scan 413 adds an independent admissibility condition for actual lifts. After fixing one section $s$, another has the form $s^{\prime}(g)=c(g)s(g)$ with $c$ a continuous nonabelian $1$-cocycle in $\pi$, and its action is $\operatorname{Int}(c(g))\alpha_s(g)$. Thus changing the section of (31) preserves the outer action but can change the actual action and its point-theoretic interpretation; this is a direct calculation from multiplication in the extension.

### RN101-04 — Boundary strata organize the degeneration question [unpursued implication; scan 101, manuscript p. 50, approximately lines 11–13]

The phrase “structure à l’infini” connects the action problem to the marked dual graph of a stable degeneration. This restates the “maquette” in `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 11, and adds an arithmetic question to the three boundary partitions of the four-pointed line on scan 61. Independent node-smoothing parameters give local boundary directions, while the graph distinguishes a separating node from a loop contributing to the Jacobian’s toric part (Deligne & Mumford, 1969, Proposition 1.5 and Lemma 1.17). Consequently the graph supplies useful test cases for the conjectural equivalences without itself determining the entire arithmetic outer action.

### RN102-01 — The meaning of for every or one splitting [unproved claim; scan 102, approximately lines 1–3, including the insertion “ou un”]

The insertion “ou un” is justified, provided a splitting of (31) exists and the assertion means that its $T$-action is inner. The geometric hyperbolic group is center-free, so $\pi\simeq\operatorname{Inn}(\pi)$ identifies an inner-valued action of $T$ with a continuous homomorphism $T\to\pi$ (Tamagawa, 1997, Proposition 1.11). This restates the distinction between splittings and kernel conjugacy in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 63: the outer restriction is independent of the selected lift. It does not assert that the resulting homomorphism $T\to\pi$ is zero, and an existential “one splitting” should not be replaced by a vacuous universal statement when no splitting is available.

### RN102-02 — The coefficient prime matters in c) and d) [own idea; scan 102, conditions c) and d); compare scan 103, manuscript p. 51, first line]

The passage from full “$\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}=H_1$” in c) to one $\ell$-adic component in d) should keep the coefficient convention and residue characteristic explicit. `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 116, restates the boundary contribution to abelianization, while scan 84 adds its cyclotomic Galois action. Our mixed-characteristic check is the good pair $\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf Q_p}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$, whose $p$-adic homology $\mathbf Z_p(1)^2$ is ramified although its prime-to-$p$ homology is unramified, so an all-primes reading of c) cannot be equated with d) in the later $p$-adic parenthesis (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 03PK). Confidence is high because the two Kummer generators give these cyclotomic modules directly; in the original residue-characteristic-zero setting, RN100-03 and RN103-01 separately disprove d)$\Rightarrow$a).

### RN102-03 — What a p-divisible-group criterion can see [unpursued implication; scan 102, parenthetical NB in condition d)]

The suggestion of a criterion “sur les groupes $p$-divisibles” is appropriate to abelian-variety data, but the Jacobian’s Barsotti–Tate group alone cannot detect every node of the curve. The compact-type construction in RN100-03 also works over a suitable mixed-characteristic trait: its Jacobian is an abelian scheme although its curve is singular, reflecting the distinction $g=\sum g_\alpha+h_1$ in `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 12 (Deligne & Mumford, 1969). A genuinely nonabelian replacement is available for smooth proper curves of genus at least two with a regular semistable model and an integral base point over a complete characteristic-zero DVR with perfect residue field of characteristic $p$: good reduction is equivalent to crystallinity of the unipotent $p$-adic étale fundamental group (Andreatta et al., 2015, Theorems 1.6 and 1.9). This differs from testing only the Jacobian’s $p$-divisible group and should remain separate from the prime-to-$p$ outer-inertia criterion.

### RN102-04 — The valid implication from good reduction to residue admissibility [unproved claim; scan 102, condition e), approximately lines 10–17; compare scan 103, first paragraph]

For “Ladite extension est admissible”, the direction a)$\Rightarrow$e) has a direct interpretation: good reduction makes the outer $G_K$-action factor through the geometric action of $G_k$ on the special fiber. This restates “réalisable” in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 410, with smooth-pair specialization providing the realizing curve (Grothendieck, 1971/2003, Exposé XIII). Every splitting of (30) then pulls back the same outer action, and a center-free kernel makes that outer action determine the extension (Tamagawa, 1997, Lemma 7.1). The converse is false in the literal stated generality by RN103-03–RN103-04, even when e) is required for every splitting.

### RN102-05 — A finite residue extension is a different operation from ramified base change [reading question; scan 102, condition e), parenthetical about an open subgroup]

The allowance of “une extension finie de $k$” restricts the residual action to an open subgroup of $G_k$. This restates the open-subgroup issue in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 412, whose reverse direction is explicitly queried by “et inversement ?”. Such a restriction should not be identified with a ramified extension of the local generic field that kills its inertia: decomposition and residue quotients distinguish those operations (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 0BSD). For instance, the twist in RN103-03 becomes constant after adjoining $\sqrt t$, whereas its residual action is already geometric over $\mathbf Q$ before making any residue extension.

### RN102-06 — Condition f) is not yet a mathematical predicate [reading question; scan 102, final paragraph, condition f)]

The qualification “loufoque (à définir convenablement !)” means that f) is a proposed research direction rather than a defined condition that can be proved equivalent to a)–e). `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 413, adds a more explicit program by defining actual admissibility through drilling a puncture and proposing $\pi^\Gamma=(1)$ as a necessary property. These contextual ideas supply candidate constraints, but do not identify “pas trop loufoque” with any one of them. ### 134.O1 — own idea

**Location:** Scan 134; Main rows 2–7 and the pi2 obstruction display. **Source anchor:** `j'ai eu un peu peur d'y mettre un zéro à g`

Take $X=\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf Q}$, let $D\simeq\mathbf P^1$ be the exceptional divisor, and consider complex points: its normal bundle is $\mathcal O(-1)$, and $U=X\setminus D\simeq\mathbf P^2\setminus\{p\}$ (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 02OS). ### 134.O2 — own idea

**Location:** Scan 134; Upper pi2-to-fiber display and final vanishing argument. **Source anchor:** `\pi_2(\bar D_J)\longrightarrow`

In a complex torus-torsor model built from the nonzero normal line bundles $L_j$, the connecting homomorphism sends a spherical class $a$ to $(\langle c_1(L_j),a_*[S^2]\rangle)_{j\in J}$, up to the choices of orientations. Pulling back to $S^2$ reduces this to the degrees of the clutching maps, with first Chern class equal to the oriented Euler obstruction (Hatcher, 2017, Example 1.10 and Propositions 1.11,3.13(c),3.22). This adds a computable description of the missing map using exactly the normal-bundle data already present in `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 122–123, and refines its sufficient $\pi_2=0$ condition in scan 130 to vanishing of those evaluations. Confidence: high in that locally trivial complex model, because pullback and clutching compute the boundary map directly; an étale or formal analogue requires its own comparison and is not asserted here.

## D. Additional connections proposed by us

### X01 — Scan 133, opening paragraph and (37); own idea — Decorate the maquette by torus data

The question « quelle est sa signification topologique? » suggests replacing the bare incidence complex by a diagram carrying the fundamental groupoids of strata, the local lattices of branches, and the normal torus torsors, before taking any homotopy colimit. **Context comparison — adds:** `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 121–123 and 128–133, already supplies the lattices, torsors, flags, and the distinction between the undecorated and decorated groupoids; cote 149 adds the arithmetic maps into the fundamental group of the complement. For fine saturated log schemes locally of finite type over $\mathbf C$, Carchedi et al. (2017, introduction and Theorem 6.4) compare the Kato–Nakayama space and the infinite root stack after profinite completion, including torus fibers whose ranks follow the strata. **Our confidence: medium**, because these fibers match the manuscript’s local ingredients, whereas identifying its particular formal or étale object $U_\infty$ with the appropriate boundary of that construction still requires a comparison theorem and cannot follow from the incidence complex alone.

### X02 — Scan 136, (40) and lower diagonal margin; own idea — Arithmetic meaning of the toric fibers

The phrase « l'action extérieure “cyclotomique” » invites a comparison between the system of maps $T^J\to\pi_{U/K}$ and the stabilizers of all roots of the boundary divisors: in characteristic zero, their geometric fibers involve $\varprojlim_n\mu_n^J$, with the arithmetic cyclotomic action and, when branches are not globally labeled, their permutation action. **Context comparison — adds:** `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 121–123, describes the corresponding integral local systems and torus torsors, while `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 73, identifies the one-branch inertia with a Tate module in its discussion of tangential splittings. Talpo and Vistoli (2018, §3 and Theorem 6.22) identify the relevant Kummer étale topos with that of the infinite root stack, providing a candidate precise setting for this comparison. **Our confidence: medium**, since the local stabilizers and actions agree in the characteristic-zero normal-crossing case, but this alone neither proves that their maps into a global fundamental group are injective nor recognizes all valuations from that group.

### X03 — Scans 132–133, model independence and the punctured formal neighborhood; own idea — Preserve the distinction between the complement and its boundary

The claim that $\hat X\setminus D$ « a un caractère invariant » should be tested by comparing the pairs or boundary maps associated with different compactifications, keeping the boundary object separate from the homotopy type of the whole complement. **Context comparison — differs in scope:** `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 127–133, builds a system on open strata and their normal torsors, not merely a single invariant of the open variety; cote 149’s formulation should retain that additional relative information. Carchedi et al. (2025; §5 of the linked 2019 preprint) identify the profinite logarithmic homotopy type with the complement’s profinite étale homotopy type for fine saturated, log regular, locally Noetherian schemes over a characteristic-zero field, but that statement by itself does not identify a punctured formal neighborhood with the whole complement. **Our confidence: high** in this distinction, because for a punctured smooth complex curve the boundary is a disjoint union of circles while the open curve usually has a connected and different homotopy type; the precise compactification-independent boundary construction remains the proposed task.

### X04 — Scan 95, middle paragraph on the conormal section; own idea — Compare tangential splittings with normal torsors, including duality

The reference to $\mathfrak m_{s_\alpha}/\mathfrak m_{s_\alpha}^{2}$ and the « faisceau conormal » suggests tracing a chosen first-order parameter through the normal-torus construction used for higher-codimension boundary strata. **Context comparison — adds a link:** `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 73–75, treats Kummer torsors of tangential splittings, whereas `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 122–123, constructs punctured **normal** line bundles and their product torsors; the conormal line on scan 95 is their dual in the Cartier-divisor situation. A useful check is to compute the effect of $t\mapsto ut$ on the associated splitting class and to track the duality when comparing Chern classes, rather than identifying the two torsor conventions by notation alone (Talpo & Vistoli, 2018, §3, supplies a modern divisor-root framework). **Our confidence: medium**, because the first-order and line-bundle data are visibly present in both manuscripts, but a full identification must specify the parameter, basepoint, and duality conventions.
