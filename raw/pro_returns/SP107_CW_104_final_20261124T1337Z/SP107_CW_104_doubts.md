# Legibility and witness comparison

The main writing is generally legible at full resolution, but the page is not uniformly secure. Long pen returns, compressed word endings, interlinear insertions and crossed mathematical symbols account for most retained doubts. The weakest readings are the words hidden by heavy cancellation and the smallest handwritten indices; they remain marked in the transcription rather than completed from context.

Plain J can resemble I, and ξ may be reduced to a long stroke or an S-shaped descent. The edition distinguishes such recurrent formations from clearly written slips. A visible x in the defining rank expression, repeated primes, inconsistent cardinalities and missing letters or accents are kept. Marginal notes were rotated for reading; insertion leaders and underlines were traced separately from cancellations.

Witness C is substantially more faithful to the visible revision layers and abbreviated wording on these nine pages. Its preferred readings are nevertheless corrected where crops show otherwise, including places it did not flag. Witness H supplies useful independent alternatives but more often expands short forms, smooths grammar and punctuation, misses marginal or canceled writing, and confuses Greek letters or J/I. Agreement between the witnesses was never counted as visual evidence.

Confidence labels are local judgments about a recorded locus, not measured error probabilities. A retained alternative is an unresolved reading, even when its first option is preferred. The written blind readings and every adjudicated difference are preserved separately; their agreement does not make obscured ink certain.
