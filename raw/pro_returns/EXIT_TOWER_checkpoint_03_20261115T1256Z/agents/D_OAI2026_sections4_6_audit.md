## Lemma4.2, printed page13

The quasi-finite Bertini argument passes. In the incidence double fibre product, the unequal-image locus is irreducible of dimension `2a+N-2`, while the equal-image locus has dimension at most `a+N-1`, strictly smaller for a>=2. Every generic hyperplane-section component has dimension a-1, so no component of its generic square can be supported in the smaller locus. The generic square is irreducible, which gives geometric irreducibility of the generic section; the usual spreading open then gives the claimed general section. For its use on the root covers, the target is smooth locally closed and the cover is etale, so the required sections are also smooth. Possible purely inseparable issues in the bare geometric-irreducibility formulation do not enter that smooth application.

## Lemma4.1, printed pages12--15

The finite root-cover test preserves the *whole relation kernel*, not merely the independence of one selected class. The simultaneous q-root cover has components of common degree equal to the order of the generated Kummer subgroup. Spreading their idempotents and geometrically integral components, then applying Lemma4.2 to each one at every hyperplane step, preserves that degree and the number of components. Old relations specialize because the tested functions have value zero. Equal subgroup orders then force equality of the old and new relation kernels.

All support degrees are retained by exact divisor orders and transverse intersection with a fixed-embedding complete-intersection curve. The curve avoids the codimension-two discarded locus. The hyperplane non-zero-divisor conditions give its Hilbert polynomial as `Delta^(d-1) P_X`; since the final curve is smooth and integral, its genus is fixed independently of the functions and q. Intermediate normality is not needed: geometric irreducibility and smoothness at the flag's generic points suffice, and the final curve avoids the original bad locus.

The valuation realization of a good fibre is valid. A valuation of the algebraically closed constant field dominates the chosen local spreading ring; a further valuation of its residue field reduces the constants to an algebraic closure of a finite field without changing the center. A Gauss extension to a separating rational subfield, followed by prolongation across the preserved irreducible polynomial, has full residue degree. The fundamental inequality then forces ramification index one and no remaining defect. The successive geometric hyperplane valuations supply exactly the required relative rank and residue transcendence degree. This also deals with a constant field of arbitrary cardinality: every finite test imposes only finitely many open conditions, which have points over an infinite algebraically closed field.

## Theorem5.1, printed pages15--17

The five-anchor estimate passes. The anchor classes give a noncyclic finite Kummer subgroup at all sufficiently high levels because distinct point valuations of k(t) prolong with nonzero integer ramification factors. The key exact normalization is justified: if `v(k(t))/vk` were nonzero, it would be cyclic and the residue of k(t) would equal kv by the valuation transcendence inequality. Every constant-adjusted unit would then be principal, so evaluation on D_v would factor through that one cyclic relative value group, contradicting the noncyclic finite test. Thus `v(k(t))=vk` exactly.

This order matters. At the outset the chosen representative on L is a unit only modulo the chosen Kummer level; it would be invalid to assume actual unit status for its completed class. The proof instead obtains exact unit status for all t-a on K from the preceding noncyclic argument. After that, the matched inertia annihilates their images exactly, and division by q is legitimate because the evaluation modules are torsion-free. The later residual class comparison is therefore justified.

Unequal valuations of two t-a make one a principal unit after a constant adjustment, contradicting its nonzero evaluation. Coincident residual anchor values make their quotient principal, contradicting distinct evaluations. The common value can therefore be removed by one constant. In characteristic zero, including0,1,ell among the anchors forces residue characteristic different from ell. The matching finite-constant point-inertia data then give equal genera by the finite-level curve kernel.

After removing the inseparable p-power, each of the five distinct anchor fibres contributes at least `(1-1/ell)n-N_i` to the ramification lower bound. The different is at least e-1 even for wild ramification, so Riemann--Hurwitz gives `(3-5/ell)n <= 2G_X-2+sum N_i`. The coefficient is positive even for ell=2. Every additional tested member has support in two fibres, at most2n points. The bound is independent of that member and of the Kummer level. Increasing finite-level supports then gives the exact support bound. I found no unproved uniformity step in this argument.

## Lemmas6.2--6.3, printed pages18--20

The infinitely many detected divisors in6.2 follow from the infinite mod-ell rank of the pencil and the finite-dimensional kernel of divisor evaluation on a smooth dense open. The latter uses ordinary finite étale cohomology with ell invertible, so wild p-torsion does not enter. The residual q-th-power assertion follows from the local correspondence: the corresponding quasi-divisor values the curve field nontrivially, so its residue is the constant residue field; zero inertia evaluation then makes the class a principal unit after constants, hence annihilated by decomposition evaluation.

The potentially delicate fixed-incidence-curve step in6.3 passes. Bounded-degree integral divisors lie in finitely many Hilbert schemes; take one positive-dimensional integral parameter locus with a dense set of the chosen divisors and geometrically integral fibres. Every integral curve T' in that locus still has a dominant incidence map to X, since a proper closed subset cannot contain infinitely many distinct prime divisors. Thus its incidence function field M is finite over l(X), and M/l(T') is regular.

For one test (g,q), work on the relative smooth unit locus Y. The dense tested fibres make the q-root cover geometrically split over the generic point of T. Descend this splitting to a finite constant extension of l(T), normalize T in that extension, and pull back Y. The pullback is normal (smooth over a normal base) and integral (flat with geometrically integral generic fibre). A generically split finite etale cover of this normal integral scheme splits everywhere by finite-birational normality. The finite normalization is surjective, so the generic geometric point of the *already fixed* T' lifts. Restriction proves the desired power assertion in the algebraic constant extension of M. The auxiliary normalization may depend on (g,q), but T', M, and P0 do not. Hence there is no intersection of infinitely many open conditions and no uncountability premise hidden here.

## Lemmas6.4--6.5 and Theorem6.1, printed pages20--22

The regular-extension Kummer descent in6.4 is the explicit Hilbert90 cocycle argument. Its uniqueness follows from relative algebraic closedness. The finite-rank intersection claim in6.5 uses the two-dimensional product of the two curve fields and extensions of each vertical point valuation; multiplication by its positive ramification factor is injective on Z_ell. The common completed classes have all point orders zero and lie in the finite-rank Picard Tate module.

For6.1, uniqueness at each finite Kummer level makes the constant classes compatible in the inverse limit. Passing to a finite normal extension, the infinite-rank common image forces the curve subfield to be Galois stable. Its intersection with L has transcendence degree one. The norm takes this curve field into the descended one, and norm after inclusion is multiplication by the total finite degree. Saturation of a relatively algebraically closed curve field's completion removes that integer factor. Applying the inverse isomorphism then gives equality, and the intersection lemma gives uniqueness. All field inclusions and norm maps are specified in the proof.

## Propositions6.6--6.7, printed pages22--23

The divisorial criterion passes: for a valuation trivial on constants, choose a curve subfield with transcendental residue, so decomposition evaluation injects its completion. For a valuation nontrivial on constants, every curve field contains a nonconstant principal unit `1+cx` with nonzero completed class. Point inertia lines then arise by prolonging each curve point valuation; saturation removes the ramification factor. The induced quotient maps are the actual duals of the completed subfield inclusions, so the displayed diagrams commute. Point-order kernels recover genus, hence exactly the rational curve subfields.
