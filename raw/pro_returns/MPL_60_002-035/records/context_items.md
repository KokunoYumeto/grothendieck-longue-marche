### C02 — Numerical semisimplicity is a distinct published theorem

**Witness:** 60/009 and 017, coefficient hypotheses; CT 16/049; **kind:** unpursued implication / literature comparison; **quote:** CT “the category of motives which is semi-simple !”. **Context query:** `semi-simple|semisimple|numérique|homologique`; **comparison:** cote 60 adds determinant tests but no equivalence-of-cycle-relations theorem. **RS:** WG2 §2.3 #7; MPL_14_087-132 M09 and MPL_14_002-044 M29 are first-pass distinctions in the register.

Jannsen (1992, Theorem 1) proves semisimplicity for the category of pure numerical motives with characteristic-zero coefficients, without proving equality of numerical and homological equivalence. These published statements should remain distinct from the manuscript's assumed polarized realizations and from Deligne's geometric semisimplicity of pure lisse sheaves.

### C04 — Oda answers the filtered de Rham question from the earlier packet

**Witness:** CT 54/007, compared with 60/009 and 017; **kind:** unpursued implication / literature comparison; **quote:** “Cette extension peut-elle se déduire de la connaissance de \(p^A\) ??” **Context query:** `Oda|p-torsion|de Rham|54:7|D05`; **comparison:** CN D05 had already proposed this connection as a first-pass lead; the present work verifies the exact published theorem and its scope. **RS:** no direct Oda theorem entry is supplied; this does not advance WG2's category-construction items.

For an abelian variety A over a perfect field of characteristic p, Oda (1969, Corollary 5.11, p. 131) canonically identifies \(M(A[p])\) with \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(A)\) and matches \(VM(A[p])\) with \(H^0(A,\Omega^1)\). Thus the finite group **scheme**, with its Dieudonné structure, determines this filtered first cohomology, whereas its geometric points alone do not. Identifying this filtered cohomology with the particular restricted Lie/group extension on CT 54/007, including its p-operation and variance, remains a separate comparison; no canonical Hodge splitting or ℓ-adic boundary theorem is asserted.

### C05 — Cartier duality has a precise Dieudonné-module counterpart

**Witness:** CT 54/005, compared with 60/009 and 017; **kind:** unpursued implication / literature comparison; **quote:** CT “dualité de Cartier”. **Context query:** `Cartier|Frobenius|Oda|D03|D04`; **comparison:** CN D03 proposed the Cartier-duality/annihilator interpretation, while D04 concerns restricted Lie algebras and height-one groups; this note verifies the module-duality theorem without certifying every earlier twist calculation. **RS:** first-pass MPL_14_002-044 A36 is thematic only; no exact matching settled entry.

For finite commutative p-primary group schemes over a perfect characteristic-p field, Oda (1969, Theorem 3.19, p. 89) identifies Cartier duality with the corresponding duality on Dieudonné modules, providing a primary anchor for the earlier question. The resulting orthogonality calculations still require the precise Frobenius twists, variance and F/V actions; an analogy with the alternating ℓ-adic pairing of cote 60 does not supply those details. This is a connection between duality formalisms, not an identification of Oda's semilinear operators with the scalar-field Frobenius used in the L-functions.
