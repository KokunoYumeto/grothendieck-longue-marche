# Legibility and limits

All 43 kept scans were read directly in one transcription stream from each whole image and its supplied halves, without OCR; every page was then visually audited again before the context was opened. The printed notices are mostly clear, although damaged type, joined typing, signatures, an incomplete stamp and one pasted label require local doubts. The handwritten mathematics is much less uniform: the densest subgroup lattices and their pale pencil additions (especially 126 and 134–140), heavily cancelled calculations (131, 154, 156, 158 and 160), and overwritten indices and arrow ends remain the least secure portions. The interval glyph in 142–148, ornamental group letters in 126/129/131, epsilon/xi forms in 154, and clipped final line in 164 have not been resolved by mathematical expectation. Drawings are line-for-line TikZ reconstructions at schematic scale; this captures their observed contours, parts, arrows and labels but is not a photographic facsimile. No prose or mathematical item has been replaced with an image, a summary or a placeholder, and no mathematical uncertainty has been encoded as illegible. Archive foliation is preserved as margin text; the record does not infer autograph pagination or a date for the mathematical writing from a reused notice. A clean LaTeX compile checks syntax only and does not certify a difficult reading. The primary readings in this final register supersede first-pass variants; the audit changes are recorded in progress.log.
