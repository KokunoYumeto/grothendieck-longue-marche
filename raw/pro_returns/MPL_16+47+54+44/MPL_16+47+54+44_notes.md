### R5 — Own idea: the elementary blow-up step in resolution independence

**Kind:** own idea.

**Location:** cote 16, scan 19, the sentence after (8.16). **Manuscript:** “indépendante de la résolution X′ choisie”.

For a blow-up of a regular surface at a rational point on the exceptional configuration (as in the algebraically closed residue-field setting), the new intersection matrix has the form
\[

Since \(S\) is unimodular and the added rank-one summand has trivial discriminant group, this step preserves the discriminant pairing in R4. The calculation isolates a concrete part of the independence claim, while reduction of arbitrary resolutions to such steps requires the relevant surface hypotheses and a common-resolution argument.

**Location:** cote 16, scan 54, the proposed proof of the lemma on pages 10–11 of the reused text. **Manuscript:** “P≠0”; “On a donc x∈R tel que P(x)≠0”.

### R10 — Own idea: isolate the adjunction in the injective-sheaf argument

**Kind:** own idea.

**Location:** cote 16, scan 37, proof of Lemma 3.3. **Manuscript:** “the same thing as to give a homomorphism from the stalk”.

Let \(i_x:(\{x\},\mathcal O_{X,x})\to(X,\mathcal O_X)\) be the canonical morphism of ringed spaces and let \(I\) be an injective \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\)-module; the adjunction gives
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}(F,i_{x*}I)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}(F_x,I).
\]
Since taking a stalk is exact, the right side is exact contravariantly in \(F\), proving that \(i_{x*}I\) is injective among all \(\mathcal O_X\)-modules. For the particular injective hull \(I=E(k(\mathfrak p))\) used on the page, the distinct geometric step is the identification \(\widetilde I\simeq i_{x*}I\): the displayed filtration reduces it to modules annihilated by powers of \(\mathfrak p\), for which localization is zero when the inverted element belongs to \(\mathfrak p\) and is an isomorphism when it does not. This separates the general adjunction argument from the noetherian algebra needed to establish that identification.

**Editorial confidence:** high. **Reason:** the argument uses exactness of stalks and an explicit localization test; it does not infer that an arbitrary quasi-coherent sheaf is injective.

### R15 — Own idea: a transparent test case for transitivity of the different

**Kind:** own idea.

**Location:** cote 16, scan 52, §2, no. 3. **Manuscript:** “la formule de transitivité marche”.

\[

\]
Consequently the Kähler differents satisfy \(\mathfrak D_{C/A}=(f_xg_y)=\mathfrak D_{B/A}C\cdot\mathfrak D_{C/B}\) in this explicit finite free complete-intersection example, by taking the maximal minors of the presentation. ### S3 — A conditional integral matrix-algebra model

**Kind:** own idea. **Status:** editorial. **Location:** cote 16, scan 14, opening question on relations; scan 15, matrix-unit definitions and multiplication table. **Confidence:** high under the stated unit-shift assumptions, because the formulas can be checked on every basis vector.

Take one free string \(S_i=\bigoplus_{a=0}^{n-i}\mathbb Z v_{i,a}\) for each \(0\leq i\leq n\), put \(\deg v_{i,a}=i+2a\), and let \(L\) and \(\Lambda\) move one step up and down with coefficient one and kill the corresponding endpoints. With \(e=1-L\Lambda\), \(r=n-i\), define
\[
e_i=e\Lambda^rL^re-e\Lambda^{r+1}L^{r+1}e,
\qquad E_i^{ba}=L^be_i\Lambda^a;
\]
then \(e_i\) selects the bottom of the string of length \(r+1\), and \(E_i^{ba}\) sends only \(v_{i,a}\) to \(v_{i,b}\). Consequently
\[
\mathbb Z[L,\Lambda]
 =\prod_{i=0}^{n}\operatorname{Mat}_{n-i+1}(\mathbb Z),
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}=\sum_{s=1}^{n+1}s^2
 =\frac{(n+1)(n+2)(2n+3)}6,
\]
and the degree projectors are the integral polynomials \(\pi_k=\sum_{i+2a=k}E_i^{aa}\). This proves a concrete normalized model; identifying it with the manuscript's integral exterior-algebra order would require the additional comparison excluded in item S2.

### S11 — Cayley–Hamilton gives the algebraic inverse conditionally

**Kind:** own idea. **Status:** editorial verification. **Location:** cote 16, scan 48, proof from \(uv=w\); scan 49, opening continuation. **Confidence:** high, by direct polynomial substitution.

For the source's maps \(u:H^{2n-i}\to H^i\), \(v=L^{n-i}\), let \(w=uv\) and write its rational characteristic polynomial as \(P(T)=T^b+c_{b-1}T^{b-1}+\cdots+c_0\), with \(c_0\ne0\). Then

\bigl(w^{b-1}+c_{b-1}w^{b-2}+\cdots+c_1\operatorname{id}\bigr),
\qquad v^{-1}=w^{-1}u,

The argument does not assert that the chosen cycle acts as zero in all other degrees, exactly as the proposition's parenthesis warns. This verifies the intended polynomial argument without changing the overwritten \(u/w\) powers in the source.

### S13 — Positivity kills the radical once the rational algebra is finite

**Kind:** own idea. **Status:** editorial verification. **Location:** cote 16, scan 49, final request for semisimplicity from \(C(X)+I(X\times X)\). **Confidence:** high under the stated algebraic hypotheses, by the following elementary argument.

Let a finite-dimensional rational algebra \(R\) have a faithful finite-dimensional realization, an involution \(*\), a rational-valued trace, and \(\operatorname{Tr}(x^*x)>0\) for every nonzero \(x\in R\); its Jacobson radical is involution-stable and nilpotent, so \(x^*x\) has trace zero for \(x\) in the radical, forcing that radical to vanish. Finite dimension over \(\mathbb Q\) should not be inferred merely from inclusion in matrices over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\); however, a rational positive trace pairing bounds the size of any rationally independent family by the ambient matrix dimension, since its Gram determinant is nonzero. This supplies the elementary algebra after the geometric work has produced the appropriate adjoint and rational positive pairing. Kleiman (1968, §§3.11–3.14) establishes the conditional positivity, semisimplicity, and splitting route.

### S14 — An injective map needs an algebraic splitting

**Kind:** own idea. **Status:** editorial clarification. **Location:** cote 16, scan 49, paragraph beginning “The same result should hold in fact if \(u\) is a monomorphism”. **Confidence:** high for the conditional algebra argument; the geometric splitting hypotheses remain essential.

The previous isomorphism argument cannot simply be applied to an injection between spaces of different dimensions, because no inverse exists on the whole target. If the correspondence algebra of the direct sum of source and target, with the required Tate twist, is semisimple, its matrix-block structure gives a generalized inverse \(g\) with \(fgf=f\), and injectivity of the realized \(f\) then yields \(gf=1\) on the source. The corner containing \(g\) is the required algebraic left inverse; surjections are treated with the opposite equation. This pinpoints why information about the ambient correspondence algebra and its products is needed, whereas semisimplicity of an isolated endomorphism algebra would not by itself control all maps between two varieties.

### S15 — Numerical motives have a later unconditional theorem

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Location:** cote 16, scan 49, parenthetical “category of motives which is semi-simple”.

Jannsen (1992, Theorem 1) proves, for characteristic-zero coefficient fields and adequate equivalence relations, that the category of pure motives is semisimple abelian precisely when the equivalence relation is numerical. Thus numerical motives provide a later unconditional realization of the broad semisimplicity goal, without assuming the standard conjectures. The manuscript's actual operators, however, act on a chosen cohomology theory and are taken modulo homological equivalence, so transferring the conclusion to that category requires identifying the relevant equivalence relations. Quotienting by numerical equivalence changes the problem unless that identification has been established.

### S16 — A nilpotent kernel is not a zero kernel

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Location:** cote 16, scan 49, semisimplicity request; scan 44, comparison with numerical equivalence implicit in the index discussion.

Under algebraicity of the Künneth components, Jannsen (1992, Corollary 1) identifies the kernel from homological to numerical degree-zero correspondence algebras with the Jacobson radical and proves it nilpotent. This isolates a precise obstruction to obtaining the draft's cohomological semisimplicity from numerical semisimplicity. Nilpotence alone permits a nonzero ideal, so it is not a proof that the two equivalence relations coincide; an additional positivity or equivalence argument must annihilate that kernel. The distinction also prevents treating a numerical projector as a canonical cohomological projector solely because some lift exists.

### S17 — Finite-field Künneth projectors illustrate a narrower later result

**Editorial confidence: high**, because dualizing the isogeny factorization supplies the annihilator statement; equal subgroup orders alone would not prove orthogonality.

### D04. Cote 54, scan 7, restricted Lie-algebra paragraph and boxed insertion

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “extension de groupes radiciels”. **Status:** established correspondence under a height restriction.

Over a field of characteristic $p>0$, restricted Lie algebras correspond to formal groups of Frobenius height at most one; finite-dimensional Lie algebras give the finite height-one case (Gabriel, 1970/2010, Corollary 4.4.1).
Consequently the $p$-operation, including the inserted assertion that it vanishes on $t_A^\vee$, is essential data for the proposed group extension.
This correspondence does not say that a Lie algebra without its restricted operation determines an arbitrary finite $p$-group scheme, or that $A[p]$ itself necessarily has Frobenius height one.

### D05. Cote 54, scan 7, final question

**Kind:** conjecture. **Quote:** “Cette extension peut-elle se déduire de la connaissance de $p^A$ ??”. **Status:** reconstruction theorem for filtered cohomology under stated hypotheses.

For an abelian variety over a perfect field of characteristic $p$, Oda (1969, Corollary 5.11) canonically identifies $M(A[p])$ with $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(A)$ as Dieudonné modules and identifies the corresponding exact sequence with the Hodge sequence.
Thus the finite group scheme, with its Dieudonné information, reconstructs the filtered cohomology asked about; its abstract group of geometric points is insufficient.
Comparing this with the particular restricted Lie extension on the sheet still requires matching the semilinear operator and the covariant/contravariant conventions, rather than discarding $F$ and $V$ after taking the underlying vector space.

### D06. Cote 54, scans 4 and 6, reused typescript: generic freeness and Proposition A

**Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “$M_f$ soit libre sur $A_f$”. **Status:** separate established statements, not an identification of provenance.

Generic freeness applies to a finite module over a finite-type algebra over a Noetherian domain after inverting one nonzero base element (The Stacks Project Authors, 2026, Tag 051R).
The manuscript's further universal flatness condition is stronger: a flattening stratum must represent the appropriate base-change condition, not merely exhibit one dense open flat locus (The Stacks Project Authors, 2026, Tag 05P7).
Editorially, the replacement involving a finite affine cover should be checked against finite presentation and the simultaneous shrinking needed on all chosen charts; the cancelled stronger wording supplies no proof by itself.
**Confidence: high**, from the difference between existence on an open set and a universal property.

### D07. Cote 44, scan 3, cancelled completion argument and final marginal assertion

**Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “est exact et fidèle”. **Status:** editorial test of the missing hypotheses.

Noetherian adic completion is flat, while the standard sufficient condition for faithful flatness requires the ideal to lie in the Jacobson radical (The Stacks Project Authors, 2026, Tags 00MB–00MC).
For $R=k[x]$, $I=(x)$ and $M=R/(x-1)$, one has $M\ne0$ but $M\otimes_Rk[[x]]=0$, since $x-1$ becomes invertible.
This does not refute the sheet's graded claim: it shows why its grading and unresolved “TF” qualification cannot be omitted when reconstructing an argument for faithfulness.
**Confidence: high**, by direct tensor computation.

### D08. Cote 44, scan 2, Theorem 3; scan 8, first paragraph of the letter

**Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “une relation d'équivalence que je ne sais pas bien identifier”. **Status:** comparison requiring a universal property.

A modern candidate is the algebraic representative, characterized by universality among regular homomorphisms from algebraically trivial cycle classes, with regularity tested on algebraic parameter families (Achter et al., 2023, §1).
Over $\mathbb C$, the Abel–Jacobi image of algebraically trivial cycles is a polarized abelian subvariety $J_a^{2i-1}$ of the intermediate Jacobian (Example 1.14).
The ambient intermediate Jacobian is a complex torus and must not automatically be called an abelian variety; neither dimension agreement nor the manuscript's provisional terminology identifies its $J^i$ with a universal representative.

### D09. Cote 44, scan 2, Theorem 2 and its implication for Theorem 3

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “majorée par un nb ne dépendant que de $X$”. **Status:** later proof in codimension two.

For a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field, Kahn (2021, Theorem 0.1 and its proof) establishes the codimension-two universal regular homomorphism and the bound $2\dim B\le b_3(X)$ for surjective regular homomorphisms to abelian varieties $B$, repairing a gap in Murre's proof.
The argument controls prime-to-characteristic torsion and explicitly addresses the fact that the point map induced by a correspondence need not be a group homomorphism.
This verifies a concrete instance of the manuscript's bounded-dimension strategy, but does not prove existence, the stated bounds, or canonical complementary dualities in every codimension.

### D10. Cote 44, scan 8, parenthesis strengthening the Tate-module statement

**Kind:** conjecture. **Quote:** “c'est même un sous-module dudit”. **Status:** hypothesis-dependent later comparison.

For smooth proper $X/K$, a surjective regular homomorphism $\Phi:\mathcal A^i_{X/K}\to B$, and a miniversal cycle $Z\in\mathcal A^i_{X/K}(B)$ defined over $K$ with $\Phi(B)(Z)=[r]_B$ for a positive integer $r$, Achter et al. (2023, Proposition 8.2) construct an injection
\[
T_\ell B\hookrightarrow H^{2i-1}(X_{K^{\mathrm{sep}}},\mathbb Z_\ell(i))/\mathrm{torsion}
\quad(i=1,2,\dim X),\qquad \ell\nmid r,\quad\ell\ne\operatorname{char}(K),
\]
where in characteristic zero the characteristic restriction is vacuous.
The selected cycle, excluded primes, and torsion-free quotient are material qualifications: this result cannot simply be substituted for the letter's unrestricted integral submodule expectation.

### D11. Cote 44, scan 8, proposed general intermediate Jacobians

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “Je suis moralement sûr”. **Status:** later obstruction to one possible identification.

Ottem and Suzuki (2020, Corollaries 1.2 and 4.2) construct a complex fourfold for which the Abel–Jacobi map on algebraically trivial codimension-three cycles is not the universal regular homomorphism.
In their example it factors through a universal regular homomorphism followed by an isogeny with nontrivial kernel.
Thus existence of an algebraic representative and identification with the algebraic Abel–Jacobi image are different questions; the example obstructs their blanket identification, not existence in that example, and does not determine the manuscript's unspecified equivalence relation.

### D12. Cote 44, scan 4, Theorem 5 and fibration margin

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “$\langle\zeta,\zeta'\rangle=(-1)^m Z\mathbin{.}Z'$”. **Status:** proposed identity, with a later comparison in another setting.

Künnemann (2001, Theorem 1.6) constructs height pairings for homologically trivial cycles on abelian varieties over number fields, extending the divisor–zero-cycle Néron–Tate pairing; the construction uses regular models and extensions orthogonal to cycles in special fibres.
This is a useful comparison for the manuscript's fibration strategy, but does not establish its formula for a generic member of a pencil on an arbitrary projective variety, nor its sign or positivity.
**Editorial confidence: high** that a proof must specify height normalization and demonstrate invariance under changes of extensions by vertical cycles, because these choices affect the proposed intersection calculation.

### D13. Cote 44, scan 8, primitive-decomposition margin; scan 9, divisor correspondence

**Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “A prouver que cette dernière est positive !”. **Status:** editorial obstruction test, not a resolution.

For the proposed $D=r_*(p^*z\cdot q^*z)$, descent to $J=A/N$ and symmetry do not themselves supply the missing ampleness argument.
Indeed, if $\lambda_L$ is a polarization on a positive-dimensional abelian variety, $-\lambda_L=\lambda_{L^{-1}}$ is still a symmetric isogeny, while $L^{-1}$ has negative degree on curves and is not ample.
This tests the sufficiency of symmetry and nondegeneracy, not the particular cycle construction; the sheet's primitive-decomposition warning and the proposed test family $T=\operatorname{Pic}^0(Y)$ are therefore substantive places to examine the sign and positivity.
**Confidence: high**, from the explicit inverse-line-bundle calculation; positivity of this particular $D$ remains unresolved here.

### D14. Cote 44, scan 6, Theorem 7 and “Matsusaka” margin

**Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “L'équivalence numérique $=$ $\tau$-équivalence”. **Status:** valid divisor comparison; contrary evidence for an unrestricted interpretation.

Grothendieck (1962, §4, p. 242), crediting Matsusaka, identifies numerical and torsion equivalence for divisors on smooth projective varieties.
If the sheet's undefined $\tau$ means that some nonzero multiple is algebraically trivial, its assertion in arbitrary codimension fails: Clemens (1983, Theorem 0.2 and equation (0.1)) gives a very general quintic threefold with non-torsion homologically trivial cycles modulo algebraic equivalence.
Such cycles are numerically trivial, so they contradict that unrestricted interpretation; the qualification about $\tau$ is essential and supplies no license to alter the diplomatic text.

### D15. Cote 54, scan 8, reused sheet, Corollaries 2–3

**Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “Il suffit d'appliquer la prop. 10”. **Status:** elementary editorial comparison; provenance unassigned.

For a locally finite arrangement of affine hyperplanes in a finite-dimensional real space, compactness of a segment reduces its crossings to finitely many hyperplanes.
At a point in the relative interior of a wall and on no other hyperplane, a sufficiently small ball has exactly two local sides; connectedness of that relative interior then explains constancy of the adjacent chamber in Corollary 3.
**Confidence: high** under these explicit topological hypotheses; this argument does not identify the sheet's author, publication, or a reflection-group structure.

### D16. Cote 16, scan 25, boxed associativity condition and final two conditions

### D17. Cote 16, scan 27, isotropic-line special case

**Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “une droite isotrope $D$”. **Status:** explicit editorial realization of the stated algebraic case.

Over $\mathbb Q$, take $\widetilde W=\mathbb Q\xi\oplus\mathbb Q w$ with $\widetilde Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, any symplectic form $\varphi$ on an even-dimensional $V$, and $\alpha(x\wedge y)=\varphi(x,y)(\xi+w)$.
Then $\alpha^*\widetilde Q=0$, but $\widetilde Q(\xi,\xi)=1$ and $\widetilde Q(\alpha(x\wedge y),\xi)=\varphi(x,y)$, so the two nondegeneracy requirements hold while $\chi=0$.
**Confidence: high**, by these identities; the example distinguishes nondegeneracy of the ambient form from that of $\chi'$ and asserts no geometric realization.

### D18. Cote 16, scan 29, tensor square and orbit assertion

**Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “dim non bornée”. **Status:** editorial distinction and normalization warning.

For fixed finite $V$, Cayley–Hamilton bounds every cyclic span in $\bigwedge^2V$ by $\binom{\dim V}{2}$, although the orbit as a set can be infinite over an infinite field; over a finite field every such orbit is finite.
The manuscript's unboundedness must therefore be assessed as $\dim V$ grows, as its parenthesis indicates.
The tensor square in $\bigwedge^*V\otimes\bigwedge^*V$ also requires a stated multiplication and antisymmetrization convention: multiplying an operator by a nonzero scalar preserves its cyclic spans but can change finite-orbit behavior.
**Editorial confidence: high**, from finite-dimensional linear algebra; the following tests use an explicit normalized operator, not an imposed reading of $u$.

### D19. Cote 16, scan 29, “$4$ (ou $6$?)”: a conditional six-dimensional test

### D20. Cote 16, scan 29, final growing-dimension parenthesis

**Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “quand” [uncertain reading] “$\dim V$”. **Status:** conditional unbounded-family construction.

For any $m\ge2$, start with the $2m\times2m$ block matrix $C_0=\operatorname{diag}(d_j\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix})_{j=1}^m$, with distinct rational $1\le d_j\le2$; the all-ones vector has cyclic span $m$, by a Vandermonde minor.
Replace every off-block zero by a sufficiently small positive rational $\varepsilon$, obtaining $C_\varepsilon$, and put $s=\mathbf1^tC_\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf1$, $C=sC_\varepsilon$, $\xi=C^{-1}\mathbf1$; invertibility, positivity of $s$, and that nonzero minor persist.
On $V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2m}(\mathbb Qe_i\oplus\mathbb Qf_i)$ use $p_i=e_i\wedge f_i$ for coordinates, define $\chi=\sum_{i<j}C_{ij}e_i^*\wedge f_i^*\wedge e_j^*\wedge f_j^*$ and $Q(a,b)=\chi(a\wedge b)$, and obtain
\[
\det Q=\det C\prod_{i<j}C_{ij}^{4}\ne0,\qquad
Q(\xi,\xi)=1,\qquad
\varphi(x,y):=Q(x\wedge y,\xi)=\sum_i(e_i^*\wedge f_i^*)(x,y),\qquad
U|_{\langle p_i\rangle}=C,\qquad U\xi=\mathbf1,
\]
where $Q^\flat(a)=Q(\,·\,,a)$, $\varphi^\sharp=(v\mapsto\varphi(\,·\,,v))^{-1}$, and $U=(\bigwedge^2\varphi^\sharp)Q^\flat$.
The cyclic spans therefore have dimension at least $m$ as $\dim V=4m$ grows, and the positive normalized row sums exceed one for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, giving infinite set-orbits too; **editorial confidence: high** for this normalized construction, by the nonzero minor and block determinants, without settling additional Hodge or geometric constraints.
