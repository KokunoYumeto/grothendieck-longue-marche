# Independent augmentation-ideal proof of the class-17 upper bound

Let `F` be the free group on `x,y`, let `Λ` be the kernel of the parity
of `y`, and let `H` be the kernel of `Λ → Λab/4`.  The Schreier basis of
`Λ` is `x, yxy^-1, y²`.  Consequently the actual quotient `P=F/H` is

\[
 P=(\mathbf Z/4\times\mathbf Z/4)\rtimes\mathbf Z/8,
 \qquad
 (a,b,k)(c,d,l)=\big((a,b)+\tau^k(c,d),k+l\big),
 \quad \tau(c,d)=(d,c).
\]

The first two coordinates are reduced modulo four and the last modulo
eight.  The generators are `x=(1,0,0)` and `y=(0,0,1)`.  The even-last-
coordinate subgroup is exactly `Λ/H ≅ (Z/4)^3`.  This gives the actual
quotient map, including its kernel.

Write `R=F₂[P]` and `I=ker(R→F₂)`.  The retained independent certificate
computes `I^k` in the actual regular module by the exact operations
`v ↦ v+xv` and `v ↦ v+yv`.  At every stage their span is `I^(k+1)`:
the group is generated by `x,y`, and
`(gh−1)v=(g−1)hv+(h−1)v`, while `I^k` is stable under `P`.
Gaussian elimination over `F₂` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\dim I^k)_{k=0}^{17}
={}&(128,127,125,121,115,107,97,85,\
&71,57,43,31,21,13,7,3,1,0).
\end{aligned}
\]

The program compares the successive differences with every coefficient
of `(1+t)^2(1+t²)^3(1+t⁴)^2`.  Thus it proves `I^17=0` and `I^16≠0`
by finite exact linear algebra; no dimension-subgroup theorem is needed
for this implication.  The separate subgroup calculation also verifies
that `P` has class three.

## Exact path-chain morphism and its weight bound

The Cayley covering graph has vertices `P` and directed edges
`g→gx`, `g→gy`.  Its chain module is `R²`, with `P` acting by left
translation.  Let `ν:F→P` be the quotient map and let `D(w)` be the
mod-two edge chain of the word path beginning at the identity.  Then

\[
 D(uv)=D(u)+\nu(u)D(v),\qquad
 D(u^{-1})=-\nu(u)^{-1}D(u).
\]

These equations give a literal homomorphism
`w ↦ (D(w),ν(w))` into `R² ⋊ P`.  On `H`, its chain is a cycle, and
the map is exactly the usual graph homology map
`H → H₁(graph,F₂)`.  Its kernel is `H²[H,H]`.  Passing to the finite
image gives the same morphism from the profinite free group and the
closed kernel `K=cl(H²[H,H])`.  Thus its image is the actual group
`Q=Π/K`, rather than an unrelated group of the same order.

For all positive `k`, one has

\[
 \nu(w)-1\in I^k,\qquad D(w)\in (I^{k-1})^2
 \quad (w\in\Gamma_k F).
\]

Here is a simultaneous elementary proof.  Put `g=ν(u)`, `h=ν(v)`.
The endpoint of a commutator satisfies

\[
 [g,h]-1=
 \big((g-1)(h-1)-(h-1)(g-1)\big)g^{-1}h^{-1}.
\]

The path-chain calculation, with `[u,v]=uvu^-1v^-1`, is

\[
 D([u,v])=(1-ghg^{-1})D(u)+(g-[g,h])D(v).
\]

If the endpoint and chain bounds hold with weights `a,b`, respectively,
the first chain coefficient is in `I^b` and the second is in `I^a`,
since

\[
 1-ghg^{-1}=-g(h-1)g^{-1},\qquad
 g-[g,h]=gh(1-g^{-1})h^{-1}.
\]

Hence the new chain lies in `(I^(a+b−1))²` and the endpoint difference
in `I^(a+b)`.  Products and inverses preserve the asserted ideals.
For normal closure, use the actual formula

\[
 D(vuv^{-1})=(1-\nu(v)\nu(u)\nu(v)^{-1})D(v)
                +\nu(v)D(u).
\]

The first term is in `(I^a)²` and the second in `(I^(a−1))²`.
This proves the simultaneous assertion by induction from the generators.
It extends to the profinite closures because the target is finite.

For `w∈Γ₁₈Π`, the endpoint is trivial since `P` has class three, and
its path chain is zero since `I^17=0`.  The proved kernel description
therefore gives `Γ₁₈Π⊂K`, establishing the upper bound seventeen.

## Independent lower-bound trace

The certificate expands the literal word starting from
`[[[x,y],x],x]` and successively brackets it with the letters of
`XXYXYXYYYYYYY`.  This is a weight-seventeen commutator, represented by
196,606 unreduced letters.  A direct edge-by-edge walk, independent of
the lower-central row-space program, has identity endpoint and chain
equal to the sum of all 128 `x`-edges and no `y`-edges.  It is a nonzero
cycle, so its image in `Q` is nontrivial.  The upper and lower bounds
prove that the actual geometric quotient has nilpotency class seventeen.
