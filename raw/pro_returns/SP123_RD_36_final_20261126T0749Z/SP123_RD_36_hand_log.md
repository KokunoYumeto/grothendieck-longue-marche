# SP123_RD_36 — Locally checked hand log

Earlier logs were treated as hypotheses. Every scope statement below refers to these scans only.
## Scope of the hand observations

The supplied earlier hand logs were used as prompts for attention, not as evidence that a particular ambiguous stroke has a fixed reading. The page tables below distinguish what recurred locally from what was confirmed at every occurrence inspected. Names of hands are descriptive; ink colour, a cursive form or mathematical subject alone does not identify a writer.

The most useful repeated observations in this selection are the looped line-reference ell on 003–008; compressed short forms that retain their written stops, hats and raised endings; the stacked-turn and long-descender forms of class symbols; and the need to distinguish an operator's underline from a low index, a connector or a cancellation. The two class-symbol occurrences on 033 retain xi/zeta alternatives, so no uniform xi reading is claimed across all related sheets. On 042, the opening xi does not resolve the later argument, which retains eta/xi with eta first. The closed body phi and open marginal varphi on the related typescripts are recorded as different graphic forms. Small doubled alphabet strokes are read locally, particularly the large G signs on 029 and the integer/rational signs in the torsion and Picard formulas.

The thin foliation is a separate layer from the mathematical writing. On the inverted sheets, its orientation is not the body orientation. Scan042 provides the clearest warning: native41 can resemble17 when the text is turned upright. Frame gaps, open contour ends, broad terminal hooks and arrowheads are also separate observations; a mathematical map does not supply an unseen head.

Retracing is recorded by its local course. On 042, the older diagram cluster is drawn without a recovered letter, while the final article un remains locally live because its retracing follows the letter forms without a separate strike. Both lie within the globally deleted draft. The thin lower cancellation stroke has pen gaps and ends in a downward curl; it is not supplied with an arrowhead.

No universal substitution of one letter for another has been inferred from these sheets. In particular, a clear local tau does not settle a dense neighboring Picard exponent, and one clear G does not remove a reservation on a more open C-shaped occurrence. The tables state those limits explicitly.
