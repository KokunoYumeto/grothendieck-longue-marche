## Legibility and witnesses

Most main-line prose and the larger formula objects are readable, but legibility is uneven within a single line. The least secure material is the overwritten or crossed small text, clipped and slanting margins, reduced basepoint indices, tiny arrow endpoints, and glyphs whose alphabet cannot be distinguished from their shape alone. Scans 018–019 and 029–031 have especially crowded additions or cancellations; 145_scan002 has a brown vertical stain through several right-hand passages. A confident reading of the neighboring words does not settle a doubtful index or cancelled fragment.

The recurring rounded extension/action-group graph remains explicitly E/Sigma where the inspected ink and the local definitions do not uniquely name its alphabet. The earlier round object-set graph likewise remains tau/calligraphic-T where unresolved, while the later modular-topos T has a distinct role and local treatment. Basepoint xi/j and occasional g/xi alternatives are scoped to their actual forms. These are shared notation uncertainties, not claims that every occurrence independently exhibits a different letter. The notation table and hand log state the evidence and the limits of each comparison.

Small diagram links deserve particular caution. A rounded pen stop, an interior projection, a very small triangular head, and a genuinely bare shaft can look similar at page scale. Where direction or head status remains open, the alternative is shown in the fragment. Nonstandard pairs of strokes and short inclusion curves are reproduced graphically. The source's missing delimiters, unmatched closing parentheses, literal references, unfinished sentences, cancellations and unconventional words are not repaired to fit the expected mathematics.
