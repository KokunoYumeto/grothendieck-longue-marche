## 1. Exact graph and groups

Let Gamma be the right-angled Artin group on

\[
a,b,d,e,p,r,\bar a,\bar b,\bar d,\bar e,\bar p,\bar r.
\]

Its edges are the four diagonal edges

\[
(a,\bar a),(b,\bar b),(d,\bar d),(e,\bar e),
\]

the pairs

\[
(d,\bar a),(b,\bar a),(b,\bar e),
(p,\bar a),(p,\bar d),(r,d),(p,r),
\]

and their barred images. There are no other edges. The involution c exchanges each vertex with its barred mate. This is a graph automorphism. The graph is bipartite, with parts

\[
\{a,b,d,e,p,\bar r\},\qquad
\{\bar a,\bar b,\bar d,\bar e,\bar p,r\}.
\]

Put B=Gamma semidirect <c>, where c has order two. Let

\[
L=\langle a\bar a,b\bar b,d\bar d,e\bar e\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
L\cong F_4,\qquad C_B(c)=L\times\langle c\rangle,
\qquad C_{\widehat B}(c)=\widehat L\times\langle c\rangle.
\tag{1}
\]

Let E1=<p,r> and E2=<e,bar b>, both full two-vertex cliques and hence copies of Z^2. For any isomorphism phi:E1→E2, form

\[
T=\langle B,w\mid w^{-1}xw=\phi(x)\quad(x\in E1)\rangle.
\]

The exact conclusions needed later are

\[
\widehat T=\operatorname{HNN}(\widehat B,\widehat{E1},\widehat{E2},\widehat\phi)
\quad\hbox{proper},\qquad
C_{\widehat T}(c)=\widehat L\times\langle c\rangle.
\tag{2}
\]

The same centralizer equality without hats follows from the ordinary HNN tree. No unspecified conjugacy of edge embeddings is allowed in (2).

## 2. Reduced-word lemma and the actual fixed subgroup

Here is the normal-form fact used, including a construction. In a graph group, encode a word by one stack per vertex. Reading v with sign epsilon, append epsilon to the v stack and append a zero to every stack of a distinct vertex not commuting with v. If the v stack instead ends in the opposite sign, cancel that sign and one terminal zero on each of the noncommuting stacks. On reachable stack configurations those zeros are present. This follows because a noncommuting letter would itself have placed a zero above the candidate sign on the v stack. Commuting intervening letters can change only indistinguishable zeros on the other stacks.

The operations for commuting vertices commute, and the two opposite-sign operations for one vertex are inverse. Thus this piling is invariant under the defining relations. Conversely, each cancellation can be performed in the word by commuting the new inverse past precisely the intervening commuting letters. Repetition gives a reduced word encoded by the piling. A nonempty reduced word has a nonempty piling. It can be recovered by successively removing a bottom tile, consisting of one sign and its matching zeros. Two bottom tiles that can both be removed have commuting labels: if their labels did not commute, one of the two stacks would have a zero below its sign. Thus different choices of bottom tiles differ only by commuting letters. This proves both faithfulness of the encoding and the usual reduced dependence-word normal form.

In particular the possible first letters of a nontrivial group element commute pairwise. An automorphism of the defining graph permutes the stacks and carries the set of possible first signed letters to the corresponding set for the image element.

Suppose g in Gamma is fixed by c and is nontrivial. Choose a possible first letter v^epsilon. Then bar(v)^epsilon is another possible first letter. The two must commute, so v belongs to one of the four diagonal edge pairs: a,b,d,e and their bars. Since the two first letters commute, both can be removed, giving

\[
g=(v\bar v)^\epsilon g_1,
\qquad c(g_1)=g_1,
\]

with reduced length two less. Induction shows Fix_Gamma(c) is generated by the four products in L.

They freely generate. Delete all vertices other than a,b,d,e, sending them to the identity. Because no two of these four vertices are joined by an edge, this defines a homomorphism Gamma→F(a,b,d,e). It sends the four displayed products to the corresponding free basis. The homomorphism from the abstract free group on those four products into Gamma therefore has a left inverse and is injective. This proves Fix_Gamma(c)=L≅F4. An element g c^epsilon commutes with c exactly when c(g)=g, proving the discrete part of (1).

## 3. Residual finiteness and finite cohomological type

For clarity these conditions do not follow merely from the word 'RAAG'. They can be verified as follows.

### 3.1 Residual finiteness

Induct on vertices. On removing a vertex v, write the graph group as the special HNN extension

\[
\langle A,v\mid [v,h]=1\quad(h\in C)\rangle,
\]

where A is the smaller graph group and C is the full link subgroup. Killing all other generators gives a retraction r:A→C, so C is separable in a residually finite A. Explicitly, if a is outside C, then a r(a)^{-1} is nontrivial. Choose a finite-index normal N of A detecting it and replace N by N intersect r^{-1}(N). The resulting subgroup is r-stable, and the image of a is outside the image of C in that finite quotient. A finite collection of elements outside C can be separated simultaneously.

Given a nontrivial Britton-reduced word in the special HNN extension, choose such a finite quotient of A preserving each of its finitely many required nonmembership conditions. The word stays reduced in the corresponding HNN extension of a finite group, so remains nontrivial. A finite-base HNN extension is virtually finitely generated free: realize the finite base by a faithful regular permutation action, choose a permutation conjugating the two isomorphic edge actions, and take the kernel of the resulting finite permutation quotient. This kernel acts freely on its ordinary tree. Residual finiteness of that free kernel and finite-index cores give residual finiteness of the finite-base HNN extension, hence a finite quotient detecting the original word. The case of a word in A is immediate. This completes the induction.

The extension B by C2 is also residually finite: separate elements in Gamma using finite-index cores in B, and separate the other coset by orientation. Gamma is finitely generated, so finite-index subgroups and their cores have the required finite indices.

### 3.2 A finite two-dimensional classifying space

The same induction supplies the finite Salvetti model. For a bipartite defining graph the link of any vertex has no edges, so its graph group is a finitely generated free group. Attach the cylinder of its finite rose to the smaller model at both ends using the full-link inclusion; its stable loop is v. The link inclusion induces an injection on fundamental groups because it has the coordinate retraction. In the universal cover, this is a tree of contractible vertex spaces and contractible edge spaces. A tree of such spaces is contractible, by the finite-subtree argument and compactness of maps from spheres. Thus the model is a finite K(Gamma,1) of dimension at most two.

Consequently Gamma is torsion-free, cd_p(Gamma)≤2, and cohomology with any finite module is finite in every degree: use its finite free cellular resolution, which is zero beyond dimension two. The finite extension B therefore has vcd_p(B)≤2. Its Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre sequence over C2 has only three possible Gamma rows, all finite for finite coefficients, so H^n(B,A) is finite for every finite A and every n. This proves the finiteness condition in finite virtual p-cohomological TYPE.

## 5. Compatible finite HNN heads

The four clique vertex sets for E1, E2, cE1c and cE2c are pairwise disjoint. The eight involved vertices are

\[
p,r,e,\bar b,\bar p,\bar r,\bar e,b.
\]

Record their eight exponent coordinates and reduce modulo m. This gives a c-equivariant map Gamma→(Z/m)^8 and hence

\[
B\longrightarrow (\mathbb Z/m)^8\rtimes C_2.
\]

The map retains orientation and intersects each specified Z^2 clique in exactly m times that clique. This conclusion also follows from their coordinate retractions; it requires no commutation between different cliques in Gamma.

Let M be any prescribed finite-index normal subgroup of B. Choose m with mE1 and mE2 contained in M; normality automatically gives the same for their c images. Intersect M with the kernel of the preceding finite map. The resulting finite-index normal N satisfies

\[
N\subset\Gamma,\qquad N\cap E1=mE1,\qquad N\cap E2=mE2.
\]

Every isomorphism of Z^2 groups sends mE1 to mE2. Thus phi descends to an isomorphism between the corresponding subgroups of the finite group A=B/N. Form the finite-base HNN head

\[
V=\operatorname{HNN}(A,E1/mE1,E2/mE2,\phi_m).
\]

The finite permutation argument from Section 3 applies even when the two edge subgroups differ: the restrictions of the regular A action along the two edge embeddings are free actions of the same finite edge group, so a permutation identifies them. Its role is the stable letter. The resulting map to a finite symmetric group embeds A. Its kernel is free of finite index and finite rank. Hence V is residually finite and virtually finitely generated free.

Each prescribed finite quotient of B is consequently retained, after refinement, in a finite quotient of T. The cliques have full topology in B by their coordinate retractions and finite-index cores. Universal properties on finite targets therefore prove that the displayed full profinite HNN in (2) is proper and is precisely hat T.

Every finite quotient of T factors through one of the heads V: apply the construction to the kernel of its B restriction, and send the head stable letter to the original stable-letter image. The edge identities are retained by construction. This is the cofinality needed next.

## 6. Selected-cone centralizer through those heads

Give the HNN stable letter even orientation. Then c remains odd in every head, while each finite edge subgroup and every conjugate of it is even. In the ordinary Bass--Serre tree of V, the involution c therefore fixes exactly its base vertex: a second fixed vertex would give a fixed connecting edge. Thus

\[
C_V(c)=C_A(c).
\]

The latter group is finite. The explicit virtually-free goodness/TYPE argument of B108 applies to every V, so the verified prime-order theorem gives C_hat V(c)=C_A(c).

If g in hat T centralizes c, its image in every hat V belongs to A. If g lay outside the compact image of hat B, a finite quotient would separate it from that image. Such a quotient factors through one of the heads V, contradicting its membership in A. Therefore

\[
C_{\widehat T}(c)=C_{\widehat B}(c)=\widehat F_4\times C_2.
\]

The ordinary equality follows directly from the HNN tree, since the actual edge cliques are torsion-free. No profinite fixed-subtree theorem is required, and no completion of an unexamined infinite-index kernel is used.
