\statement{B.104}{Proved}{Faithful edge coordinates, genuine source Fox operators, and a finite complex for the entire orbit problem}
\label{stmt:B104}
Retain the actual paired generic-fiber marking of B.91--B.102.
Write $W=F(p_1,\ldots,p_{21})$, let $T=F(y_1,\ldots,y_5)$ be

\[

 (y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4,y_5)=(p_1,p_4,p_9,p_{14},p_{19}).

The $A$ generators have indices $i\in J$ and their $B$ partners
have indices $i+10$. Let $G=(R_A\times R_B)\rtimes T$ and
$\Omega=\mathbb Z[G]$ have exactly B.94's actual normal forms.
Put $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=\ker(W\to G)$. Then the product-square module

\begin{equation}\label{eq:B104-two-models}
 \begin{aligned}
 P=N_{\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{ab}}
   &\cong\ker\bigl(\Omega^{21}\xrightarrow{d_{21}}\Omega\bigr),
       &d_{21}(e_j)&=\overline p_j-1,\\
 P&\cong\ker\bigl(\Omega^{16}\xrightarrow{d_{16}}\mathbb Z[G/T]\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both identifications preserve $G$ and the actual based involution $F$.
The sixteen rows are $A_i,B_i$, $i\in J$. A positive product-tree edge
at $(u,v)$ with common generator $(g,i)$ has coefficient $(u,v,g)$
in row $A_i$; a $B$ edge with common generator $(h,j)$ has coefficient
$(u,v,\beta(h))$ in row $B_j$.

Every actual old or source automorphism has its genuine semilinear

On a sixteen-row cycle its action is obtained by the unique retained-
forest completion to twenty-one rows, that actual Fox operator, and
projection. No action on arbitrary sixteen-row chains is inferred
from quotient-factor automorphisms alone.

There is also a direct exact Tate extractor. For $u\in P$ with
$F(u)=-u$, let $v$ be its sixteen-row cycle and let $h$ be the
$A$-edge part of $v$. Then $v=h-F(h)$ and $q=d_{16}(h)$ is invariant.
Its Tate coordinate under B.91's identification is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B104-extractor}
 [u]\longmapsto
 \sum_{a\in R}\bigl(q_{(a,a)}\bmod2\bigr)a
          \quad\hbox{in }I_{\mathbb F_2[R]}.
\end{equation}
All sums are finite. For an actual finite node-chain representative
of B.92's residual map, this computes the value before quotienting
by $J_I$, with all transported words retained.

Finally pass to the actual even old-base cover and let
$S=F(s_1,\ldots,s_{11})$ be its eleven actual fixed-source lifts.
Write $\psi_i$ for their pushes, with convention
$s_i^{-1}x s_i=\psi_i(x)$. Put
\[
 G_{\mathrm{ev}}=\langle G,s_1,\ldots,s_{11}\mid
                    s_i^{-1}g s_i=\psi_i(g)\rangle,
 \qquad \Lambda=\mathbb Z[G_{\mathrm{ev}}].
\]
This is the actual split extension $G\rtimes S$, with the displayed
conjugation convention. The actual cover $C_E$ of B.102 has a finite
free $\Lambda$ cellular model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B104-finite-complex}
       \Lambda^{359}\xrightarrow{D_2}
       \Lambda^{32}\xrightarrow{D_1}\Lambda.
\end{equation}
It includes all $231$ source mapping-cylinder faces and all $128$
node faces of the two sheets. The source-face boundary is injective,
and the actual equivariant relative sequence is
\[
 0\longrightarrow Z_2\longrightarrow\Lambda^{128}
       \longrightarrow P\longrightarrow M^{\mathrm{ab}}
       \longrightarrow0.
\]
Every node-coordinate relation therefore has a unique source-cylinder
completion. Its second homology is the full
$Z_2$ of B.102, with the actual involution transported from the
equivariant geometric construction below. It is not an old-word
truncation. No finite generation of its kernel, vanishing of
$\kappa$, or nonlinear or profinite fixed-lifting conclusion follows.

\begin{proof}
\emph{B.104.1. The actual generic quotient.}
In the free product $W=T*F(A)*F(B)$, the retained kernel is
$R_A*R_B$. On the $A$ side its actual Schreier peripheral is
$x_{g,i}=g p_i g^{-1}$. On the $B$ side use $\beta$ of the
physical retained whisker, as in B.94. Thus conjugation by $t\in T$
sends $x_{g,i}$ literally to $x_{tg,i}$ on $A$ and acts by
$\tau_{\beta(t)}$ on $B$. Killing the mixed commutators gives
\[
 (u,v,t)(a,b,s)=
       (u\tau_t(a),v\tau_{\beta(t)}(b),ts),
 \qquad F(u,v,t)=(v,u,\beta(t)).
\]
This recovers the actual $G$, including its coefficient convention.
The kernel $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is the mixed commutator kernel before
the node fillings. It is distinct from the actual filled group $M$
in B.102 and from the section kernel named $N$ in B.99.

\emph{B.104.2. The full twenty-one-row cover.}
The $G$-cover of the $W$ rose has vertices $G$ and positive edges
$(g,j)$ from $g$ to $g\overline p_j$. Its cellular chain groups are
$\Omega^{21}$ and $\Omega$, with the first boundary in
\eqref{eq:B104-two-models}. Its fundamental group is
$N_{\mathrm{gen}}$, so its cycle group is
$N_{\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{ab}}$.
For a literal word, a positive letter contributes its preceding
$G$ prefix to its row; a negative letter contributes minus its
following prefix. The boundary telescopes to the final group
element minus the initial element. This is the full evaluated
left Fox chain, including the five retained rows.

Every certified old or source automorphism preserves the two
peripheral-family filling kernels; $F$ exchanges them. Consequently
all preserve $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$ and induce actual automorphisms of
$G$. For an automorphism $\alpha$, its chain map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B104-Fox-map}
 \sum_j a_j e_j\longmapsto
      \sum_j\alpha_G(a_j)\operatorname{Fox}_G(\alpha(p_j)).
\end{equation}
The word identity
$\operatorname{Fox}(uv)=\operatorname{Fox}(u)+\overline u\operatorname{Fox}(v)$
proves the semilinear chain rule and compatibility with the boundary.
The actual inverse word map supplies the inverse. In particular the
matrices are not composed as ordinary linear matrices with a fixed
coefficient action. The complete actual mixed conjugators are part
of \eqref{eq:B104-Fox-map}.

\emph{B.104.3. The retained forest and sixteen free edge rows.}
The five retained edge types form the disjoint union of the Cayley
trees on the right cosets $gT$. Their collapse has vertex set
$G/T=R_A\times R_B$ and retains the sixteen deleted edge types.
The collapse is $G$-equivariant on chains and induces an isomorphism
on first homology. It is not asserted to be a $G$-homotopy equivalence
of spaces: collapsed vertices acquire stabilizers $T$.

The retained forest boundary is injective and has image
$\ker(\Omega\to\mathbb Z[G/T])$. Therefore every collapsed cycle
has a unique retained-forest chain completing it to a full cycle.
This proves the inverse comparison without choosing an unproved
equivariant section of the collapse.

For an $A$ edge its initial vertex $(u,v)$ and generator label
$(g,i)$ determine the unique coefficient $(u,v,g)$.
For a $B$ edge with label $(h,j)$ they determine
$(u,v,\beta(h))$. Conversely these coefficients give precisely
the prescribed edge, by B.94's action. This proves actual freeness
of the sixteen-row module. The collapsed graph is the one-skeleton
of $T_A\times T_B$. Filling its product squares gives a contractible
two-complex, hence
\[
 \partial_2:C_2(T_A\times T_B)\hookrightarrow C_1(T_A\times T_B)
\]
is injective with image the cycle group. This identifies that group
with $P$ and proves both identifications in
\eqref{eq:B104-two-models}.

These statements use no monotonicity of free rank under injection
over $\Omega$. In particular B.94's free rank-$64$ submodule and
the sixteen-row ambient module do not contradict each other.
No Ore field, division-ring extension, or commutative rank bound
has been assumed.

\emph{B.104.4. The exact Tate coordinate.}
The actual involution sends
\[
 F(a A_i)=F(a)B_i,\qquad F(a B_i)=F(a)A_i.
\]
Thus the underlying abelian $C_2$-module of the sixteen-row group
is a direct sum of induced two-element permutation modules and is
Tate-acyclic. Put $I_{\mathrm{vert}}=\ker(\mathbb Z[E]\to\mathbb Z)$.
The exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow\Omega^{16}
       \xrightarrow{d_{16}}I_{\mathrm{vert}}\longrightarrow0
\]
identifies $H^1(C_2,P)$ with $\widehat H^0(C_2,I_{\mathrm{vert}})$.
On vertices $F$ exchanges the two $R$ coordinates. Its fixed
vertices are exactly $(a,a)$, and the permutation-module
calculation identifies this last Tate group with
$I_{\mathbb F_2[R]}$ by the diagonal coefficients modulo two.

For an anti-chain take $h$ to be the $A$ part of its boundary cycle
$v$. Anti-invariance gives $v=h-F(h)$, and the fact that $v$ is a
cycle gives $F(d_{16}h)=d_{16}h$. The connecting map sends this
invariant vertex chain to $[u]$, up to the irrelevant sign in an
exponent-two group. This proves \eqref{eq:B104-extractor}.
The augmentation is zero, because nonfixed vertex pairs have equal
integral coefficients and the complete vertex boundary has sum zero.

To fix the normalization, the square made from the two root paths
$1\to a$ has $A$ boundary part $H(a,1)-H(a,a)$. Its diagonal
vertex coefficients are minus one at $1$ and at $a$, giving
$a-1$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Diagonal translation by $a^{-1}$ gives
$a^{-1}-1$, the right-defect convention of B.91 and B.102.

The full twenty-one-row module can be used as well. Its paired
rows are exchanged. The inverted $p_1=y_1$ row sends
$e_g$ to $-e_{F(g)y_1^{-1}}$. A fixed coefficient index would
require $\beta(t)y_1^{-1}=t$, conjugating the two distinct cone
involutions in $T\rtimes C_2=C_2*C_2*F_2$.
Thus this row also has only two-element signed orbits and the
full chain module is Tate-acyclic. The sixteen-row $A$ half is
the convenient explicit choice used in the certificate.

The finite model and B.102's universal-base nodal model are

\[
