\statement{B.95}{Proved}{The single old-zero overlap is a free rank-four induced module, and its two-summand Tate image is a source ideal}
\label{stmt:B95}
Retain $G,\Omega=\mathbb Z[G],P,L_{\mathrm{ret}}$ and the actual
columns $c_\nu$ of B.94. Let $\alpha_0$ be the geometric push along
the actual positive old-base lasso about $q=0$, in the corrected paired
marking. Put
\[
 L=L_{\mathrm{ret}},\qquad L_0=\alpha_0(L),\qquad L_+=L+L_0.
\]
The operator $\alpha_0$ is the actual pointed source operator named
\texttt{old\_0}; it commutes with $F$ and preserves the actual source
kernel $I$. These are discrete submodules of the actual $P$, with all
coefficients in $\Omega$, rather than finite coefficient samples.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B95-intersection}
 L\cap L_0=
   \Omega c_{(6,2)}\oplus\Omega c_{(6,3)}
     \oplus\Omega c_{(6,4)}\oplus\Omega c_{(6,7)},
 \qquad L_+\cong\Omega^{124}.
\end{equation}
The displayed intersection has two exchanged $F$-orbits,
\[
       (6,2)\longleftrightarrow(6,4),\qquad
       (6,3)\longleftrightarrow(6,7),
\]
with explicit group-unit coefficients. Its underlying $C_2$-module is
induced, so all its positive-degree cohomology vanishes. Consequently
the two inclusions give an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B95-cohomology}
 \begin{gathered}
 H^1(C_2,L)\oplus H^1(C_2,L_0)
       \xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(C_2,L_+),\\
 H^1(C_2,L_+)\cong\mathbb F_2[R]^8,
 \qquad H^2(C_2,L_+)=0,\\
 \operatorname{im}\bigl(H^1(C_2,L_+)\longrightarrow H^1(C_2,P)\bigr)
       =J_4+\alpha_0(J_4)
       =\sum_{i=1}^4\mathbb F_2[R](\rho_i-1)
         +\sum_{i=1}^4\mathbb F_2[R](\alpha_0(\rho_i)-1)
       \subseteq J_I.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here the four $\rho_i$ are the literal source elements of B.93.
This is exactly the sum of the identity and one old-zero translate.
It neither asserts invariance under $\alpha_0$ nor determines a higher
iterate or the full old-base orbit module. In fact $L_0$ is not contained
in $L$, as the independent additional columns below show.

\begin{proof}
\emph{B.95.1. The actual operator and its coefficient action.}
Write $p_1,\ldots,p_{21}$ for the paired generic-fiber basis used in

The row named \texttt{old\_0} has empty source correction and its

The actual old-base lasso about zero lifts to a closed loop in the
selected source. Thus $\alpha_0$ is conjugation by an actual source
element, with the geometric-push convention fixed in that package,
and $\alpha_0(I)=I$.

All $21$ literal equations
\[
                    \alpha_0(F(p_j))=F(\alpha_0(p_j))
\]
and both sets of inverse substitutions are checked in the independent
certificate cited below. Monodromy preserves the two deleted divisor
families and their filling maps. It therefore preserves the mixed
commutator kernel $N$ of the generic fiber and induces an automorphism
of $G$ and a semilinear automorphism of $P=N^{\mathrm{ab}}$.
In particular $L_0$ is an $F$-stable free $\Omega$-module of rank $64$,
with basis $\alpha_0(c_\nu)$. Semilinearity is included here: the
coefficient automorphism is not silently replaced by the identity.

\emph{B.95.2. Four exact identities and no other incidence collisions.}
The $128$ complete integral columns are the $64$ original columns and

\[
                  \mathcal C=\{(6,2),(6,3),(6,4),(6,7)\}.
\]
There are no cross-label coincidences: the physical slot pairs of each
layer run through the same $8$ by $8$ product exactly once, and the
remaining retained relative words are unequal. This is the complete
finite comparison in
\path{records/old0_retained_incidence18.json}.

For these four coincidences we use actual word identities, not the
augmented comparison alone. If $r_\nu$ is the actual paired node
commutator, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B95-word-relations}
                   \alpha_0(r_\nu)=h_\nu r_\nu h_\nu^{-1}
                   \qquad(\nu\in\mathcal C).
\end{equation}
Here is a complete finite encoding of the conjugators. For a signed
integer list write
\[
 [a_1,\ldots,a_m]_p
       =p_{|a_1|}^{\operatorname{sgn}(a_1)}\cdots
          p_{|a_m|}^{\operatorname{sgn}(a_m)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B95-conjugators}
 \begin{aligned}

Their retained $F_5$ heads, in B.94's $y$-basis, are respectively
\[
 y_2^{-1}y_5^{-1}y_4^{-1},\quad1,\quad

Let $u_\nu\in G$ be the actual image of $h_\nu$ under the generic-fiber

\end{equation}
The coefficient is a genuine group unit. The separate verification

For the raw zero-based column order of B.93, these four columns are

\emph{B.95.3. A complete independent set and the intersection.}
Remove the four translated columns in \eqref{eq:B95-unit-relations}.
Keep all $64$ original columns and the other $60$ translated columns.
Their $124$ nonzero augmented rows are distinct. Selecting these rows
in column order gives a $124$ by $124$ matrix with diagonal signed

$124$ rows. A left coefficient relation first has every coefficient

omitted column is the exact group-unit multiple in
\eqref{eq:B95-unit-relations}. This proves the second assertion of
\eqref{eq:B95-intersection}, and shows directly that the $60$ new
columns do not lie in $L$.

Now let $x\in L\cap L_0$. Express it in the $64$ original basis
columns and in the $64$ translated basis columns. Substitute the

displayed four-column span. Conversely every displayed column lies

\eqref{eq:B95-intersection} over the full infinite coefficient group.

\emph{B.95.4. The involution and the overlap cohomology.}
The exact $F$-mate identities of B.94 restrict to the two pairs
displayed in the statement. The signs $\delta_\nu$ in B.94 are $+1$
on the pair $(6,2),(6,4)$ and $-1$ on the pair $(6,3),(6,7)$.
In either case each pair can be written with basis $c,F(c)$,
because the intervening coefficient is a group unit. The involution
then exchanges these two basis elements and applies the coefficient
involution. Its underlying integral module is a direct sum of
two-element permutation modules. Thus, writing $J=L\cap L_0$,
\[
                            H^n(C_2,J)=0\qquad(n>0).
\]
No assertion about fixed coefficient points of a physically fixed
node is needed: none of these four physical nodes is fixed.

The exact sequence of actual equivariant modules
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B95-overlap-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow J\xrightarrow{x\mapsto(x,-x)}L\oplus L_0
        \xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto a+b}L_+\longrightarrow0
\end{equation}
therefore makes the inclusion-induced map on $H^1$ an isomorphism.
Also $H^2(C_2,L)=H^2(C_2,L_0)=0$ by B.94 and the actual
$F$-commuting automorphism $\alpha_0$. The next part of the exact
sequence, using $H^3(C_2,J)=0$, gives $H^2(C_2,L_+)=0$.
This proves the cohomology assertions of \eqref{eq:B95-cohomology}.

\emph{B.95.5. Actual source values and the exact remaining scope.}
The needed naturality is a statement about right defects, not an
assumption that $\alpha_0$ preserves the chosen free factors before
passing to the quotient. In the exact extension
\[
 1\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow
   (R_A*R_B)/[N,N]\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow1,
\]
the operator induces $\alpha_E$ on $E$ and $\alpha_P$ on $P$ and
commutes with $F$. For any lift $h$ of a fixed target, its right
defect satisfies the literal identity
\[
 \alpha_0(h^{-1}F(h))=\alpha_0(h)^{-1}F(\alpha_0(h)).
\]
Therefore
$\delta(\alpha_E(r,F(r)))=\alpha_P\delta(r,F(r))$ in
$H^1(C_2,P)$. By B.91 the original class is $r^{-1}-1$, so
the induced map sends it to $\alpha_R(r)^{-1}-1$, where
$\alpha_R$ is the induced automorphism of the first quotient
factor. These elements span the augmentation ideal, proving the
asserted cohomological naturality even when the original meridians
have mixed conjugators. We write this induced action as $\alpha_0$
as well. Hence the four source values for $L_0$ are
$\alpha_0(\rho_i)-1$. The old-zero operator is an actual source
operator, so all these elements lie in $I$, as do the original
$\rho_i$. Taking their diagonal left translates gives exactly the
two ideals in \eqref{eq:B95-cohomology}. The isomorphism from
\eqref{eq:B95-overlap-sequence} rules out any additional class from
this particular intersection. This conclusion is stronger than
checking a bounded set of integral anti-chains: the coefficients
range over every element of $\Omega$.
