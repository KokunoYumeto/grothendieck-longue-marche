# Relative fiber-product injectivity fails even at a maximal transverse intersection

This is a complete countermodel to a possible auxiliary generalization.
It is not a counterexample to the actual mu5 source-kernel assertion.

Let D be the hyperbola xy=1 in the affine complex plane, and let A and B
be the axes x=0 and y=0. The axes meet transversely once, which is their
full degree product, and that point avoids D. Put X=C² minus D and
W=X minus (A union B). The involution swaps x and y.

On W use x and q=xy. This identifies W with C* times (C minus {0,1}).
At the fixed base point (x,y)=(1/2,1/2), write its fundamental group as

`G=Z(z) times F(a,c)`.

Here z is positive x winding with q constant, and a,c are based positive
q meridians about 0 and 1 with x constant. The standard whiskers may be
chosen on the real interval at q=1/4. Filling B, while A is still removed,
gives coordinates (x,q) in C* times (C minus {1}); the quotient kills a
and has group Z². Filling A instead gives coordinates (y,q) and another
Z². The maps send z to x winding +1 and y winding -1, a to y winding +1,
and c to q=1 winding +1. Filling the remaining axis kills its meridian
in Z², so pi1(X)=Z generated by the q=1 meridian. This last assertion
also follows directly from van Kampen: the connected smooth axis has
a single normally generating meridian for the filling kernel.

Consequently the actual comparison is the surjection

`G -> Z² times_Z Z² = Z³`,

with coordinate matrix

`z -> (1,-1,0), a -> (0,1,0), c -> (0,0,1)`.

Its kernel contains [a,c], which is nontrivial already under the map to
S3 sending a to (12) and c to (23). In fact its kernel is exactly the
commutator subgroup of F(a,c). Thus maximal transverse intersection,
even with all intersections disjoint from the retained divisor, does
not imply relative fiber-product injectivity or commutation of the
two normal deletion kernels.

The example deliberately distinguishes this failure from fixed lifting.
The actual involution has

`sigma(z)=z^-1, sigma(a)=z a, sigma(c)=c`.

The first formula follows from y=q/x. The last two follow from the
winding of q along the chosen two q loops, and z is central. Therefore
an element z^m w(a,c) is fixed exactly when the a exponent of w is 2m.
The fixed target subgroup is all triples (m,m,l), and each has the
explicit fixed lift z^m a^(2m) c^l. Fixed-element lifting is surjective
in this countermodel despite the nontrivial comparison kernel.

The actual fixed locus of W is the punctured diagonal x=y. Its q map
is s -> s² from C minus {0,1,-1} to C minus {0,1}; it is the index-two
cover measuring parity of the a exponent. The fixed subgroup just
computed is exactly its actual source image. This independently
illustrates why the fixed-lifting question is the appropriate next
target in the mu5 geometry, rather than relative product injectivity.
