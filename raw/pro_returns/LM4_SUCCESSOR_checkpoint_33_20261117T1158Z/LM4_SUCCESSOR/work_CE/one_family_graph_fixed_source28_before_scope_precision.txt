2. Literal coarse section and the free a-kernel before filling

Put Q=Q_f semidirect S with the two actual actions just displayed. Killing a gives a homomorphism from the pre-node group onto Q. Its generator-wise section is literal because neither signed coarse operator contains a. Both node relators lie in the normal closure of a, so this coarse section survives the node quotient too.

The kernel L of Z(a)*Q_f -> Q_f is free on
    a_g = g a g^-1,  g in Q_f.
For completeness, the normal form is the standard free-product Schreier normal form: read a word by retaining its current Q_f prefix; every occurrence of a^epsilon contributes the free letter a_g^epsilon at that prefix. Reversal reconstructs the original word. Equivalently, collapsing the Q_f factor in the free-product Bass--Serre graph gives exactly one independent a-loop for each Q_f element. Thus these are simultaneous free generators, not separately primitive elements.

The pre-node group is L semidirect Q. Source actions permute this actual free basis. To retain their full noncommutative coefficients, define h_s in Q_f for every signed word s in S by
    alpha_s(a)=h_s a h_s^-1,
    h_1=1, h_m=1, h_v=f^-1,
    h_(st)=alpha_s(h_t) h_s.
The inverse rule is h_(s^-1)=alpha_s^-1(h_s^-1). These follow directly by composing the actual operators. No commutative replacement of the coefficient or its order is used. Then
    (g s) a_j (g s)^-1 = a_(g alpha_s(j) h_s)
for g,j in Q_f and s in S.

3. Actual stabilizer and complete graph

The stabilizer of a_1 in Q is exactly
    H={h_s^-1 s:s in S}=<m,fv>.
Indeed (g s) sends a_1 to a_(g h_s), so it fixes that free generator exactly when g=h_s^-1. The cocycle order in section2 proves that this set is a subgroup. Projection Q->S restricts to an isomorphism H->S, with inverse m->m, v->fv. Hence H intersect Q_f=1.

The Q-set of basis indices is therefore Q/H. Its identification with Q_f sends gH to g, after using sH=h_sH. Define the undirected simple graph Gamma by
    vertices: Q/H;
    edges: {qH,q C H} and {qH,q tau H}, for every q in Q.
Repetitions define the same edge; neither seed is a loop since C,tau are outside H. The action of Q on left cosets preserves the entire edge set by construction.

In L, the two defining commutators are precisely the commutators of the basis vertices H with CH and tau H. Their Q-conjugates are exactly the displayed edge commutators. The full normal closure in L semidirect Q is the L-normal closure of all those Q-conjugates. It follows, in both directions directly from presentations, that
    E_a = A(Gamma) semidirect Q,
where A(Gamma) is the graph group with the specified complete vertex basis and all specified commuting edges. Conversely the graph group semidirect product satisfies the two original node relators and the exact generic source equations, so the inverse homomorphism fixes every original generator. This is an exact presentation identity, not an assumption about node disks filling every square of a product-of-trees chain.

4. Coarse selected-cone centralizer

Every element of S fixes tau. If q=g s in Q, then
    q tau q^-1 = g tau g^-1.
In the discrete free product Q_f=C2(C)*Z(f)*C2(tau), the centralizer of tau is exactly <tau>. One elementary reduced-word proof peels any final tau-factor from g; if the remaining g is nonempty, the conjugate g tau g^-1 is reduced outside the tau factor and cannot equal tau. Therefore
    C_Q(tau)=<tau> x S.
Also C and tau are not conjugate in Q. A Q-conjugate of tau is a Q_f-conjugate of tau by the formula above, and the two different free-product factors are not conjugate. This statement uses all of Q, not only its regular fiber.

The original orientation on Q has kernel Q0. Since S is even and tau is odd,
    C_Q(tau) intersect Q0=S.
Every graph vertex a_g is even, so
    Gamma_a=A(Gamma) semidirect Q0.
The selected involution acts on A(Gamma) by the graph permutation induced by left tau and on Q0 by conjugation by tau.

5. Every tau-swapped commuting pair is a source pair

There is no tau-fixed vertex. If tau qH=qH, then q^-1 tau q lies in H. Its projection to S is1, whereas H->S is injective, forcing q^-1 tau q=1, impossible.

Suppose a pair of distinct vertices exchanged by tau is an edge. By the definition of Gamma, its endpoints can be written rH,r tH with t=C or tau. The equality tau rH=r tH gives
    r^-1 tau r=t h,  h in H.
Project to S. The left side has projection1, t has projection1, so h has projection1 and hence h=1. Thus r^-1 tau r=t.

If t=C this contradicts section4's actual nonconjugacy. If t=tau it says r belongs to <tau>xS. Consequently the unordered pair is exactly
    {sH,tau sH},  s in S.
Conversely every such pair is a translate of the tau seed edge and is exchanged by tau. This proves the complete classification, with no coset or source-word depth bound.

Its diagonal product is
    a_(sH) a_(tau sH)
      = alpha_s(a) delta(alpha_s(a))
      = alpha_s(a delta(a))
      = alpha_s(A_s).
The equality uses actual commutation alpha_s delta=delta alpha_s, already verified on every generator for both signed source generators, and then valid for every word s. These products are actual source conjugates; no normal closure of the source subgroup has been substituted for the source subgroup itself.

6. Elementary discrete fixed-graph lemma

Let an involution iota permute the vertices of any graph without fixed vertices, acting on the graph group by that permutation and not by inversions. Then its fixed subgroup is generated by
    x_v x_iota(v)
for those two-vertex orbits that form edges.

Here is a direct proof valid for an infinite graph as well. Use the reduced graph-word normal form: cancellation removes inverse letters only when every intervening letter commutes with that vertex; any two reduced representatives of an element differ by interchanges of adjacent commuting letters. This normal form follows by orienting those interchanges with a total order and the usual cancellation rule, or by the finite support piling construction used in B112. Each individual word involves finitely many letters, so no infinite-word premise is involved.

If a nontrivial element g is fixed, choose an initial signed letter x_v^epsilon of a reduced representative. Applying iota gives another reduced representative beginning x_iota(v)^epsilon. These are distinct signed letters. Since both can be initial in reduced representatives of the same element, the normal-form interchange rule forces their vertices to commute. Both can then be placed successively at the front of a reduced word: move the second initial-capable occurrence left until immediately after the first; it commutes with all the letters it crosses, including the first. Their product is fixed. Removing it leaves a fixed element of reduced length two less. Induction proves the claimed generation, including inverse products when epsilon=-1. Conversely every displayed commuting pair product is fixed.

This proves a generation theorem only. It is sufficient here and does not assume that all these generators are free, nor assert a completed analogue.

8. What this comparison does and does not settle

The complete actual projection Gamma0->Gamma_a carries the selected source to the entire selected fixed subgroup of Gamma_a. It can kill elements of the original fixed kernel and cannot detect all original source relations. The conclusion therefore supplies neither injectivity of the original source, nor surjectivity of fixed lifting, nor equality Gamma0^delta=image(source).

The nontrivial exact inner-face kernel of B135 remains part of the actual whole presentation. In this quotient it is one graph edge among its full source orbit. The C seed contributes a second full coarse edge orbit. Section5 explains why the latter creates no additional selected fixed generators here: C and tau remain distinct coarse cone classes and H projects injectively to S. With the other families restored, the corresponding actual peripheral transports have nonlinear conjugators in the family kernel; the single common free-basis permutation used in section2 has not been proved for them. This is the precise obstruction to extending this argument by inspection.

All graph-normal-form and fixed-subgroup arguments above are discrete. Although the input quotient maps are actual group maps and hence extend functorially to full completions, this note does not identify the completion of the infinite graph kernel with its induced topology or compute its completed fixed subgroup. No finite-support induction is applied to a profinite element.
