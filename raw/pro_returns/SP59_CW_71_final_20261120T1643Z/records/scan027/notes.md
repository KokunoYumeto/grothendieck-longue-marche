N1 (targeted counterexample to an unrestricted uniqueness reading). A finite convex hull can have more than one half-plane representation. The unit square is the intersection of x>=0, x<=1, y>=0, y<=1; adding x+y<=2 gives the same square with another supporting line. Thus the sketch’s “droites uniquement determinées” requires a canonical or irredundant edge-supporting family, not an arbitrary collection of half-planes. Such an additional restriction is not inserted into the transcription. This explains why the underlined wording needs care; it does not disprove the convex-hull polygon assertion of Lemma II.

N2 (targeted check of Lemma III’s proof wording). For a convex polygon each edge lies in the boundary line of its supporting closed half-plane, while the polygon’s interior lies in the associated open half-plane. In the unit square, the lower edge y=0 is not contained in its corresponding open half-plane y>0. The manuscript’s nearest antecedent to “un tel demi-espace” is an open half-plane; interpreted as the half-plane corresponding to that edge, the claim is false. If it means some other half-plane, it does not identify the edge’s supporting line and leaves the argument incomplete. The supporting-line statement gives the claimed one-sidedness of the interior. The text’s wording and the two marginal question marks remain unchanged.

N3 (independent short proof of Lemma IV under the stated convention). Let x lie outside compact convex C with nonempty interior. The finitely many lines through x and a vertex cannot cover an open disk in the interior; choose z off them. The line d=xz meets C in a compact nondegenerate interval, because z is interior and C is compact convex. Its nearer endpoint y is not a vertex by construction. The open segment from x to y misses C, so y is (R²−C)-visible from x and belongs to C−S(boundary C), as claimed. Compactness is needed for the bounded interval conclusion and was supplied by the preceding convention. The proof uses d for the chosen line although the prose has not explicitly named it; no missing definition is supplied.
