N1 (targeted proof check). The connectedness argument works with a closed triangle T containing C in its interior. That containment is needed for its boundary to avoid C but is not stated in the written choice of triangle. For any x in T−C, a line through x either misses C or meets the compact convex C in a closed segment. In the latter case the ray starting at the nearest endpoint and going through x continues away from C and reaches the triangle boundary, giving a C-free segment from x to that boundary. In the former case either direction reaches the boundary without meeting C. Thus T−C is path connected through the connected boundary. The outside region together with the boundary is connected as well (for example join points outward to a large surrounding circle); it meets T−C in the boundary. This supplies the missing size choice, but the transcription does not insert it. The annotation “fermeture d’un connexe” is read connexe; the closure of a connected space is connected, so that word fits the use.

N2 (targeted proof check of the final assertion). At a noncollinear boundary vertex of a convex polygon, the triangle made with the two neighboring vertices is nondegenerate and lies in C. Every interior point of that triangle has a small ball in the triangle, hence in C, so lies in the interior of C. No boundary vertex lies in that triangle interior. This verifies the good-vertex condition of Definition 2 on scan 023.

N3 (independent short proof of the hull assertions). Every edge is the convex hull of its two endpoints, so the convex hull of Gamma equals the convex hull of S(Gamma). An extreme point of the hull of a finite set must be one of those points: any convex combination involving a different point with weight strictly between zero and one expresses it on a nontrivial segment. With the canonical boundary polygon convention of scan 027, this yields S(partial E(Gamma)) subset S(Gamma), the substance of the heavy insertion below b). It does not justify supplying missing parentheses in the ink.
