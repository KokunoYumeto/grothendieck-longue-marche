# Full profinite recognition: Haran's rank-three construction and peripheral test

We searched directly for torsion-free virtually free full profinite groups and opened the complete original Dan Haran article, *Non-free torsion-free profinite groups with open free subgroups*, Israel Journal of Mathematics50(4)(1985),350–352. Its unnumbered Proposition provides a counterexample for every free open rank at least three. We reconstructed the first case, rank three and index two, rather than relying on the proposition alone.

The reconstruction starts with H=Fhat(l,a,b), epsilon_3=−1 and epsilon_p=1 at other primes, and kappa_2=1 with the other kappa-components zero. The formulas alpha(l)=l^epsilon, alpha(a)=b, alpha(b)=l^kappa a l^(−kappa) satisfy alpha²=Inn(l^kappa), alpha(l^kappa)=l^kappa. The paper writes the complete associative multiplication on H times C2. It identifies the subgroup generated by l and the coset lift with the explicit mixed-prime virtually procyclic group, constructs a retraction onto it, and proves torsion-freeness of the full extension.

The group maps onto (C3)^2 semidirect C2 by inversion. This quotient needs three generators, whereas a free profinite group with a rank-three subgroup of index two would have rank two. The independent Python standard-library program `haran_rank3.py` executes all5832 associativity checks and all324 ordered-pair generation checks in this18-element quotient. Pair-generated subgroup orders1,2,3,6,9 have counts1,27,32,216,48. Three displayed generators generate all18 elements.

The surface peripheral hypothesis is tested explicitly. For type(1,2), every loop-preserving involution has determinant on first homology equal to the sign of the puncture permutation: its cusp multiplier is lambda times that sign, and the filled torus determinant is lambda, with lambda²=1. Haran's outer action instead has determinant−epsilon, a sign varying with the prime. This excludes every type(1,2) marking on this kernel.

For type(0,4), we chose the free basis l,a,b^epsilon and fourth loop (l*a*b^epsilon)^(-1). The first three loops have the required action. The fourth fails: mapping l to(12), a to(23), and b to(123) in S3 sends P=l*a*b^epsilon to1 but sends alpha(P) to(123). The actual exponent residues are epsilon=5 and kappa=3 modulo6. The program exhausts all216 assignments and records24 failures, including this one.

These first tests establish the nonfree underlying extension and exclude the displayed peripheral candidate. D.29 subsequently constructs a different invariant (0,4) marking and supplies an exact isomorphism to this extension, completing the counterexample to D(d). The basis-independent restrictions and the finite failures below remain part of its verification.

## Further basis-independent restriction and a second finite certificate

For every invariant type-(0,4) marking, the trace on ordinary first homology forces either a single transposition with multiplier epsilon, or a double transposition with multiplier minus epsilon; the identity permutation is already impossible modulo 3. The canonical character sending every cusp generator to -1 is one of the three alpha-invariant characters chi1=(-1,1,1), chi2=(1,-1,-1), chi3=(-1,-1,-1) on (l,a,b).

The explicit cocycle matrix in (D.26g) gives determinants 1, -epsilon_p, 1 on the three two-dimensional twisted H1 spaces. The all-cusp-negative double cover is of type (1,4); restriction identifies twisted H1 with its anti-invariant part, and filling identifies this with H1 of a torus. All maps are stated and their equivariance checked, so its determinant equals the original peripheral multiplier. At p=3 this excludes every single-transposition marking. At p=5 the double-transposition case requires chi2. Thus only the double-transposition case with multiplier -epsilon and character chi2 remains.

The explicit candidate (a,b^(-epsilon),b^epsilon*l,(a*l)^(-1)) has exactly those necessary properties but its third cusp condition reduces to a^epsilon*l^epsilon being conjugate to (a*l)^epsilon. In S4, epsilon is 5 modulo 12. Taking a=(2 3 4), l=(1 2 3 4), b=1 gives the transposition (1 4) versus the four-cycle (1 3 2 4). The executed program tests every one of the 576 ordered S4 pairs and finds 96 nonconjugacy witnesses. The two finite candidates are excluded. The construction in D.29, described next, supplies a different double-transposition marking satisfying all the necessary conditions proved here.

## Resolution by D.29

The earlier arbitrary-marking obstruction has now been resolved affirmatively. D.29 chooses a genuine three-cusp automorphism tau with multiplier lambda=-epsilon and glues it to Inn(l^kappa) tau^(-1) on the two complementary pants. The resulting outer involution preserves the complete tuple (a,a^(-1)l,b,b^(-1)l^(-1)). Its basis (l,a,b'=tau(a)) identifies the extension exactly with the Haran extension proved above: alpha(l)=l^epsilon, alpha(a)=b', alpha(b')=l^kappa a l^(-kappa). Thus the nonfree torsion-free extension does admit an invariant full (0,4) marking, completing the counterexample to full profinite recognition.
