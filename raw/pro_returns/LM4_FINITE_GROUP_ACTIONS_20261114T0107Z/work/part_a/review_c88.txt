1. Retained C81 hypotheses and precise dependencies
-------------------------------------------------

same open disk O, the closed discrete free proper action Q on O, and an
actual transitive subgroup G of the boundary-fixed centralizer. The
Polish source used in the compact-core argument is exactly C81's closure
L of G in the original map-and-inverse topology. It is transitive and
commutes with the actual operators. This uses C81's stated construction
and does not assume that the initial subgroup G itself is Polish.

I reread C81's proof of d_Q's original interior topology, its actual

2. The displacement descends and the image is a group
----------------------------------------------------
The equality of the d_Q and original interior topologies makes the
function x->d_Q(hx,x) continuous for every original interior homeomorphism
h. Commutation with Q and invariance of d_Q make this function constant
on each original fiber. The quotient topology therefore gives exactly
the continuous delta_h on N.

The descended homeomorphism bar h has the specified inverse
bar(h^{-1}). Direct substitution gives
  delta_(h^{-1})=delta_h composed with bar h^{-1}
and the triangle inequality gives
  delta_(hk)<=delta_h composed with bar k + delta_k.
Pullback by a homeomorphism of N preserves C_0(N), because the inverse
image of a compact exceptional set is compact. Hence V_Q is a group
before the extension argument is used. No uniform-continuity condition
was added to this interior definition.

3. Necessity and the common compact-family tail
----------------------------------------------
For a continuous family a_p in the original closed-disk group, compactness
of P and joint evaluation on P times the compact disk give one compact
C inside O such that every displacement outside C is small. The sequential
argument in the text is valid: choose a sequence escaping an interior
compact exhaustion, then convergent subsequences of the parameters and
closed-disk inputs. The latter limit is on the boundary, fixed by every
member of the family.

K=pi(C) is compact. An x with pi(x) outside K has qx outside C for every
actual q. Thus the same bound controls the supremum over q and proves
the uniform C_0 condition. Starting with epsilon/2 gives the strict
displayed bound. The same proof applies to the already continuous
inverse family, retaining the exact map-and-inverse requirement.

5. Sufficiency and joint inverse continuity
------------------------------------------
Start only with the stated jointly continuous family of equivariant
interior homeomorphisms and the uniform quotient-tail condition. On a
sequence p_j->p, x_j->z at the original circle, a contrary subsequence
has a fixed positive failure bound. An exhaustion of N gives a further
subsequence whose quotient points either escape every compact subset,
or all lie in one compact K. In the first case the uniform tail makes
d(h_(p_j)x_j,x_j) tend to zero, contradicting the failure.

In the second case finite covering sections give a compact C in O with
pi(C)=K. To obtain equality, take a finite closed refinement of a cover
of K by evenly covered neighborhoods and the images of those closed
pieces under the chosen sections. Write x_j=q_j c_j. Joint interior
continuity on the compact P times C gives the compact set
C'={h_p(c)} in O. The compact-core estimate gives q_j C'->z uniformly.
Equivariance puts h_(p_j)x_j in q_j C', again contradicting failure.
This proves the actual joint boundary continuity of the identity paste.

Each paste is bijective: it is the specified homeomorphism on O and
the identity on the separate boundary. The displayed product map

Hausdorff spaces. Its inverse is continuous with its literal fiberwise

assuming it for the starting interior family. Joint continuity on

original uniform map-and-inverse topology. Applying the already proved
