1. Actual surface and refinement.
The original free proper discrete action supplies the quotient charts,
and the open quotient of a countable base proves second countability.
No orientability is needed for the cited topological triangulation
theorem. The compact exhaustion and locally finite sum of the stated
functions give a continuous proper a, with the indicated lower bound
outside each compact level. In the compact case a=0 is proper.

Each V_y is obtained by shrinking an actual isometric covering
neighborhood from C.81. Both its diameter and its a-oscillation are
then controlled. Haver's k=0 subdivision conclusion concerns the
union of the closed simplices meeting a vertex, so it is exactly
the closed star used here. Pulling these simplices back through
covering charts gives the original lifted triangulation and original
deck action. The star lies in one sheet; its projection is injective.
The inequality a(v)<a(y)+1 gives precisely the reciprocal bound in
C.82.4. Local finiteness survives this actual covering pullback.

2. Nullity in the original disk.
For a compact chosen lift C, the actual compact-covering lemma
supplies T with C contained in T(x_0). The equality
qz'=ts^{-1}(qz) uses only commutation in O. Thus all translates
of C based near an original boundary point are controlled by one
compact boundary-fixed family TT^{-1}. Compact evaluation gives
C.82.6 uniformly; it does not require a boundary value for q.

The proof of finite-large-star nullity exhausts both possibilities.
If the quotient vertices escape the proper a-sublevels, the d_Q
diameter bound tends to zero. Otherwise only finitely many quotient
vertices occur, and a subsequence of the lifted stars consists of
translates of one compact chosen star. The compact-core estimate
then forces its diameter to zero as well. Local finiteness excludes
an interior limit for points chosen in distinct lifted stars.
The passage to triangles is valid because each star has only
finitely many incident simplices. Avoidance of any fixed finite
list of compact triangles gives the exact boundary form C.82.7.

3. Group isomorphism with exact inverse maps.
A deck transformation preserving a chosen lifted triangle is
identity because its projection is injective. Therefore the
prescription q h_sigma q^{-1} is unambiguous on every face.
It agrees with the identity on all common edges and vertices.
Interior local finiteness gives continuity there, and the null
triangle property gives original-boundary continuity for arbitrary
tuples. The same argument applied to coordinate inverses supplies
a continuous inverse map. Reindexing by left multiplication in Q
proves the exact commutator equations.

Conversely, a skeleton-fixed homeomorphism fixes every triangle
boundary. Its bounded complementary component has compact closure
in O; the other component does not. An interior homeomorphism
preserves this property, so the triangle is preserved. Restriction
and equivariant pasting are consequently genuine inverse group
maps, with no unspecified face permutation.

4. Product topology, including inverses.
Restriction to each invariant compact triangle is continuous.
For the pasting direction, finitely many actual lifted triangles
have diameter above epsilon/4. Convergence of their finitely many
factor coordinates, followed by their fixed compact conjugations,
controls both uniform distances on these triangles. On all other
triangles, both compared images remain in that same triangle and
are epsilon/4-close regardless of the coordinate maps. The same
observation controls inverse maps. This proves continuity from
the product topology into the exact original closed-disk topology.

5. Whole-group contraction and retained scope.
The Alexander formula agrees across its radial seam, has inverse
A_r(h^{-1}), and displaces every point and inverse point at most
2r near r=0, uniformly in h. It is jointly continuous in the full
two-sided compact-open topology. Taking the coordinate product
and transporting through the proved topological isomorphism gives
the asserted contraction of the whole skeleton stabilizer, fixing
the group identity. This contraction does not require the chosen
triangle coordinates to be equivariant: the actual equivariance
is supplied by the specified pasting formula.

The statement and metadata correctly leave the deformation of an
arbitrary original sphere family into the skeleton stabilizer
unproved. No inverse limit of C.81's finite evaluation bundles is
used. No numerical producer is claimed or needed for this proof.
