1. Actual quotient and intrinsic target topology

Forward and inverse homeomorphic limits preserve the frontier of the
open G-orbit. A limit sends an interior point into its closure and
cannot put it on the invariant frontier, so L really preserves O.
The Polish transitive-action theorem applies to this actual L-action,
and its orbit quotient is O in the given topology.

The inclusion J <= L_z proves both well-definedness and equivariance
of Phi_z. The joint action on Fix(J) times L descends through the
open product quotient. Since O is locally compact, its adjoint map
into the compact-open function space is continuous. This proves
the stated evaluation homeomorphism, including inverse continuity.

For a line target, each compact coordinate disk has Peano image.
C28 excludes a triod and a Jordan curve in that intrinsic orbit,
so the image is a point or an arc. The arc between its endpoints
is the unique arc in B and lies in one of C28's compact intrinsic
arcs; its intrinsic and ambient topologies agree. This justifies
continuity into the intrinsic line without assuming that the whole
intrinsic line has its ambient subspace topology.

The point-evaluation formula Phi_z(lgx) proves continuity of every
restricted evaluation. Ordered compact partitions, together with
the inverse evaluations, then prove continuity into Homeo(B).
The orientation character is consequently positive by density of
G and the original based-loop paths. A second application of the
Polish orbit-map theorem gives B=L/L_z in this exact topology.
Thus J <= L_z really produces an open quotient.

2. Source/target covers and the compatible lift group

The source universal covering surface is a plane. A lift F exists
because its source is simply connected, and local covering charts
make F open. All three defining equations of the compatible group
are closed point-evaluation conditions in the displayed product of
Polish groups. They are preserved by multiplication and inverse.
The group is therefore a closed Polish subgroup with the indicated
continuous actions.

Every source lift of an l in L is a homeomorphism and has exactly one
compatible target lift: both candidate maps lift the same map to B,
and a target deck translation matches their values at one point.
Uniqueness of lifts gives the full equality Fa=bF. This does not
require a continuous choice of lifts or prior surjectivity of F.
Source deck transformations act transitively in each source-cover
fibre, proving transitivity of the compatible group on the plane.

The image of F is an open interval preserved by every compatible
increasing b. Any finite endpoint would be fixed by all such b,
and its projection would be a common fixed point of the transitive
L-action on the nontrivial circle B. Hence the interval is R.
The line-target case is directly surjective from the covering equation.

3. Separating components, Polish stabilizers and accessibility

Frankel's lemma applies to the closed fibre, including when it is
unbounded. Its printed proof chooses the complementary component
V of the closure of the first complementary domain. The manuscript
correctly uses the connected frontier of V, rather than the printed
final phrase's missing frontier symbol.

The compatible subgroup fixing t is closed and transitive on the
fibre. A group element carrying two points in one component to one
another preserves that component, so its component stabilizer is
transitive. This stabilizer is closed: both forward and inverse
limits preserve the closed component. The component is a closed
Polish subspace of the plane, so the Polish orbit-map theorem
applies to this stabilizer action.

For a complementary domain D, fixing d in D makes its stabilizer
the open condition a(d) in D. Images of its orbits under the open
orbit map are open in the connected component C; they partition C,
so there is just one orbit. This is the precise use of Effros.

There is an accessible boundary point of D by minimizing distance
from an interior point to the nonempty closed frontier. The segment
up to that nearest point lies in D. Transitivity of the domain
stabilizer transports it to every point of C. Thus all points of
C are accessible from each fixed complementary domain; this is
stronger than mere accessibility from some varying domain.

4. Exactly two domains and the compactification argument

Three distinct points of C and three complementary domains would
give three embedded Y-shaped trees. In each domain one first joins
two accessible end-cuts by an interior arc and then joins a third
end-cut to its first intersection with that arc. The endpoints on
C are distinct, and every other point of the tree is in the chosen
domain. The three interiors are therefore disjoint. Their union
is an embedded K3,3, contradicting its planar Euler inequality.
This proves exactly two domains because separation already gives
at least two.

For an unbounded closed connected C, adjoining infinity gives a
compact connected proper subset of the sphere, with the same two
complementary domains. All possible inaccessible points from either
domain are confined to infinity. Choosing a pole in a complementary
domain produces a compact planar set. Removing that one interior
point leaves its domain connected, and a shortened boundary
end-cut avoids the pole. Thus Polulyakh's exact compact, connected,
two-sided, zero-dimensional-defect hypotheses all hold.

The resulting Jordan curve cannot be a bounded component of the
original real-valued fibre: on the closed Jordan disk the constant
boundary value forces an interior extremum unless the whole disk
is constant, and either alternative contradicts openness of F.
The unbounded component is consequently a Jordan curve with its
point at infinity removed. It is closed in the covering plane,
so this line embedding is proper.

The compact downstairs component assertion uses only actual
ambient neighborhood homeomorphisms from L_z and the transitive
component stabilizer. Such a component is a nondegenerate proper
compact continuum in the sphere, satisfying Brechner--Erkama's
Theorem 2. No covering restriction onto an arbitrary downstairs
component is asserted.

5. The spiral action and its exact scope

On each moving interval of the lifted sine flow the coordinate
log(tan(delta/2)) translates by b. The flow fixes pi Z and moves
every angle by at most pi. This uniform bound gives continuity
at r=1 and properness at infinity for B_b and its inverse.
Its definition is independent of an angular representative.
A_a changes both s and theta by a, so it preserves s-theta and
commutes exactly with B_b.

The spiral-strip parameter map is injective by the 2pi angular
ambiguity and the width pi of the delta interval. In the coordinates
(t,log(tan(delta/2))) the action is ordinary R2 translation.
The current proof correctly first shows that a homeomorphic limit
preserves O, and only then uses evaluation in O to recover finite
parameters. This establishes closedness without assuming that a
sequence of strip points cannot approach its frontier.

The triangular parameter loop generates both real coordinate axes
and contracts by scaling its parameters. The cyclic operators
A_(-2pi n) commute with it and converge on compact subsets of O
to the displayed circle quotient. The whole fibre is exactly the
union indexed by t=theta0+2pi k. The real lift has one proper line
per value. The symbolic program checks these formulas, not the
topological theorems.
