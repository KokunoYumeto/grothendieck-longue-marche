Original-topology obstruction to the compact-open exhaustion estimate

This is a record of one exact failed transfer in the noncompact C.12
attack. It is not a claimed obstruction to a contraction of the original
group: C.87 already contracts the entire radial group used below.

Retain C.84's original j:R2 -> Int D, j(z)=z/(1+|z|), and the individual
operator T(x,y)=(x+1,y). Its quotient is N=(R/Z)xR. The extended plane
translations give the actual transitive boundary-fixed commuting group,
so C.81 and C.88 apply to precisely this disk and covering.

For an integer m>=1 put R=m+1 and K_m=(R/Z)x[-m,m]. Define
  beta(u)=max(0,1-4|u|),
  F_(R,t)(x,y)=(x+t R beta(y-R),y),  0<=t<=1.
The inverse subtracts the same t R beta(y-R), since y is unchanged.
Thus each F_(R,t) is a homeomorphism, and the same formula proves
F_(R,t) T^q=T^q F_(R,t) for every integer q. Both map and inverse are
continuous in (t,x,y). Their quotient supports lie in
(R/Z)x[R-1/4,R+1/4], disjoint from K_m. The whole path fixes K_m,
starts at identity and ends at F_(R,1).

For fixed R, the Euclidean displacement of both map and inverse is at
most R, uniformly over t. As |z| tends to infinity, bounded displacement
makes both the radial length and direction of j(F_(R,t)(z)) converge
uniformly in t to those of j(z). Consequently h_(R,t)=j F_(R,t) j^{-1},
extended by identity on S1, is a continuous path in the original
closed-disk centralizer, including maps and inverses. It belongs to
the identity path component of the original stabilizer of pi^{-1}(K_m).

At z=(0,R), beta(0)=1, so F_(R,1)(z)=(R,R). The horizontal coordinates
of their radial images are respectively0 and R/(1+sqrt(2)R). Thus
  sup_(w in D) |h_(R,1)(w)-w| >= R/(1+sqrt(2)R) > 2/5.
The last inequality holds for every real R>=1. Set R=1+s with s>=0.
Then 5R-2=3+5s>0 and
  (5R-2)^2-8R^2 = 1+14s+17s^2 >0.
Squaring against the positive 2sqrt(2)R gives
5R-2>2sqrt(2)R, which is exactly the asserted bound.

It follows that the diameters of these actual stabilizers, even their
identity path components, do not tend to zero for the original uniform
disk metric as the compact quotient cores K_m exhaust N. In contrast,
the same sequence of descended maps and inverses converges to identity
on every compact subset of N, because its supports eventually avoid
that subset. The lift fixing any specified point over K_1 is precisely
the displayed F_(R,1). This gives the exact map and sequence witnessing
the failure of inverse continuity from this compact-open quotient
subspace to the original disk topology.

Source comparison
The retained primary Yagasaki2000 PDF, Section4, Lemma4.4 and the proof
of Proposition4.1(1), printedpages11-12, constructs a contraction by
successively fixing compact cores. Its metric is the ordinary weighted
compact-open metric, in which the complete core stabilizer has diameter

transfer; it leaves the C.87 contraction valid and the general
noncompact-quotient family-tail construction unresolved.
