1. The actual full lift group

The closure Q of the restricted operator image is the group from C.30 with its actual homeomorphism topology. In particular Q is closed and acts freely and properly. Every homeomorphism of O lifts to its universal cover. A lift is determined by its downstairs map and its value at one point.

For convergent downstairs homeomorphisms and compatible convergent chosen lift values, a finite chain of covering charts along a compact path transports the selected sheet; finitely many such chains and local neighborhoods cover any compact subset. Applying the same argument to the inverses gives convergence in the actual homeomorphism-group topology. Conversely, finitely many local source sections control downstairs maps on compact subsets. A limiting lift therefore descends to a homeomorphism in the closed group Q, proving closedness of the full lift group.

For properness, convergence of x_n and a_n x_n first supplies convergence of a subsequence of the downstairs q_n by Q-properness. In a covering chart around x, the convergence of x_n and of their lifted images fixes the target sheet and forces a_n x to converge to y. The lift with a x=y is unique, and the preceding compact covering-chart comparison gives convergence of the lifts and their inverses. This proves the proper-action criterion in the metrizable spaces actually present. Freeness follows first downstairs and then from deck freeness.

Evaluation is consequently an embedding onto a closed orbit. That closed subset of the plane is locally compact, so the full lift group is locally compact before Hilbert--Smith is applied. Its free proper orbits are embedded manifolds after that application, giving dimension at most two.

4. The dense lattice and its exact foliation conjugacy

For Z squared with injective translation character, the two real image generators have irrational ratio, so the image is dense. Saturating an F-fibre is exactly the inverse image of its dense translate set of levels. Openness of F makes this saturation dense. Therefore every descended leaf on the quotient torus is dense. The quotient torus is orientable, and deck maps preserve both the surface and the transverse orientation, so the leaf orientation descends.

The oriented flowbox construction supplies a nonsingular continuous flow whose orbits are these leaves. Minimality guarantees a return to a small transverse interval. The first-return arc is embedded and its interior avoids the interval; its endpoints are distinct because there is no periodic orbit.

The tilt construction is valid with an explicit sign choice. In the transverse orientation, suppose the return endpoint lies to the right of the initial point. Take a sufficiently thin embedded flow strip around the compact first-return arc and tilt its transverse coordinate strictly to the left by a small amount. The new return endpoint remains strictly between the original endpoints. The connector back to the initial point along the original transverse interval has the same leftward transverse direction. The reversed-order case uses the opposite sign. Local flowboxes straighten both corners, keeping the resulting curve embedded and everywhere transverse. The arc's compact interior is separated from the transverse interval; choosing the strip sufficiently thin avoids additional intersections.

Every dense leaf meets this closed transversal. The auxiliary flow is minimal on a compact space, so its forward and backward limit sets are the whole torus. All returns therefore exist. A compact flowbox collar gives a positive lower bound for immediate returns; the compact interior of a fixed return arc avoids the transversal, and the terminal flowbox supplies the continuous return time and return point. This proves the stated continuous first-return map. It is orientation preserving by the oriented transverse holonomy and is minimal by the density of every leaf.

Following each point for a fraction of its positive continuous return time gives precisely the mapping-torus homeomorphism. Every point has a last transversal intersection before it, giving surjectivity and uniqueness modulo the stated endpoint relation. Compactness of the mapping torus supplies inverse continuity.

The orbit-average invariant measure has full support by minimality and has no atoms because a positive atom would give infinitely many equal masses along a nonperiodic orbit. Its cumulative distribution is therefore a circle homeomorphism. Conjugating by it gives an increasing Lebesgue-measure-preserving circle map; equality of interval lengths makes that map a rotation.
