# The full prefix fiber hull need not admit a continuous base lift

This is a limitation of a particular lifting prescription. It supplies neither a failure of C12 nor a failure of lifting the base median itself. C62 remains applicable: the interval hulls considered here lie in the actual three-word images, which are a larger class of allowed values.

## The actual action and original quotient

For positive real λ and real c,d, let

\[
A_{\lambda,c,d}(x,y)=(\lambda x+c,e^d y).
\]

These maps form the closed plane group Aff⁺(R) × R. The coordinates of a homeomorphic limit are recovered by evaluating two points of the horizontal axis and two points of one vertical line; injectivity excludes a zero horizontal or vertical scale. Thus the group is closed with its stated Lie topology. It acts transitively on each open half-plane. Its only nonopen orbit is the horizontal axis B, on which its action is the affine line action.

Take the original operator group H to be the vertical scaling subgroup. Every displayed map commutes with H. On the upper half-plane O use the actual homeomorphism

\[
\chi:O\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2,\qquad
\chi(x,y)=(u,v)=(\log y,x).
\]

The action in this chart is exactly

\[
(u,v)\longmapsto(u+d,\lambda v+c).
\]

At the source point (x,y)=(0,1), the stabilizer consists of the maps with c=d=0. Its fixed set in the sole nonopen orbit B is {(0,0)}. Consequently the full family of actual nontrivial global quotients has exactly one member for that source label:

\[
\Phi(x,y)=(x,0),\qquad F(u,v)=v.
\]

No target or source period occurs.

## A based original loop

Interpolate each of λ,c,d linearly between these parameter values:

| Time | λ | c | d |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 1/6 | 5/4 | 1 | 2 |
| 1/3 | 3/4 | 1 | 3 |
| 1/2 | 3/2 | 1 | 2 |
| 2/3 | 3/2 | 1 | 3 |
| 5/6 | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

The positive values of λ make every interpolated map a homeomorphism. Write f_t for this based loop. It is already null-homotopic in the H-centralizer: replace its parameters by

\[
\lambda_r(t)=1-r+r\lambda(t),\qquad
c_r(t)=r c(t),\qquad d_r(t)=r d(t),
\quad 0\le r\le1.
\]

The subgroup generated by the loop is the whole displayed group. On the segment from 1/2 to 2/3, taking relative maps gives an interval of pure vertical translations in u; these generate the full vertical R. After cancelling this vertical factor, the first segment gives dilations about the line point -4. On the segment from 1/3 to 1/2, the base maps have the form v↦λv+1. Taking a relative map A_λ A_λ₀^(-1) gives v↦(λ/λ₀)v+1-λ/λ₀, which is a dilation about 1; its vertical factor is again cancelled by the already available vertical subgroup. An interval of positive dilation parameters generates every positive parameter, so both complete dilation groups are present. For equal parameters λ, the product of a dilation about p with the inverse dilation about q is the translation by (1-λ)(p-q). The two distinct centers therefore supply an interval of nonzero translations, hence every translation and the full affine group. The original open orbits and quotient just specified are thus exactly those of the loop-generated group.

## The median base and its full-prefix crossings

Fix the prefix length ρ=1/3 and the endpoint time T=5/6. For |v|<1/4 the running maximum of the real base path up to ρ is

\[
M(v)=1+v+\frac{|v|}{4}.
\]

Indeed the affine base coordinate is linear on every time segment, so its prefix maximum is the maximum at the vertices 0,1/6,1/3. The initial value v is below both other values near zero. The chosen endpoint is b_T(v)=v+3>M(v). C27's median base at these parameters is therefore exactly M(v).

For each source point (u,v), now allow **all** original times in the larger prefix [0,T], and take the hull in the real fiber F=M(v) of every crossing

\[
f_s(u,v),\qquad 0\le s\le T,\qquad b_s(v)=M(v).
\]

For 0<v<1/4, the crossing at time 1/6 has fiber shift 2. There is one further crossing on the segment from 1/3 to 1/2. Its fractional position on that segment is exactly

\[
\frac{(1+5v/4)-(1+3v/4)}{(1+3v/2)-(1+3v/4)}
=\frac23.
\]

The fiber shift there is 3+(2-3)(2/3)=7/3. Every other segment is strictly below or strictly above the level except for the already listed shared endpoints. Thus the full-prefix fiber hull has first-coordinate interval

\[
[u+2,u+7/3]\qquad(0<v<1/4).
\]

For -1/4<v<0, the prefix maximum is attained at time 1/3, with fiber shift 3. The segment from 2/3 to T later crosses the same level, but its fiber shift is identically 3. All remaining crossings are the same shared endpoint. Consequently the full-prefix fiber hull is the singleton

\[
\{u+3\}\qquad(-1/4<v<0).
\]

At v=0, all times from 1/6 through 2/3 lie at base level 1; their shifts fill [2,3]. The fiber hull at zero is [u+2,u+3].

## No continuous selection exists from these hulls

Any lift of the specified median base whose value at (u,v) is required to lie in this full-prefix fiber hull has fiber coordinate u+3 for every negative v close to zero, but has fiber coordinate at most u+7/3 for every positive v close to zero. Evaluating at the fixed source value u=0 proves that such a lift cannot be continuous at v=0. The larger hull at v=0 cannot change these incompatible one-sided limits.

This obstruction permits an arbitrary choice anywhere in the whole filled interval and allows the entire original prefix up to T, not just the shorter prefix that defines the running maximum. Thus pointwise interval filling alone does not supply a transverse continuous lifting rule. The explicit null-homotopy above shows exactly why this is a limitation of that rule rather than a failure of the underlying centralizer problem.
