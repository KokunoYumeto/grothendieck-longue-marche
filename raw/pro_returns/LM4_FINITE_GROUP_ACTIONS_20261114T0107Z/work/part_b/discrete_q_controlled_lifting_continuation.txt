CONTINUATION AUDIT AFTER C81-C83
Unregistered research note. Previously accepted fragments are unchanged.
All assertions called proved below have their maps and proofs in this note.
No general family-to-skeleton deformation is asserted.

1. Q-NATURAL FILLED CARRIERS AND SIMPLY CONNECTED BALL COMPONENTS

Use the actual unit disk Dbar with its Euclidean distance d, its interior
O, the original free proper operator action Q, and C81's compatible
invariant metric d_Q(x,y)=sup_(q in Q)d(qx,qy).

For compact K subset O, let Fill(K) be K together with the bounded
components of its complement in the ambient Euclidean plane. Since K
lies in the convex open disk O, Fill(K) is compact in O. Equivalently,
it is O minus the unique component of O\K whose closure is not compact
in O. Uniqueness follows from the circle collar outside a compact
Euclidean subdisk containing K; every other complementary component
lies in the compact convex hull of K.

For each actual q in Q, homeomorphism invariance of compactness and of
complementary components therefore proves the exact equation

       q Fill(K) = Fill(qK).

No extension of q to the original circle is used. Also Fill(K) is
contained in the Euclidean convex hull of K: any point outside that
convex hull has a separating line and hence a path to infinity in
the complement of K.

Let K be compact inside B_(d_Q)(x,r). Compatibility of d_Q gives
s=max_(k in K)d_Q(x,k)<r. For every q, the compact set qK lies in
the closed Euclidean ball centered at qx of radius s. Its filled
hull lies there by convexity. Consequently

       Fill(K) subset {z:d_Q(x,z)<=s} subset B_(d_Q)(x,r).

Every component C of B_(d_Q)(x,r) is simply connected. To see this,
put an arbitrary loop in C inside the interior of a compact connected
polygonal planar surface W subset C. Such a W is obtained from a
finite cover of its compact image by small disks inside C, joined
within C and replaced by a finite polygonal regular neighborhood.
Filling W's finitely many holes gives a closed topological disk.
The preceding estimate puts that entire fill inside the same ball;
it is connected and meets C, hence lies in C. The loop is null
there, with its basepoint retained. This also supplies a compact
disk carrier for each individual filling.

This statement controls individual carrier sets. It gives no
continuous choice over all centers or all families, and no ambient
equivariant lift of those fillings.

2. SMALL CARRIERS CAN HAVE LARGE CONNECTED SATURATION

Retain C83's literal radial coordinate
j(z)=z/(1+norm(z)), its inverse w/(1-norm(w)), and
Q=<j T_(1,0) j^(-1)>.
For n>=2 let K_n be the closed Euclidean radius-one disk in the
plane centered at (0,n^2), transported by j to O.

For z,w in the original planar radius-one disk and each integer m,
the C83 radial estimate gives

 |j(z+(m,0))-j(w+(m,0))|
 <=2|z-w|/(1+|w+(m,0)|)
 <=4/n^2.

Indeed the last planar norm is at least n^2-1. Hence
diam_(d_Q)(K_n)<=4/n^2.

The disks translated by consecutive integers overlap, so their
union is connected. It contains the full horizontal line y=n^2:
every real x is within 1/2 of an integer disk center. The radial
image of this line has limit points (-1,0) and (1,0). Its diameter
has supremum 2. Thus

       diam_d(union_(q in Q) qK_n)=2

while diam_(d_Q)(K_n) tends to zero. These carriers are compact
disks, so filling them does not change the calculation. The setup
retains C81's transitive actual boundary-fixed translation subgroup.

Therefore a displacement estimate for a proposed lift cannot be
deduced merely by bounding the diameter of this saturated support.
This example does not rule out a controlled trajectory construction
inside the overlapping saturation.

3. THE LITERAL FABEL PATH AND THE ACTUAL Q-LABEL EQUATIONS

Primary source inspected:
Paul Fabel, Isotopies of planar compacta need not extend to isotopies
of planar continua, arXiv:math/0202163v1:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0202163
Corollary 7 and its displayed construction are on printed pages 5-6.
The construction uses the convergent sequence {0,1,1/2,1/3,...};
on each active interval one label circles its neighbor while every
other label stays fixed. This note imports no conclusion from that
construction for an operator action.

Here is the exact comparison. For an actual Q-equivariant labelled
configuration path, indexed by a Q-invariant set V, the equations are

       z_(qv)(t)=q z_v(t),       z_v(0)=v.

Since q is injective,
z_v(t) differs from v exactly when z_(qv)(t) differs from qv.
Thus the set of moved labels at every time is Q-invariant. A free
infinite Q-action has only infinite orbits, so a nonempty finite set
of moved labels cannot occur. In particular, the literal Fabel
one-moving-label intervals cannot satisfy these equations on even
one, or on finitely many, actual free infinite Q-orbits.

This is a direct obstruction to assigning the required labels to
that path. It does not assert that no modification of the example
could ever interact with an operator action. No such modified
example has been constructed in this work.

4. EXACT SUBLINEAR DESCRIPTION IN THE RADIAL TRANSLATION MODEL

Again fix exactly C83's radial disk and Q, and write a plane
homeomorphism commuting with T_(1,0) as

       F(x,y)=(X(x,y),Y(x,y))
             =(x+u(x,y), y+w(x,y)).

Both u and w are periodic of period one in x. Let C_rad be the
actual original-disk centralizer, conjugated to plane maps using j.
Then F belongs to C_rad exactly when

       lim_(R->infinity)
       sup_(0<=x<=1, |y|>=R)
       norm(F(x,y)-(x,y))/|y| = 0.                 (A)

Its inverse satisfies the same condition. No coordinate is changed
after this choice of j.

Proof of necessity, including compact families.
Let F_i range in a compact family of actual boundary-fixed maps.
Suppose |y_i| tends to infinity and 0<=x_i<=1. Choose integers
m_i^+,m_i^- with

       x_i+m_i^+ = y_i+e_i^+,
       x_i+m_i^- = -y_i+e_i^-,
       |e_i^+|,|e_i^-|<=1/2.

After a subsequence sign(y_i) is constant. The two radial input
points converge to the two corresponding diagonal boundary
directions. Compactness of the actual family and pointwise fixation
of the original circle force the same two output limits. Periodicity
keeps the two output second coordinates equal to Y_i=Y_Fi(x_i,y_i).
It follows that

       (X_Fi(x_i,y_i)+m_i^+)/Y_i -> 1,
       (X_Fi(x_i,y_i)+m_i^-)/Y_i -> -1.

The ratios are defined eventually because the limiting boundary
directions have nonzero second coordinate. Subtraction gives
(m_i^+-m_i^-)/Y_i ->2. Since
(m_i^+-m_i^-)/y_i ->2, we get Y_i/y_i ->1.
Either ratio then gives u_Fi(x_i,y_i)/y_i ->0.
Thus (A) holds uniformly for every compact actual family. Applying
this to the compact family of inverses gives the inverse assertion.

Proof of sufficiency.
For any epsilon>0, (A) gives displacement <=epsilon |y| when
|y|>=R, uniformly in all x by periodicity. For |y|<=R, periodicity
and compactness bound displacement by a finite M_R. Consequently
norm(F(z)-z)/(1+norm(z)) tends to zero as norm(z) tends to infinity.
The explicit radial estimate C83.3 gives
norm(jF(z)-j(z)) tending uniformly to zero. The map jFj^(-1)
therefore extends continuously by the identity to the original
circle. It is a continuous bijection of the compact closed disk,
so its inverse is continuous. Necessity applied to that inverse
proves its version of (A).

The topology also has an exact metric description. Put

 ||F-H||_* =
 sup_(0<=x<=1, y in R)
 norm(F(x,y)-H(x,y))/(1+|y|),

 and delta_*(F,H)=||F-H||_*+||F^(-1)-H^(-1)||_*.

This is finite on C_rad and gives precisely the original disk
map-and-inverse topology under j.

For the forward implication, a convergent sequence together with
its limit is a compact actual family. The uniform form of (A)
controls its weighted tails, and actual convergence gives ordinary
coordinate convergence on [0,1] times every compact y-interval.
These two facts give convergence in ||.||_*; repeat for inverses.

Conversely, for a fixed H in C_rad the number

       c_H=sup_(x,y) (1+|y|)/(1+norm(H(x,y)))

is finite. At large |y|, (A) implies |Y_H(x,y)|>=|y|/2;
on the remaining y-band the numerator is bounded. The radial
estimate with basepoint H(x,y) consequently gives

       sup_z |jF(z)-jH(z)| <= 2c_H ||F-H||_*.

The inverse version gives the other term of the original disk
metric. This proves both continuous inverse topology maps.

5. AN EXPLICIT CONTRACTIBLE SUBSPACE WITH BOTH ACTUAL CONTROLS

Let M be the subspace of C_rad consisting of the F for which,
for every real x, y |-> Y_F(x,y) is an increasing homeomorphism
of R. The following is an actual contraction of all of M,
fixing the identity; it preserves the original Q labels.

Define

       V_F(x,y)=(x,Y_F(x,y)),
       H_F=F V_F^(-1).

Each V_F is a plane homeomorphism. Its inverse is jointly continuous:
at a converging sequence of parameters x and target values, monotonicity
and two bracketing y-values bound the inverse, and any convergent
subsequence must solve the same strictly monotone equation.
Periodicity gives V_F T_(1,0)=T_(1,0) V_F.
Condition (A) for V_F follows from that for F; hence V_F is an
actual element of C_rad. Thus H_F is also in C_rad, and it has
the form

       H_F(x,y)=(h_F,y(x),y),
       h_F,y(x+1)=h_F,y(x)+1,

with each h_F,y an increasing homeomorphism of R.
The inverse equations show that F=H_F V_F exactly.

For 0<=r<=1 put

       V_F,r(x,y)=(x,(1-r)y+rY_F(x,y)),
       H_F,r(x,y)=((1-r)x+r h_F,y(x),y),
       A_r(F)=H_F,r V_F,r.

The convex combinations are strictly increasing and proper on
their indicated real fibers; the displayed periodicity is unchanged.
They are plane homeomorphisms commuting with the original translation.
Their displacements are scalar multiples of the original respective
fiber displacements, so (A) holds uniformly for r in [0,1].
They therefore extend by the identity to the original circle.
The second coordinate of A_r(F) is the increasing function
(1-r)y+rY_F(x,y), so A_r(F) remains in M.
The faces are A_0(F)=identity, A_1(F)=F, A_r(identity)=identity.

Here are the actual parameter-topology checks. F |-> V_F is continuous
in weighted forward displacement, by the formula. The preceding
radial bound makes its disk extensions continuous in uniform forward
distance. On a compact space, uniform convergence to a homeomorphism
implies uniform convergence of inverses:

 sup |A_n^(-1)-A^(-1)|
 <= omega_(A^(-1))(sup |A_n-A|),

where omega_(A^(-1)) is a modulus of continuity of the fixed inverse.
Consequently F |-> V_F is continuous in the actual group topology.
The group operations then make F |-> H_F continuous.
For the interpolation, weighted forward differences are bounded by
r||F-H||_*+|r-s| ||H-identity||_* on either fiber subgroup.
The same radial bound and compact inverse argument give joint
continuity in (r,F) for both interpolated factors. Their product
therefore gives the asserted actual contraction, including the
inverse topology. This contracts based compact families lying
in M with every original boundary and Q label retained.

The inclusion M subset C_rad has not been replaced by an equality.
For an explicit map outside M, choose mutually disjoint Euclidean
disks of radius 1/10 centered at (k+1/2,0), k in Z, and rotate each
through angle pi on its radius-1/20 core, tapering the angle
continuously to zero at the radius-1/10 boundary. Use identical
radial formulas on all integer translates and the identity elsewhere.
This is a bounded-displacement Q-commuting plane homeomorphism, so
it belongs to C_rad. On the vertical line through a core center,
its second coordinate reverses the order of the two nearby points.
It therefore lies outside M.

The unresolved step in this model is a controlled deformation of
an arbitrary original family into M, or another explicit contracting
subspace, while retaining the weighted topology (equivalently the
original map-and-inverse topology). No such deformation is supplied
by the coordinate characterization or by the contraction above.
