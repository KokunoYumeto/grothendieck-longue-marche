# Varying the odd prime: an ordinary full three-primary tower and a successful degree-seven cover

Status: complete candidate, awaiting independent/root review. Integrated A files remain unchanged during the checkpoint08 build. Written 14 November 2026.

Proposed number: A.C2.13. This is a positive result for the stated curve and a simultaneous obstruction to using any of its abelian three-power étale covers. It does not decide universal A1.

## 1. Curve and exact statement

Let α²+α+1=0 in F4 and let

\[
 C/\mathbf F_4:\qquad y^2+y=x+\frac{\alpha}{x}
                                +\frac{\alpha^2}{x+1}.
\]

Then:

1. The Raynaud theta divisor of C contains no point of three-power order, including the origin. Every geometrically connected finite étale abelian three-group cover of C is ordinary; every nontrivial such deck group acts faithfully on geometric two-primary torsion.
2. Nevertheless, theta contains an explicit point of exact order seven. The associated connected étale cyclic degree-seven cover has genus eight and two-rank two, with trivial deck action on its geometric two-primary torsion.
3. Consequently A1 holds for C over every finite extension, for every finitely generated form of this geometric curve, and for its hyperbolic open subcurves.

The first assertion forces the odd prime to vary for this theta/deck-kernel construction. It is not an obstruction to all solvable odd covers, and it is not a point-section counterexample.

## 2. Geometry and theta convention

Let W0,W1,W∞ be the unique points over 0,1,∞. The three poles of the Artin–Schreier function are simple. Their different exponents are two, so Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus two and

\[
 \operatorname{div}(dx)=2W_0+2W_1-2W_\infty.
\]

The logarithmic differentials dx/x and dx/(x+1) are regular: at W0,W1,W∞ their valuations are respectively (0,2,0) and (2,0,0). They are independent and Cartier-fixed, so the curve is ordinary and has two-rank two.

The locally exact differential line bundle in the absolute-Frobenius exact sequence is

\[
 \mathcal B_C\simeq\mathcal O_C(B),\qquad
 B=W_0+W_1-W_\infty.
\]

This follows directly from the local module generated by an exact differential, whose divisor is half its ordinary differential divisor, exactly as in A.C2.11. Put

\[
 \Theta=\{\,\mathcal O_C(R-B):R\in C(\overline{\mathbf F}_2)\,\}.
\]

It is the Raynaud theta divisor for the convention
\(L\in\Theta\iff H^0(C,\mathcal B_C\otimes L)\ne0\).
Since 2B is canonical, Riemann–Roch gives Θ=−Θ. It is geometrically irreducible and defined over F4.

The ordinary cubic criterion A.C2.2 has obstruction
\[
 (1+\alpha+\alpha^2)^3+1\cdot\alpha\cdot\alpha^2=1\ne0.
\]
Thus every connected cyclic étale cubic cover is ordinary. Together with ordinariness of C, the all-character Frobenius criterion A.C2.5 gives
\[
 \Theta\cap J_C[3](\overline{\mathbf F}_2)=\varnothing.
\]
This includes the origin: ordinary C has H0(B_C)=0.

## 3. The entire three-primary theta packet is empty

Exact finite-field counts are

\[
 \#C(\mathbf F_4)=3,\qquad \#C(\mathbf F_{16})=15.
\]

They are independently enumerated by the certificate using the Artin–Schreier trace criterion, counting the three branch points separately. Newton identities and the genus-two functional equation give the Frobenius characteristic polynomial

\[
 P(X)=X^4-2X^3+X^2-8X+16.
\]

Let π be the F4-Frobenius endomorphism of J. The following exact integer identities are checked coefficient by coefficient:

\[
 X^5-1\equiv3X^3+6X^2-33\pmod{P(X)}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(X^5-1)(-207X^3-57X^2+248X+856)-40344\\
 &\quad=P(X)(-207X^4-471X^3-487X^2-1303X-2575).
\end{aligned}
\]

The first shows that π^5 fixes J[3]. The fixed-field theorem A.C2.12 therefore puts every three-primary point η on Θ in J(F4^5), so \((\pi^5-1)\eta=0\). Evaluating the second identity at π yields \(40344\eta=0\). Since

\[
 40344=3\cdot13448,\qquad 3\nmid13448,
\]

we have 3η=0. The preceding theta-three emptiness proves

\[
 \Theta\cap J[3^\infty](\overline{\mathbf F}_2)=\varnothing.
\]

There is no scalar-Frobenius or lattice-splitting assumption in this step. The polynomial identities are identities of endomorphisms because P(π)=0; they act on the whole three-primary group.

Now let D→C be any geometrically connected finite étale abelian three-group cover; equivalently, formulate this assertion over the algebraic closure. After extending the constants to split the characters, its direct-image algebra is the sum of the corresponding torsion line bundles Lχ. Every nontrivial one has three-power order, so none belongs to Θ. Each H1(C,Lχ) has dimension one, since C has genus two and Lχ is nontrivial of degree zero. The Frobenius arrows from χ to 2χ have zero kernel by the exact locally-exact-differential sequence, hence are isomorphisms. On the invariant summand H1(C,O), Frobenius is bijective because C is ordinary. Thus Frobenius is bijective on H1(D,O), proving D ordinary.

Every nontrivial character appears in the geometric two-primary representation: the Artin–Schreier identification gives the Frobenius-stable cohomology representation, and geometric two-torsion has the dual character representation. After scalar extension to Fbar2, all nontrivial characters therefore occur (duality only inverts them). Characters separate a finite abelian group of odd order, so its deck action is faithful. Equivalently one may use that the maximal odd abelian character summands all survive, as in A.C2.9. Since every quotient abelian variety of an ordinary abelian variety is ordinary, none of these curves has a nonconstant map to a positive-dimensional two-rank-zero abelian variety. These are method obstructions for this entire abelian three-primary tower.

## 4. An explicit point of order seven

Choose β with

\[
 \beta^2+\beta=\alpha,\qquad R=(\alpha^2,\beta).
\]

The point lies on C, since at t=α² the right side is α. The equation for β has no solution in F4 because the absolute trace of α is one; β lies in F16, and it can be chosen as a root of β⁴+β+1=0 with α=β²+β.

Put

\[
 u=x(x+1),\qquad a=x+\alpha^2,\qquad b=x^2+\alpha^2,\qquad
 h=a+b(y+\beta).
\]

Let
\[
 F=x^3+x^2+x+\alpha=u\left(x+\alpha/x+\alpha^2/(x+1)\right).
\]
The exact polynomial identity over F4 is

\[
 u(a^2+ab)+b^2(F+\alpha u)=(x+\alpha^2)^7.
\]

Consequently \(N_{C/\mathbf P^1}(h)=(x+\alpha^2)^7/u\).
At R, a(α²)=0 and b(α²)=1, so h(R)=0 and the value of its Artin–Schreier conjugate at R is one. Since the x-map is étale there, the zero has exact order seven and lies only at R.

At W0 and W1, b has nonzero values α² and α, respectively; the y-term therefore gives a simple pole at each. At W∞ the term x²y has pole order five and is the unique term of that order. There are no other poles. Thus

\[
 \operatorname{div}(h)=7R-W_0-W_1-5W_\infty.
\]

Because \(\operatorname{div}(u)=2W_0+2W_1-4W_\infty\),

\[
 \operatorname{div}(h/u^3)=7(R-B).
\]

Hence η=O(R−B) lies on Θ and is killed by seven. It is nonzero: if R∼B, then 2R is canonical, which on a genus-two curve makes R a ramification point of its hyperelliptic map. But x(R)=α² is different from 0,1,∞. Thus η has exact order seven.

The associated Kummer cover, for example z⁷=h/u³ after adjoining seventh roots of unity, is connected and étale. Its connectedness follows from the exact order of η; all valuations of h/u³ are divisible by seven, so its normalization is unramified. Its genus is 1+7(g(C)−1)=8.

## 5. Frobenius blocks and section separation

The six nonzero characters of C7 form two doubling orbits,

\[
 \{1,2,4\},\qquad \{3,6,5\}=-\{1,2,4\}.
\]

The point η hits Θ in the first orbit and −η hits Θ in the second, by symmetry. All nontrivial H1 character summands are one-dimensional. Each of the two Frobenius cycles therefore contains a zero arrow, so every nontrivial character summand is nilpotent. The invariant summand has stable rank two. Thus the cover has two-rank exactly two and its full order-seven deck group acts trivially on geometric two-primary torsion. This is the precise all-character orbit argument of A.C2.5–A.C2.8, not an inference from nonordinariness alone.

Apply A.C2.7: a collision on C would lift to a two-primary point-divisor difference upstairs. A nontrivial order-seven deck transformation fixes this difference, producing a linear equivalence between distinct effective divisors of degree two. The upstairs curve is nonhyperelliptic, since a free odd-order automorphism cannot act on a hyperelliptic curve: its descended action on the line has fixed points and would fix a point in a fibre of at most two points. The degree-two equivalence is therefore impossible. This proves A1 for the stated curve over every finite extension. Finite-base-field extension and the already proved A.8 spreading/proper-to-open reductions give the final scope.

The complete three-primary failure and seven-primary success coexist. No assertion about nonabelian three-group covers, arbitrary ordinary genus-two curves, or a universal choice of an odd prime is made.
