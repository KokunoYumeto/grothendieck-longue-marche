# Candidate extension of D.25 to every number field

**Status:** independently reviewed and approved by root; integrated as the separate strengthening D.26 in `sections/D_density_extension.tex`. The original D.25 proof is preserved.

## 1. Compatible almost-everywhere Kummer detection

Let k be a number field and let

\[
A\in\widehat{k^\times}=\varprojlim_n k^\times/k^{\times n}.
\]

Suppose that, for each n≥1, the image of A in k_v^×/k_v^{×n} is trivial at all but finitely many finite places v. The exceptional finite set may depend on n. Then A=1.

### 1.1 Roots lie in the cyclotomic field

Fix a prime power n=ell^r and represent the component of A by b∈k^×. Put F=k(mu_n,b^{1/n}). Suppose Gal(F/k(mu_n)) has a nonidentity element tau. It acts on the n roots of X^n−b by a nontrivial translation, so it fixes none. Every conjugate has the same fixed-point property. Chebotarev gives infinitely many finite places v, unramified in F and outside the finite sets dividing n or b, whose Frobenius conjugacy class is that of tau. At such a place the polynomial has no root in k_v: a local root would give a Frobenius-fixed root in the unramified splitting extension. This contradicts the hypothesis. Thus a chosen root alpha belongs to k(mu_n).

Write Gamma=Gal(k(mu_n)/k), and regard the Kummer class as the inflation of the cocycle

\[
c(\sigma)=\sigma(\alpha)/\alpha\in\mu_n,
\qquad c\in H^1(\Gamma,\mu_n).
\]

### 1.2 The cocycle is trivial on every cyclic subgroup

For every sigma∈Gamma, Chebotarev applied to the finite Galois splitting field k(mu_n) shows that sigma fixes at least one root of X^n−b. Otherwise the primes with this Frobenius would supply infinitely many failures of the local-root condition. If sigma fixes alpha*zeta, then c(sigma)=zeta/sigma(zeta). The cocycle restriction to the cyclic subgroup generated by sigma is therefore a coboundary. Thus c lies in the kernel of restriction to every cyclic subgroup of Gamma.

The argument uses only finite places away from n and b. It does not rely on a false assertion of single-level almost-everywhere injectivity.

### 1.3 Odd primary classes vanish; the 2-primary classes have exponent at most two

For odd ell, Gamma embeds in the cyclic group (Z/ell^r Z)^×, so Gamma itself is cyclic. The preceding paragraph gives c=0. Hence b is an ell^r-th power in k.

For ell=2 and r≥3, put

\[
\Gamma_0=\Gamma\cap(1+4\mathbf Z/2^r\mathbf Z).
\]

The ambient subgroup 1+4Z/2^rZ is cyclic, so Gamma_0 is cyclic; its index in Gamma is at most two. Restriction of c to Gamma_0 is zero. Inflation–restriction therefore puts c in the image of

\[
H^1(\Gamma/\Gamma_0,\mu_{2^r}^{\Gamma_0})
\longrightarrow H^1(\Gamma,\mu_{2^r}).
\]

Positive-degree cohomology of the group Gamma/Gamma_0 is killed by its order, which is at most two. Thus 2c=0, equivalently b²∈k^{×2^r}. For r=1,2, Gamma is cyclic, and the same exponent bound follows directly.

Consequently every odd-primary component of A is trivial, and the square of every 2-primary component is trivial. Chinese remaindering in the power completion gives A²=1.

### 1.4 The only possible residual torsion is excluded

The group k^×/mu(k) is free abelian. Indeed the unit subgroup modulo roots of unity is finitely generated free abelian, the image of the divisor map is a subgroup of the free abelian group on finite primes, and a short exact sequence with free abelian quotient splits. The power completion of a free abelian group is torsion-free: relative to a basis, it embeds in the product of copies of Zhat. Hence the torsion of the completed group is exactly the finite group mu(k), and its 2-torsion is {±1}. We have reduced to A=1 or A=−1.

Choose r large enough that mu_{2^{r+1}} is not contained in k. The roots of X^{2^r}+1 are exactly the primitive 2^{r+1}-st roots of unity. Every nonidentity element of Gal(k(mu_{2^{r+1}})/k) acts without a fixed point on this set: it acts by a unit u different from 1 modulo 2^{r+1}, and multiplication by an odd exponent cannot turn u−1 into zero modulo 2^{r+1}. Chebotarev again gives infinitely many finite places where −1 is not a 2^r-th power. This contradicts the stipulated local condition. Thus A=1.

This is a complete all-number-field proof. The original positive-real-radical argument for k=Q is unnecessary. The single-level example 16 at eighth powers remains a valid illustration over Q, and may be retained as an explicit warning inside the more general theorem.

## 2. Extension of the Legendre theorem

Replace Q by an arbitrary number field k and rational primes by its finite places. Let U=P1_k−{0,1,infinity}. Then every noncuspidal continuous section G_k→pi1(U) is asymptotically integral and integral at a set of finite places of Dirichlet density one. The same holds for every geometrically connected finite étale cover X→U.

The existing proof of D.25 applies with the following checked substitutions.

1. Every finite completion k_v is a finite extension of some Q_p, so D.6/D.19 gives its actual interior point or cusp. At the real places the real theorem gives the correct connected component, and complex places give no further issue. Thus the section is Selmer in Stix's sense over k.
2. Stix's Theorem 10 and its Legendre dichotomy are stated over an arbitrary number field. The full Legendre 2-torsion remains rational over U, and the geometric monodromy matrices and their three distinct modulo-2 lines are unchanged.
3. At a sufficiently good place v of odd residue characteristic, the arithmetic Frobenius has chi_2(Frob_v)=N(v), a positive integer greater than one. Thus chi_2 and chi_2^{-1} are distinct on G_{k_v}, including in the split cuspidal case. The same unique cyclotomically normalized line determines one of the three **k-rational** cusps at every such v. It is constant outside a finite set.
4. The unit f regular at that cusp with value1 is still one of 1−t, t, or t/(t−1). Its pullback A belongs to the completed k^×. At ordinary degenerate places it is a principal unit, and at exact cuspidal places it is1, independently of the tangent class. For each n, Hensel's lemma applies outside the places dividing n and a finite model set. The new compatible Kummer lemma gives A=1.
5. Local Kummer injectivity over every finite extension k_v of Q_p excludes all finite-local interior points, as before. Stix's Corollary5 and the corrected finite-place Stoll8.2 argument are valid for arbitrary number fields. The finite support is the same three-point boundary; no real boundary-point assertion is added. Hence the triangular alternative is cuspidal.
6. For X→U, the cusp decomposition/open-subgroup argument again preserves noncuspidality. Normalize P1 over O_k[1/S] in k(X) and enlarge finite S to a good marked model. Finite properness lifts integral local points integrally. The finite list of horizontal ramification indices transfers boundary multiplicity divisibility by d_base=e*d_cover. Removing S does not change Dirichlet density.

These modifications give the strengthened statement and no cofinite-integrality conclusion. In particular, they do not lift a section through an arbitrarily added interior puncture, and they do not prove the global section conjecture.

The source statements already opened for D.25 are sufficient: Stix Theorem10, Lemmas17–19, the local calculation in equation(5.1) of Theorem20, and Corollary5 are all used with their original number-field scope. The compatible Kummer lemma above is proved from Chebotarev, elementary structure of cyclotomic Galois groups, inflation–restriction, and the unit/divisor sequence.
