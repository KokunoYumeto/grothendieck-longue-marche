The criterion is genuinely stronger than point-section injectivity: a finite neighbourhood that kills a character on every residue field over a exists precisely when no character-nontrivial Galois element has its two section images geometrically conjugate. Compactness of one fixed conjugacy set gives the equivalence, with no finite-generation or section-space compactness assumption.

When such a cover exists, the new symbol extends to its Brauer group because every new residue is zero. An H2(mu_n) lift can be pulled back by the lifted section; D.23 removes the Picard ambiguity. This proves the desired test for that character, including ordinary equality places (where the character becomes locally zero) and existing cuspidal evaluations.

Every accessible cover has a rational point above3, hence a real point above2 by path lifting on the interval(1,infinity). Its residue field cannot containQ(i).
