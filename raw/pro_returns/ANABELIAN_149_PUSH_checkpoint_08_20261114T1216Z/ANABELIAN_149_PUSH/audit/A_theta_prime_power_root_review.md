# Independent review of the finite prime-power theta bound

The root reviewer read the full candidate and its integer-polynomial certificate. The Frobenius orbit argument is valid for every odd prime different from the characteristic, including three; it does not use scalar action or semisimplicity. If sigma is trivial on J[ell], its image on each finite ell-power torsion module is an ell-group. For the last nontrivial cyclic orbit, tau^ell fixes eta, and the operator `(1+tau+...+tau^(ell-1))/ell` is an integral automorphism congruent to one modulo ell. Thus `T=(tau-1)eta` is a nonzero ell-torsion point fixed by tau. The whole ell-element coset lies on both theta and its translate. The theta principal polarization has trivial translation stabilizer, and two distinct irreducible theta divisors have intersection number two, excluding at least three distinct common points. This proves actual rationality over the first-level torsion splitting field.

The two polynomial remainders were independently recomputed by SymPy polynomial division over the integers: `rem(X^12-1, X^4-X^2+16)=705*X^2-3345` and `rem((X^12-1)*(176+47*X^2)+27*41440, X^4-X^2+16)=0`. Cayley–Hamilton therefore makes Frobenius^12 trivial on J[3] without any assumption on the integral Tate lattice. On its fixed three-primary subgroup the second identity annihilates by 27 times a three-adic unit. The bound by 27 follows. No exact order for Frobenius modulo three is asserted or needed.

The root reviewer independently opened Boxall–Grant (2006), Proposition 2, printed page 16, and Pop–Saidi (2003), Proposition 2.1 and the quoted primary-projection statement, printed pages 112–113. The latter fixes selected primary components and does not eliminate every other component. Its proof uses pairwise coprime orders, not prime orders. The candidate's distinction is correct.

Approved for integration as A.C2.12. It gives a finite search reduction and an obstruction to the proposed arbitrary prime-power divisibility argument. It does not prove universal existence of a useful theta point. The exact order-nine point is supplied separately by A.C2.11; existence or nonexistence at order 27 is not claimed by this bound.
