# A finite orbifold centralizer detects the remaining fibre ambiguity

Status: root-reviewed, accepted, and integrated as A.29l. The historical candidate filename is retained. Root independently opened the full Boggi–Zalesskii lemma and checked every arithmetic normalizer and centralizer step. The exact external input is Lemma3.1 in its pro-S form; it is not a transfer from the full profinite result.

## Proposed assertions

Let k be a finite field of characteristic p, C/k smooth proper geometrically connected of genus at least two, and f:C→B a geometrically Galois map of degree prime to p. Make a finite constant extension to define its full deck group G and all initially chosen points.

**Fibre criterion.** Suppose every pair of equal prime-to-p point sections on C has equal images on B, and this remains true after every finite constant extension. Then A1 holds for C.

Consequences:

1. A1 holds for every such C admitting a prime-to-p Galois map to a genus-one curve, in every characteristic, with no commutativity hypothesis on G and no ordinary/supersingular restriction. The fibre hypothesis is exactly A.21, since the Galois map is ramified and a branch fibre has a common nontrivial prime-to-p ramification index.
2. A1 ascends through a prime-to-p Galois cover of any smooth proper hyperbolic curve already known to have A1 over all finite extensions. This includes étale covers as well.
3. A1 holds for every solvable prime-to-p Galois cover of a genus-zero curve. Induct on the deck-group order: the prime-order case is A.22; for a proper nontrivial normal subgroup N, the quotient B=C/N has smaller deck group over the original genus-zero base. If g(B)≥2, use induction and consequence2. If g(B)=1, use consequence1. If g(B)=0, use induction on the smaller group N acting on C over B.

Consequently every odd-order Galois cover of a genus-zero curve in characteristic two is included, using the odd-order theorem. Its exact primary statement was opened in Feit–Thompson (1963), ChapterI, Section1, printed p.775, at https://msp.org/pjm/1963/13-3/pjm-v13-n3-p01-p.pdf (webref turn472view0). This deep external theorem is cited as a known input and is not reproved here. The prime-to-p Galois hypotheses do not follow from an arbitrary tame map or arbitrary Galois closure, so universal A1 is not concluded.

The geometric assertions extend to fields finitely generated over F_p by A.8 after a finite extension of the base field and spreading. Hyperbolic open subcurves follow as before.

## 1. The centralizer of a point section inside the geometric group is trivial

Write Π=Π_C^(p′), E=E_C^(p′), and s=s_P for an actual rational point P. Then

C_Π(s(G_k))=1.

Here is a proof from A.1–A.2, which does not assume A1. Let h centralize s(G_k). For an open characteristic N⊂Π, use the P-pointed characteristic cover C_N associated with N s(G_k). After a finite extension L/k killing the action on Π/N, it is an étale Galois cover with prime-to-p group Π/N. The image of h is a deck transformation. It sends the distinguished point P_N to an L-rational point R_N because it commutes with s(G_L).

The point sections of P_N and R_N are equal in E_{C_N,L}^(p′). Indeed their images in E_{C_L}^(p′) are s and hsh⁻¹=s, and the prime-to-p group of C_N identifies with the corresponding open subgroup, by the cofinality argument already proved in A.3. Thus A.1 says [R_N−P_N] is p-primary. If h has nontrivial image in Π/N, A.2 applied to that freely acting deck transformation contradicts this. Therefore h∈N for every characteristic N, and h=1. The argument works over every finite extension.

There is also an independent finite-neighbourhood proof using the usual Frobenius weights. If h≠1, choose a characteristic open N detecting h and let H be the closed subgroup generated by N and h. It is open and stable under s(G_k), and H/N is a nontrivial cyclic group generated by the image of h. The subgroup Hs(G_k) defines a pointed finite étale curve neighbourhood C_H over k. Its geometric prime-to-p group is H by the same cofinality argument. The image of h in H^ab is nonzero because its further image in the cyclic group H/N is nonzero. It is fixed by Frobenius, since h was fixed. But H^ab is the product of the prime-to-p Tate modules of J_{C_H}; Frobenius has no eigenvalue one on any rational Tate module, by its weight-one Weil eigenvalues. Thus Frobenius minus one is injective on every integral Tate module, and hence on their product. This is a contradiction. The argument does not assume that every element of a nonabelian group is seen in the original abelianization: the detecting abelianization is that of the specifically chosen neighbourhood H.

## 2. The geometric orbifold extension and the precise pro-S theorem

Let S⊂B be the branch set, B°=B−S, and C°=C−f⁻¹(S). Its Galois group G has prime-to-p order. For a geometric branch point b, let e_b be its inertia order; every e_b divides |G|. The tame prime-to-p local inertia in π₁(B°_kbar)^(p′) is procyclic, and write x_b for a generator. Define

Γ=π₁(B°_kbar)^(p′) / closure-normal-subgroup-generated-by {x_b^{e_b}:b∈S}.

There is an exact sequence

1→Π→Γ→G→1.

To justify the kernel, before filling the punctures the kernel of the map to G is the full prime-to-p group of C°: its induced topology is cofinal because G has prime-to-p order, using cores and finite products exactly as in A.3. Filling the punctures of C kills precisely the conjugates of x_b^{e_b}. Their normal closure in that kernel is normal in the entire group because all points above every branch point are filled. This gives the displayed sequence. Geometric inertia I_R in Γ has order e_b and maps injectively to G as the stabilizer of the corresponding geometric point R on C. This is equivalently the geometric prime-to-p group of the tame quotient stack [C/G], but no extra stack theorem is needed for the construction.

SGA1, ExposéXIII, Corollary2.12, identifies the punctured geometric prime-to-p group, with its inertia classes, with the prime-to-p completion of the standard surface presentation. Thus Γ is the pro-S completion of the discrete orientation-preserving orbifold group

Λ=<a_i,b_i,x_b : product_i[a_i,b_i] product_b x_b=1, x_b^{e_b}=1>,

where S is the class of all finite groups of order prime to p. This class is closed under subgroups, quotients, and extensions. The induced homomorphism Λ→G is onto and every x_b has exact order e_b in G. Its kernel is a torsion-free surface group: the associated topological branched cover has full prescribed local orders, so the corresponding orbifold covering is a surface. Its genus is g(C), by the same Riemann–Hurwitz calculation. Moreover

2g(B)−2+sum_b(1−1/e_b)=(2g(C)−2)/|G|>0,

so Λ is hyperbolic. It therefore satisfies every hypothesis of the following opened pro-S result.

Boggi and Zalesskii, *A restricted Magnus property for profinite surface groups*, Lemma3.1, gives: nontrivial geometric inertia subgroups have trivial intersection with any distinct conjugate, are self-normalizing, and every nontrivial finite subgroup of Γ is contained in one of them. In particular each inertia is a maximal finite subgroup, and a nontrivial finite subgroup determines a unique containing maximal inertia.

The exact primary PDF is https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.5419 , opened 14November2026. Lemma3.1 is on printed p.7/PDF index6, with its proof on pp.7–8. Its hypotheses explicitly require a torsion-free normal subgroup with quotient in S, not the full profinite completion. This is supplied above by C. Web records turn452view0, turn453view0–1, and turn457view0. The source's terminology “decomposition group” in Lemma3.1 refers to finite geometric orbifold inertia; it is distinguished here from an arithmetic decomposition group.

If S is empty, Γ is torsion-free and the same theorem says that it has no nontrivial finite subgroup. This will handle the étale case automatically.

## 3. Arithmetic extension and an explicit normalizer identity

Use the analogous quotient of the geometrically prime-to-p arithmetic group of B° by the same closed normal subgroup generated by x_b^{e_b}. Denote it by Γ_ar. The subgroup is stable under arithmetic conjugation because Galois permutes the branch points and sends a tame inertia generator to a conjugate of a unit power. Equivalently it is the geometrically prime-to-p arithmetic group of [C/G]. There are compatible exact sequences

1→Γ→Γ_ar→G_k→1,

1→E→Γ_ar→G→1.

The second comes from the G-torsor C→[C/G], or directly from the punctured cover and the same kernel calculation as in Section2. Its restriction to Γ is the map Γ→G above. The field-of-constants quotient is retained in full throughout.

For a branch point R on C, after making R rational and its finite inertia split, a choice of geometric paths gives an arithmetic subgroup

D_R=I_R⋊s_R(G_k)⊂Γ_ar.

It is obtained from the local tame decomposition subgroup at f(R), quotienting its inertia by the e_b-th-power subgroup; its intersection with E kills that finite inertia and is the usual point section of R. In particular

D_R∩Γ=I_R, D_R→G_k is onto, D_R∩E=s_R(G_k).

After adjoining the relevant roots of unity the displayed semidirect action is trivial, so I_R centralizes s_R. The injectivity I_R→G makes the last intersection identity exact: a product i s_R(σ) lies in E precisely when i=1.

We claim

N_{Γ_ar}(I_R)=D_R.

The inclusion of D_R is the local decomposition construction. Conversely take γ in the normalizer, and choose d∈D_R with the same G_k-image. Then γd⁻¹∈Γ and normalizes I_R. Boggi–Zalesskii's geometric self-normalization implies γd⁻¹∈I_R, so γ∈D_R. No assertion about an arbitrary section or an unproved arithmetic inertia normalizer is being imported.

The same description applies to an arbitrary conjugate I=γ₀I_Rγ₀⁻¹ with γ₀∈Γ. Conjugating D_R gives the local subgroup at the point g₀R of C, where g₀ is the image of γ₀ in G; an additional element of Π only changes the chosen geometric path. Thus its intersection with E is a representative of that actual point section. Passing to a finite constant extension makes this actual point rational and supplies the required local splitting.

## 4. The finite centralizer and the fibre criterion

Assume that P,Q are rational points of C whose sections are equal up to geometric conjugacy. The fibre hypothesis gives f(P)=f(Q). Since f is Galois, choose g∈G with gP=Q. If P≠Q, then g≠1. The action-groupoid description of Γ_ar, or functoriality under deck transformations, gives a lift γ∈Γ of g whose conjugation carries s_P to a representative of s_Q. Adjusting γ by an element of Π, using the assumed section equality, gives

γs_P(σ)γ⁻¹=s_P(σ) for every σ∈G_k.

Set H=C_Γ(s_P(G_k)). By Section1 its intersection with Π is trivial, so H→G is injective. Hence H is finite; it contains the nonidentity γ. Boggi–Zalesskii places H in a unique maximal inertia I. Arithmetic conjugation by any s_P(σ) sends I to another geometric inertia group, and the conjugate contains H because s_P centralizes H. Their intersection is nontrivial, so they agree. Therefore s_P(G_k) normalizes I.

Represent I as the inertia at an actual geometric point R of C, as in Section3. Make R rational over a finite extension L and split its inertia. The normalizer identity gives

s_P(G_L)⊂D_R∩E_L=s_R(G_L).

Both groups project isomorphically onto G_L, so these homomorphisms are equal, with the selected geometric path. Thus P and R have equal prime-to-p point sections over L. Applying the fibre hypothesis again gives f(P)=f(R). Since R is ramified, P is ramified as well.

Let I_P be the canonical full inertia at P for the original representative s_P. After a further finite extension splitting the finite stabilizer and its tangent character, I_P centralizes s_P. Put H_L=C_Γ(s_P(G_L)); it remains finite by Section1 and still contains γ. It also contains the nontrivial I_P. By the finite-subgroup theorem it lies in some maximal inertia J. Since I_P⊂J and inertia groups have trivial intersection unless equal, J=I_P. Consequently γ∈I_P. Its image g belongs to the stabilizer G_P, so Q=gP=P, a contradiction.

If f is étale, the earlier nontrivial finite H is already impossible, because Γ has no inertia and is torsion-free. Thus no ramification exception was suppressed.

This completes the fibre criterion. It uses section equality only for actual points, retains full arithmetic G_k, and never lifts an arbitrary section across a ramified cover.

## 5. Consequences and remaining boundary

For a genus-one quotient, Riemann–Hurwitz forces f to be ramified because g(C)≥2. Since it is Galois, any branch fibre has a common index e>1 dividing |G| and prime to p. A.21 therefore supplies the required fibre property for every finite extension. This proves consequence1, including ordinary elliptic quotients in characteristic two and nonabelian groups.

If the proper base B already has A1 over every finite extension, functoriality of point sections gives the fibre property. This proves consequence2.

The normal-subgroup induction stated at the start proves consequence3. For the genus-zero intermediate, the deck group is N of smaller order; for a hyperbolic intermediate, its map to the original genus-zero base has the smaller solvable group G/N. No claim about a degree that fails to decrease is used.

Finite-generation spreading requires first extending the base field to define the full G-action and quotient, then shrinking to preserve the smooth curves, the group action, and its tame quotient. The accepted finite-field theorem applies to the fibres and A.8 returns the desired separation.

Universal A1 still remains outside these assertions. An arbitrary tame map need not have prime-to-p Galois closure and need not give an admissible cover of the source. The genuinely nonsolvable prime-to-p genus-zero Galois case is also not supplied by the solvable-group induction alone. All earlier exact Cartier, Prym, and auxiliary obstructions remain correct statements about their respective methods even where their curves are now covered by the stronger theorem.
