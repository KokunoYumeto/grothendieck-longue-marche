# Independent review of the ramification-divisible auxiliary criterion

Reviewer: `/root/degeneration`. Reviewed `audit/A_branch_auxiliary_candidate.md` in full on 14 November 2026. This note records a mathematical review; it does not replace the author's exact finite-group certificate or source ledger.

**Verdict: passes.** The normalized-product argument correctly chooses a component and descends it after a finite extension. At a tame geometric local field, the ramification index of the compositum is the least common multiple, so the divisibility hypothesis makes the map to C étale. Its geometric Galois closure remains in the compositum with the prime-to-p Galois closure of the auxiliary. The map to H is Galois because C/P1 is Galois, even when the whole fibre product is disconnected.

When H has genus one, the source V has genus at least two because it is étale over C. Riemann–Hurwitz therefore forces V/H to ramify. The Galois property makes every point in a branch fibre have the same nontrivial prime-to-p index, exactly the all-fibre condition required by A.21. Equality of H-images gives equality only on the common base P1; the proof then invokes A.29l's fibre criterion. It correctly avoids claiming that the two normalization coordinates jointly distinguish points at ramified fibres.

The projective triple S, ST, T^-1 has product one and exact orders 2,3,r. Upper and lower elementary unipotents prove generation of PSL2(F_r), and the displayed diagonal commutator proves perfectness without needing an unquoted simplicity theorem. The involution and order-three fixed-point counts on P1(F_r), the two cycle-count Riemann–Hurwitz formulas, and all nine Borel quotient genus values were checked algebraically. For r=7, the normalizer of the translation subgroup is its point stabilizer, of order21; exactly three points are fixed in the branch fibre with inertia7, giving genus-zero cyclic quotient as stated.

The proof uses the original SGA1 realization theorem only for a prime-to-characteristic group and explicit generating inertia triple; it does not presume good reduction of a particular plane or modular model. The finite-field statement is asserted over every finite extension, as needed for the collision lift and A.29l. The finitely generated spreading statement properly allows a finite extension of the whole base field, rather than only its field of constants. The restrictions on characteristic and on the three branch divisibilities are explicit. No universal assertion about arbitrary nonsolvable signatures is made.
