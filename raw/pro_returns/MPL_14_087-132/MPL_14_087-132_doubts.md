## Notation register

This register was begun before the first scan and completed before the context archive was opened. The transcription is a diplomatic fragment: the same mathematical object can carry different notation on different sheets, and a later definition is not used to rewrite earlier ink. The root reader alone read, transcribed, and visually audited the 41 scans; a separate mechanical worker checked LaTeX syntax without reading the manuscript images or interpreting its text.

## How to use the doubt tables

The first table is generated from every actual `\unsure` and `\illegible` occurrence in the frozen page files, grouped only when exactly the same marked reading recurs on one scan. Line numbers refer to the individual `.tex` page file, not to a reconstructed manuscript line. The first reading is the preferred ink reading, not an editorial correction; “no separate alternative proposed” means precisely that the single reading remains tentative. Confidence is qualitative: **medium** means a plausible but insecure reading, **low** means the local strokes do not decide the supplied alternatives or the words are physically obscured, and **high** is used only where the page record explicitly warrants it.

The second table preserves the more descriptive locations and reasons recorded during the first reading. Those records are a history of the work, not additional readings to insert into the final transcription: the current in-place macro and first table control whenever the audit changed a preference. No unmarked text is warranted to be infallible by its absence from these tables.

## Legibility and limits of the diplomatic rendering

The typescript usually gives reliable continuous wording, but handwritten overwriting, typewriter cancellation, physical edge loss, and crossed paragraphs make some deleted words unrecoverable. The most difficult sustained handwriting is the marginal Cohen addition on scan 097; the overwritten primitive-group formulas on 103 and 113; the small labels and long arrows in the scan 116 table; and the crowded equivalence graph on 128. Scans 087, 096, 100 and 132 contain isolated signs for which mathematical plausibility cannot decide the ink. Eleven `\illegible` spans are used for obscured words, never for a mathematical symbol; the large cancelled prose spans on 104 are especially weak.

All 41 whole scans and all 82 supplied halves were looked at directly in the first reading; every page then received a second root-only visual reading after the initial transcription of all pages was complete. Further enlarged or rotated crops were used for difficult passages. No OCR was used. The audit record for each page states the checks and changes, and `progress.log` records the order and times.

TikZ reproduces diagrams, enclosures, leader lines and nonsemantic pen strokes, but it is not a facsimile of pen pressure or every cancellation hatch. The intended endpoint of the questioned diagonal arrow in scan 128 is not determined by the ink: the transcription places it beside the cancelled phi-times-psi annotation without asserting the logical implication that an editor might expect. The relative geometry of that graph and its sweeping enclosures is the least secure drawing reconstruction. The circled surjectivity insertion on 126 and boxed arrow label on 128 are retained explicitly. The large isolated upper strokes on 132 are drawn rather than silently assigned a numeral reading.

The fragment deliberately retains unfinished expressions, surprising dimensions and degrees, changes of notation, and incomplete typed arrows or brackets. Examples include `A^{2d-i}` on 098, the cone dimension `2n+1` on 113, the initial `Spec` inside the definition of `A'_0` on 114, `Alb(A)` after a source involving X on 126, and the y-prime in the second equality on 131. These are not repaired from mathematical expectations. Possible consequences or contextual reinterpretations belong only in the separate research notes.

Mechanical checks establish balanced LaTeX and successful compilation under the supplied structural QA wrapper; they are not an independent palaeographical reading. The QA wrapper intentionally suppresses the visual styling of editorial macros and is not a proposed publication layout. The final transcription is frozen before contextual research; its SHA-256 values are checked again at delivery.
