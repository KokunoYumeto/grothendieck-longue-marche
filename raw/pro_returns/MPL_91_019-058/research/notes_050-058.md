### C01 — The affine hypothesis in the questioned smoothness theorem

**Source:** cote 91, scan 050, theorem heading, inserted (i), and lifting proof. **Kind:** self-flagged. **Quote:** “Théorème?”; “Supposons T/S affine” (the words in the insertion remain doubtful). **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension (pages 49 à 53)”; notes/RESULTS_STATE.md, **MPL_31_002-051 N070**, first-pass lead about missing noetherian assumptions. **Relation:** differs from Hua's smoothed statement and adds a precise hypothesis audit to N070.

The page replaces a finite-flat assumption by a wider setting, retains local noetherian hypotheses, and then inserts affineness specifically in (i), where the proof uses the affineness of the pulled-back test scheme. Hua's proposition on page 49 does not retain that affine qualification, so it should not be cited as the manuscript's exact theorem. A modern verification can apply the infinitesimal lifting criterion after checking local finite presentation and the written affine test; [Stacks, Tag 02H6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02H6) supplies that criterion, but does not justify deleting the affine condition from this Hom argument.

### C02 — A direct repair of the étale argument

**Source:** cote 91, scans 050–051, part (ii), opening of the proof on 051. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “Ce raisonnement vaut aussi sans supposer T/S affine”; “ses fibres sont … finies”. **Confidence:** high for the formal-lifting argument, because unique étale lifts glue; its use requires the representing morphism to be locally of finite presentation. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, pages 49–50; **MPL_31_002-051 N070**, first-pass hypothesis lead. **Relation:** adds an alternative proof and differs from Hua's account of the written proof.

The manuscript first obtains smoothness and then tries to prove finite fibres, whereas Hua describes an unramifiedness argument followed by a flatness reduction. Instead, an étale \(g\) gives unique lifts across nilpotent thickenings of the pulled-back source, including nonaffine sources, and adjunction gives formal étaleness of the represented restriction map. Once local finite presentation has been checked, [Stacks, Tags 02HF, Lemma 37.8.2, and 02HM](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02HF) give étaleness directly, without the separate numerical bound questioned in C03–C04. This is a proposed verification of the theorem under its hypotheses, not a replacement of the frozen proof.

### C03 — A section is not determined by one point without separation

**Source:** cote 91, scan 051, paragraph after the two small diagrams. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “Mais le relèvement est connu dès qu'on connaît l'image d'un pt.” **Confidence:** high, because the two standard sections of the doubled-origin line are explicit. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, page 50; same_cote_91/MPL_90+91_002-018_notes.md, N91-14, cote 91 scan 014, concerns a different principal-homogeneous quotient and does not supply separation here. **Relation:** identifies a missing condition in the written proof, not a contradiction of the étale-preservation theorem.

Let \(T=\mathbf A^1_k\) and glue two copies of \(T\) along \(T\setminus\{0\}\): the resulting map \(X\to T\) is étale, but its two sections agree at every nonzero point and differ at the origin. Thus the quoted one-point determination needs a separation condition, which is not visible in scan 050's statement. The primary comparison [Stacks, Tag 024V](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/024V) explicitly assumes both unramifiedness and separation, together with equality on the residue-field maps; Hua's modernised proof does not expose this particular missing input.

### C04 — The \(n^\nu\) bound has a representable counterexample

**Source:** cote 91, scans 051–052, Corollary 1. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “le maximum analogue … est \(\leq n^\nu\)”. **Confidence:** high, because the section choices give an explicit constant representing scheme. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, pages 49–51; no matching counterexample occurs in the supplied context search. **Relation:** contradicts the unqualified numerical bound under the visible hypotheses, while leaving C02's étaleness conclusion intact.

Over a field \(k\), glue two copies of \(T=\mathbf A^1_k\) along \(T\setminus\{0,1\}\), and let \(g:X\to Y=T\) be the resulting étale morphism: \(\nu=1\) and the maximum geometric fibre cardinality is \(n=2\). A section independently chooses a lift of \(0\) and of \(1\), giving four sections, and over an arbitrary test base these choices are two clopen partitions, so \(\operatorname{Res}_{T/k}X\) is the constant four-point scheme. Both required restrictions therefore exist and are locally of finite type, but \(4>2^1\). Adding separatedness excludes this example; it is a necessary repair to this argument, not a claim that no other hypotheses could suffice.

### C05 — The written dimension equality is reversed

**Source:** cote 91, scan 052, live Corollary 2 and its “En particulier”. **Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “Alors \(D(s)=nD'(s)\)”; “dimension \(nd\)”. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, page 51, including its explicit correction footnote. **Relation:** repeats Hua's identified discrepancy, with the literal frozen formula retained.

The two live clauses point in opposite directions: the set equation multiplies \(D'\) by \(n\), while the following pure-dimensional conclusion multiplies the original fibre dimension by \(n\). Hua explicitly changes the formula to \(D'=nD\), and also restricts the assertion to a local geometric fibre; both are editorial changes, not alternative ink readings silently adopted here. The elementary test \(T=\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), \(X=\mathbf A^1_T\), \(Y=T\) gives \(D=\{1\}\), \(D'=\{2\}\), \(n=2\), so the printed direction cannot hold in this case.

### C06 — Reversing the dimension equation alone does not repair it

**Source:** cote 91, scans 052–053, dimension sets and product reduction. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “d'après \((\coprod f_i)_*(X/T)=\prod_i f_{i*}(X_i/T_i)\)”. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, page 51 footnote and page 52 proof. **Relation:** develops Hua's explicit mixed-dimension warning; this example is credited to his project's interpretation, not claimed as a new observation.

For \(T=\operatorname{Spec}k\amalg\operatorname{Spec}k\) and the same fibre \(\mathbf A^1_k\amalg\mathbf A^2_k\) over each point, restriction is the product of those two disjoint unions and has component dimensions \(2,3,4\). Hence even the repaired scalar set \(2\{1,2\}=\{2,4\}\) misses dimension \(3\). The product formula on scan 053 explains the issue: one must sum the choices of dimensions over the distinct geometric source components, while the pure-dimensional \(nd\) statement avoids this mixing.

### C08 — Deleting finiteness breaks the exact-degree formula

**Source:** cote 91, scan 054, Corollary 1 bis, especially the deleted “Supposons T/S fini”. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “exactement \(\lambda^{\nu_s}\) points”. **Confidence:** high, because a Laurent-polynomial exponent calculation rules out every section. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, page 53; **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**, candidate/statement-level intake, is expressly finite locally free. **Relation:** tests the manuscript's broadened formula and supports retaining the finite-base boundary of the registered finding.

Take \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k\), with \(k\) algebraically closed of characteristic different from \(2\), \(T=Y=\mathbf G_{m,k}\), and the finite étale degree-two map \(g:X=\mathbf G_{m,k}\to T\), \(z\mapsto z^2\). The source \(T/S\) is affine of finite type and \(\nu=1\), but a section would require a Laurent polynomial whose square is \(t\), which is impossible; the same geometric-point test shows that the restriction functor is represented by the empty scheme over every nonempty test base. Thus \(\operatorname{Res}X=\varnothing\to\operatorname{Res}Y=S\) has degree zero, not \(2^1\), despite the standing existence and finite-type conditions. The expected formula is valid in the finite-source calculation used for the finite locally free criterion, whereas its visible deletion of finiteness needs repair.

### C09 — All four conditions, with their actual quantifiers

**Source:** cote 91, scan 054, Corollary 3, items (i)–(iv) and marginal cycle. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “Conditions équivalentes”; “est fini sur S ?”. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, page 53; **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**, supplied candidate with statement-level intake only. **Relation:** supplies the complete four-condition source statement and its uncertainty beyond the truncated register excerpt.

The four written conditions are local constancy of \(\nu(s)\), preservation of **morphisms** that are finite étale, preservation of **objects** finite étale over \(T\), and finiteness of the single binary test \(f_*(T\amalg T)\); the last line ends with a question mark. The manuscript replaces “T/S fini” by “Sous les cond. du cor. 1”, so the broader scope cannot simply be identified with Hua's finite locally free finding. The finite locally free version has a complete verification recorded in etale_criterion_review.md, using local splitting into finite flat universal homeomorphisms and topological invariance of étale schemes; that verification is an editorial mathematical check, not a proof transcribed from these leaves.

### C10 — Why the binary test detects the component count

**Source:** cote 91, scan 054, item (iv) and arrow back to (i). **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** \(f_*(T\amalg T)=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(T,S\amalg S)\). **Confidence:** high in the finite locally free setting, because geometric fibres count idempotents of a finite algebra. **Context:** Hua, VI, page 53; **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**. **Relation:** adds the short implication omitted from the source's arrow cycle, without claiming novelty.

For a finite geometric fibre with \(\nu_s\) local factors, a map to two points independently labels each factor, so the binary restriction has \(2^{\nu_s}\) geometric points. It is étale by the earlier preservation result, and if item (iv) makes it finite, its finite locally free rank is locally constant. Since \(m\mapsto2^m\) is injective on nonnegative integers, this proves (iv)\(\Rightarrow\)(i), including the empty-fibre value \(2^0=1\); the argument explains exactly what the question-marked test measures.

### C11 — “Homogeneous” means this component-count condition

**Source:** cote 91, scan 055, first paragraph. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “homogène”; “dans le cas des morphismes finis du moins”; “invariante par composition”. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, page 53; earlier package same_cote_91/MPL_90+91_002-018_notes.md, N91-14, scan 014, uses “formellement principal homogène” in a different sense. **Relation:** adds the source's finite-case qualification and distinguishes two uses of the same word.

Here “homogène” is defined by local constancy of the number of geometric connected components for a flat morphism, not by a transitive group action. Base-change stability is immediate from geometric fibres, while composition needs the finite-source analysis or the preservation criterion and the composition isomorphism for restriction. The explicit bracket “du moins” should remain attached to that claim, and the principal-homogeneous vocabulary of the earlier quotient notes supplies no proof of it.

### C12 — A concrete warning at the prime \(2\)

**Source:** cote 91, scans 054–056, component count, binary test, and homogeneous finite-group conclusion. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “\(\nu(s)\) est loc. constant”; “sous réserve que T\(\to\)S soit homogène”. **Confidence:** high for the example and rank obstruction; low for any further implication for the unrelated integral extension classification. **Context:** notes/RESULTS_STATE.md, **LM1 D.11**, Open (return's claim), cote 49 scans 608–610, integral \(\mu_2\)-extensions at \(2\); **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**. **Relation:** adds a method-level caution under LM1 D.11 and leaves its status Open.

For \(T=\mu_2\to S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z_2\), the finite free rank is two, but the geometric generic fibre has two points and the special fibre has one. Consequently \(\operatorname{Res}_{T/S}(T\amalg T)\) is étale with geometric fibre cardinalities four and two and cannot be finite étale over the connected base. This demonstrates why a generic finite-group calculation may fail to remain a finite étale object at \(2\); it does not classify the integral extensions sought by LM1 D.11 or settle any part of that open entry.

### C13 — Product identities and local triviality are separate torsor inputs

### C14 — Length and geometric component count measure different effects

**Source:** cote 91, scans 052–054, \(nd\), \(\lambda^{\nu_s}\), and the grouped marginal symbols. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “\(n=\deg T/S\)”; “\(\lambda=\deg X/Y\)”; “\(\nu_s\)”. **Confidence:** high, by the explicit two-dimensional affine calculation and the idempotent calculation. **Context:** Hua, VI, pages 51–53; Hua, VII, “Le cadre, et les exemples”, pages 60–66; same_cote_91/MPL_91_099-143_notes.md, RN126-01 and RN128-01, scans 126 and 128, first-pass leads about a different notion of thick point. **Relation:** adds a comparison of the three numerical invariants, while distinguishing it from later thickness terminology.

For \(T=\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), restriction of \(\mathbf A^1_T\) is \(\mathbf A^2_k\), but restriction of a constant \(r\)-point étale scheme over \(T\) has \(r\), not \(r^2\), geometric points. Thus nilpotent length contributes to smooth dimension through \(n\), while only the number of local factors contributes to the finite étale degree through \(\nu\). This is a concrete bridge to Hua's later Greenberg ring-scheme discussion, but the “points épais” of the neighbouring package concern inverse systems and are not defined by this length calculation.

### C15 — The finite group after restriction is not generally the same group

**Source:** cote 91, scan 056, first complete paragraph and following finite-set question. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “de groupe \(f_*(Y/T)\times_S f_*(T\times G/T)\)”; “l'intérêt de déterminer”. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Torseurs, et les composantes connexes relatives”, pages 54–55; **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**. **Relation:** develops the displayed group formula already present in Hua's summary.

Over a geometric point and a finite source with \(\nu_s\) local factors, the restricted constant group has underlying group \(G^{\nu_s}\), so its order is \(|G|^{\nu_s}\). For a nontrivial finite \(G\), homogeneity is precisely the condition needed here to keep that rank locally constant; the trivial group has constant rank one regardless of \(\nu\). The formula therefore carries both a change of group and a base change to \(f_*(Y/T)\), which must be retained when comparing principal covers.

### C16 — The cancelled finite-set route and the later equivariant route

**Source:** cote 91, scans 056–057, cancelled block beginning “On sait”, and the live “Plus généralement”. **Kind:** withdrawn in the same text. **Quote:** “petit raisonnement”; “exact à gauche”. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Torseurs, et les composantes connexes relatives”, pages 55–57. **Relation:** restores the source's two-stage argument and its cancellation, which the modernised presentation merges.

The cancelled route starts from ordinary finite sets \(I\), then equips a representing set with a fundamental-group action; the live route instead begins with finite equivariant sets. ### C18 — Left exactness alone does not imply internal-Hom form

**Source:** cote 91, scan 058, opening assertion. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “un tel foncteur est nécessairement de la forme \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\pi_1}(A,E)\)”. **Confidence:** high, by a two-object categorical counterexample. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Torseurs, et les composantes connexes relatives”, pages 56–57, repeats the general claim; **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**, candidate, concerns the geometric application. **Relation:** contradicts the broad categorical premise as written, without refuting the finite locally free geometric criterion.

Let \(G=C_2\) and let \(F(E)\) be the underlying finite set of \(E\) equipped with trivial \(G\)-action; this endofunctor preserves finite limits, finite colimits, and surjections. If \(F(E)\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(A,E)\) naturally as \(G\)-sets, evaluation on the trivial two-element set gives \(2=2^{|A|}\), hence \(|A|=1\) and \(A\) has trivial action. Internal Hom from this singleton is the identity functor, but \(F(G)\) has trivial action whereas the regular \(G\)-set does not, a contradiction. The geometric functor needs extra locality or base-change structure; adding ordinary exactness or preservation of surjections still does not suffice.

### C19 — The marked compatibility is a mathematical obligation

**Source:** cote 91, scan 058, long margin, formula (*), and the passage to sections. **Kind:** self-flagged. **Quote:** “à détailler”; “Il y a un pt de compatibilité à établir” (several connecting words remain doubtful). **Context:** Hua, VI, “Torseurs, et les composantes connexes relatives”, pages 56–58. **Relation:** adds the exact proof obligation behind Hua's identification with a relative component object.

Formula (*) is an isomorphism of **internal** Hom objects, whereas taking global sections yields only a bijection of ordinary Hom sets. An ordinary representability statement does not automatically produce the internal statement after every base change, and C18 shows why the distinction matters in the equivariant formulation. A repair must identify the two-variable naturality, the category indicated by the bracket after (*), and compatibility with localization; the margin explicitly acknowledges unfinished work of this kind.

### C25 — Even a local geometric source does not fix the full dimension set

**Source:** cote 91, scans 052–053, \(D(s)\), \(D'(s)\), and the reduction to connected \(T\). **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “on est ramené au cas où T est connexe”. **Confidence:** high, because one target component admits no section after any nonempty base change. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Lissité, étalitude, dimension”, page 51, modernised proposition assuming local \(T_s\). **Relation:** gives a further limitation of Hua's repair, in addition to his split-source warning recorded in C06.

Let \(T=\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), \(Y=T\amalg\operatorname{Spec}k\), and \(X=\mathbf A^1_T\amalg\mathbf A^2_k\), with \(g:X\to Y\) the two projections and both second components mapped to the reduced point of \(T\). Then \(g\) is smooth and the set of its geometric fibre dimensions is \(\{1,2\}\), but \(T_U\) cannot map over \(T\) to that reduced target component for any nonempty \(U\), since \(\epsilon\) remains nonzero in \(\mathcal O_U[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\). Consequently \(\operatorname{Res}Y=S\), \(\operatorname{Res}X=\mathbf A^2_k\), and \(D'=\{2\}\ne2D=\{2,4\}\), even though the geometric source is local. A corrected dimension-set assertion must restrict to dimensions attained along actual sections of \(Y\); the pure relative dimension \(nd\) assertion is unaffected by this example.

### C26 — Ordinary representability can survive when internal compatibility fails

**Source:** cote 91, scan 058, formula (*) and the passage to its global sections. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “Prenant … les sections des deux côtés”. **Confidence:** high, because the strictly henselian base trivializes all finite étale targets and the generic fibre can be counted explicitly. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Torseurs, et les composantes connexes relatives”, pages 55–58; **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**. **Relation:** makes the compatibility distinction in C19–C20 concrete. **Primary comparison:** [Stacks, Tag 04GK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GK), the finite étale equivalence for a henselian local ring and its residue field.

Take \(S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C[[t]]\) and \(T=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf C[[t]][x]/(x^2-t))\), which is connected and finite free of rank two over \(S\). Every finite étale \(S\)-scheme \(E\) is constant, so choosing a map from connected \(T\) to \(E\) is the same as choosing a map from \(S\), and \(A=S\) represents the ordinary finite-étale-target Hom functor. After geometric generic base change, however, \(T\) has two points and \(A\) one, so the corresponding internal-Hom identity and componentwise base-change compatibility fail. This does not contradict a universal property restricted to ordinary global Hom sets, and therefore sharpens rather than merely repeats the warning about jumping \(\nu\).
