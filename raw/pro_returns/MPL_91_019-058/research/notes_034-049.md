## B. Vector bundles and the intersection problem

### B11 — An identity-map test explains the sheaf distinction

**Location:** 91.pdf, scan 039, cancelled assertion and margin. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high, because restriction to the displayed affine open computes both intersections. **Quote:** “ce n'est pas le faisceau … mais … l'inf dans les faisceaux qu. coh.” **Search:** B11. **Context/relation:** adds a test to **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, “Fibrés vectoriels, et une question” (p. 38 = scan 039); no equivalent in Earlier or Later. **RS bearing:** RS-N, method only; no registered result refuted.

Take \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}k[[t]]\), \(f=\mathrm{id}_Y\), \(F=\mathcal O_Y\) and \(F_i=t^i\mathcal O_Y\): \(\bigcap_i t^ik[[t]]=0\), so its associated quasi-coherent sheaf is zero. Ordinary sheaf intersection restricts on \(D(t)\) to \(k((t))\), since every \(F_i\) is \(\mathcal O_{D(t)}\) there. It contains no nonzero coherent subsheaf on \(Y\), because such a subsheaf is a submodule contained in every \(t^ik[[t]]\). This tests the repaired coherent-subobject assertion and shows why bare sheaf-intersection language can misstate it even for a proper flat identity map.

### B12 — A conditional completion of the vanishing step

**Location:** 91.pdf, scan 039, final paragraph and margins. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the conditional proof; medium as a completion of the leaf, because cofinality and the noncomplete opening base are separate tasks. **Quote:** “les M_i tendent vers 0 dans M (?)”; “Arranger les notations” (latter uncertain). **Search:** B12. **Context/relation:** adds a proof dependency to **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, “Fibrés vectoriels, et une question” (p. 38 = scan 039); Earlier N91-17 (91/016) is a different coherent-module task. **RS bearing:** RS-N; no direct settled/open result.

Assume the needed cofinality: for every \(q\) some \(M_i\subset\mathfrak m^qM\). At a point of the closed fibre, coherent \(F'_1\) contained in every \(f^*F_i\) lies in all \(\mathfrak m^q(f^*F)\), so has zero stalk by noetherian local intersection. Its closed support is proper over the local base and would meet the closed fibre if nonempty, hence \(F'_1=0\). The remaining task is cofinality for the actual filtered family over the complete noetherian local ring and justification of the completeness change; asserting arbitrary intersection commutation bypasses both.

### B15 — The “préciser” step has a largest-open formula

### B18 — An ordinary open cover need not cover the restriction

**Location:** 91.pdf, scan 044, union formula and qualification; compare040–043. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high, because the section functors are directly computed. **Quote:** \(f_*(X/T)=\bigcup_i f_*(U_i/T)\); “une hyp. qui assure” (uncertain). **Search:** B18. **Context/relation:** adds a test for the qualification in **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, “Ce que la restriction de Weil préserve” (p. 43 = scan 044); its “Existence” (pp. 39–42 = scans 040–043) supplies the whole-image condition. **RS bearing:** RS-R, gluing boundary, not a counterexample to its stated hypothesis.

Thus even a finite open cover need not induce a cover of the restriction, and the whole-multisection condition in040–043 is indispensable. Commutation with fibre products does not extend without argument to unions.

### B20 — Surjectivity needs the context in which it is used

**Location:** 91.pdf, scan 044, (*) margin's Chow argument. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high, because a proposed section must kill and preserve the same nilpotent. **Quote:** “si X′→X est surjectif, alors f_*(X′)→f_*(X) l'est”. **Search:** B20. **Context/relation:** adds a boundary test to **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, “Ce que la restriction de Weil préserve” (p. 43 = scan 044); differs from its smooth-surjectivity assertion in “Lissité, étalitude, dimension” (p. 54 = scan 055). **RS bearing:** RS-R, hypothesis check; no direct settled/open-result match.

For \(B=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), take \(T=\operatorname{Spec}B\), \(X=T\), and the surjective closed immersion \(X'=\operatorname{Spec}k\to T\). A T-section over a nonzero k-algebra A would require a B-algebra map \(k\to B\otimes_kA\), impossible because \(\varepsilon\) is zero in the source but nonzero in the target, so \(\operatorname{Res}_{T/k}(X')=\varnothing\) whereas \(\operatorname{Res}_{T/k}(X)=\operatorname{Spec}k\). This refutes an unrestricted finite-locally-free rule, not the separable context of (*) or the later smooth-surjective case. Those two qualifications should be kept separate in completing Chow's step.

### B21 — The proper-descent question says non-finite

**Location:** 91.pdf, scan 044, (*) margin's final question. **Kind:** conjecture. **Quote:** “Si T/S est fidèlement plat non fini”; “Oui dans le cas S loc. noeth.”. **Search:** B21. **Context/relation:** contradicts “fini” in **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, “Ce que la restriction de Weil préserve” (p. 43 = scan 044); Earlier N91-13 (91/013) is a different fpqc quotient argument. **RS bearing:** RS-N; no direct settled/open-result match.

The manuscript asks if properness descends along faithfully flat non-finite T/S and gives a proposed affirmative answer only for a locally noetherian base. Stacks Lemma 35.23.16 proves properness fpqc-local without noetherianness, supplying an answer for an fpqc covering, including faithfully flat quasi-compact maps [SP-02L1]. “Non fini” neither asserts nor denies quasi-compactness, so the theorem's covering hypothesis must be retained rather than applied to every interpretation of an arbitrary faithfully flat map.

### B22 — The determinant and normalized norm differ by a base line bundle

**Location:** 91.pdf, scans 044–045, determinant claim and proposition. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for normalization, from determinants of multiplication in trivializations; no independent proof of all ampleness claims is proposed. **Quote:** “det f_*(L)”; “un faisceau ample”. **Search:** B22. **Context/relation:** differs in normalization from **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, “Ce que la restriction de Weil préserve” (p. 44 = scan 045), which calls the determinant “la norme”; no corresponding identity in Earlier or Later. **RS bearing:** RS-R, construction detail; no direct settled-result match.

Put \(R=\operatorname{Res}_{T/S}(X)\), \(p:R\times_ST\to R\), and let ev be evaluation: \(N_p(\mathrm{ev}^*L)=\det p_*\mathrm{ev}^*L\otimes(\det p_*\mathcal O)^{-1}\). The omitted factor is pulled back from \((\det f_*\mathcal O_T)^{-1}\) on S, so it does not change relative ampleness locally on the base but matters for the literal norm and multiplicativity. The transition-function and determinant definitions in Stacks § 31.18 verify this comparison [SP-0BCX, SP-0BD2]. This normalizes notation only; evaluation/Grassmannian arguments are still needed for the ampleness conclusion.

### B32 — The endomorphism scheme has an additional infinitesimal equation
