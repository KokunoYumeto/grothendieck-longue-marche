The margin could require a different intended domain for its symbol $\rho$, but that interpretive repair belongs only in notes.

### C18 — Left exactness alone does not imply internal-Hom form

**Source:** cote 91, scan 058, opening assertion. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “un tel foncteur est nécessairement de la forme \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\pi_1}(A,E)\)”. **Confidence:** high, by a two-object categorical counterexample. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Torseurs, et les composantes connexes relatives”, pages 56–57, repeats the general claim; **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**, candidate, concerns the geometric application. **Relation:** contradicts the broad categorical premise as written, without refuting the finite locally free geometric criterion.

Let \(G=C_2\) and let \(F(E)\) be the underlying finite set of \(E\) equipped with trivial \(G\)-action; this endofunctor preserves finite limits, finite colimits, and surjections. If \(F(E)\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(A,E)\) naturally as \(G\)-sets, evaluation on the trivial two-element set gives \(2=2^{|A|}\), hence \(|A|=1\) and \(A\) has trivial action. Internal Hom from this singleton is the identity functor, but \(F(G)\) has trivial action whereas the regular \(G\)-set does not, a contradiction. The geometric functor needs extra locality or base-change structure; adding ordinary exactness or preservation of surjections still does not suffice.

### C25 — Even a local geometric source does not fix the full dimension set

### C26 — Ordinary representability can survive when internal compatibility fails

**Source:** cote 91, scan 058, formula (*) and the passage to its global sections. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “Prenant … les sections des deux côtés”. **Confidence:** high, because the strictly henselian base trivializes all finite étale targets and the generic fibre can be counted explicitly. **Context:** Hua, VI, “Torseurs, et les composantes connexes relatives”, pages 55–58; **Hua, findings register, 91-weil-restriction-finite-etale-criterion**. **Relation:** makes the compatibility distinction in C19–C20 concrete. **Primary comparison:** [Stacks, Tag 04GK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GK), the finite étale equivalence for a henselian local ring and its residue field.

Take \(S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C[[t]]\) and \(T=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf C[[t]][x]/(x^2-t))\), which is connected and finite free of rank two over \(S\). Every finite étale \(S\)-scheme \(E\) is constant, so choosing a map from connected \(T\) to \(E\) is the same as choosing a map from \(S\), and \(A=S\) represents the ordinary finite-étale-target Hom functor. After geometric generic base change, however, \(T\) has two points and \(A\) one, so the corresponding internal-Hom identity and componentwise base-change compatibility fail. This does not contradict a universal property restricted to ordinary global Hom sets, and therefore sharpens rather than merely repeats the warning about jumping \(\nu\).
