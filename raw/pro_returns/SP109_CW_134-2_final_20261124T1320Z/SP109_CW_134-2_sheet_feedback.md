## Recurring mechanisms and refinements for later runs

These observations refine mechanisms already named in the error sheet; they do not propose a new general error class from this seventeen-page selection. The category table above counts logged places. It is not a token error rate, and a count of places against C cannot be compared with a count against H as an accuracy estimate without a common denominator and a treatment of their differing uncertainty conventions.

### Locator key

All witness locators refer to the supplied originals. `Cnnn:Lx` identifies line x of `codex/<folder>/pages/<folder>_134-2_scannnn.tex`, with folder `H134-02` for 153, `H134-03` for 154–161, and `H134-04` for 162–169. `H08:Lx` and `H09:Lx` identify file lines in `hua/transcripts/134-2/batch-08.fr.tex` and `batch-09.fr.tex`; H's page is scan minus one. ### 2. Distinguish an allograph decision from a mathematical repair

The k family illustrates the distinction. A field letter can have a looped or straight ascender and just one low shoulder, rather than two printed-k arms. The completed audits on 162–167 compare that form with a clearer local k and the same explicitly named field. They resolve the ambiguous form to k; they do not report a clear h as an authorial slip. [C162:L6,8 / H09:L52,54, audits 162_B04/B14; C163:L7,13 / H09:L74,80, audits 163_B04/B12/B15/B16; C164:L3 / H09:L86, audit 164_B02; C165:L7 / H09:L108, audit 165_B07; C166:L3,5 / H09:L117,119, audits 166_B01/B04/B07; C167:L3 / H09:L134, audits 167_B02/B13/B21.] The three resolved k forms on 167 yield only one C doubt resolution: the other two were already k in both originals. Their shared object and local comparison do not establish a universal h/k rule.

Two related examples require accurate attribution. The tall pi on 159 and 162 is resolved by the curved right foot and recurring fundamental-group notation; original C already has π at both places, so the first-pass π/Π doubt must not be reported as a C misreading. The barred m on 159 and 163 is compared with the complete m in *mod* on 159. Original C159 had an m/n doubt, while original C163 already had m; H expands both occurrences to *même*. [C159:L2 / H08:L220, audit 159_B03/B04; C162:L4 / H09:L50, audit 162_B02; C163:L7 / H09:L74, audit 163_B08; comparison C159:L4 / H08:L222, audit 159_B27 and its complete crop.]

Such comparisons settle the named occurrences. They are not global replacement rules for h, Π or n. The coefficient F on 165 is another locally resolved rounded form: the upper stroke does not securely close a P bowl, and the same identity names F on its right. The audit checks the whole identity before resolving C's P/F doubt. [C165:L5 / H09:L106; audit 165_B05.]

### 4. Read each stop and small mark independently

Inserted `p.ex` lacks its final point on 155, 158, 165 and 166, while nearby baseline `p.ex.` retains one on 158, 165 and 166. This is a recurring contrast, not an insertion rule: the inserted item on 154 has `p.ex.`. Both witnesses also supplied the absent final point of marginal `i.e.` on 156. These decisions concern local ink and position, not a blanket preference for adding punctuation. ### 6. Word identity and attachment are separate decisions

A leader can cross a live word without cancelling it. On 156, the guide does not strike *convenable*; on 158, the long insertion guide does not delete *il semble que tout s'écroule*. On 167, the recorded Berthelot guide reaches the gap before *que*, rather than the position selected in H's body. These are distinct questions from reading the added words themselves. [C156:L8 / H08:L187, record 156, live *convenable* row; C158:L7 / H08:L215–216, record 158, guide/strike row; C167:L3 / H09:L134–135, record 167, *line 7, insertion attachment*.]

Attribution must remain local here too. H already reads *lié* on 167. Resolving C's *lié/liée* doubt does not establish an H ending error, although other nearby words do differ. [C167:L3 / H09:L134; record 167, *line 3, after un peu*.]

### Counting discipline

The changes log distinguishes original witness readings from our provisional first reading. Resolving an original C doubt is a recorded reading even when its first candidate stands; adding a new doubt without changing the preferred reading is recorded in the doubts table rather than counted as a textual correction. Returning from an added first-pass doubt to an unchanged original C reading is not a new C error. Macro syntax alone is excluded, while actual underline extent, quotation shape, insertion attachment and correction state are retained as substantive differences. The examples above identify the appropriate original C and H locations so that these distinctions remain checkable.
