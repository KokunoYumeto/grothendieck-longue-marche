# Two primary-reference checks

Checked 2026-11-17 after the recorded transcription freeze, without reading manuscript scans or transcription files. These leads came from Michel Hua's project; the checks concern the external mathematical texts.

## SGA 4, Exposé IV, exercise 9.5.10

**Source:** Grothendieck–Verdier, *SGA 4 I*, Exposé IV, exercise 9.5.10(a–c), original pp. 470–471; [primary PDF](https://www.normalesup.org/~forgogozo/SGA4/04/04.pdf), p. 96 (index 95), version 71766d9 (2024-07-30).

Part (a) identifies the pieces with presheaves on a sieve and its complementary full subcategory. Part (b) reconstructs the category from an arbitrary bifunctor C′op×C″→Ens. Part (c): gluing two presheaf topoi yields a presheaf topos iff its gluing functor preserves small products, equivalently has a left adjoint. Replacing products with all small limits uses the gluing functor's existing left exactness (inference).

## Gurski–Johnson–Osorno, Theorem 1.11

**Source:** *Extending homotopy theories across adjunctions*, [arXiv:1508.00054 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.00054.pdf), v3 (2017-09-22), pp. 3–4; Definitions 1.3–1.4, 1.9, Lemma 1.7, Theorem 1.11(c). Internal date: 2016-12-07.

Weak equivalences include isomorphisms and satisfy two-out-of-three; creation means exact detection. The theorem assumes Q⊣i with i an identity-on-objects inclusion. Creation by Q and a weak counit imply a weak unit and equivalence of homotopy theories. The unrestricted-adjunction application is a generalization, justified by the triangle identity and counit naturality, also objectwise on diagrams; it is not a literal application of the stated theorem.
