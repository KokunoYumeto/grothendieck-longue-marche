## The main positive result and its mechanism

The strongest class theorems now prove A1 for every abelian prime-to-p Galois cover of a genus-zero curve in every characteristic, and every such cover of a genus-one curve in odd characteristic or with supersingular quotient in characteristic two. There is no exponent bound in either theorem. These results include all geometrically hyperelliptic curves in odd characteristic and many nonhyperelliptic curves. The proof proceeds through an odd-characteristic hyperelliptic theorem, an all-characteristic elliptic-image test, quotient inductions, and finally an explicit torsion-divisor construction that retains the required inertia information. The initial constructive Hesse route handled p>=5 with p congruent to 2 modulo 3; a separate exact four-torsion configuration handled p congruent to 3 modulo 4. A prime-to-p torsion-density argument then removed both congruence restrictions for hyperelliptic curves.

For any elliptic curve E over Fbar_p, p odd, and any prescribed nondegenerate cross-ratio c, the locus of four points on E whose images in E/{±1} have cross-ratio c is an integral ample divisor in E^4. Its open part is a degree-16 Kummer cover of PGL_2. Four evaluation divisors prove independence of the four square classes. Finite flatness extends generic integrality across every diagonal. Ampleness excludes containment in a translate of a proper abelian subvariety. The Anderson–Indik density consequence, in the exact form opened in Yang (2016), Proposition 2.3, therefore gives four prime-to-p torsion points in the required open locus. Root and a second agent independently checked this source, including the overline on F_p, and independently checked the complete geometry. The published underlying curve-surjectivity theorem of Bogomolov and Tschinkel (2005), Theorem 1.1, was also opened with its proof.

Choose c to be a supersingular Legendre parameter. A root of the Deuring polynomial supplies such a c in every odd characteristic; its values at 0 and 1 are nonzero. If g:D→E is a ramified double cover and n kills the four torsion points, pulling g back by [n] makes its branch locus contain the four opposite pairs. Pulling back the supersingular double cover then cancels all quadratic ramification. The resulting connected component W→D is finite étale, and its Galois closure has order prime to p: it is controlled by a 2-group extended by E[n]. Connectedness of D×_E E is proved using the ramified quadratic extension versus the unramified [n] extension; n need not be odd.

The resulting map W→E_0 detects a point up to reflection in the chosen elliptic branch origin. Equal prime-to-p point sections lift to equal sections on every cover. Their divisor difference is p-primary, so their images on the supersingular elliptic target agree. Two branch origins whose difference has exact order ell, ell different from 2 and p, eliminate the reflection ambiguity. Consequently a collision on any ramified double cover of an elliptic curve has equal elliptic images.

For a hyperelliptic curve, first choose an elliptic double cover of the quotient line branched at four of its branch points. Their normalized fibre product is an étale double cover of the original curve and a ramified double cover of the elliptic curve. The preceding result forces a collision to have equal hyperelliptic coordinates. An induction splits the branch set into subsets of sizes 2 and 2g. The corresponding genus-zero and genus-(g−1) double covers have disjoint branch sets, so their fibre product is smooth and their diagonal involution is free. It is an étale double cover of the original curve. The two projection maps jointly distinguish every point, including all branch and infinite fibres. This proves the induction. Specialization transfers the result to fields finitely generated over F_p with the required comparison maps proved in A.8.

A.20 first extends the conclusion to curves with a faithful elementary abelian two-group action and genus-zero quotient. Induction on the group rank handles every involution quotient; three quotient maps from a Klein-four subgroup distinguish every geometric point.

A.21 proves a more general elliptic-image test in **every characteristic**. If a separable map D→E has a fibre whose ramification indices all share a factor m>1 prime to p, a collision on D must have equal E-images. Pullback by a prime-to-p multiplication map produces many such fibres at torsion points. In odd characteristic, the controlled construction in Poonen (2005), section 3.1, supplies a Kummer target that is hyperelliptic. In characteristic two, choose three unramified inversion fibres from nonzero odd-torsion points and use H:z^e=x(x−1), where e is an odd prime divisor of m. Its alternative equation x²+x=z^e permits an explicit differential calculation: the Cartier operator on z^i dz strictly lowers every surviving index and is nilpotent. A nonzero geometric two-torsion line bundle would produce a nonzero regular Cartier-fixed differential df/f, a contradiction. Thus its Jacobian has no geometric 2-primary torsion, and Kummer already separates its points. The inversion quotient may be wild elsewhere, but the selected nonzero odd-torsion pairs have trivial stabilizers and are unramified; the Kummer pullback to D is therefore étale. Every Galois closure used has only the selected prime-to-p prime divisors. Two branch origins whose difference has odd prime order again eliminate reflection.

A.22 proves the cyclic prime-degree class by a branch-count induction. A.23 treats every cyclic n prime to p. Coprime factors reduce to two lower-degree quotient curves; their normalization is point-bijective because the two local tame indices are coprime. For n=ell^a, the unique degree-ell quotient is branched exactly at the full-index branch points. A genus-zero quotient gives a lower-degree induction step. Otherwise an independent fibre-rigidity lemma forces a collision to have equal base coordinates: pull back a selected hyperelliptic or elliptic degree-ell cover branched only at full-index fibres, and apply A.19 or A.21. A second induction reduces the number of full-index branch points by dividing the Kummer function by a genus-zero two-branch Kummer function of order n. The auxiliary normalized cover is étale of degree n over the original curve. Its two quotient maps have equal images on a lifted collision. Those images determine the point because, away from the two selected totally ramified fibres, one quotient is étale over the base and the fibre product is already normal. Distinct points with equal base coordinate cannot lie in either selected fibre. This explicitly handles partial ramification at every other branch value and avoids inferring point separation from a function-field compositum alone.

A.24 treats all finite abelian prime-to-p groups by induction on their order. Each cyclic character quotient of genus at least two is covered by A.23; a genus-one quotient is covered by A.21; a genus-zero quotient gives a smaller-group cover. At a common geometric fibre, write Q=gP and let I be the stabilizer of P. Equality in every character quotient gives g in (ker chi)I for every character chi. Characters of G/I separate elements, so g lies in I and Q=P. This argument includes all ramified fibres. Spreading the tame action and specialization in A.8 transfer the results to fields finitely generated over F_p.

A.25 adds every ramified double cover of an elliptic curve in odd characteristic, including every bielliptic curve. Choose two branch points and a double cover of the elliptic base branched exactly there; divisibility of Pic^0 guarantees that this auxiliary cover exists, and its genus is two, so A.19 applies. For an original cover with at least four branch points, the normalized fibre product is an étale double cover of the source. Its third quadratic quotient removes the chosen two branch points and has genus one less. Induction gives injectivity on that quotient. The two auxiliary branch sets are disjoint, so their fibre product is smooth and their projections jointly separate every geometric point. The two-branch base case is genus two. Induction on the rank of an elementary abelian two-group and the same Klein-four orbit argument extend this to every such group with genus-one quotient. This produces curves beyond the abelian genus-zero class.

A.26 treats a nonabelian class: S3-Galois covers of a genus-zero curve in characteristic different from two and three, whose reflection quotient has genus at most two. A collision has equal images in the normal C3 quotient, which is hyperelliptic, elliptic, or a rational base for an already settled cyclic cover. Its images in every reflection quotient agree as well. Stabilizers can only be trivial, C2, or C3; the normal quotient together with the three reflection quotients separates each of these cases, including every ramified fibre. The branch pattern of six transposition branch points and one three-cycle branch point gives genus six with all four proper nontrivial subgroup quotients of genus two. An explicit product-one generating tuple realizes this pattern by the verified prime-to-p presentation in SGA1. No assertion about the complete automorphism group is needed.

A.27 is retained as an independent earlier proof for abelian elliptic covers of exponent dividing 120. Its base cases are cyclic degrees 3, 4 and 5 with two fully ramified branch points. Inversion exchanging the branch pair lifts explicitly: its action is minus one on Pic^0, so the Kummer norm has the required n-th root. For degree four, an apparent order-four lift is excluded because that root has even valuation at every inversion-fixed point, whereas an anti-invariant function would have odd valuation there. The reflection quotients have genus one or two. A.21 or A.19 forces equality of their images; two conjugate reflection quotients and the elliptic image then separate every point. Branch-count cancellation handles cyclic degrees three and five. In degree four, the number of full-index branch points decreases by two, while twisting through E[4]→E[2] ensures the required disjoint quadratic subfields. Degree eight is included by choosing a reflection with six fixed points: its genus-three quotient is bielliptic, so A.25 applies. Twists through E[8]→E[2] and the full-index induction handle arbitrary cyclic degree-eight covers. A.29 now subsumes the conclusion, without changing the validity of this earlier proof.

### Removing every elliptic-cover exponent bound

A.29 gives a direct construction for a cyclic n-cover C→E, for every n prime to p. Pull back by [N], with N=2n in odd characteristic and N=n in the characteristic-two supersingular case. A branch point becomes a whole coset of E[N], with the same valuation residue a modulo n at each point. The exact sequence q_i=binom(i,2) is periodic modulo n with period N and has second difference one. On each cyclic row, the constant divisor vector is therefore the sum of q_i times the principal triples (x+d)+(x−d)−2(x). The coefficients retain a factor a, so every auxiliary Kummer ramification order divides the original one, even at partial branches.

Each nontrivial auxiliary curve has an equivalent equation s^m=x−b, with m dividing n. In odd characteristic its equation becomes y²=R(s^m+b), a squarefree polynomial of degree 3m; it is hyperelliptic of genus (3m−gcd(m,2))/2≥2, so A.19 separates its points. For a supersingular elliptic base in characteristic two, the equation becomes y²+y=(s^m+b)^3+A(s^m+b)+B, an Artin–Schreier curve with one pole of odd order 3m. The explicit Cartier calculation gives 2-rank zero, so A.1 separates its points. Adjoining all the auxiliary roots is étale over the original isogeny pullback; the composite cover has prime-to-p Galois closure by the finite wreath-product bound.

Divide the original Kummer root by all auxiliary roots. The residual cover H→E is unramified and has genus one. The common cover V→H retains a uniformly nontrivial original branch index, so A.21 gives equal H-images of a collision. At a common base point let I be inertia and e the original cyclic index. Equal auxiliary images mean that each auxiliary character of the deck displacement lies in μ_e. The residual character is trivial on the displacement, and the original character is its product with all auxiliary characters. Thus the original displacement lies in its own inertia subgroup, exactly the condition that the two original points coincide. This argument treats every ramified fibre; it does not infer joint point separation merely from an equality of function fields. All cyclic character quotients then give the abelian theorem, without an exponent bound. Root and the Part-A agent independently checked the construction and the character/inertia conclusion.

These arguments establish the stated classes. They do not prove that every curve has an admissible cover with such a quotient, nor that a ramified cover of a curve already satisfying A1 also satisfies A1. No priority claim is made.

## A2: the comparison is complete

A.2 proves a uniqueness statement needed for the reverse implication. If an automorphism of order m prime to p acts freely on the open curve, then a difference between a point and its image cannot be killed in the generalized Jacobian by an integer coprime to m. The proof fixes the orientation of the function-field action and its norm, normalizes the norm in the proper case, and uses Hilbert 90. At a boundary orbit, tame inertia of order e acts on a uniformizer by an e-th root of unity; the modulus condition forces the valuation of the Hilbert-90 function to be divisible by e. Every invariant divisor orbit therefore contributes a multiple of m to the degree. This contradicts the degree of the distinguished point divisor.

It follows that in a geometric prime-to-p Galois cover at most one lift of Q has p-primary difference from a chosen lift of P. For a characteristic pointed neighbourhood over K, a finite Galois extension L/K kills the action on its geometric deck quotient and makes it arithmetically Galois of prime-to-p degree. The restrictive A2 condition applies there. Uniqueness gives G_K-invariance of the lift and therefore descent to K. Norms give compatibility between neighbourhoods. Compactness then gives a fixed path in the inverse limit and hence an actual geometric conjugator between the full sections. This proves both covering-condition equivalences, including all field-extension and compatibility steps. It does not identify nonabelian equality with one finite abelian calculation.

A.29a supplies an exact obstruction to using direct p-rank-zero targets over an ordinary elliptic base in characteristic two. A.29b pushes this obstruction through a specific further cover. ## Attempts that do not yet prove universal A1

1. **Finite norm kernels and two primes.** A.7 proves the general hyperplane incidence identity. On a common refinement of elementary abelian ell- and m-covers, the two corresponding joint kernels intersect trivially because they have coprime annihilators. When the norm images are p-primary, this removes only the prime-to-p component of the original divisor difference. A nonzero p-primary difference remains possible. Tests on separate base covers do not automatically give all tests on a common refinement. The next needed input is an obstruction to such a point-divisor class being p-primary on every refinement, not another coprime-kernel identity.

2. **The local-system proposal.** For ell different from p, p does not divide |GL_d(F_ell)| precisely when the multiplicative order of ell modulo p exceeds d. This is impossible when d>=p−1; in particular the proposed positive-dimensional abelian-scheme representation cannot meet that numerical condition for p=2 or p=3. Moreover a smooth elliptic scheme over a proper connected curve has constant j, since every morphism from that curve to A^1 is constant. An affine nonisotrivial elliptic-family argument cannot be inserted on a proper base without additional work. Higher-dimensional complete families are not excluded by this observation, but no family satisfying all necessary monodromy and separation conditions has been constructed.

3. **Exceptional-set growth.** The verified identity |E_V|=d|E_C| grows at the same rate as g(V)−1=d(g(C)−1). Ordinary point-count estimates therefore do not contradict a nonempty exceptional set. The inspected finiteness proof does not provide the required sublinear bound in an étale tower. A precise next step would be a uniform bound o(d) for the relevant exceptional sets along a specific admissible tower, with its hypotheses checked.

4. **The Lang tower.** A.12 gives a genuine additional necessary condition for a collision. Along an explicit sequence of prime-to-p Lang covers of degree d_m, all points in both distinguished fibres are rational. If the unique compatible divisor difference has order p^{a_m}, then p^{a_m}>=2d_m/(q^m+1)>=2(q^{m/2}−1)^{2g}/[p^v(q^m+1)]. Thus these orders must grow without bound. This is not a contradiction: no uniform upper bound on the exponent has been proved. Bounded multiplicities of individual prime-to-p deck characters in cohomology do not bound the p-primary exponents as characters and covers vary. A quantitative bound specifically for these two-point divisor classes remains the missing input.

5. **Pure inseparability.** A.11 shows that a rational function realizing a difference of exact order p^a is not a p-th power, hence is separable. Positive-p-rank genus-two curves already have point differences of arbitrarily large p-power order, because C−C=J. Infinitely many such pairs alone therefore cannot imply pure inseparability. What would need to be used is compatibility through all admissible covers.

6. **Supersingular quotients and nonabelian covers.** A.10 supplies a sufficient criterion using maps from an admissible étale cover to p-rank-zero abelian varieties. The correspondence, quotient, and divisor arguments now settle every abelian prime-to-p cover of a genus-zero curve, every such cover of a genus-one curve in odd characteristic, and supersingular elliptic quotients in characteristic two. The former degree-7 and degree-16 obstacles to the reflection/branch-pair route are bypassed by A.29's torsion-divisor construction. They are no longer unresolved elliptic-cover cases in odd characteristic. Raynaud's solvable-cover nonordinarity theorem and Yang's cyclic-cover non-new-ordinarity theorem do not by themselves produce a p-rank-zero abelian quotient; nonordinary need not mean p-rank zero. A single dominant map to a known separated target only forces a collision into one fibre. For a nonabelian Galois cover, character quotients identify a point only modulo the commutator subgroup and its stabilizer. Removing that remaining fibre ambiguity, or finding analogous controlled principal divisors over bases of genus at least two, remains a precise next step. A tame map to P^1 alone does not resolve it: the Galois closure may have order divisible by p and may be ramified over the original curve.

7. **Characteristic two.** A.21–A.24 settle all abelian odd-order covers of a genus-zero curve; A.29 adds every abelian odd-order cover of a supersingular elliptic curve. For an ordinary elliptic quotient, the shifted auxiliary Kummer curves have Artin–Schreier poles at several finite points as well as infinity. A.29a proves that some exact auxiliaries admit no nonconstant map to any p-rank-zero abelian variety, so direct replacement by such a target is impossible in general. A.29b proves that the first translated cubic refinement still has no such target. A.29c nevertheless settles A1 for this exact genus-four auxiliary by a different method: a Prym Frobenius bound gives a finite 2-primary point packet, and the same translated refinement eliminates that packet. Extending this method to arbitrary ordinary-elliptic auxiliaries requires a uniform packet-elimination argument; the direct-target obstruction alone supplies none. The quadratic branch-splitting proof for arbitrary hyperelliptic curves in A.19 also does not transfer to wild Artin–Schreier double covers: higher ramification and odd geometric Galois closure must both be controlled. Additional ordinary characteristic-two families and explicit covers are treated in separate reviewed sections and ledgers.

## Configuration-space route: a small exact counterexample

A.28 refutes the universal left exactness proposed as a sufficient route in Saïdi–Tamagawa (2009), Remark 3.13. For the smooth proper genus-two curve C:y²=x⁵−x+1 over F5, the map from the prime-to-five fundamental group of C minus infinity to that of (C×C) minus the diagonal is not injective. This curve still satisfies A1 by the odd-characteristic hyperelliptic theorem A.19.

The obstruction is finite and explicit. The genus-two pure surface braid group acts by point pushing on the free group F4 of the punctured fibre. The corrected handle-indexed formulas in Bardakov–Bellingeri (2009), Proposition 4, preprint p.5, give a word A1 B2 A2 whose action on Hom(F4,S4)/S4 has a cycle of length five. The manuscript lists its five tuples and all five simultaneous conjugators. Their distinctness has a short group-theoretic proof: four tuples share a first pair generating S3 with trivial centralizer in S4; the fifth first pair generates S4. Since S4 has order24, this is a prime-to-five fibre quotient with unavoidable five-monodromy.

If the completed fibre injected into the pro-five-prime total group, compactness would make its topology the induced topology. An open normal subgroup of the total group would restrict inside the S4-kernel. The order of the point-pushing word in the resulting finite five-prime quotient would then kill its representation orbit, contradicting the orbit period five. SGA1 XIII, Corollary2.9 and section2.10, applies to the smooth proper pair (C×C,diagonal), and to the punctured fibre, and transports the map functorially from characteristic zero to five. The right-hand polynomial has derivative−1 modulo5, and infinity supplies a smooth section. The degeneration agent independently reviewed the compactness argument and this specialization step.

### Attempted repair using the full-base relative quotient

The relative extension supplied by the current configuration literature has the full prime-to-p fibre but retains the full tame base. It cannot descend to the prime-to-p base in the characteristic-five example. Let H be the kernel of the full geometric base group Π→Π^(5′). The 5-primary component of the procyclic closure of the point-pushing word lies in H and still acts in the exhibited five-cycle on S4-torsors. Thus H has nontrivial outer action on the full prime-to-five fibre. An extension descended from Π^(5′) would have trivial outer action of H, which is impossible.

The same issue blocks the arithmetic section comparison: the relative object can be pulled back along a full point section, but a prime-to-p section does not provide the full lift needed by this construction. One must not conclude that the curve or relative group is abstractly undetermined: Saïdi–Tamagawa's finite-field theorem reconstructs the curve from its arithmetic prime-to-p group. The unresolved step is how to identify the full geometric lift attached to one quotient section without already assuming the point-injectivity assertion. A different possible repair would require an anabelian theorem for the proper quotient of the punctured fibre that survives in the full prime-to-p configuration group. The opened punctured-curve theorem does not assert this. This is the exact remaining loss, rather than a generic appeal to an unproved exactness statement.

Both assertions are proved by low-degree hyperelliptic divisor arguments.
