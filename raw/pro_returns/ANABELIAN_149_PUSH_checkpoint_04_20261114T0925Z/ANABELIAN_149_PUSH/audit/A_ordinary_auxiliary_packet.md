# General finite-packet bound for ordinary elliptic auxiliaries

## Statement proved here; no universal A1 conclusion

Let m≥3 be odd. Let F_q have characteristic two and contain μ_m and nonzero a,b. Let B be the smooth proper curve

\[
 y^2+y=(s^m+b)+a/(s^m+b),
\]

and let E be its elliptic quotient with x=s^m+b. Choose δ(s,y)=(ζ_m s,y), and put

\[
 A=(\ker\operatorname{Nm}_{B/E})^0,\qquad \gamma(P)=[P-\delta P].
\]

Write f=ord_m(2) and r=2(2^f−1). Then every geometric point P with γ(P) 2-primary belongs to B(F_{q^r}). Thus the entire point packet is effectively computable in a field of degree depending only on m over a field of definition containing μ_m. In particular r=6 works for every cubic auxiliary.

This statement bounds the field, not uniformly the exponent of the entire Prym 2-primary subgroup. It does not assert that only Weierstrass points occur in this packet. It does not settle A1 for every ordinary elliptic quotient.

## 1. Geometry, 2-rank, and the two divisor lemmas

There are m simple finite Artin–Schreier poles, at s^m+b=0, and one pole of order m at infinity. Riemann–Hurwitz gives g(B)=(3m−1)/2. The divisor of ds has order2 at each finite Weierstrass point and order m−3 at infinity: the local different exponents are2 and m+1, respectively. A basis of regular differentials is therefore the m logarithmic forms ds/(s−s_i), together with s^j ds for 0≤j≤(m−3)/2. Their independence follows from partial fractions in the rational subfield. Cartier fixes each logarithmic form, kills the even-index polynomial forms, and strictly lowers every surviving odd index. Hence the stable Cartier rank is m, proving that the 2-rank is m. Thus A has dimension (3m−3)/2 and 2-rank m−1.

Let W_i be the m finite Weierstrass points and O the point at infinity. The classes [W_i−O] form an independent basis of J_B[2](Fbar2): twice each is principal, and a nonempty subset is a reduced effective divisor of degree at most m≤g(B), so cannot be zero. All their norms are the same nonzero point T∈E[2]. Consequently A[2] is exactly the even-subset subspace, also generated by γ(W_i), since δ cyclically permutes the W_i.

The map γ is injective off its zero fibre. If γ(P)=γ(Q), then P+δQ∼Q+δP. Equality of the two divisors gives P=Q or both points δ-fixed. Otherwise both divisors are hyperelliptic fibres, so δQ=ιP and δP=ιQ; hence δ²P=P. Since m is odd, P and then Q are δ-fixed. The zero fibre consists precisely of O and the two points above s=0.

No two non-Weierstrass γ-points differ by a nonzero element of A[2]. To prove this, represent such an element by an even nonempty subset S of the finite W_i, partitioned into equal halves S_1,S_2. Because 2W_i∼2O, its class is [D_1−D_2], where D_j is the sum over S_j. The two D_j are nonzero, disjoint, and have degree at most (m−1)/2. An equality γ(P)−γ(Q)=[D_1−D_2] would give

\[
 P+\delta Q+D_2\sim Q+\delta P+D_1.
\]

Their degree is at most (m+3)/2≤g(B). Each divisor has at most one hyperelliptic pair, formed by its two non-Weierstrass summands. If neither has a pair, reduced-divisor uniqueness contradicts the disjoint Weierstrass summands. If only one has a pair, the reduced degrees differ. If both have pairs, their residual divisors D_1,D_2 would be equal. Every case is impossible. The reduced-divisor uniqueness can alternatively be proved here by the bidegree genus bound: a degree d≤g function outside the separable quadratic rational subfield would give genus at most d−1.

## 2. The full étale 2-adic module

Let

\[
 R=\mathbb Z_2[T]/(1+T+\cdots+T^{m-1}).
\]

This is a finite product of unramified discrete valuation rings, since m is odd. The factor degrees f_j divide f=ord_m(2). The norm relation makes T₂A an R-module. Its reduction modulo2 is the augmentation representation on the m independent finite Weierstrass classes. Therefore each factor occurs with multiplicity one. A finite torsion-free module over each factor DVR is free; hence T₂A is free of rank one over R. This supplies the precise module structure, rather than inferring it from point counts.

Frobenius Φ commutes with δ and is multiplication by a unit λ=(λ_j)∈R*. The unit group of an unramified degree-f_j factor modulo4 has exponent dividing 2(2^{f_j}−1). Indeed its residue units have order2^{f_j}−1 and its kernel 1+2O_j modulo4 has exponent2. Thus λ^r≡1 modulo4 for r=2(2^f−1).

Put b_j=v₂(λ_j^r−1)≥2. These are finite: Φ^r−1 is an isogeny, with finite kernel A(F_{q^r}), so no λ_j^r equals1. The geometric 2-primary point group of A is the product of Frac(O_j)/O_j.

## 3. A field bound from a 2-torsion Galois difference

Let D=γ(P) be 2-primary, and let its j-th component have exact order2^{a_j}, with a_j=0 if that component vanishes. If M=max_j(a_j−b_j)>0, set k=M−1. Repeated squaring, starting at valuation at least2, gives

\[
 v_2(\lambda_j^{r2^k}-1)=b_j+k.
\]

Consequently (Φ^{r2^k}−1)D is killed by2 in every component and is nonzero in at least one. If P is non-Weierstrass, its Frobenius translate is also non-Weierstrass, contradicting the divisor lemma. If P is Weierstrass then γ(P) already has order at most2, so M>0 was impossible anyway. Hence all a_j≤b_j and Φ^rD=D.

If D≠0, injectivity of γ gives Φ^rP=P. If D=0, P is δ-fixed; infinity is rational and the two s=0 points are defined over at most a quadratic extension. Since r is even, these points also belong to B(F_{q^r}). This proves the full packet bound.

## 4. A useful stronger cubic criterion

When m=3, every nonzero A[2] class is [W_i−W_j]. A non-Weierstrass γ(P) cannot have order4: otherwise 2γ(P)=[W_i−W_j] gives

\[
 2P+W_j\sim2\delta P+W_i,
\]

an equality of distinct reduced divisors of degree3≤g(B)=4. Order2 is excluded by the same argument with degree2. Thus if a Frobenius power Φ^u acts on T₂A by λ^u with v₂(λ^u−1)=2, the preceding squaring argument bounds the order by4, forcing every nonzero 2-primary γ-point to be one of the three finite Weierstrass points.

If the symmetric triple defining this cubic auxiliary is centred at O and its two other points are ±d with d of exact order3, the translated-cover proof of A.29c then applies without any finite enumeration. Therefore A1 holds for each such cubic auxiliary satisfying this explicit Frobenius-valuation criterion. Equivalently, it suffices that the 2-adic valuation of |A(F_{q^u})| be4. This is a conditional, checkable positive criterion; this note does not claim that every ordinary elliptic auxiliary satisfies it.

## Exact remaining issue

For general coefficients, the finite packet can be computed by the Riemann–Roch or Mumford method used in A.29c. A uniform argument that removes every possible non-Weierstrass member under admissible translated covers is not supplied. For m>3 the separate translation geometry also requires examination: the order-three Heisenberg refinement used in A.29c is a specific construction, not an automatic consequence of this packet bound.
