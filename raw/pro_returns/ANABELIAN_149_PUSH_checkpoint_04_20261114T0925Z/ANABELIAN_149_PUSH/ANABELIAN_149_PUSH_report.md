## The main positive result and its mechanism

The strongest class theorems now prove A1 for every abelian prime-to-p Galois cover of a genus-zero curve in every characteristic, and every such cover of a genus-one curve in odd characteristic or with supersingular quotient in characteristic two. There is no exponent bound in either theorem. These results include all geometrically hyperelliptic curves in odd characteristic and many nonhyperelliptic curves. The proof proceeds through an odd-characteristic hyperelliptic theorem, an all-characteristic elliptic-image test, quotient inductions, and finally an explicit torsion-divisor construction that retains the required inertia information. The initial constructive Hesse route handled p>=5 with p congruent to 2 modulo 3; a separate exact four-torsion configuration handled p congruent to 3 modulo 4. A prime-to-p torsion-density argument then removed both congruence restrictions for hyperelliptic curves.

For any elliptic curve E over Fbar_p, p odd, and any prescribed nondegenerate cross-ratio c, the locus of four points on E whose images in E/{±1} have cross-ratio c is an integral ample divisor in E^4. Its open part is a degree-16 Kummer cover of PGL_2. Four evaluation divisors prove independence of the four square classes. Finite flatness extends generic integrality across every diagonal. Ampleness excludes containment in a translate of a proper abelian subvariety. The Anderson–Indik density consequence, in the exact form opened in Yang (2016), Proposition 2.3, therefore gives four prime-to-p torsion points in the required open locus. Root and a second agent independently checked this source, including the overline on F_p, and independently checked the complete geometry. The published underlying curve-surjectivity theorem of Bogomolov and Tschinkel (2005), Theorem 1.1, was also opened with its proof.

Choose c to be a supersingular Legendre parameter. A root of the Deuring polynomial supplies such a c in every odd characteristic; its values at 0 and 1 are nonzero. If g:D→E is a ramified double cover and n kills the four torsion points, pulling g back by [n] makes its branch locus contain the four opposite pairs. Pulling back the supersingular double cover then cancels all quadratic ramification. The resulting connected component W→D is finite étale, and its Galois closure has order prime to p: it is controlled by a 2-group extended by E[n]. Connectedness of D×_E E is proved using the ramified quadratic extension versus the unramified [n] extension; n need not be odd.

The resulting map W→E_0 detects a point up to reflection in the chosen elliptic branch origin. Equal prime-to-p point sections lift to equal sections on every cover. Their divisor difference is p-primary, so their images on the supersingular elliptic target agree. Two branch origins whose difference has exact order ell, ell different from 2 and p, eliminate the reflection ambiguity. Consequently a collision on any ramified double cover of an elliptic curve has equal elliptic images.

For a hyperelliptic curve, first choose an elliptic double cover of the quotient line branched at four of its branch points. Their normalized fibre product is an étale double cover of the original curve and a ramified double cover of the elliptic curve. The preceding result forces a collision to have equal hyperelliptic coordinates. An induction splits the branch set into subsets of sizes 2 and 2g. It is an étale double cover of the original curve. The two projection maps jointly distinguish every point, including all branch and infinite fibres. This proves the induction. Specialization transfers the result to fields finitely generated over F_p with the required comparison maps proved in A.8.

### Removing every elliptic-cover exponent bound

A.29 gives a direct construction for a cyclic n-cover C→E, for every n prime to p. Pull back by [N], with N=2n in odd characteristic and N=n in the characteristic-two supersingular case. A branch point becomes a whole coset of E[N], with the same valuation residue a modulo n at each point. The exact sequence q_i=binom(i,2) is periodic modulo n with period N and has second difference one. On each cyclic row, the constant divisor vector is therefore the sum of q_i times the principal triples (x+d)+(x−d)−2(x). The coefficients retain a factor a, so every auxiliary Kummer ramification order divides the original one, even at partial branches.

Each nontrivial auxiliary curve has an equivalent equation s^m=x−b, with m dividing n. In odd characteristic its equation becomes y²=R(s^m+b), a squarefree polynomial of degree 3m; it is hyperelliptic of genus (3m−gcd(m,2))/2≥2, so A.19 separates its points. For a supersingular elliptic base in characteristic two, the equation becomes y²+y=(s^m+b)^3+A(s^m+b)+B, an Artin–Schreier curve with one pole of odd order 3m. The explicit Cartier calculation gives 2-rank zero, so A.1 separates its points. Adjoining all the auxiliary roots is étale over the original isogeny pullback; the composite cover has prime-to-p Galois closure by the finite wreath-product bound.

Divide the original Kummer root by all auxiliary roots. The residual cover H→E is unramified and has genus one. The common cover V→H retains a uniformly nontrivial original branch index, so A.21 gives equal H-images of a collision. At a common base point let I be inertia and e the original cyclic index. Equal auxiliary images mean that each auxiliary character of the deck displacement lies in μ_e. The residual character is trivial on the displacement, and the original character is its product with all auxiliary characters. Thus the original displacement lies in its own inertia subgroup, exactly the condition that the two original points coincide. This argument treats every ramified fibre; it does not infer joint point separation merely from an equality of function fields. All cyclic character quotients then give the abelian theorem, without an exponent bound. Root and the Part-A agent independently checked the construction and the character/inertia conclusion.

These arguments establish the stated classes. They do not prove that every curve has an admissible cover with such a quotient, nor that a ramified cover of a curve already satisfying A1 also satisfies A1. No priority claim is made.

## A2: the comparison is complete

A.2 proves a uniqueness statement needed for the reverse implication. If an automorphism of order m prime to p acts freely on the open curve, then a difference between a point and its image cannot be killed in the generalized Jacobian by an integer coprime to m. The proof fixes the orientation of the function-field action and its norm, normalizes the norm in the proper case, and uses Hilbert 90. At a boundary orbit, tame inertia of order e acts on a uniformizer by an e-th root of unity; the modulus condition forces the valuation of the Hilbert-90 function to be divisible by e. Every invariant divisor orbit therefore contributes a multiple of m to the degree. This contradicts the degree of the distinguished point divisor.

It follows that in a geometric prime-to-p Galois cover at most one lift of Q has p-primary difference from a chosen lift of P. For a characteristic pointed neighbourhood over K, a finite Galois extension L/K kills the action on its geometric deck quotient and makes it arithmetically Galois of prime-to-p degree. The restrictive A2 condition applies there. Uniqueness gives G_K-invariance of the lift and therefore descent to K. Norms give compatibility between neighbourhoods. Compactness then gives a fixed path in the inverse limit and hence an actual geometric conjugator between the full sections. This proves both covering-condition equivalences, including all field-extension and compatibility steps. It does not identify nonabelian equality with one finite abelian calculation.

## Attempts that do not yet prove universal A1

1. **Finite norm kernels and two primes.** A.7 proves the general hyperplane incidence identity. On a common refinement of elementary abelian ell- and m-covers, the two corresponding joint kernels intersect trivially because they have coprime annihilators. When the norm images are p-primary, this removes only the prime-to-p component of the original divisor difference. A nonzero p-primary difference remains possible. Tests on separate base covers do not automatically give all tests on a common refinement. The next needed input is an obstruction to such a point-divisor class being p-primary on every refinement, not another coprime-kernel identity.

2. **The local-system proposal.** For ell different from p, p does not divide |GL_d(F_ell)| precisely when the multiplicative order of ell modulo p exceeds d. This is impossible when d>=p−1; in particular the proposed positive-dimensional abelian-scheme representation cannot meet that numerical condition for p=2 or p=3. Moreover a smooth elliptic scheme over a proper connected curve has constant j, since every morphism from that curve to A^1 is constant. An affine nonisotrivial elliptic-family argument cannot be inserted on a proper base without additional work. Higher-dimensional complete families are not excluded by this observation, but no family satisfying all necessary monodromy and separation conditions has been constructed.

3. **Exceptional-set growth.** The verified identity |E_V|=d|E_C| grows at the same rate as g(V)−1=d(g(C)−1). Ordinary point-count estimates therefore do not contradict a nonempty exceptional set. The inspected finiteness proof does not provide the required sublinear bound in an étale tower. A precise next step would be a uniform bound o(d) for the relevant exceptional sets along a specific admissible tower, with its hypotheses checked.

4. **The Lang tower.** A.12 gives a genuine additional necessary condition for a collision. Along an explicit sequence of prime-to-p Lang covers of degree d_m, all points in both distinguished fibres are rational. If the unique compatible divisor difference has order p^{a_m}, then p^{a_m}>=2d_m/(q^m+1)>=2(q^{m/2}−1)^{2g}/[p^v(q^m+1)]. Thus these orders must grow without bound. This is not a contradiction: no uniform upper bound on the exponent has been proved. Bounded multiplicities of individual prime-to-p deck characters in cohomology do not bound the p-primary exponents as characters and covers vary. A quantitative bound specifically for these two-point divisor classes remains the missing input.

5. **Pure inseparability.** A.11 shows that a rational function realizing a difference of exact order p^a is not a p-th power, hence is separable. Positive-p-rank genus-two curves already have point differences of arbitrarily large p-power order, because C−C=J. Infinitely many such pairs alone therefore cannot imply pure inseparability. What would need to be used is compatibility through all admissible covers.

6. **Supersingular quotients and nonabelian covers.** A.10 supplies a sufficient criterion using maps from an admissible étale cover to p-rank-zero abelian varieties. The correspondence, quotient, and divisor arguments now settle every abelian prime-to-p cover of a genus-zero curve, every such cover of a genus-one curve in odd characteristic, and supersingular elliptic quotients in characteristic two. The former degree-7 and degree-16 obstacles to the reflection/branch-pair route are bypassed by A.29's torsion-divisor construction. They are no longer unresolved elliptic-cover cases in odd characteristic. Raynaud's solvable-cover nonordinarity theorem and Yang's cyclic-cover non-new-ordinarity theorem do not by themselves produce a p-rank-zero abelian quotient; nonordinary need not mean p-rank zero. A single dominant map to a known separated target only forces a collision into one fibre. For a nonabelian Galois cover, character quotients identify a point only modulo the commutator subgroup and its stabilizer. Removing that remaining fibre ambiguity, or finding analogous controlled principal divisors over bases of genus at least two, remains a precise next step. A tame map to P^1 alone does not resolve it: the Galois closure may have order divisible by p and may be ramified over the original curve.

### Ordinary family

The parameterization is b=av³, c=a(v+1)³ with v≠0,1. The explicit cubic torsor is obtained from

### Exhaustiveness of the cubic obstruction

The proof treats every nonzero geometric 3-torsion class. Its unique reduced degree-two divisor cannot have reduced degree one, because that would require a function with a single pole of order three at a genus-two Weierstrass point. The torsion function lies in L(6∞)=⟨1,x,x²,x³,Y⟩ and has nonzero Y coefficient: an invariant divisor would represent a class killed by two. The general norm equation is therefore exactly A²+hA+f=L²u³ with u monic quadratic; no exceptional torsor was discarded.

The quotient plane cubic has Hasse scalar Lu1. Setting u1=0 and comparing coefficients gives u0³=(b/a)² and u0²+u0+1=((b+c)/a)². These imply the stated parameter condition. The converse is the explicit positive construction. The proof of J_D isogenous to J_C×E² uses the dihedral action on an auxiliary rational Tate module and verifies that the two reflection-fixed subspaces intersect trivially and span the Prym. It remains valid in characteristic two.

The result is an obstruction to a **specified degree-three method**. It is not a counterexample to point-section injectivity, to all odd-degree covers, or to all correspondences.

A=x⁵+sx⁴+x³+x²+dx+s⁵, u=x²+s², and Norm(A+Y)=u⁵.

## Exact theta criterion and unresolved order selection

`sections/A_char2_theta.tex` contains A.C2.5 (Proved) and A.C2.6 (Unresolved), both independently reviewed by the parent. The criterion applies to every genus-two curve in characteristic two. The locally exact differential line bundle satisfies B²=ω, so its theta translate is symmetric. For an odd-order line bundle L, each nonzero H1(L^i) is one-dimensional, and its Frobenius map vanishes precisely when L^i lies on theta. Thus the new 2-rank is exactly the number of character indices in multiplication-by-two orbits that miss theta altogether.

If every orbit meets theta, a lifted hyperelliptic involution gives a genus-(n−1)/2 quotient of 2-rank zero. The complete proof includes the lift, wild different exponents, the general odd-dihedral Jacobian isogeny, and joint point separation by two reflection quotients. For prime ell, a nonzero ell-torsion theta point suffices when the subgroup generated by 2 and −1 is all of (Z/ell)×. This includes ell=7, where the two doubling orbits are exchanged by sign; primitivity of 2 alone is unnecessary.

The unresolved step is finding such a point, or enough simultaneous theta intersections, for an arbitrary curve over Fbar2. Yang's density theorem does not control its order or the distribution of its multiples among doubling orbits. Composite orders require proper-conductor blocks as well as primitive characters. This distinction is concrete: A.C2.2 implies that a 2-rank-one genus-two curve has theta∩J[3]={0}, so every cyclic degree divisible by three retains an ordinary order-three block. For ordinary curves off the cubic special locus, the corresponding statement is that **nonzero** theta 3-torsion is absent; zero is not on their theta divisor either.

`code/A_char2_theta_checks.py` and its output contain **fourteen passing arithmetic checks**, including the single cycle at thirteen, the two sign-exchanged cycles at seven, an unhit cycle at seventeen, the surviving order-three block at degree nine, 480 exact stable-rank cases, the odd-dihedral generator condition, the exact deck kernels at degrees 9, 15, 25 and 27, and the three-dimensional free-orbit example below. These are finite certificates for the arithmetic examples, not a substitute for the general proof.

The exact ratio f(D)/n is f(C)/n plus the proportion of character indices in unhit cycles. Therefore one theta intersection does not imply that normalized 2-rank can be made arbitrarily small. No such quantitative tower construction is claimed.

## A weaker deck-action criterion

`sections/A_char2_deck.tex` contains A.C2.7–8. These strengthen the theta application without asserting a 2-rank-zero quotient. Any nontrivial odd Galois étale cover D of a proper genus-at-least-two curve C is nonhyperelliptic: an odd-order deck transformation would otherwise descend to P1 and have a fixed point above a fixed base point. On this nonhyperelliptic D, a difference [Q′−P′] across distinct fibres has trivial deck stabilizer, because a stabilizer element would produce two distinct linearly equivalent effective degree-two divisors.

A hypothetical point-section collision supplies such a difference in two-primary torsion. Hence the deck action on J_D[2] must be faithful: a finite odd-order matrix congruent to the identity modulo two is the identity on the full two-adic étale Tate module. The averaging projector splits off the f(C)-dimensional invariant part integrally, so the remaining f(D)−f(C) dimensions must carry a faithful F2-representation. This proves a precise representation-theoretic lower bound. It does not establish a lower bound linear in the degree.

For cyclic genus-two covers the kernel is computed exactly by gcd(n, surviving character indices). At degree ell^a, eliminating every primitive-character orbit leaves a nontrivial subgroup of order at least ell in the kernel even when smaller-conductor blocks survive. Thus a prime-power torsion theta point with <2,−1> equal to all units suffices. Degree nine can therefore work despite the universal ordinary order-three block of the rank-one family. This is a conditional criterion: no suitable nine-torsion theta point is asserted to exist on an arbitrary curve. At degree fifteen, surviving order-three and order-five characters together give a faithful action, illustrating why merely eliminating all primitive characters is not enough for a general composite degree.

The faithful-action obstruction supplies a rigorous portion of the proposed fibre-pairing/logarithmic-differential route. The stronger assertion that such a collision forces f(D) at least the cover degree, and the existence of towers whose normalized p-rank tends to zero, remain unproved here.

The proposed linear-independence inference already fails for abstract modules. Let alpha satisfy alpha³+alpha+1=0 in F8, let a generator g of the cyclic group of order seven act by multiplication by alpha, and take v=1. The orbit of v consists of all seven nonzero vectors of the three-dimensional F2-vector space F8 and has trivial stabilizer. Nevertheless v+gv+g³v=0. The exact field computation is included in the certificate. Thus a free deck-equivariant orbit of torsion classes or logarithmic differentials does not by itself supply seven independent differentials. This is an obstruction to that abstract inference, not a geometric counterexample to a possible stronger theorem.

### Prime-power extraction attempt: exact failure

Let L in theta have odd order n=ell^a*m with gcd(ell,m)=1. The algebraic extraction L^m has ell-power order, but no argument preserves theta under this multiplication map. Theta is not a subgroup. In the rank-one genus-two case B=O(W) for a Weierstrass point W, a nonzero odd-order theta point is L=O(R−W) with R non-Weierstrass. Already L² cannot lie on theta: that would give 2R linearly equivalent to S+W for a point S, a pair of distinct effective degree-two divisors, forcing 2R to be the hyperelliptic fibre and R to be Weierstrass. This supplies a direct geometric warning against silently preserving theta under scalar operations.

Passing to an intermediate cover does not fix the missing step. If Y→C is the cyclic cover of degree m associated with the order-m subgroup generated by L^(ell^a), then the pullback of L has exact order ell^a. The projection formula decomposes H1(Y, pullback L^i) into m summands H1(C,L^(i+j*ell^a)). The single zero edge arising from L in theta kills one one-dimensional summand, not the whole m-dimensional character space on Y. One must still eliminate every summand carrying the relevant primitive ell-power character. This is exactly the surviving-character condition of A.C2.8 in different coordinates.

An additional primary source was checked to see whether it supplied prime or prime-power orders. Pop and Saïdi (2003), *On the Specialization Homomorphism of Fundamental Groups of Curves in Positive Characteristic*, printed pp.113–114, Propositions2.1–2.3, at <https://library.slmath.org/books/Book41/files/pop.pdf>, says “pairwise prime orders.” The proof on p.113 constructs a point whose order is relatively prime to a previously fixed integer r while prescribing one s-primary component. Consequently the phrase means **pairwise coprime orders** in that proof; it does not supply points of individually prime order. Other primary components remain uncontrolled. The stronger prime-degree phrasing in some later summaries is not used here. The full thirteen-page primary PDF was opened and the exact proof, not merely the theorem wording, was inspected.

The finite-field primary-component surjectivity behind that proof and behind Bogomolov–Tschinkel is useful, but a surjective projection of theta to one primary torsion subgroup does not assert that theta contains that subgroup's nonzero points: the unwanted components of the lifted point need not vanish. The extraction route therefore remains incomplete at a precisely identified geometric step.

## Decisive corrections and strengthened conclusions

**L4 confuses splittings with classes of splittings.** For a chosen reference splitting, an actual continuous splitting is a continuous 1-cocycle. Conjugation by inertia adds a coboundary. Therefore the splittings form a torsor under Z1, whereas their inertia-conjugacy classes form a torsor under H1. A nonzero coboundary at complex conjugation gives distinct actual splittings with identical cohomology class. The finite outer-inertia criterion, the root-of-uniformizer field and the twist formula survive with this correction. The finite C2-action calculation in the code illustrates the distinction.

**R13 needs a typed codomain.** The finite index mu occurs for the map from G_K to the fundamental group of the good total model, identified with the special-fibre arithmetic group. Its cusp inertia image is mu T, and its residue cocycle is mu times the splitting class plus the Kummer class of the leading unit. A generic section G_K→pi1(U_K) meets the geometric kernel trivially, so it cannot have finite index mu in a generic cusp decomposition group with infinite geometric inertia. This is a correction to an inherited formulation, not to the local Kummer multiplication calculation.

**The full residue-prime table is much more restrictive in mixed characteristic.** SGA 7 I, IX, Proposition 5.14(b) is stated for a trait, and gives infinite determinant inertia at p. # Independent D5 construction: an action with no normalized finite quotient

Status: complete proof independently reviewed and accepted by the root agent, 14 November 2026; directly insertable version in `sections/D_extension.tex`, claims D.10–D.11. This note belongs to the Part F agent's cross-review work. It does **not** settle the universal assertion that every abstract action has a unique fixed pro-point. It constructs an abstract action that has exactly one fixed pro-point and normalizes no deck group of any finite hyperbolic quotient.

## Statement

Let U = P1 over Qbar minus {0,1,infinity}, let Omega be the union of the function fields of its connected finite étale covers, and let the universal pro-cover be the corresponding inverse limit of affine curves. There exists a semilinear action rho of G_Q on that pro-cover such that:

1. the orbit of the base coordinate lambda in Omega is infinite;
2. rho normalizes no deck group of a finite hyperbolic quotient;
3. the fixed set of G_Q^circ on the completed pro-cover consists of precisely one geometric closed pro-point, the chosen cusp above lambda = 0.

All uses of a completed pro-point mean compatible systems of geometric closed points, as in D.7, and exclude the generic scheme point.

## 1. Modular realization and a finite-adele group of automorphisms

Choose an embedding Qbar into C. The classical modular function lambda identifies H/Gamma(2) with U(C). Put q = exp(pi i tau). Its expansion is

    lambda(q) = 16q - 128q^2 + 704q^3 + ... in Q[[q]].

The precise product formula, rationality, and uniformization are in the primary published source Calegari, Dimitrov, and Tang (2025), equation (1.0.2) and the following paragraph, p. 628. The proof below uses this classical setup, not the unbounded-denominators theorem proved in that paper.

Use the cusp tau = i infinity to embed Omega in the field of formal Puiseux series

    P = union_(n>=1) Qbar((q^(1/n))).

Equivalently, an element of Omega is realized as a meromorphic modular function on H for some finite-index subgroup of Gamma(2), meromorphic at the cusps, with its selected algebraic Puiseux germ at infinity. The analytic cover is the analytification of its algebraic finite étale cover. The field embedding is faithful, and the coordinate ring of the pro-cover consists of those functions holomorphic on H, with poles allowed only at cusps.

Write A_f for the additive finite adeles and Zhat for its standard compact open subgroup. The quotient A_f/Zhat is canonically Q/Z: every finite adele is r+z with r rational and z in Zhat, and Q intersect Zhat = Z. Choose the character

    psi : A_f -> Q/Z -> mu_infinity(C),  psi(r+z) = exp(2 pi i r).

For a in A_f define a formal automorphism T_a of P by

    T_a(sum_k c_k q^(k/n)) = sum_k c_k psi(k a/n) q^(k/n).

This is independent of the denominator used to write a Puiseux expansion; multiplication of the denominator by m replaces k by mk and leaves the factor unchanged. The maps satisfy T_a T_b = T_(a+b), fix Qbar, and preserve the q-valuation.

### Preservation of Omega and of the affine pro-cover

For z in Zhat, T_z fixes q and hence lambda(q). It is the usual cusp-inertia action on Puiseux roots, and preserves Omega because Omega is normal over Qbar(lambda), with allowed branch locus {0,1,infinity}. It also preserves the affine coordinate ring.

For r in Q, T_r is analytically the substitution tau -> tau + 2r. Its matrix has rational entries and commensurates Gamma(2). Explicitly, after clearing a denominator d, conjugation of Gamma(2d^2) lands in Gamma(2); applying the same observation to the inverse gives commensurability. Hence if f is invariant under a finite-index subgroup Gamma_f of Gamma(2), then f(tau+2r) is invariant under the finite-index subgroup

    Gamma(2) intersect u(2r)^(-1) Gamma_f u(2r).

The transformed function is meromorphic at every cusp. It is thus algebraic over C(lambda), and its associated covering is unramified over U(C). Its Puiseux coefficients are still algebraic, since T_r multiplies the original coefficients by roots of unity. To verify descent without presuming a chosen modular correspondence model, take a polynomial relation over C(lambda). After clearing its denominators, the finitely many possible monomials in lambda and T_r f satisfy a homogeneous linear system whose coefficients lie in Qbar, obtained by comparing Laurent coefficients. A nonzero solution over C implies a nonzero solution over Qbar: the rank is determined by finite minors. Thus T_r f is algebraic over Qbar(lambda). Its branch locus is still contained in {0,1,infinity}, so it belongs to Omega. If f has no poles on H, neither does f(tau+2r), and therefore this also preserves the affine coordinate ring. Apply the argument to -r for the inverse.

Every a is r+z, so T_a preserves Omega and the universal pro-cover. No unique-root assertion in the full automorphism group is needed.

The action extends to the completed pro-cover: the image of a finite-level function field lies in some further finite level, and the induced rational map of smooth proper compactifications extends uniquely everywhere by properness and normality for curves. These extensions commute with the transition maps and composition. The inverse operators make them automorphisms of the completed pro-object.

### Faithfulness and the stabilizer of lambda

From the first term 16q of lambda(q),

    T_a(lambda) = lambda  iff  psi(a) = 1  iff  a belongs to Zhat.

For a in Zhat, the functions lambda^(1/n) belong to Omega for every n, and their leading Puiseux terms show that an element acting trivially on all of Omega satisfies psi(a/n)=1 for every n. Thus a is in every n Zhat, hence a=0. Therefore a -> T_a is an injective group homomorphism

    A_f -> Aut_Qbar(universal pro-cover),

and the stabilizer of the single function lambda in A_f is precisely Zhat. All these automorphisms fix the selected completed cusp.

## 2. Exact cyclotomic Galois normalization

Let rho_0(g) act coefficientwise on P, fixing q^(1/n) for every n. Since lambda(q) has rational coefficients, this restricts to the continuous tangential semilinear action on Omega. One may regard it as the splitting supplied by Spec Q((q)) -> U, lambda -> lambda(q). A rational formal parameter is sufficient; q need not be an element of Omega.

For every a and g, coefficientwise evaluation gives

    rho_0(g) T_a rho_0(g)^(-1) = T_(chi(g) a).

Indeed g(psi(a/n)) = psi(chi(g) a/n), where chi(g) acts coordinatewise on A_f. This verifies Galois stability and its precise character directly on faithful germs, including the nonintegral rational translations.

## 3. A surjective Kummer quotient on G_Q^circ

Fix any odd prime ell, and enumerate distinct rational primes p_i different from ell. Choose coherent ell-power roots of the p_i and of unity. The Kummer cocycles give a continuous product cocycle

    kappa : G_Q -> V = product_(i>=1) Z_ell,
    kappa(gh) = kappa(g) + chi_ell(g) kappa(h).

Its restriction to G_Q^circ is a continuous surjective homomorphism. Here is a finite-level proof including the abelian-intersection issue.

For a finite set of indices and m>=1, let K=Q(mu_(ell^m)) and L=K(p_i^(1/ell^m)). The extension K/Q is unramified at each p_i, so the valuations at these primes make the classes of the p_i independent and of full order in K^*/K^(*ell^m). Kummer theory gives

    T = Gal(L/K) = (Z/ell^m)^r.

The extension L/Q is Galois. A lift sigma of a cyclotomic automorphism with character 2 modulo ell acts on T by that scalar. Consequently [sigma,T]=T, since chi(sigma)-1 is a unit. The quotient Gal(L/Q)/T is abelian, so T is exactly the commutator subgroup and L intersect Qab = K. Therefore G_Q^circ maps onto T. Surjectivity to V follows from all these finite-level surjections and compactness.

## 4. An unbounded linear functional with dense kernel

The coordinate vectors e_i are linearly independent in the Q_ell-vector space V tensor_(Z_ell) Q_ell. Extend them to a basis and choose a Q_ell-linear functional taking e_i to ell^(-i). Restriction supplies a Z_ell-linear function

    f : V -> Q_ell,  f(e_i)=ell^(-i).

The basis extension uses the axiom of choice. This is a specified abstract cocycle construction, not an effective algorithm to evaluate it on arbitrary Galois elements.

Its image contains ell^(-i) Z_ell for every i and hence is all Q_ell. Its kernel is dense in V: given finitely many prescribed coordinates, realize them by a finitely supported vector v. Choose j outside those coordinates so large that ell^j f(v) belongs to Z_ell, and replace v by

    v - ell^j f(v)e_j.

The result has the prescribed coordinates and is killed by f. This argument proves density for every extension of the stated values; no special basis choice or unjustified automatic continuity is used.

Embed Q_ell in A_f by its ell component and zero at the other primes, and put a_g = f(kappa(g)). Linearity gives

    a_(gh) = a_g + chi(g) a_h.

Define rho(g) = T_(a_g) rho_0(g). Section 2 and this identity give the action law. It is semilinear for the specified usual action on Qbar. On G_Q^circ its image under a is all Q_ell, while

    K_0 = ker(a|G_Q^circ)

is dense in G_Q^circ because kappa is a continuous surjective homomorphism of profinite groups and ker(f) is dense in its quotient. Continuous surjective homomorphisms of compact groups are open quotient maps.

## 5. The infinite orbit rules out every normalized finite hyperbolic quotient

The action rho_0 fixes lambda. Hence rho(g)(lambda)=T_(a_g)(lambda). Since a(G_Q^circ)=Q_ell and Stab_(A_f)(lambda)=Zhat, the orbit contains a copy of Q_ell/Z_ell and is infinite.

Suppose, to the contrary, that rho normalizes the deck group H of a finite hyperbolic quotient W. It descends to a semilinear action on W and on its function field F=Omega^H. Choose a finite number field K over which W and finitely many function-field generators are defined. For g in G_K, compare the descended action to the natural coefficient action associated to that K-model. The quotient is a geometric automorphism of W. The latter group is finite because W is hyperbolic. Thus the chosen generators have finite orbits under G_K, and then under G_Q since G_K has finite index. Constants have finite orbits as well, so every element of F has a finite orbit.

Every element of Omega is algebraic over F. Its polynomial equation has coefficients in F with finite orbits, so its G_Q orbit lies among the roots of finitely many polynomials and is finite. This contradicts the infinite orbit of lambda. The argument uses no continuity assumption and no unproved congruence-quotient comparison.

## 6. Determination of the fixed set

All T_a and rho_0(g) preserve the q-valuation and the selected cusp, so that cusp is fixed by the full action.

If a completed geometric closed pro-point P is fixed by rho(G_Q^circ), it is fixed by rho_0(K_0), because a vanishes on K_0. The stabilizer of P under the continuous action rho_0 is closed: use cofinal characteristic finite levels, each descending over Q under rho_0; the stabilizer of the algebraic closed point at any such level is open and closed, and intersect these stabilizers. Density of K_0 implies that P is fixed by rho_0(G_Q^circ).

For completeness, the unique fixed cusp for rho_0 follows from the same abelian-variety Kummer argument already proved in D.7 (or F.3). If another fixed pro-point existed, choose a characteristic cover separating it from the selected cusp and dominating the degree-nine Fermat cover x^3+y^3=1 of U. The compactification then has positive genus. It descends over Qab under rho_0, and the two projected Qab-rational points have the same section class of its proper fundamental group. Their Abel–Jacobi difference has zero Kummer class. Injectivity of the Kummer map for abelian varieties over finite extensions of Qab makes the difference zero. Distinct points on a positive-genus proper smooth curve cannot have principal difference, since that would give a degree-one map to P1. This contradiction proves uniqueness.

Thus the constructed action has exactly the original selected cusp as its G_Q^circ-fixed set.

## Scope

The action is not a refutation of the first, universal fixed-point assertion in D5: it satisfies that assertion. It answers the second demand by proving existence of actions outside all the finite-quotient normalization cases of R19. The proof does not assert that no logically different reduction of B-prime to B is possible. It does show that direct finite-quotient descent cannot cover all abstract actions.
