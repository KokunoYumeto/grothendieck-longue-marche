The reduction modulo $m$ in the operation
\[
\handwritten{a(n)=b(n)-b(n-1)-b(n-2)+b(n-5)+b(n-7)-b(n-12)\ldots}
\]
is performed only at the end(and not after each individual $+$ or $-$ ), so that to avoid overflow there is a restriction that $m\unsure{=|:}(N/6)^{1/2}$ be less than $6.7\times10^{10}$.

Finally multiplication(division) of two given general series takes $O(N^{2})$ \handwritten{\tikz[x=1em,y=1em,baseline=-.1em]{\draw (.08,-.55)--(.57,1.05); \draw (.19,-.24) .. controls (.30,-.08) and (.32,-.49) .. In view of this a certain amount of modular(rather than programming)ingenuity was required; in one case a natural definition $g^{2}(x)$ with $g(x)$ already computed was replaced by $g^{2}(x)=$ a sum of three terms each involving several $\handwritten{\vartheta}$-multiplications and divisions.

The possibility of multiplying(dividing) by Jacobi's series
\[
f^{3}(x)=1-3x+5x^{3}-\ldots=\handwritten{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(2n+1)x^{\frac12 n(n+1)}}
\]
was considered,but it turned out to be slightly slower than 3 successive uses of Euler's series,and also involved a heavier restriction on the size of $m$ and $N$.
