## Legibility and witnesses

The ordinary running prose is generally more legible than the dense cancellations, small raised indices and additions in the margins. The least secure places are the overwritten passages and crowded indices on 142_scan111 and 142_scan115–118, the compact annotations on 142_scan120–121, the long marginal additions on 143_scan002, the cancelled note beside (17) on 143_scan006, and writing that meets the scan edge on 143_scan004 and 143_scan007. The two pale case-caption digits on 142_scan106 remain qualified as 3 or 2. The signature on the typed notice, 142_scan109, remains a graphic with local uncertainty. Missing strokes beyond a scan edge are not reconstructed.

The transcription distinguishes uncertain identification from uncertain meaning. A visible capped shaft can be drawn faithfully even when it is unclear whether the author meant a dash or an unfinished arrow. Conversely, an obscured letter, index or cancellation remains marked in place. Small accents, detached dots, primes and crossing strokes have been judged locally; grammatical or mathematical plausibility alone has not supplied them. Hand-drawn geometry is approximated in TikZ, with the observed labels, connectivity, directions, terminal details and surviving revisions retained.

Both supplied witnesses are AI readings of the same scans. Their agreement was not treated as independent evidence. C often preserved shorthand and revision structure that H expanded or regularized; H also supplied useful alternatives where C misread a word or ending. The disagreement totals describe this particular collation, including punctuation, placement and drawing details, and are not an independently measured accuracy rate for either witness. Font or macro choices alone were excluded from substantive counts.

H’s separate drawing files were not supplied. Comparisons with its figures therefore rely on the supplied local code, explicit assertions and individually named labels. A generic description of a family of curves has not been treated as evidence that every unnamed local stroke was absent from H’s unavailable drawing. H’s editorial summaries and notes were distinguished from source text; explicit claims in a note about placement or cancellation were checked where they bore on the transcription.

No OCR, external text, published reconstruction or TeX compilation was used. Mathematical checks are limited to the actual events in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv, with their assumptions and scope stated. Reading a manuscript claim has not been reported as proving it. The remaining doubts table is part of the edition: the chosen first alternative records the preferred ink reading, while the surviving alternatives and reasons identify where another inspection could still change the result.
