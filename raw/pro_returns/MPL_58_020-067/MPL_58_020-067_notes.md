**File/scan:** 58:21, left margin and proof. **Kind:** conjecture / unproved claim. **Context search:** N02; H, “Du local au global,” their p. 20, restates and expands the same point. **RS:** R0.

### N04. Connected generic fibre alone does not give the special-fibre claim

**File/scan:** 58:23, Theorem 2, hypotheses (a)–(b). **Kind:** own idea, refining a context diagnosis. **Context search:** N04; H, “Les fibres spéciales d'un morphisme propre,” their pp. 22–26, explicitly adds “géométriquement” to the generic-fibre condition. **RS:** R0. **Confidence:** high for the example, because the fibres are computed explicitly.

M says that X₁ is “connexe en dim ≥ h,” without geometrically, and H's diagnosis can be tested while retaining the written lower bound dim X₁≥h+1: take B=Spec ℤ_(5), D=ℤ_(5)[i], and X=ℙ¹_D→B, with h=0. The base is regular and geometrically unibranch, X is integral and proper over B, its sole component dominates B, and its generic fibre ℙ¹_ℚ(i) is connected of dimension one. Since T²+1=(T−2)(T+2) over 𝔽₅, the special fibre is the disjoint union of two copies of ℙ¹_𝔽₅, so the stated conclusion fails for this literal connected-generic-fibre formulation. This product construction strengthens H's finite-cover example to meet the dimension condition; it does not establish that every other hypothesis in a repaired geometrically connected version is sufficient.

### N05. Formal equidimensionality is explicitly conjectural in the reduction

**File/scan:** 58:25, second half and margin. **Kind:** self-flagged: unverified step. **Context search:** N05; H, “Les fibres spéciales,” their p. 24, adds excellence/formal-equidimensionality discussion rather than deriving the reduction from the written assumptions. **RS:** R0.

The reduction is labelled “facile (?)” and the margin says “conjecture” beside the passage to a formally equidimensional local base. This is a real gap in the reduction as presented, even if a theorem with stronger hypotheses is available. A proof must specify an allowed alteration or base change, show that it preserves the required connectedness assertion, and justify the subsequent dimension formula; “quotient of a regular ring,” excellence, and universal catenarity are different possible inputs and should not be silently substituted for one another.

**File/scan:** 58:25 and 27, displayed dimension equality and first paragraph; 58:26 is intervening reused typescript. **Kind:** reading question / unproved claim. **Context search:** N06; H, “Les fibres spéciales,” their pp. 24–26, deliberately reindexes the dimension count. **RS:** R0.

## II. Definitions, test objects, and the first applications

### N12. Weak comparison, comparison on every neighbourhood, and effectivity

**File/scan:** 58:33. **Kind:** reading question / unpursued implication. **Context search:** N12; H, “Définitions et premières remarques,” their p. 32, systematises these distinctions. **RS:** P58, statement-level intake only.

The page distinguishes “LG faible” on X from LG on every open neighbourhood U of Y, and then adds algebraisation of formal vector bundles on some such U. Published SGA 2 X §2 uses Lef and Leff for the neighbourhood comparison and its effective version (Grothendieck, 1968/2005). The rewritten effective definition has a damaged or omitted name in M, so the later “stricte” and “effective” terminology may explain its intended role without repairing the line.

### N13. The redundant endpoint: proved for n=0, less clear in general

**File/scan:** 58:33–34, “Inutile,” Remarque 1 and insertion. **Kind:** self-flagged question / unproved claim. **Context search:** N13; H, “Définitions et premières remarques,” their pp. 32–33, supplies a separate projective-over-affine argument. **RS:** R0.

The extension-splitting argument proves the stated n=0 implication from H⁰ comparison to injectivity on H¹, because an extension of locally free modules remains locally free. The general question is answered “Oui” with a cramped reference to a “résolution injective,” but an injective sheaf need not belong to the locally free class quantified in the hypothesis. H's argument using an embedding in sufficiently positive twists and dimension shifting addresses a more specific projective setting; it should be retained as H's proposed repair, not presented as a proof of the completely general question written here.

### N14. A generating family and the two endpoints of the five-lemma argument

**File/scan:** 58:34–35. **Kind:** self-flagged step, subsequently developed. **Context search:** N14; H, “Définitions et premières remarques,” their pp. 33–34, restates the generating-family proof. **RS:** P58, relevant proof technology but not a proof of permanence by itself.

The argument needs an embedding F→L with locally free quotient G, then separate surjectivity in degree n and injectivity in degree n+1; the correction from “bijective” to “surjective (donc bijective)” records precisely that distinction. The doubtful induction bound on 58:34 is not silently corrected, and the generating-family condition must be checked wherever this device is reused.

### N15. Neighbourhood stability of weak LG is withdrawn and qualified

**File/scan:** 58:36–37. **Kind:** withdrawn in the same text. **Context search:** N15; H, “Définitions et premières remarques,” their pp. 35–36, follows the revised discussion. **RS:** R0.

### N16. Associated points are excluded; depth two is still a stronger question

**File/scan:** 58:33, margin; 58:37, lemma. **Kind:** self-flagged question / unpursued implication. **Context search:** N16; H, “Définitions et premières remarques,” their pp. 32 and 36, includes both the marginal question and the associated-point lemma. **RS:** L58, a related depth theme, not an established identification.

### N17. A possible local-cohomology route to the stronger depth question

**File/scan:** 58:33, 36–37, read with 58:56–62. **Kind:** own idea. **Context search:** N17; H, “Définitions et premières remarques,” their pp. 32–36, separates the associated-point argument from “Les questions, et la dualité,” their pp. 55–58; no completed solution to this exact marginal question was identified in BT or BN. **RS:** L58, possible methodological connection only. **Confidence:** medium, because the needed localisation and twisting arguments are not supplied here.

One possible route is to translate comparison on every neighbourhood into vanishing of H⁰ and H¹ with support in closed sets disjoint from Y, using the local-cohomology sequence for sufficiently many twists. If the requisite localisation and global-generation steps hold, those two vanishings would imply the desired depth bound. This is a proof programme rather than a result: extending sections of arbitrary vector bundles and passing from global supports to a particular local ring are the two steps that need independent justification.

### N19. From sections to finite étale algebras

**File/scan:** 58:38–39. **Kind:** unpursued implication / unproved claim. **Context search:** N19; H, “Composantes connexes, groupe fondamental, groupe de Picard,” their pp. 37–38, expands this argument. **RS:** LM6 F.6, Open in the return, only as possible geometric input.

The manuscript passes from “structures d'algèbre” on a completed vector bundle to fully faithful restriction of finite étale covers. The mechanism is concrete: multiplication, unit, commutativity, and morphism identities are tensor-Hom data, so full faithfulness can descend them, while étaleness of an algebraised finite locally free algebra has to hold on a sufficiently small neighbourhood. This explains a possible input to geometric π₁ arguments, but does not settle the arithmetic section-sufficiency and number-field reduction left open in LM6 F.6.

### N20. The neighbourhood limit is essential; purity is promised later

**File/scan:** 58:38–39. **Kind:** unproved claim / self-flagged postponement. **Context search:** N20; H, “Composantes connexes…,” their pp. 37–38, preserves the inverse limit; BT 44:4 uses “Lefschetz” for a different restriction theorem. **RS:** LM6 F.6, Open; WG2 §2.4 #4, unchecked planning question.

Strict LG gives the displayed comparison with lim_U π₁(U), not immediately an isomorphism with π₁(X). “Nous verrons plus loin” promises conditions from purity under which π₁(U)→π₁(X) becomes bijective, correcting the earlier word “surjectif.” Neither a hypothesis-free π₁ isomorphism nor the missing purity theorem should be read into this packet's statement.

### N21. Two distinct stages in Picard restriction

**File/scan:** 58:40–41, proposition and corollary. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context search:** N21; H, “Composantes connexes…,” their pp. 39–40, restates the two stages; BT 44:4, Theorem 6, concerns a substantially stronger cycle-theoretic restriction problem. **RS:** WG2 §2.4 #4 and §2.3 #12, unchecked planning items.

LG first controls Pic(U)→Pic(X_/Y), with a colimit over neighbourhoods, while the vanishings of H¹(Y,Λ⁻ⁿ) and H²(Y,Λ⁻ⁿ) concern passage from the formal neighbourhood to Y itself. The marginal instruction “introduire aussi les Y_n” identifies the missing finite-thickening framework for the latter stage. This supplies a concrete Picard tool for comparison with the Jacobian programme, but neither identifies arbitrary-codimension Pⁱ nor proves its fixed-factor assertion.

### N22. Picard-group comparison does not imply categorical faithfulness

**File/scan:** 58:41, lemma (i)–(ii). **Kind:** own idea, verifying a context diagnosis. **Context search:** N22; H, “Composantes connexes…,” their p. 40, corrects “pl. fidèle” and “équiv. de catégories” to assertions about isomorphism classes. **RS:** R0. **Confidence:** high, by an explicit automorphism.

Following H's objection, take X=Spec k[[t]] and Y=Spec k, with ideal (t): every positive ideal layer has vanishing H¹ and H² on Y, but the distinct automorphisms 1 and 1+t of the trivial formal line bundle restrict to the same automorphism on Y. Thus the written H¹/H² hypotheses do not make restriction faithful, whether one considers the groupoid of line bundles or the category with all module homomorphisms. Both Picard groups in this example are zero, so it leaves the intended Picard-group assertion intact. SGA 2 XI 1.1 explicitly keeps that distinction (Grothendieck, 1968/2005); the missing information concerns infinitesimal morphisms, not merely obstruction classes.

### N23. Is the H¹ condition cancelled in part (ii)?

**File/scan:** 58:40, corollary (ii). **Kind:** reading question. **Context search:** N23; H, “Composantes connexes…,” their p. 39, retains H¹ together with H². **RS:** R0.

M marks as uncertain whether “H¹(Y,Λ⁻ⁿ)” in part (ii) is deleted, while H keeps it in the modern statement. H² controls successive existence of liftings, whereas an isomorphism of Picard groups also needs uniqueness information, so there is a mathematical reason for H's choice. That reason does not decide whether a stroke on the page is a cancellation, and the doubtful state of the term remains part of the frozen evidence.

## III. Permanence and the punctured-local comparison

### N26. A derived route to the cohomological permanence assertion

**File/scan:** 58:42(i), 58:43 Corollary 1. **Kind:** unproved claim; context proof proposal. **Context search:** N26; H, “Permanence,” their pp. 41–42, supplies its own perfect-complex argument. **RS:** P58, partial mathematical analysis, not a novelty verdict.

The manuscript gives no proof of part (i), “un isom pour i≤n, un mono. pour i=n+1,” while H proposes representing Rf_*F′ by a bounded complex of vector bundles in nonnegative degrees and transferring (LG)_n term by term. This is a useful route, provided the corrected flatness hypothesis, proper support, a suitable global representative, and compatibility of proper formal comparison with that complex have all been justified. Those requirements explain the hypotheses' roles and the link to the test-object argument at 58:34–35; H's sketch should not be mistaken for a proof written on 58:42 or for a proof of part (iii).

### N27. Effectivity is stronger than the perfect-complex comparison

**File/scan:** 58:42(iii)–43. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context search:** N27; H, “Permanence,” their pp. 41–42, explicitly says it does not verify (iii). **RS:** P58, strongest effective extension still requiring a proof audit.

The text's “existe un voisinage ouvert U de Y” asserts algebraisation of a coherent formal module, flat over the completed base, on f⁻¹(U). A comparison of cohomology or Hom groups does not itself construct that algebraic module, and the scope of the preceding proper-support hypothesis in this newly quantified formal-object clause needs clarification. Even after algebraisation, passing from a formal vector bundle to a vector bundle over all f⁻¹(U) requires removing the non-locally-free locus by a justified neighbourhood argument, using properness where it is available.

### N28. Projective formal schemes and faithful coherent modules

**File/scan:** 58:43, Corollary 2 and margin. **Kind:** unproved claim / conjectural extension. **Context search:** N28; H, “Permanence,” their p. 42, repeats the formal-scheme statement and its marginal extension. **RS:** P58, related but stronger categorical assertion.

The page asserts full faithfulness for projective flat schemes and, under strict LG, existence of an algebraic projective flat model, then suggests generalising to schemes “munis de faisceaux coh. rel. plats et fidèles.” A proof must descend the relevant algebra or graded algebra, its relations, and projectivity data, rather than only an underlying coherent sheaf. The faithful-module margin may indicate a way to package those data, but its short wording does not specify a category or supply the necessary multiplication and descent checks.

### N29. Proper support and shrinking on the base

**File/scan:** 58:42–43. **Kind:** own idea. **Context search:** N29; H, “Permanence,” their p. 42, uses local freeness near the formal locus in its corollary discussion. **RS:** P58, proof obligation. **Confidence:** high for the stated proper case, by the behaviour of a closed nonfree locus under a proper map.

For the projective corollary, suppose an algebraised coherent sheaf is locally free along f⁻¹(Y), and let B be its closed nonfree locus. Since f is proper, f(B) is closed and misses Y, so deleting f(B) yields a base neighbourhood U with local freeness on all f⁻¹(U). This supplies the precise shrinking step suggested by H for that corollary; it does not justify the same step for an arbitrary quasi-projective nonproper f or repair an unspecified support condition in part (iii).

### N30. Omitting quasi-projectivity is an expectation, with an uncertain exception

**File/scan:** 58:44. **Kind:** conjecture / reading question. **Context search:** N30; H, “Permanence,” their p. 43, converts the final qualification into a smooth exception clause. **RS:** P58, broader conjectural range.

“Il est possible” introduces omission of quasi-projectivity of X and X′, but the end of the remark is weakly legible and is recorded as “sans doute non plus dans” Corollary 2(ii). H interprets that as a possible exception for existence of an algebraic scheme; the direction and exact scope of the qualification remain a reading question. A modern extension would have to replace the global vector-bundle constructions used in the preceding proof strategy and separately address algebraisation of projective formal objects.

### N31. Essential flatness and properness: examples are not supplied

**File/scan:** 58:44. **Kind:** unproved claim / withdrawn in the same text. **Context search:** N31; H, “Permanence,” their p. 43, restates essentiality but supplies no explicit examples there. **RS:** P58, hypothesis testing still needed.

### N79. The off-Y′ Hom assertion is a checked consequence of Raynaud's comparison

**File/scan:** 58:46–48, final Hom/section assertion. **Kind:** own idea: literature deduction, independently checked in this run. **Context search:** N79; H, “Une forme locale,” their pp. 45–47; Hua findings register L58; the supplied context contains no such Raynaud derivation. **RS:** L58, supplied as candidate; assessment here: the precise Hom claim below follows from a published comparison, without settling effectivity. **Confidence:** high under the explicitly stated completeness and closed-point conventions.

Let $(A,\mathfrak m)$ be a noetherian local quotient of a regular noetherian ring, separated and complete for $(t)$, with $t\in\mathfrak m$, $P=\operatorname{Spec}A-\{\mathfrak m\}$, $D=V(t)\cap P$, and let G be coherent with depth at least two at every point **closed in P** and outside D; then $\operatorname{Hom}_P(F,G)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat P}(\widehat F,\widehat G)$ is an isomorphism for every coherent F. The elementary closure-dimension and depth-localisation argument in `records/local_hom_inference.md` verifies the n=2 hypotheses of Raynaud (1974, I.1.1 and I.1.3, pp. 31–32), applied to $\mathcal Hom(F,G)$. In the only nontrivial intermediate case, a two-dimensional local domain admits a height-one specialization avoiding t, and depth at that specialization forces depth at least one at its generic point (The Stacks Project Authors, 2026, Tag 0FCC). This is our checked literature inference supporting Hua's target-only refinement, not a statement that the manuscript's dévissage is complete or that its revised essential image and algebraisation assertions follow.
