## 1. Initial objects exclude weak and local test categories

**Verdict: the elementary argument is valid for the ordinary weak-homotopy localizer.** Let \(0\) be initial in \(A\), and let \(X:A^{op}\to\mathrm{Set}\). Define a functor from the category of elements \(A/X\) to the discrete category \(X(0)\) by
\[
(a,x)\longmapsto X(0\to a)(x).
\]
Every morphism preserves this value. The full fiber over \(t\in X(0)\) has initial object \((0,t)\): the unique arrow \(0\to a\) is a morphism of elements precisely when \(X(0\to a)(x)=t\). Therefore \(A/X\) is a disjoint union of contractible categories indexed by \(X(0)\), and its nerve has the weak homotopy type of the discrete set \(X(0)\). This includes the empty case. Thus no image of the category-of-elements functor has the homotopy type of a circle, so its localization cannot be essentially surjective onto all homotopy types, as required of a weak test category.

For every \(a\in A\), the slice \(A/a\) has initial object \(0\to a\); the same argument excludes weak-test status for every such slice and therefore excludes local-test status. Since \([0]\) is terminal in \(\Delta\), it is initial in \(\Delta^{op}\). The argument does not claim that categories with initial objects fail every possible relative \(W\)-test notion; it uses the usual weak homotopy types.

There is also a direct check against the asphericity definition of weak test, requiring no appeal to essential surjectivity. Take \(D=[1]\), which has a terminal object, and \(X=i_A^*D\). The set \(X(0)=\operatorname{Fun}(A/0,[1])\) contains the two distinct constant functors. Consequently \(A/X\) has at least two connected components and is not aspherical. For definitions, see E. Cavallo and C. Sattler, [*Relative elegance and Cartesian cubes with one connection*](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008414X25101466), Definitions 7.9, 7.11, and 7.12, explicitly working with the ordinary weak-homotopy localizer; Definition 7.12 cites Cisinski, Définitions 4.1.8 and 4.1.12. The argument in this paragraph is an independent application of those definitions.

## 2. Cote 113, scan 004: enough projectives versus ultraprojective generators

**Verdict: the missing compact/ultraprojective generation hypothesis is genuine, not automatic. Under the usual meaning of “enough projectives,” the unrestricted assertion has a primary-source counterexample.** A Grothendieck category with enough projectives has a projective generator: choose a projective object mapping epimorphically onto a generator. This does not by itself make that projective object compact or make compact projectives generate. In an additive presheaf category on a small preadditive category, the representable functors form a small family of compact projective generators; that property is invariant under equivalence.

Primary comparison: Manuel Saorín and Jan Šťovíček, *t-Structures with Grothendieck hearts via functor categories*, Selecta Mathematica 29, article 77 (2023), §5 opening, DOI [10.1007/s00029-023-00872-9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00872-9). The authors describe module categories as \(\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal S\) for a **set** of small projective generators, and then explicitly state that Grothendieck categories with enough projectives need not be module categories. Their terminology therefore includes many-object rings.

Their cited original construction is S. Bazzoni, I. Herzog, P. Příhoda, J. Šaroch, J. Trlifaj, [*Pure Projective Tilting Modules*](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/26531), Documenta Mathematica 25 (2020), 401–424. Proposition 6.2 constructs a pure-projective 1-tilting module \(T\). Corollary 4.5 (p. 412) makes its HRS heart \(\mathcal H\) Grothendieck; the paragraph immediately preceding that corollary identifies \(T[0]\) as a projective generator. Theorem 6.3 and the example \(R=U(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C))\), pp. 420–421, show that \(T\) is not tilting equivalent even to a direct sum of finitely presented modules. The example uses the augmentation ideal generated by \(h,e,f\); \(R\) is right noetherian.

**Independent bridge checking the many-object issue.** For any right-noetherian \(R\) and pure-projective 1-tilting \(T\), every projective object of its HRS heart is \(P[0]\) with \(P\in\operatorname{Add}(T)\). Such a \(P\) is pure projective as an \(R\)-module, hence a retract of \(\bigoplus_j M_j\) with the \(M_j\) finitely presented. Let \(t\) be the torsion radical for \(\operatorname{Gen}(T)\). Applying \(t\) to the retraction exhibits \(P\) as a retract of \(\bigoplus_j t(M_j)\); torsion radicals in a module category preserve direct sums. Each \(t(M_j)\) is finitely presented because \(R\) is right noetherian. These stalks lie in the heart and their direct sum is also their coproduct there. If \(P[0]\) is compact in the heart, its splitting map into that coproduct factors through a finite subsum. Thus \(P\) itself is finitely presented over \(R\).

If a family \(\{P_\lambda[0]\}\) of compact projectives generated \(\mathcal H\), projectivity of \(T[0]\) would split a generating epimorphism onto it. This gives
\[
\operatorname{Add}(T)=\operatorname{Add}\!\left(\bigoplus_\lambda P_\lambda\right),
\]
where every \(P_\lambda\) is finitely presented by the preceding argument. That contradicts the cited example's conclusion. Hence this is a counterexample to a **small family** of compact projective generators, not just to one compact generator.

**Suggested editorial status:** “False with ordinary projectives in this generality; a small generating family of compact projectives supplies the usual sufficient replacement.” Preserve the frozen wording. If the manuscript intends “projective” here as shorthand for an already strengthened ultraprojective notion, that is a separate terminological interpretation to record, not something to insert silently into the transcription.

## 3. Cote 115, scan 014: a caution about ordinary categorical pushouts

**Verdict: do not silently identify an envelope or an ambient full-subcategory union with a pushout in \(\mathrm{Cat}\).** A minimal example already shows the issue: take ambient \(M=[1]\), with full one-object subcategories \(M_1=\{0\}\) and \(M_2=\{1\}\), and empty intersection \(N\). Their pushout \(M_1\amalg_N M_2\) is the discrete two-object category. Its canonical functor into \(M\) is not full, because it misses \(0\to1\). By contrast the full subcategory of \(M\) on the union of their objects is all of \(M\). This is an elementary counterexample to an unrestricted ordinary-pushout interpretation, not a refutation of the frozen page's unresolved/uncertain construction. Any proposed universal property for its \(\Sigma'\) or \(E(A)\) must be stated separately and must account for cross morphisms and ambient relations.
