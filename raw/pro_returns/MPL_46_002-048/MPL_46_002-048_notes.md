## II. Twisted torsors, Lang's theorem, and unit Frobenius modules

**Kind:** reading question / unpursued implication. **File/scan:** 46:005–007, marked-object insertion and automorphisms. **State:** LM1_GROUP_SCHEMES_rerun_20261112 A.9/B.8, concrete comparison only.

### N12 — The noncommutative simplification needs qualification

**Kind:** own verification. **File/scan:** 46:006, two consecutive displays. **State:** no direct entry; LM1_GROUP_SCHEMES_rerun_20261112 B.8 as a limited equalizer comparison.

The displayed equation “u a f(g)=b f(ug)” gives $b=uaf(u)^{-1}$, whereas the following “b^{-1}a=f(u)u^{-1}” is not that equation for an arbitrary noncommutative group. The latter is valid after commutative rearrangement, and the neutral-object case still yields the expected Lang-map condition and fixed subgroup. Hua already points out this noncommutative correction and the neutral-object argument in the subsection cited in N11, and both are independently checked here; the discrepancy remains in the diplomatic text. **Confidence: high**, because substitution at g=1 and cancellation give the first identity directly; this diagnoses the local algebra, not a refutation of the gerbe conclusion.

### N16 — Finite presentation makes local freeness automatic

**Kind:** own verification answering a manuscript question. **File/scan:** 46:009, final paragraph; compare 008's isomorphism $E^{(p)}\to E$. **State:** no directly matching settled entry; WG2 §1.1 #3; methodological link to context 16:052.

For a finitely presented module M over any characteristic-p ring R, an isomorphism $F_R^*M\simeq M$ implies finite local freeness: base change of Fitting ideals gives $I_i=I_i^{[p]}$, where $I_i=\operatorname{Fitt}_i(M)$ and $I^{[p]}$ is generated by p-th powers. Each I_i is finitely generated, and at a local ring a proper I_i satisfies $I_i^{[p]}\subseteq\mathfrak m I_i$, so Nakayama makes it zero; the Fitting criterion then gives freeness locally. This answers part of “les hypothèses de locale liberté et de type fini” and connects concretely to the Fitting/base-change discussion in context 16:052 (§2 nos. 1–2; notes 16:52:1, 16:52:4, R14), without attributing the proof to that sheet. **Confidence: high**, by this explicit argument and an independent proof check; The Stacks Project Authors (2026a, Lemmas 15.8.4,15.8.8; 2026b, Lemma 51.17.1) supply the Fitting facts and the Noetherian local finite-module version, respectively.

### N17 — Finite generation alone admits a counterexample

**Kind:** own verification. **File/scan:** 46:009, same question. **State:** no directly matching settled entry; same methodological context as N16.

Let $R=\mathbf F_p[t^{1/p^n}:n\geq0]$, $I=(t^{1/p^n}:n\geq0)$, and $M=R/I\cong\mathbf F_p$; then $I=I^{[p]}$ gives $F_R^*M\cong M$, although M is cyclic and not locally free at I. Indeed, R is a domain and the nonzero element t kills the nonzero module $M_I$, while $I=\bigcup_n(t^{1/p^n})$ is a strictly increasing union, explaining why M is not finitely presented. This sharpens the question in Hua's Frobenius-vector-bundle subsection and complements the finite-presentation result rather than conflicting with it. **Confidence: high**, because all assertions follow directly from the displayed ring and ideal; a detailed proof is retained in `records/research/frobenius_module_check.md`.

### N18 — The remaining infinite-rank question is not settled by these examples

**Kind:** own idea / scope boundary. **File/scan:** 46:009, classification by $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbf F_p)$. **State:** no direct entry; WG2 §1.1 #3.

The results in N16–N17 distinguish finite presentation from finite generation, but do not classify all quasi-coherent modules with invertible Frobenius linearization or all possible infinite-rank local systems. The chosen Frobenius isomorphism remains part of the classification data, and fixed-rank GL(n) statements require rank n or separation into rank strata. **Confidence: high** in this limitation, since neither the Fitting argument nor the cyclic counterexample proves an infinite-rank descent theorem; context 16:052 and Hua's discussion supply methods and a question, not that theorem.

**Kind:** unpursued implication / proof comparison. **File/scan:** 46:011, proposition and proof. **State:** no direct entry; WG2 §1.1 #3, programme-level relation.

### N20 — Frobenius powers and ordinary ideal powers must be distinguished

**Kind:** own verification / reading question. **File/scan:** 46:011, final ideal calculation. **State:** no direct entry; context 16:052 supplies an adjacent finite-generation method.

The uncertain “$\mathcal J^p$” describing the Frobenius inverse image is naturally compared with $\mathcal J^{[p]}$, the ideal generated by p-th powers, which need not equal the ordinary power $\mathcal J^p$. For example, in $\mathbf F_2[x,y]$ with $J=(x,y)$, xy belongs to $J^2$ but not $(x^2,y^2)=J^{[2]}$. The intended conclusion still follows from finite generation and $J=J^{[p]}$ by local Nakayama, so this is a notation/proof clarification rather than a counterexample to the criterion; Hua's Frobenius-criterion subsection is the direct context comparison. **Confidence: high**, by the explicit ideal calculation and the same local argument used in N16.

**Kind:** own verification / context disagreement. **File/scan:** 46:018, replacement proof. **State:** 46-homeomorphism-affine.

## IV. Picard p-torsion, Cartier, restricted Lie data, and Verschiebung

### N30 — Global units explain the first Picard identification

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **File/scan:** 46:020, first exact sequence and A. **State:** WG2 §1.1 #3; no directly matching settled entry.

The margin “X réduit et propre sur k parfait” identifies the assumptions used to make the finite reduced k-algebra of global functions perfect and eliminate the cokernel of p-th powers on its units. Thus the first identification of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)[p]$ with global sections of the Zariski quotient sheaf $\mathcal O_X^*/\mathcal O_X^{*p}$ is separated from the later use of normality. Hua's *La p-torsion du groupe de Picard* is the direct comparison, while context 16:019,021 treats the other prime regime through Picard/NS and Kummer–Tate sequences.

### N31 — The normal-case injectivity question is answered on the continuation

**Kind:** self-flagged, subsequently addressed. **File/scan:** 46:020, “Cet hom est-il injectif?”; 021, opening. **State:** no direct settled entry; WG2 §1.1 #3.

Normality makes a rational p-th root of a regular function regular by integrality, and for a unit the inverse root is regular as well, completing the opening continuation's dlog injectivity argument. Oda (1969, p. 126, immediately before Theorem 5.10) states the corresponding normal-case kernel of dlog as the image of Frobenius on the multiplicative sheaf. This independently supports the argument already recorded by Hua's Picard subsection, without settling the separate converse at the bottom of scan 021.

### N32 — The final quotient on scan 021 is noninjective as literally written

**Kind:** own verification. **File/scan:** 46:021, final display. **State:** no direct entry; WG2 §1.1 #3.

The asserted injection starts from “$\mathcal R_X^*/\underline O_X^*$”, but rational p-th powers already map to zero under the logarithmic derivative. In the regular local ring $\mathbf F_p[t]_{(t)}$, the nonunit rational function $t^p$ gives a nonzero class modulo units and zero logarithmic differential, so that displayed map is not injective. The proposed factorization $f=g^ph$ instead points to the quotient by $\mathcal O_X^*\mathcal R_X^{*p}$, as Hua's Picard discussion also recognizes. **Confidence: high**, by this explicit regular-ring example; it identifies an omitted quotient factor, not a counterexample to the corrected local problem.

### N33 — Normality does not justify the stated reduction to regularity

**Kind:** unproved claim. **File/scan:** 46:021, final line. **State:** no direct settled entry; WG2 §1.1 #3.

“on est ramené au cas où X est … régulier” is not accompanied by an argument extending a local regular-locus solution across the singular set. Oda (1969, Definition 5.5(iii), pp. 121–122) proves the logarithmic fixed-form criterion under local p-base hypotheses, such as smoothness over a perfect field, and p. 125 warns that rational Cartier need not preserve regular forms on a nonsmooth normal scheme. These precise printed comparisons leave the normal-to-regular reduction in Hua's Picard discussion open; a putative divisor-class counterexample must also specify whether it uses ordinary Kähler or reflexive differentials.

### N34 — The smooth proper case has a precise differential conclusion

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **File/scan:** 46:020–021, “Cω=ω”. **State:** WG2 §1.1 #3; compare context 16:019,021.

For smooth proper X over a perfect field, the global-units argument and Oda's local logarithmic criterion identify $\operatorname{Pic}(X)[p]$ with the additive group of global closed one-forms fixed by Cartier (Oda, 1969, Definition 5.5(iii), pp. 121–122). This gives a characteristic-p differential counterpart to the prime-to-characteristic Picard/Kummer discussion in the older context. It is a smooth-case consequence, not a proof of the manuscript's more general normal-case converse.
