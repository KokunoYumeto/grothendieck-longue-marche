## Frobenius proof: hypotheses and boundary

Both observations in frobenius_module_check.md are correct. Assume a commutative ring R with p = 0 for a prime p, a finitely presented module M, and an isomorphism of R-modules \(F_R^*M\simeq M\). Fitting base change gives \(I=I^{[p]}\); finite presentation supplies finite generation of I. At a local ring, a proper such ideal lies in the maximal ideal and satisfies \(I=I^{[p]}\subseteq\mathfrak m I\), so Nakayama gives I = 0. The adjacent Fitting ideals 0 and R give freeness, including rank zero; their finite generation allows this conclusion on a neighbourhood. No Noetherian, reduced, perfect, connected or Frobenius-flat hypothesis is missing. This is an editorial deduction using the Fitting properties, not the verbatim assertion of Stacks Lemma 51.17.1, whose stated assumptions are Noetherian and local.

The counterexample \(R=\mathbb F_p[t^{1/p^n}:n\geq0]\), \(I=(t^{1/p^n}:n\geq0)\), M = R/I is valid even though R is perfect. The ideal is Frobenius-stable, maximal and not finitely generated; M is cyclic but not finitely presented. Its nonzero localization at I is annihilated by nonzero t in the domain R_I, so it cannot be free there. This explicitly avoids relying on a global torsion observation alone. For scan 009, the conclusion is precisely that finite presentation can replace finite local freeness, whereas finite type alone cannot on arbitrary bases. On locally Noetherian schemes the finite-type quasi-coherent case is locally finitely presented. A chosen Frobenius-linearization isomorphism and rank n remain data of the fixed GL(n) classification. Neither observation settles every possible deletion of the finite-type hypothesis. The old-cote-16 scan 052 Fitting discussion (notes 16:52:1, 16:52:4, R14) is a method link, not evidence of the proof's presence there.
