## Literature access and use

The comparisons above concern the particular assertions identified in each note; they are not a claim to have exhaustively searched all literature or established that any manuscript idea is new. The following access distinctions were used.

| Source | Material inspected and role in these notes |
|---|---|
| Cartan (1976) | Publisher metadata and bibliographical confirmation; the complete original article was not obtained for argument-level comparison. |
| Miller (1978) | Bibliographical entry verified in the primary Cenkl and Cenkl–Porter literature and an AMS publication bibliography; the complete original paper was not read. |
| Cenkl & Porter (1982) | Official journal-hosted opening page/description, including the bigraded functor and the integration-map qualification; used for the nearby Cartan–Miller comparison, not as a substitute for checking an isomorphism with the manuscript. |
| Cenkl (1989) | Primary paper, introduction, coefficient setup, main comparison, and §5, Theorem 2; used with its stated hypotheses. |
| Dwyer (1979) | Publisher metadata; its detailed model-category theorem was not independently checked in the original article. |
| Scheerer & Tanré (1991) | Primary paper’s abstract and introduction, used to delimit the tame comparison lead. |
| Ekedahl (2002) | Author’s arXiv abstract and journal first-page material; used for the stated scope of numerical models, not a full reconstruction of the proof. |
| Mandell (2006) | Primary publication record and abstract; the classification, faithfulness, and nonfullness distinctions are used at that level. |
| Toën (2020) | Published PDF, introduction and Theorem 0.1, including the Hilbert additive group and the stated comparison limits. |
| Horel (2024) | Author-hosted paper, introduction, statements concerning completion, and §8, especially Remark 8.5; used to distinguish binomial models from ordinary affinization. |
| Hatcher (2002) | Author-hosted textbook, Theorem 3.19, for the explicit projective-space cohomology ring in the counterexample. |
| Stacks Project | Official sections on Tate resolutions and Koszul regular sequences, including the warning about divided powers of boundaries; used as precise modern formulations, not as evidence of the manuscript’s intended wording. |
