## Uniform finite-erasure proofs

Set R'=nR, M=product P, Q_v=(1-e_P)R', T_v=e_P R'. Enlarge one finite S to contain P,3,bad reduction, splitting ramification, vertical function divisors, and representatives of the finitely many global mod2 characters. At v outside S the P-primary local subgroup is finite prime-to-residue torsion, Q_v is nontorsion, and the hypothesized Selmer section is forced by full compact Kummer injectivity to have point centre Q_v. All the relevant unit evaluations therefore have even valuation outside S.

Consider R_j=[M^(2j)]R'. If R_j reduces to d in E[3], primary decomposition gives Q_v reducing to d0 in E[3] and [M^(2j)]T_v=d1 in E[3], with

 d=M^(2j)d0+d1, R_j-d=[M^(2j)](Q_v-d0).

If 3 notin P, then d1=0 because it is both P-primary and 3-torsion. Since M²=1 mod3, d=d0. Thus if R_j approaches a cusp in the J1 block, Q_v approaches the SAME cusp with the same formal distance. At each cusp at least one of the two V4 unit characters has odd order3, so its even valuation forces that distance even. Fix one f_ij. Its values at all R_j therefore have even valuations outside the same S. They lie in finitely many S-squareclasses, and the resulting quadratic twists of w²=f_ij have genus2. Faltings contradicts infinitely many R_j. This proves (1).

For Gamma4 use f=1-J alone. If 3 notin P, the same argument applies whenever R_j approaches its zero/pole divisor, because Q_v approaches the same cusp there. If 3 in P, then Q_v has no3-primary component. Its 3-torsion reduction d0 is consequently O. Whenever R_j approaches the divisor of f, Q_v approaches O with the SAME formal distance; f has odd pole order3 at O, so that distance is even. At every R_j, the valuation of f is zero or +/-3 times a distance proved even. All f(R_j) again lie in finitely many S-squareclasses. This time the double covers have six branch points, hence genus4, and Faltings contradicts infinitely many R_j. This proves (2).

The only origin condition used in the X4 argument is the odd pole of f at O. It is supplied by placing the rational flex origin in the infinity block. Over a finite field extension the same proof applies to a twist after choosing such a flex and writing the compact class relative to that origin. It does not automatically transport the stated scalar expression from a different non-torsion origin, nor provide a scalar parametrization of a torsor without a rational point.
