## What the original handwriting actually says

- **168, (358)(a)–(b):** The questions explicitly distinguish each Kummer map being surjective from its image merely being open, and then ask the corresponding joint question for distinct primes. The target at(358) uses the manuscript's older negative twist notation. Equation(359) decomposes by coefficient prime.
- **169, (360)–(364):** The manuscript passes through the power isogenies of the multiplicative group, the local decomposition group and its cyclotomic character, and defines the cyclotomic-kernel part of local inertia. This is a separate local subgroup, not a synonym for the whole global cyclotomic kernel.
- **170, (365):** The written question asks whether the restriction to that local inertia kernel is surjective, or at least has open image. D.3 and D.4 correctly separate the joint global and local questions.
- **171, (366):** The original definition is the projective plus Fermat curve with every coordinate invertible and with homogeneous sum `x0^n+x1^n+x∞^n=0`. The heading is the reflection on Fermat's theorem, and the text says that the affine curve has no rational point. Equation(367) gives the torus quotient by the diagonal multiplicative group; (368) gives its power-isogeny tower.
- **172, (369)–(371):** The genus calculation is followed by the fundamental extension and the distinction between ordinary rational-point splittings and splittings at infinity. The phrase distinguishing splittings at infinity is genuinely present in the handwritten original.
- **173:** The manuscript explicitly discusses proving the *inexistence* of splittings of(371) other than those coming from points at infinity. It then proposes progressively finer approximations to the geometric group and extension. These are questions and a research programme, not a proved assertion.
- **174, (372):** The symmetric-group action is incorporated into a richer extension, after which the manuscript discusses its initial approximation.
- **175, (373)–(374):** The commutative diagrams compare the original arithmetic/symmetric extension with the cyclotomic and abelian approximation. The stated conjugating module is visible in the diagram.
- **176, (375)–(377):** The chosen basepoint/splitting produces semidirect-product descriptions and an extension involving the submodule denoted M-hat[n]. This leads directly to the conjugacy-class question on the following recto.
- **177, (378)–(379):** The original explicitly asks to determine all possible M-hat[n]-conjugacy classes of those splittings, and then to examine which can reasonably correspond to a rational point. The cohomological display(379) follows that question. D.2's interpretation of this as a quotient-level classification question is faithful to the handwriting.
- **178:** The original explicitly says that the approximation under discussion is much too coarse and concludes that finer approximations are necessary. The displayed sum has right side **one**, not zero. The strokes are unambiguous in the supplied rendering.

## The final-page equation

The right side one on ordinal178 is incompatible with the homogeneous definition at(366) on ordinal171 and with a literal equation on the torus modulo simultaneous scaling. It must not silently replace the original homogeneous curve or determine a different arithmetic fundamental group. The current D.2 statement of this inconsistency is supported by the original images, not merely by the transcription.
