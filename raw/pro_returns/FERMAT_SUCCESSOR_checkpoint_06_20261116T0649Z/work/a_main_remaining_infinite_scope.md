# Exact residual scope after A.69–A.71

The main new results classify every finite alteration of every integer at the actual joint compact/X3 quotient, uniformly on rational-origin cubic twists with a nontorsion rational point. A.72's work proof additionally makes every surviving integer fibre a singleton at this quotient. A.65 and A.66 settle substantial infinite-change families for the full abelian Selmer quotient, but they do not make the finite-congruence classification complete for all of Zhat.

The joint-tower argument used in A.69 and A.71 depends essentially on finite coefficient support around0 (or finite changes of a fixed integer). At good residue characteristicq outside that finite support, prescribing finitely many primary components forces an exact torsion local centre. For an arbitrary infinite support, its q-primary component can retain a nontorsion formal part; the same finite Frobenius condition does not identify the full local point. It would be an error to repeat the finite-support proof unchanged.

Fixing the global quadratic flex-unit class gives a genus-two cover z²=cg. The remaining question can be phrased as an intersection of its adelic image inE with the closure ofZR, but this is a relative question about a cover, not automatically an instance of a proved adelic Mordell–Lang theorem for a closed subvariety of an abelian variety. An inference from compact rankone or finite Sha alone is invalid.

## Opened primary sources checked for a possible shortcut

1. Brendan Creutz, *Adelic Mordell-Lang and the Brauer-Manin obstruction*, arXiv:2510.25931. Actual author PDF opened at https://www.math.canterbury.ac.nz/~b.creutz/Papers/AML_BM.pdf . Theorem1.1 and Theorem1.4 on printedpp2–3 assume a GLOBAL FUNCTION FIELD and no nonzero geometrically isotrivial quotient of the abelian variety. The number-field analogues in§7 are conditional on an adelic Mordell–Lang conjecture. The introductory discussion onp3 explicitly retains that conjecture over number fields. These results do not supply the needed unconditional theorem overQ.

2. Michael Stoll, *Finite descent obstructions and rational points on curves*, revised primary PDF reopened at https://www.mathe2.uni-bayreuth.de/stoll/papers/covers-revised.pdf . The rank-zero target hypotheses remain essential in the proved excellence theorem. The finite-support theorem8.2 cannot be used before an actual fixed finite global subscheme supporting the local points has been proved.

3. Qing Liu and Fei Xu, *Very strong approximation for certain algebraic varieties*, primary arXiv:1405.1988v2 opened at https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.1988 . Theorem1.2 (=Corollary3.6), printedp2, proves very strong approximation with Brauer–Manin obstruction for a subvariety of a split torus overQ (also imaginary quadratic fields), off a finite setS. Definition1.1 and the preceding definition onpp1–2 use actual RESTRICTED-PRODUCT adelic points. The punctured cubic is very affine, but the unrestricted local Selmer centres may meet the boundary modulo infinitely many primes. Thus this theorem applies to a finite-S situation, not automatically to the entire Selmer family.

The last distinction is concrete already for Gm. If p_j is an enumeration of rational primes, the completed multiplicative class product p_j^(j!) is well defined in the profinite completion ofQ×: modulo every fixed exponent only finitely many terms are nontrivial. At every finiteq it has an ordinary integer valuation and a convergent local unit part, hence is locally represented by an element ofQq×. Yet its valuations are nonzero at infinitely manyq, so the local family is not an adelic point ofGm in the restricted-product sense. This example is a torus scope witness, not a constructed Selmer ghost on the punctured cubic.

No source above proves that the particular remaining cubic problem is open in every possible formulation. They establish only that the proposed shortcuts do not check the required hypotheses. The exact unproved step is still to control arbitrary infinite coefficient changes while retaining the global finite-cover character and all local Selmer conditions.
