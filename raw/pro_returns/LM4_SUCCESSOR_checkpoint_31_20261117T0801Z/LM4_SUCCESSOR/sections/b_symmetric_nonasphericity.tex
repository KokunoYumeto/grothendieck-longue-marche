% B.100 candidate: complete actual words, global winding retraction and sphere.
\statement{B.100}{Refuted}{The actual symmetric complement is not aspherical: its fundamental group retracts onto an explicitly marked copy of $\mathbb Z^3$}
\label{stmt:B100}
Let $Z=X\setminus(A\cup F(A))$ and $\Gamma=\pi_1(Z,P)$ be the
actual complex surface and discrete group of B.89, based at
$P=(399/200,1/200)$.  The proposed use of $Z$ as an aspherical
surface is false.  More precisely, in the actual corrected paired
generic-fiber marking of B.94 put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B100-three-loops}
 a=p_2,\qquad b=p_{12}p_{13}p_{12}^{-1},\qquad
 t=\text{the positive old-$0$ far base lift}.
\end{equation}
Use the common based connectors of B.91--B.94 for all three loops;
the definition does not permit independent conjugations of them.
They generate a subgroup $H\leq\Gamma$ for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B100-retract}
 \mathbb Z^3\xrightarrow[\sim]{\ \iota\ }H\hookrightarrow\Gamma,
 \qquad \rho:\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^3,
 \qquad \rho\iota=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z^3},
\end{equation}
where $\iota(m,n,k)=a^m b^n t^k$ and $\rho$ is the global
three-factor winding map specified below.  There is also a concrete
nonzero class in $\pi_2(Z,P)$: map the three edges and the three
commutator faces of the standard two-skeleton of the three-torus
to these loops and their actual commutator nullhomotopies.  The image
of its remaining three-cell attaching sphere is not nullhomotopic.
This holds for every choice of the three nullhomotopies, and the
resulting class has infinite order.  For the actual universal cover
$Z^{\mathrm{univ}}\to Z$, one also has
$H_2(Z^{\mathrm{univ}};\mathbb F_2)\ne0$.
No nonzero value of the equivariant obstruction $\kappa$ in B.92
is asserted.

\begin{proof}
\emph{B.100.1. The actual commuting words and their physical divisors.}
The corrected paired-basis conversion gives
\[
 p_2=x_4,\qquad p_{12}p_{13}p_{12}^{-1}=x_{16},
\]
where $x_j$ is the original nodal far-cut generator.  In its actual
initial ordering these two generators have physical labels $3,14$,
respectively.  They are the positive meridians of
\[
 A_v(\zeta):v=\zeta,
 \qquad B_v(\zeta^4)=F(A_v(\zeta^4)):
              vw+(\zeta^4-1)v-\zeta^4=0,
 \qquad \zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}.
\]
These are distinct irreducible components of the actual deleted
divisor.  The second polynomial is linear in $w$, with leading
coefficient $v$ and nonzero constant term at $v=0$; hence it is
irreducible.  On a generic fiber $q=vw$ it is
$q+(\zeta^4-1)v-\zeta^4$, whose simple zero gives the same positive
meridian as the cut generator.

The actual node indexed $(6,3)$ has these two based meridians
and the literal paired relator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B100-node}
 [2,12,13,-12,-2,12,-13,-12]_p=[a,b].
\end{equation}
Its filling gives $[a,b]=1$ in $\Gamma$, by the actual local
van Kampen calculation of B.91.1.  The corrected old-$0$ geometric
push satisfies the three literal identities
\[
 \alpha_0(p_2)=p_2,\qquad
 \alpha_0(p_{12})=p_{12},\qquad
 \alpha_0(p_{13})=p_{13}.
\]
The convention $t^{-1}x t=\alpha_0(x)$ therefore gives
$[t,a]=[t,b]=1$ in the same based group.  These statements do
not require the generic fiber to inject after nodal filling.
They are relations in the actual total group obtained from its
based presentation.  In particular \eqref{eq:B100-three-loops}
defines the homomorphism $\iota$ in \eqref{eq:B100-retract}.

\emph{B.100.2. Global winding maps prove independence and splitting.}
The three regular functions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B100-functions}
 f_A=v-\zeta,\qquad
 f_B=vw+(\zeta^4-1)v-\zeta^4,\qquad q=vw
\end{equation}
have no zero on $Z$.  Dividing each by its value at $P$ gives a
based continuous map
\[
 \Psi:(Z,P)\longrightarrow((\mathbb C^*)^3,(1,1,1)).
\]
Identify the fundamental group of the target with $\mathbb Z^3$
by positive complex winding.  This defines the genuine group
homomorphism $\rho=\Psi_*$.  A small positive meridian of a
simple component has winding one for its defining factor and zero
for the other two factors, because they extend nonvanishingly
over its small transverse disk.  Based whiskers do not change
these abelian characters.  Consequently
\[
 \rho(a)=(1,0,0),\qquad \rho(b)=(0,1,0).
\]
For completeness, in the literal old far marking the fiber
coordinate is $v_\infty=10^6/\eta$, where
$\eta=1+(371/1000)i$.  Along the old-$0$ lasso, $f_A$ is constant
and
\[
 f_B=(\zeta^4-1)v_\infty-\zeta^4+q.
\]
The certified lasso lies in $|q|<6$, whereas the modulus of the
constant term in this expression is greater than $10^5$.
Thus this function has winding zero, while $q$ has winding one.
The common connectors contribute a path followed by its reverse
under every winding character.  It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B100-winding-matrix}
 \rho(t)=(0,0,1),\qquad
 \bigl(\rho(a)\ \rho(b)\ \rho(t)\bigr)=I_3.
\end{equation}
This proves \eqref{eq:B100-retract}, including injectivity of
$\iota$, without any presentation-independence assumption.
Even if another common far-lift marking added integers $m,n$
to the first two entries of $\rho(t)$, the matrix would still
have determinant one, and replacing the first two winding
characters by $\operatorname{wind}(f_A)-m\operatorname{wind}(q)$
and $\operatorname{wind}(f_B)-n\operatorname{wind}(q)$ would give
the identical splitting.  The displayed literal marking needs
no such adjustment.

\emph{B.100.3. The actual complex surface has no third homology.}
We check the hypothesis of the dimension theorem explicitly.
With $U=\mu_5\setminus\{1\}$, define the polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B100-D}
 \begin{aligned}
 D(v,w)={}&vw(v-w)(v^5-1)(w^5-1)((vw)^5-1)\\
 &\cdot\prod_{u\in U}
       \bigl(vw+(u-1)v-u\bigr)
       \bigl(vw+(u-1)w-u\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
B.89's surface is exactly $Z=\{D\ne0\}\subset\mathbb C^2$.
The map
\[
 (v,w)\longmapsto(v,w,D(v,w)^{-1})
\]
is a biholomorphic embedding onto the closed hypersurface
$sD(v,w)=1$ in $\mathbb C^3$.  It is smooth: the derivative
of $sD-1$ with respect to $s$ is $D\ne0$ on this hypersurface.
Thus it is a closed complex submanifold of complex dimension two.

\citet[Theorems7.1--7.2, pp.~39--41]{BMilnor1963} proves that
such a manifold has the homotopy type of a CW complex of dimension
at most two.  We checked the complete proof at this application:
a generic squared-distance function is proper on the closed
submanifold and has only nondegenerate critical points; the
complex second fundamental form pairs opposite normal
eigenvalues, so every Morse index is at most the complex
dimension; the resulting handle construction supplies the stated
CW dimension.  We use this theorem on the displayed closed
embedding, not on an unverified compactification.  In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B100-H3}
                         H_3(Z;\mathbb Z)=0.
\end{equation}

\emph{B.100.4. The torus attaching sphere is nonzero.}
Give $\mathbb T^3=(S^1)^3$ its product CW structure, with one
vertex, three edges, three commutator two-cells and one three-cell.
Send its edges to $a,b,t$.  The three actual commutation relations
proved in B.100.1 allow continuous extensions across the three
two-cells.  Denote any resulting based map by
\[
 h:(\mathbb T^3)^{(2)}\longrightarrow Z.
\]
Let $\omega:S^2\to(\mathbb T^3)^{(2)}$ be the attaching map of
the remaining product three-cell.  If $h\omega$ were
nullhomotopic in $Z$, the cell-extension criterion would give a
continuous map $\widetilde h:\mathbb T^3\to Z$ extending $h$.
Its composite with $\Psi$ and the radial deformation retraction
$(\mathbb C^*)^3\to\mathbb T^3$ induces $I_3$ on the three
first-homology generators, by \eqref{eq:B100-winding-matrix}.
The product of their three dual first-cohomology classes is the
oriented generator of $H^3(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb Z)$.
Naturality of cup products therefore makes the composite induce
the identity on third homology.  This is impossible: its third
homology map factors through the zero group in
\eqref{eq:B100-H3}.  Hence $[h\omega]\ne0$ in $\pi_2(Z,P)$.
No compatibility of the chosen nullhomotopies is assumed; their
failure to extend over the three-cell is precisely the conclusion.

\emph{B.100.5. The universal cover is not even mod-two acyclic.}
Choose the two-dimensional CW model $K\simeq Z$ from B.100.3.
The homotopy equivalence and its inverse lift to homotopy inverses
on the universal covers, by lifting their homotopies.  Covering
invariance of higher homotopy and the degree-two Hurewicz theorem
give
\[
 \pi_2(Z,P)\cong\pi_2(K^{\mathrm{univ}})
        \cong H_2(K^{\mathrm{univ}};\mathbb Z);
\]
we checked \citet[Proposition4.1, p.~342, and Theorem4.32 with
its proof, pp.~366--367]{BHatcher2002} at this use.
There are no three-cells, so this last group is the kernel of
the cellular boundary from the free abelian two-chain group.
It is torsion-free, which proves the infinite-order assertion.
Take a nonzero finite integral two-cycle and divide all its
coefficients by their largest common power of two.  The resulting
chain is still a cycle: its boundary multiplied by that power
of two is zero in the torsion-free one-chain group.  Its reduction
modulo two is nonzero.  Since there are no three-cells, it cannot
be a boundary over $\mathbb F_2$.  This proves
\[
 H_2(Z^{\mathrm{univ}};\mathbb F_2)
   \cong H_2(K^{\mathrm{univ}};\mathbb F_2)\ne0.
\]
We do not assert that the particular torus attaching sphere is
primitive or itself nonzero after reduction modulo two.

\emph{B.100.6. Consequences and limits for the main obstruction.}
This is a counterexample to asphericity of the actual $Z$, not
just a failure of a sufficient asphericity theorem.  In particular
its universal cover is neither contractible nor mod-two acyclic.
The asphericity of
$X,Y,V$ used in B.89 is a different assertion and is unaffected:
those three spaces are the bundles proved there.
The sphere uses an actual old-$0$ relation among nodal relations.
B.95's overlap calculation places the corresponding column slots in
paired involution summands with vanishing second involution cohomology.
The required geometric-to-presentation comparison is supplied in B.102.
B.105 then chooses explicit node and cylinder disks, proves that their
sphere survives in the actual $Z_2$, and identifies its free rank-two
induced involution block. Its existence alone still gives no nonzero
class in the domain or image of $\kappa$. The full equivariant relation
kernel, its Smith edge, and the nonlinear and compact fixed-lifting
steps remain to be determined.
