# Independent review of the canonical-image section test

Complete note reviewed:
`work_A/canonical_image_section_test_boundary21.md`.
Status: PASS with precisely its stated limitation; no generation or
Fox-unit conclusion is obtained.

The actual closed canonical image H is stable under the involution.
Its extension E_H is generated by the three displayed actual
involutions, which represent three distinct ambient section classes.
The already audited real-projectivity/prescribed-class lifting
property applies to this closed subgroup. For any finite selection
of distinct involution classes, compactness of conjugacy classes
separates them in a finite quotient. Prescribing arbitrary binary
values over those classes in `F2 times Q` gives independent
evaluations in `H^1(E_H,F2)`. Three generators bound this dimension
by three. Thus E_H has exactly three classes and its map to the
ambient section classes is bijective, without any assertion H=F.

The fixed-coset argument is correctly based. A fixed coset gH gives
the section `g^-1 alpha g` in E_H in the ambient class of alpha.
Its H-conjugacy to alpha makes a product of g and an H element
centralize alpha. The actual ambient centralizer in F is trivial,
so g belongs to H. This proves the uniqueness of the selected fixed
coset, rather than assuming that uniqueness from projectivity.

The completed dyadic permutation-module argument also passes. At
each finite quotient, the selected fixed point splits augmentation.
The remaining permutation module decomposes as induced two-point
summands and trivial summands from other fixed points. Compactness
of the inverse system of finite fixed-point sets implies that at a
sufficiently fine level every fixed point maps to the selected one
at any prescribed coarse level. Hence the inverse system of Tate
classes is pro-zero, in every degree and modulo every power of 2.

For clarity, passing to the compact limit does not require surjective
maps between cohomology groups. Given a cycle in the limit, at each
finite level the class is zero because it is the image of an actual
finer cycle. The finite sets of solutions to the corresponding
periodic boundary equation have the finite-intersection property:
one solution at a common finer level solves any finite collection
of projected equations. Their compact inverse limit is therefore
nonempty and supplies a boundary in the completed module.

Consequently the reduced coset module is Tate-acyclic. It is not
proved to be zero, and the associated canonical Fox element is not
proved to be a unit. The exact proper-image, marked third-cusp,
and unit questions remain Open. This review uses the note's
previously proof-audited real-projectivity inputs; it does not
introduce a new free-product recognition theorem.
