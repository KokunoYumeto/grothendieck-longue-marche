# Spherical cycles and the full equivariant obstruction domain

This is an exact conditional reduction, not a new vanishing result.
Use the actual E-cover C_E of the symmetric complement, with
pi1(C_E)=M and the based involution F. The independently reviewed
nodal comparison identifies

  Z2 = H2(C_E; Z).

The original universal cover has nonzero pi2 and nonzero mod-two H2
by B100. The intermediate E-cover is a different space, with generally
nontrivial fundamental group M. Its second homology need not be
generated by spheres.

## The exact Hopf quotient

Let S be the image of the Hurewicz map

  pi2(C_E) -> H2(C_E; Z).

The ordinary Hopf exact sequence, natural under based self-maps,
gives the F-equivariant short exact sequence

  0 -> S -> Z2 -> H2(M; Z) -> 0.                         (1)

For completeness, this quotient can be obtained directly by adjoining
three-cells to a CW model of C_E along representatives of pi2, and
then adjoining cells of dimensions at least four to kill the remaining
higher homotopy. The result is a K(M,1). The three-cells quotient H2
by precisely the spherical Hurewicz image; higher cells do not change
H2. The map and the exact sequence are natural on homology, so the
based involution acts on (1). Equivalently, the low-degree homology
sequence for the universal-cover fibration gives the same quotient.
No claim that M is free or has vanishing H2 is used.

The sphere group pi2(C_E) is identified with pi2(Z) by the covering
map, but its map to H2(C_E) can have a kernel. Consequently the
particular B100 torus sphere, although nonzero and of infinite order
in pi2 and H2 of the universal cover, is not automatically nonzero in
Z2. Its actual transported node chain must still be evaluated. The
new nodal homotopy comparison makes that evaluation meaningful; it
does not remove the need to perform it.

## A sufficient two-part criterion

Let kappa:H²(C2,Z2)->I_F2[R]/J_I be the actual B92 edge map, now
identified geometrically in A's nodal-cover research note. Applying
group cohomology to (1) gives the exact segment

 H²(C2,S) -> H²(C2,Z2) -> H²(C2,H2(M;Z)) -> H³(C2,S).     (2)

It follows that the following two independently proved statements
would be sufficient for kappa=0:

1. kappa vanishes on the image of H²(C2,S).
2. H²(C2,H2(M;Z)) vanishes.

Indeed (2) would make the first arrow surjective. This conclusion
does not follow from S being a submodule of a signed permutation
module, nor from a collection of spherical representatives being
paired by F. The first condition concerns all spherical classes in
their actual module, including their relations; the second concerns
the remaining nonspherical quotient. Neither condition has been
proved for the actual surface.

A weaker usable version would identify just the image of
H²(C2,Z2) in H²(C2,H2(M;Z)) and show that its lifts have zero edge
value, while treating the spherical image separately. This still
requires an actual computation, not a statement about induced
ambient cell modules.

## Fixed-component interpretation and limits

The actual fixed graph has components R/I. Under the reviewed Smith
comparison, a nontrivial fixed-component difference can disappear in
H¹(C2,M_ab) only through the image of kappa. The reduction above
therefore separates two sources of potential such disappearance:
transported spherical identities and nonspherical second homology
of M. It does not prove that either source occurs or vanishes.

In particular, neither B100's nonasphericity nor the existence of its
torus sphere supplies a value of kappa. The full source block
L8=L_ret direct-sum alpha_b^-1(L_ret) and its actual source-operator
overlaps remain a direct way to study the full obstruction. No
completed-module, nonlinear fixed-lifting, or main-group conclusion
is added here.
