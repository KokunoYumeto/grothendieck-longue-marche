# Exact regional far-loop centralizer and its intersection with the neck

Status: complete proposed structural proof, research only. The group R is
the actual B112 bad annular vertex *before* the two B114 cross relations.
No nonmembership or injection conclusion is transferred to the final
one-neck quotient. All normal forms below use the complete infinite
regional graph, not a finite-height truncation.
All centralizers and intersections in this note are discrete. No
completed centralizer assertion for the infinite-order element k is made.

## 1. Exact regional presentation in the z marking

Use C for the adapted cone, k for the actual far/base loop, and
U=(a,b,d,e). Put z=C h. B112's two generic equations give

\[
 [C,z]=[k,z]=1,\quad C^2=1,\quad [k,U]=1,
 \qquad f=Czk^{-1}.
\]

Write U_n=z^n U z^{-n} and bar(U_n)=C U_n C. In these variables
the complete regional graph relations, at every integer n, are

* [U_n,bar(U_n)] for each U;
* (a_n,b_n), (bar(a_n),d_n), (bar(a_n),e_n), (d_n,bar(e_n));
* (b_n,bar(d_(n+1))), (b_n,bar(e_(n+1)));
* k commutes with every unbarred U_n, and bar(k) with every barred U_n.

Here an unordered pair denotes its commutator relation, and C-conjugates
are included. This is just B112's exact graph after h=Cz; its old parity
exchange becomes the displayed bar in the interlevel edges. Conversely
these relations recover the original h presentation. There is no
additional edge between k and bar(k).

## 2. A complete Reidemeister-Schreier graph kernel

Let

\[
 D=\langle C,k\mid C^2=1\rangle=C_2*\mathbb Z,
 \qquad T=\langle k\rangle.
\]

Killing all four U-types gives the actual retraction
R -> D times Z(z). Its section is generated by C,k,z. Define a graph
Omega whose vertices are

\[
 (gT,U,n),\qquad gT\in D/T,\ U\in\{a,b,d,e\},\ n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Its edges are all D-translates, at every n, of the following pairs:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(T,U,n)\;--\;(CT,U,n) &&(U=a,b,d,e),\\
 &(T,a,n)\;--\;(T,b,n),\\
 &(CT,a,n)\;--\;(T,d,n),\quad
   (CT,a,n)\;--\;(T,e,n),\\
 &(T,d,n)\;--\;(CT,e,n),\\
 &(T,b,n)\;--\;(CT,d,n+1),\quad
   (T,b,n)\;--\;(CT,e,n+1).
\end{aligned}
\]

D acts on the coset coordinate by left multiplication; z shifts n.
These actions commute and preserve Omega. There is an exact isomorphism

\[
 R\cong A(\Omega)\rtimes(D\times\mathbb Z_z).
 \tag{1}
\]

For completeness, this is a presentation equality, not an appeal to
an unspecified graph-kernel theorem. Map the vertex (gT,U,n) to
g z^n U z^-n g^-1 in R. The choice of g in its coset is immaterial
because k commutes with U_n. Every displayed edge maps to a conjugate
of one of the complete regional commutators, so this defines one map.
In the other direction send the actual C,k,z to the acting subgroup
and U to the vertex (T,U,0). All regional relations hold by the graph
definition and the stabilizer T. These maps are inverse on C,k,z,U
and on every named vertex. In particular the normal kernel is the
actual graph group A(Omega), with no extra relation or omitted translate.

## 3. The fixed support of k

The cyclic free factor T is malnormal in D=C2*Z. Indeed, if g is not
in T, remove its initial and final powers of k; its remaining reduced
free-product word starts and ends with the C2 letter. For every m!=0
the reduced conjugate g^-1 k^m g still contains that letter and cannot
lie in T. Thus the k-action on D/T fixes exactly the coset T, and
every other orbit is infinite: a finite orbit would yield
g^-1 k^m g in T for some m!=0.

A graph-group element has a well-defined finite support, the vertices
in any reduced dependence word. Graph automorphisms carry this support
to the corresponding support. If an element of A(Omega) is fixed by k,
its support is a finite k-invariant set. The preceding orbit calculation
forces it entirely into the vertices (T,U,n). Conversely k fixes every
one of those generators. Hence

\[
 A(\Omega)^k=A(\Omega_T),
 \qquad \Omega_T=\{(T,U,n):U,n\}.
\]

The induced graph on these vertices has precisely the edges
a_n--b_n. No edge involving one coset CT can have both translated
endpoints at T, since that would require an element and its C-translate
both to lie in T. Consequently

\[
 A(\Omega_T)=*_{n\in\mathbb Z}
       (\mathbb Z^2(a_n,b_n)*\mathbb Z(d_n)*\mathbb Z(e_n)).
\]

An arbitrary centralizer element has projection to D times Z_z in
C_D(k) times Z_z=T times Z_z. The same free-product normal form gives
C_D(k)=T. Writing it as n k^r z^m, its remaining centralizer equation
is exactly n in A(Omega)^k. Therefore

\[
 C_R(k)=\mathbb Z_k\times
 \bigl(\mathbb Z^2(a,b)*F(d,e,z)\bigr).
 \tag{2}
\]

The last equality eliminates all level generators using the z shift.
It is a presentation isomorphism, not a rank count. The z letter is
odd for the original orientation, whereas k,a,b,d,e are even; this
parity distinction is retained if an ordinary-subgroup version is used.

## 4. Exact intersection with the actual common K

The actual common disk is K=F(a,B,d,e,f), where B=C b C. Put
r=C k^-1 in D, so f=r z. On K the projection in (1) kills a,B,d,e
and sends f to (r,z). A word of f-exponent m which centralizes k
would give r^m in C_D(k)=T. Reduced free-product normal form forces
m=0. Hence every element of C_R(k) intersect K lies in the free
Schreier kernel K0, whose complete free basis is

\[
 f^n U f^{-n}\quad(U=a,B,d,e;\ n\in\mathbb Z).
\]

In (1) these are literally graph vertices:

\[
\begin{cases}
 (r^nT,U,n),&U=a,d,e,\\
 (r^nCT,b,n),&U=B.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

Let Omega_K denote this set of vertices. The subgroup they generate
is the full graph subgroup A(Omega_K), which is K0 by (3). The usual
dependence-word support criterion gives the exact intersection of two
full graph subgroups by intersecting their vertex sets. Thus

\[
 K0\cap A(\Omega_T)=A(\Omega_K\cap\Omega_T).
\]

The identity-coset conditions have only the following solutions:
r^n in T iff n=0, and r^n C in T iff n=-1. In the latter case
r^-1 C=k. These assertions follow immediately by reducing the words
(C k^-1)^n and (C k^-1)^n C in C2*Z. Accordingly the intersection
vertices are a_0,d_0,e_0,b_-1. No two of these four are adjacent in
Omega_T. They freely generate, and

\[
 \boxed{\ C_R(k)\cap K
       =F(a,d,e,f^{-1}Bf).\ }
 \tag{4}
\]

The fourth generator is indeed b_-1: using f=C z k^-1 and [k,b_n]=1,
one obtains f^-1(C b C)f=z^-1 b z. This calculation is exact in the
actual annular vertex and includes all integer levels.

## 5. Scope for the actual source test

Equation (4) enlarges the previously used sufficient subgroup
<a,d,e> by the explicit generator f^-1 bar(b)f. If an actual marked
outer contact has form v c_+ v c_+ with v in (4), the new relation
[c_+,k]=1 makes that contact commute with k in G_ann. This would
be an actual source-image relation only after its marked v is verified.

Conversely a v outside (4) need not remain noncentral in G_ann:
the two cross relations can add further relations, and vertex injection
there is not proved. In particular eta(a)=a B a^-1 B^-1 a^-1 is
not automatically centralized by k. The exact source words must still
be checked. Neither source-image freeness nor a final centralizer
formula follows from (4).

## 7. A selected-cone subgroup avoids all edge conjugates

Let c=a C a^-1 be the actual selected common cone and set
S=<c,k>. Since [a,k]=1, S=a D a^-1, so S is C2*Z. The actual
projection (1) is injective on S and maps it isomorphically to
D times {0}; its restriction to K has cyclic image generated by
(C k^-1,z). For x in S intersect gKg^-1, the z coordinate of x
is zero. A conjugated element of that cyclic image has z coordinate
equal to its exponent, so the coarse image of x is1. Injectivity
on S gives x=1. Thus S intersect gKg^-1=1 for every g in R.

This particular argument also gives a full completed avoidance
statement, unlike the infinite-support centralizer argument above.
The map R -> D obtained from (1) by forgetting z has the section
C -> c, k -> k, hence S is an actual retract and has its full
topology. In the completed coarse product Dhat times Zhat, the
compact image of Khat is the diagonal procyclic group
{((Ck^-1)^lambda,z^lambda):lambda in Zhat}. Its second coordinate
is primitive. For any g in Rhat, an element of Shat intersect
g Khat g^-1 therefore has lambda=0 and trivial coarse image;
the completed projection is injective on Shat. The intersection
is consequently trivial.

This is not a free-factor or filling theorem. In particular the
actual element a f a^-1 belongs to K and normalizes S: f=(Ck^-1)z,
and z centralizes D. This nontrivial edge normalizer prevents any
automatic inference of a relative one-relator Freiheitssatz from
edge avoidance alone. All cross-relator translates remain required.
