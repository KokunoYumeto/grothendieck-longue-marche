Exact cone-centralizer intersection and cone-loop normalizer inside K
CE research, checkpoint23. Regional statements before the cross quotient.

1. The actual retraction forces a centralizing neck word to be a branch power

Use R,H,K,C,k,f,a as in B112/B114, with H the ordinary subgroup and
K=F(a,B,d,e,f). The proved ordinary retraction r:H->K is identity
on K and satisfies

 nu=r composed with Inn(C) restricted to K:
          (a,B,d,e,f) -> (1,1,1,1,f).

If x in K centralizes C, apply r to CxC=x. It gives nu(x)=x, hence
x=f^m for an integer m. The same argument is literal in the full
completion. Khat embeds in Hhat by the retraction, C normalizes Hhat,
and nuhat has precisely the procyclic image generated by f. Thus a
centralizing x in Khat has the form f^m, m in Zhat. This procyclic
parameter is unique because f is a member of the actual free basis
of K and has its primitive exponent retraction.

2. A uniform actual finite family detects every nonzero parameter

Use the actual split quotient
 R -> (C2(C)*Z(k)) times Z_z,
which kills the A-letter graph kernel, with f=(C k^-1)z. For each
integer n>=2, compose with the finite quotient

 C2(C)*Z(k) -> (C2)^(Z/n) semidirect Z/n,
 C -> lamp at0,       k -> positive cyclic shift.

The lamp subgroup is abelian. Conjugation by any element of this
wreath product moves the position of a lamp solely by its cyclic
shift coordinate. Therefore (C k^-1)^m conjugates the lamp at0 to
the lamp at(-m mod n). These are distinct whenever m is nonzero
modulo n. If f^m commutes with C, its image forces m=0 modulo n
for EVERY n. This proves m=0 for integers and for profinite integers.
No finite cutoff or search is used: it is one symbolic quotient family.

Consequently

 C_R(C) intersect K=1,
 C_Rhat(C) intersect Khat=1.                                (1)

The selected actual gap cone is c=a C a^-1, with a in K. Conjugating
the entire intersection by a gives the same assertions with c:

 C_R(c) intersect K=1,
 C_Rhat(c) intersect Khat=1.                                (2)

The reflected vertex has the identical conclusion under the actual
eta/common-edge comparison. No generator-wise reflection convention
is required.

3. Exact K-normalizer of the regional cone/base-loop subgroup

Let S=<c,k>=a<C,k>a^-1, using[k,a]=1, and F_gap=a f a^-1 in K.
The previously proved split coarse map pi restricts to an isomorphism
S->D=C2*Z and sends every K word to a power of(r,z), r=C k^-1.
The element F_gap normalizes S because f=r z with z centralizing D;
its conjugation on S is the inner conjugation by c k^-1.

Suppose x in K normalizes S. Its coarse image is(r^m,z^m). The element
n=x F_gap^-m belongs to K, has trivial coarse image, and normalizes S.
For s in S, the elements n s n^-1 and s have the same image under pi,
and both lie in S. Injectivity on S forces n to centralize all of S.
In particular n centralizes c, so(2) gives n=1. Hence

              N_R(S) intersect K=<F_gap>.                  (3)

Exactly the same proof applies to closed subgroups in the full
completion: pi is injective on Sbar, a Khat element has its unique
profinite parameter m, and the continuous power F_gap^m exists in
Khat. Thus

       N_Rhat(Sbar) intersect Khat=closure<F_gap>.           (4)

The inclusions of Sbar and Khat, and the primitive Zhat coordinate,
are justified by actual retractions; no completion of an infinite
normal kernel is identified. Equations(1)--(4) hold in the regional
vertex BEFORE B114's two added relations. They do not survive into
that quotient without a new proof and do not decide its centralizer.

This describes the exact branch normalizer which prevents replacing
relative edge avoidance by a free-factor/filling assertion. It is a
uniform all-word result, not another bounded subgroup computation.
