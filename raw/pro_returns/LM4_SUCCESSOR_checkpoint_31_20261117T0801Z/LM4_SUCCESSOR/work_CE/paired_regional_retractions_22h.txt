Paired ordinary retractions for the actual first-neck vertices
CE research, checkpoint22. This concerns only the simply connected slit
bases of shared_five_generator_neck_21h.txt, not the full annular bases.

1. Exact groups and based maps

For either bad regional vertex write R for its orbifold group, H for
its ordinary orientation kernel, and tau for the actual order-two
cone at the chosen bad-side fixed point. Thus R=H semidirect <tau>.
Let alpha be conjugation by tau on H. Let K=F5 be the literally based
common disk subgroup, with four A puncture meridians and one retained
branch meridian. The common connector is that of the regular neck;
no independent changes of individual meridian whiskers are made.

On the simply connected slit q base, filling B maps the ordinary
group H into the actual one-family bundle group, identified with its
fiber F7 by the inclusion at the same base value. The inclusion of
that fiber is an isomorphism on pi1 because both pi1 and pi2 of the
base vanish. Write this ordinary map as f:H->F7. It is not asserted
to be equivariant, and it is not a map on the whole group R.

The geometric disk giving K contains five of the eight remaining
punctures. The three outside are the other branch puncture and the
two retained punctures t=1,-1. Choose a geometric free basis of F7
which contains the five literal K meridians, a meridian y of the
other branch puncture, and a meridian z of one of the retained
punctures; omit the other retained meridian using the sphere relation.
Disjoint arcs outside the disk give such an extension of the already
chosen disk arc system. Thus

  F7 = K * F(y,z).

Let p:F7->K fix K pointwise and kill y,z. Put r=p*f:H->K. This is
an actual retraction of the specified K.

2. Why the cone-conjugate K is killed, with all connectors retained

The actual holomorphic involution exchanges each A puncture with its
B partner and exchanges the two branch punctures. Consequently, for
each of the four A generators k_j of K, alpha(k_j) is a based
conjugate of a B peripheral. Filling B kills its entire normal
closure. Explicitly, if that based peripheral is h_j*m_B*h_j^-1,
then f(alpha(k_j))=f(h_j)*1*f(h_j)^-1=1. This does not remove or
ignore h_j; the map kills the conjugated core.

For the fifth generator, alpha(k_branch) is a based conjugate of
a peripheral of the OTHER branch point. After filling B, it has
the form h*y*h^-1, for an actual word h in F7 and either consistent
positive orientation. The projection p kills y and hence kills
p(h)*1*p(h)^-1. Again no assertion that h is trivial, lies in K,
or is shared with a different generator is needed.

The possible common connector change between the global based
involution and the bad-side cone is also a based conjugation of
these peripheral words. The two normal-killing statements just
proved remain literal under that change. Therefore

  r|K=id_K,       r(alpha(K))=1.

This is stronger than the isolated virtual-retract statement, and
it has used the specific actual single-family/branch marking.

3. The paired quotient and exact finite edge kernels

Define

  Phi:H -> K x K,    Phi(h)=(r(h),r(alpha(h))).

It is a homomorphism, and Phi(alpha(h)) swaps its coordinates.
It extends to the actual homomorphism

  R -> (K x K) semidirect_(swap) C2,
  tau -> the swapping involution,    k in K -> (k,1).

No action of F on the one-family target F7 is asserted. Equivariance
arises only after using BOTH ordinary maps r and r*alpha.

For every finite-index normal N in K, compose with K/N in both
coordinates. The resulting finite quotient of R induces EXACTLY
the kernel N on K. If M is a prescribed finite-index normal subgroup
of R, choose N contained in M intersect K and intersect M with the
kernel of this finite paired quotient. Its restriction to K is still
exactly N. The identical N can be used for the two regional vertices.
Hence arbitrary prescribed finite vertex quotients admit refinements
with identical finite edge kernel.

This supplies the finite-edge compatibility needed by the separate
one-neck profinite-amalgam argument. It does not itself prove a
centralizer theorem for finite amalgams or for the whole surface.
Root's records/profinite_one_neck_retract_proposal21.md gives a
more general fully-invariant-kernel construction using only virtual
retracts; the actual paired retraction here avoids that extra
restriction on N in this specific geometric case.

4. Scope

The base must be the simply connected slit region. On the full
annular base the one-family target is F7 semidirect Z, and killing
the base generator need not define a retraction onto this K; none
is assumed. The standard middle-neck rank-five groups have actual
regional kernels, so the present single-family retraction cannot
be substituted for their quotient-edge comparison. No whole M,
all-neck, kappa or main fixed-source conclusion is made.
