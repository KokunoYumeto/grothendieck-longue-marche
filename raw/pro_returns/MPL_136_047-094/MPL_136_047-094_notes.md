### The mathematical distinction behind the answer

Write \(w\) for **total weight** and \(j\) for **exterior/cohomological degree** in the convention introduced on scan 081. The corresponding term is

\[
K^{w,j}_{u,k}=\Gamma_k^{w-j}(\Phi_k)\otimes_k\Lambda_k^j(\Psi_k),
\qquad 0\leq j\leq w,
\]

and the differential preserves \(w\) and increases \(j\). Under the manuscript's exactness and flasqueness hypotheses, its theorem reads

\[
H^j\Gamma(X,K^{w,\bullet}_{u,k})\cong H^j(X,k)\,T^{[w]}
\quad(j\leq w),
\qquad
H^j\Gamma(X,K^{w,\bullet}_{u,k})=0\quad(j>w).
\]

This restates the **claim on the page**, with the two indices made explicit; R029 gives the dimension-shifting check of its range. Each fixed weight can recover a finite cohomological range. Multiplication changes weight, however, and the coefficient multiplication is

\[
T^{[r]}T^{[s]}=\binom{r+s}{r}T^{[r+s]}.
\]

Thus identifying each rank-one coefficient group separately with \(\mathbb Z\) is not a multiplicative identification of all weights with a single coefficient ring. The exact structured comparison with cup products remains a proof obligation; neither the Stokes relation nor an additive quasi-isomorphism supplies it by itself. The point is consistent with the historical Cartan–Miller literature's weighted comparisons and later higher-homotopy work, but the literature is not used to rewrite any symbol in the manuscript (Cenkl & Porter, 1982, §1; Cenkl, 1989, p. 21).

### R061 — A counterexample to the arbitrary-presheaf interpretation of the flagged step

**File/scan:** 136, 064; compare 058–062. **Kind:** own idea. **Ink anchor:** “sans hypothèse de loc. constance” and “à vérifier ce point.” **Confidence:** high for the explicit presheaf calculation, independently checked; applicability is to the literal arbitrary-presheaf interpretation, not an unstated restricted coefficient category.

Let \(X=\Delta^1/\partial\Delta^1\), let \(k\) be a field, and define a presheaf \(F\) on \(\Delta/X\) by \(F(\sigma)=k\) on simplices degenerate from the unique vertex and \(F(\sigma)=0\) on simplices degenerate from the nondegenerate edge, with identity and zero restriction maps as appropriate. Every section of \(C_X^q\otimes_kF\) is zero: the edge forces its vertex value to vanish, and maps from vertex-degenerate simplices to that vertex force all remaining values to vanish. In contrast, embed \(F\) diagonally into the injective presheaf \(I\) right-adjoint to vertex evaluation, for which \(I(\sigma)=k^{\dim\sigma+1}\); then \(\Gamma I=k\), \(\Gamma(I/F)=k^2\), and the map between them is diagonal, so the long exact sequence gives \(H^1(X,F)\cong k\). Thus the pointwise exact tensor resolution does not compute derived sections for this arbitrary coefficient sheaf, and its terms cannot all have the claimed acyclicity; the full category/variance verification is recorded in `research/algebra_checks.md`.

**Context search:** `constant`, `sections`, `coefficients`; `ps7.tex`, §138, lines 1296–1320 and §139, lines 1747–1760, PS pp. 581–582, 591, distinguish coefficient scopes, while `ps6.tex`, §127, lines 4753–4783, PS pp. 536–537, warns about sections. **Relation/register:** supplies a concrete counterexample to one interpretation of the manuscript's flagged step under **WG2 §2.4 #5**; it neither refutes nor re-proves the differently formulated **GALOIS D.2**/**BISITE A.19** entries.

### R062 — A divided-power test using an ordinary integral cup ring

**File/scan:** 136, 077, 083 and 094. **Kind:** own idea. **Ink anchor:** divided powers on the positive-degree ideal and \(d(x^{(i)})=x^{(i-1)}dx\). **Confidence:** high under the explicitly strengthened single-complex/product-preserving hypothesis; the divisibility contradiction is elementary.

If one ordinary integral cochain algebra had compatible divided powers on every positive-degree cochain and its cohomology ring were the usual ring of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), choose a degree-two cocycle \(x\) representing its generator (for the cup ring, see Hatcher, 2002, online version, Theorem 3.19). The identities \(x^2=2\gamma_2(x)\) and \(d\gamma_2(x)=x\,dx=0\) would make the generator \([x]^2\) of \(H^4(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) divisible by two, a contradiction. This gives a small test of the ordinary-product interpretation; the manuscript's different weights and coefficient multipliers prevent that interpretation from being silently imposed on its actual object.

**Context search:** `divided powers`, `operations`, `binomial`; `ps6.tex`, §120, lines 3131–3198 and §119, lines 2951–2989, PS pp. 506–507, 501–502. **Relation/register:** an explicit constraint for **WG2 §2.4 #5**, without claiming that \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) is a manuscript example or that this elementary obstruction is new.

### R063 — Different weights explain how alternating products can retain useful information

**File/scan:** 136, 081–083. **Kind:** own idea. **Ink anchor:** the two gradings and the coefficient algebra \(k\{T\}\). **Confidence:** high conditional on the weighted product comparison; the coefficient calculation is exact.

Over \(\mathbb F_2\), the central binomial coefficient \(\binom{2w}{w}\) is even for \(w>0\), so the square of an odd class represented in a single weight can vanish consistently with alternation. Yet the degree-one class of \(BC_2\) represented once in weight one and once in weight two has product coefficient \(\binom31=3=1\) in \(\mathbb F_2\), giving its nonzero ordinary square in weight three under the comparison. Consequently the test in R050 rejects identifying all these representatives with one unweighted algebra element; it does not show that the weighted system loses every nonzero cup product.

**Context search:** `binomial`, `operations`, `Steenrod`; `ps6.tex`, §119, lines 2951–2989 and `ps7.tex`, §139, lines 1742–1760, PS pp. 501–502, 591. **Relation/register:** refines the product question in **WG2 §2.4 #5**, preserving the distinction between an obstruction and a possible structured comparison.

### R064 — The broad flasqueness inequality fails in the constant corner

**File/scan:** 136, 075–076; compare 074. **Kind:** own idea. **Ink anchor:** “le noyau ... est flasque si \(p\geq0\).” **Confidence:** high for the universal-flasqueness interpretation used to pass to sections for every \(X_*\); the constant corner supplies a direct test.

The formula on 074 gives \(DR_*^{0,0}\cong k_*\), which cannot be universally acyclic on all simplicial slices: for the simplicial circle its first cohomology is \(k\). Likewise the global-sections sequence beginning \(0\to k_*\to\Phi_*\to\Psi_*\to0\) can have a cokernel measured by \(H^1(X_*,k)\), so surjectivity cannot be inferred uniformly in complementary degree zero. This supports the exceptional \(p\ne0\) qualification on 076 and identifies a mathematical problem with the broader inequality on 075, without changing the written sign.

**Context search:** `sections`, `acyclic`, `projective`; `ps6.tex`, §127, lines 4753–4783, PS pp. 536–537, supplies the later distinction between sheaf equivalences and sections. **Relation/register:** a concrete test within **WG2 §2.4 #5**, related to but not settled by the separate tensor-acyclicity lead **MPL_134-8_043-087 R033**.

### R065 — Absolute acyclicity and acyclicity on slices have different scopes

**File/scan:** 136, 064, 075, 081 and 091. **Kind:** own idea. **Ink anchor:** “si \(X_*\in\operatorname{Ob}SS\), \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_*,-)\) ... donne encore une suite exacte” on 075. **Confidence:** high for the presheaf-topos interpretation with degeneracies; the initial-object calculation and the slice counterexample are explicit.

For \(SS=\operatorname{Set}^{\Delta^{op}}\), absolute global sections of an abelian presheaf are its value at \([0]\), hence form an exact functor, whereas global sections on \(SS/X\simeq\operatorname{Set}^{(\Delta/X)^{op}}\) need not be exact. Thus the passage to \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_*,-)\) for every \(X_*\), and the relative \(f_*\) comparison, require a stronger scope of acyclicity than the absolute statement alone. Tensoring with arbitrary coefficients intrinsic to a slice is a further quantifier, because such coefficients need not be restrictions from the base topos. The counterexample in R061 addresses that slice-coefficient assertion; it does not by itself refute the example asserted on 091 before “flasque” and its tensor-universality have been specified precisely.

**Context search:** `sections`, `fibers`, `coefficient`; `ps6.tex`, §127, lines 4753–4783 and `ps7.tex`, §135, lines 438–493, PS pp. 536–537, 563–564. **Relation/register:** sharpens the hypotheses to be tested under **WG2 §2.4 #5** and the coefficient-scope warning relevant to **WG H6**; the supplied later passages distinguish scopes but do not prove the 1976 example.
