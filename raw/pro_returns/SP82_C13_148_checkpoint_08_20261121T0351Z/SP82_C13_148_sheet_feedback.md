## Recurring observations beyond the listed categories

The ten main categories already encompass most findings. The following recurring details are useful refinements for later runs, not additional mutually exclusive error counts.

- **Identify the face of reused paper before reading its machine text.** Reversed listings and large faint letter grids led C to preserve many apparent words and doubt marks on scans 021, 029, 033, 038, 039 and 043. A whole-page orientation check distinguishes show-through from machine text actually belonging to the inspected face. Counts of such editorial places should not be interpreted as independent character errors.
- **Keep thin foliation separate from the author's numbering.** Both can occur on the same leaf: scans 044, 046 and 047 carry the author's 1, 2 and 3) as well as thin margin numbers 43, 45 and 46. The thinner hand is not attributed without evidence.
- **A drawing or leader can cross text without cancelling it.** Scan 026's covering sentence and scan 046's interlinear boundary-circle definition require the line's path and endpoint to be read separately from the word. In scan 047, the weighting caption lies outside the large cancellation and remains live.
- **Heavy points can retain a small light center.** This affects scans 026 (three-point ovals and ring chain), 027 (central paired circles) and 047 (central and terminal graph points, and the lower loop vertex). A uniformly filled schematic point can lose an observed feature.
- **A faint or interrupted stroke need not be a missing edge.** Scan 024's middle small sphere has a short free stroke; scan 047 has pale, interrupted free arms. Preserve the observed connections, gaps and line weights without completing a plausible graph from its expected combinatorics.
- **Keep hooked paths distinct from conventional symbols.** Scan 046's category connector has a left hook and long shaft without an ordinary rightward arrowhead; neither a conventional subset symbol nor a full hooked arrow matches it. Scan 044's lower note has a separate hooked leader beside an exclamation mark. Both paths are drawn explicitly in their respective contexts.
- **Editorial repetition can masquerade as source text.** H carries a heading from the following sheet at the end of scan 028 and repeats scan 047's decomposition formulas in a separate align block. The local source order and number of occurrences decide what belongs in the page.
- **A crossed earlier glyph can be separate from a retouched live glyph.** Scan 024's doubtful F before the first quoted J and scan 047's earlier map labels must not be merged with their replacements. Conversely, retouching alone is insufficient to invent an additional old letter.
- **Unbalanced punctuation can be the source's punctuation.** Scans 042, 046 and 047 retain openings or closures without supplying a matching mark that the ink does not show. This applies independently of whether a phrase would be easier to parse with a complete pair.
- **Repeated terminology does not force a repeated word length.** On scan 046 the first compos. is abbreviated, while a later live occurrence is fully written composantes; a crossed occurrence remains uncertain. Each ending must be read locally.
