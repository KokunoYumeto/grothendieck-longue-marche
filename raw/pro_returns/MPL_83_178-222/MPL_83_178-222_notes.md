### Q07 — A precise strict definition, expressly ours

**File / scans / location:** 83 / 183, 205; comparison only, not an authorial definition. **Kind:** own idea. **Anchor:** “structure spéciale orthogonale”; “sommets adjacents”. **Context comparison:** formalizes one restricted model compatible with Esquisse, scans 119/21–23. **Register:** README ring-definition question. **Confidence / reason:** high for precision of this definition, medium for its coverage of the author's intended objects.

For a commutative ring $R$, define a **strict R-realization of a regular 2-map** to consist of a rank-three abstract polytope (with the diamond condition and strong flag connectivity) whose flag automorphism group $G$ acts simply transitively, a rank-four projective $R$-module $E$, a homomorphism $G\to\mathrm{PGL}(E)(R)$, and injective equivariant assignments of vertices, edges, and faces to locally direct summands of $E$ of ranks one, two, and three. Require incidence to be exactly containment and every realized maximal flag to arise from an abstract flag. Choosing one flag gives a pinned object. This is a usable definition for a strict geometric model; it is not claimed to recover every infinite object or degenerate specialization intended in the Esquisse.

### Q08 — Flag adjacency and geometric symmetry are different actions

**File / scans / location:** 83 / 201, 205; permutation and adjacency language. **Kind:** own idea. **Anchor:** “Via opération du groupe symétrique”; “faces adjacentes”. **Context comparison:** makes explicit a distinction implicit in the regular-map discussion of Esquisse, scans 119/20–23, and Hua VI–VII. **Register:** no direct row. **Confidence / reason:** high, from the regular action on a pinned flag set.

### E05 — The scalar invertibility criterion is not intrinsic under ordinary extension isomorphisms

**File / scans / location:** 83 / 180–181; split parameter and equivalence criterion. **Kind:** own idea. **Anchor:** “ssi $\beta$ est inversible dans $k$”; “l'idéal somme … et $\lambda k$ est $k$”. **Context comparison:** exposes a tension also repeated, unresolved, in Hua XI, pages 179–180. **Register:** no direct row. **Confidence / reason:** high for the shear calculation, medium for its application until the precise earlier morphism category is fixed.

Write the split model as $u_\beta(a,x)=\lambda a+\beta x$; the shear $T_c(a,x)=(a+cx,x)$ satisfies $u_\beta T_c=u_{\beta+\lambda c}$ and fixes the kernel injection and quotient. For $\lambda=1$ this identifies $E_0$ with $E_1$, and over $\mathbf Z$ with $\lambda=2$ it identifies $E_3$ with $E_1$ although 3 is not a unit. The split product has parameter $\beta\gamma$, so invertibility modulo $\lambda$, namely $(\beta,\lambda)=k$, is the natural isomorphism-invariant condition and agrees with the intrinsic criterion written later. A category in which the ordinary splitting itself is fixed could change the comparison, but scan 180 calls for choosing a splitting rather than clearly including it among the data.

## 3. Rank-four Azumaya algebras and three-variable traces

### A01 — The full trace pairing remains perfect in characteristic two

**File / scan / location:** 83 / 183; reconstruction argument. **Kind:** own idea — correction of a context claim. **Anchor:** “celle-ci est non dég.”. **Context comparison:** contradicts the characteristic-two footnote in Hua IX, “Le critère”, pages 92–99; agrees with the local argument and Hua XII's use of the full pairing. **Register:** no direct row. **Confidence / reason:** high, from a matrix-unit Gram determinant over $\mathbf Z$.

On $M_2(k)$ the pairing $(X,Y)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(XY)$ has Gram determinant $-1$ in a matrix-unit basis, so it is perfect over every commutative ring. This property descends to rank-four Azumaya algebras after a faithfully flat splitting. Hua's earlier statement that this full trace form degenerates in characteristic two is therefore incorrect; a restriction to the trace-zero submodule is a different pairing and can degenerate there.

### A02 — The same letter names two different bilinear forms

**File / scans / location:** 83 / 183, 188; definitions of $\varphi$. **Kind:** reading question. **Anchor:** “$\operatorname{Tr}xy$” underbraced as $\varphi$; “$\varphi(u,v)=\operatorname{Tr}u\operatorname{Tr}v-\operatorname{Tr}(uv)$”. **Context comparison:** differs from Hua's global conventions, which reserve separate symbols for the trace pairing and the polar form of the determinant; Hua XII, pages 182–187. **Register:** no direct row.

The local notation changes from the trace pairing to the determinant's polar form. Both forms are useful, but their Gram matrices and diagonal entries are different. Subsequent calculations must declare the chosen form explicitly; Hua's cleaner notation is not evidence that the diplomatic occurrence on either leaf should be replaced.

### A03 — Orientation is proposed as the missing multiplication datum

**File / scan / location:** 83 / 183; opening and opposite-algebra discussion. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Anchor:** “$\det,1$ sans $e$ ne suffisent”; “mod passage à l'inverse”. **Context comparison:** differs substantively from Hua XII's opening paraphrase, which says $\det,1,e$ still leave the ambiguity; compare `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 245 and 249–250, for a different Azumaya reconstruction problem. **Register:** no direct row.

The page proposes recovering multiplication from the norm, the identity element, and a top-exterior orientation by recovering every triple trace and using the perfect trace pairing. Over a base with 2 invertible, the canonical conjugation $X\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(X)I-X$ reverses multiplication and has linear determinant $-1$, explaining why orientation can distinguish the two products. The phrase “sans $e$” is essential, and the claimed reconstruction requires a separate analysis at 2 rather than Hua's altered paraphrase.

### A04 — Ordinary volume orientation does not repair reconstruction in characteristic two

**File / scans / location:** 83 / 183, 192; reconstruction and the exceptional characteristic. **Kind:** own idea. **Anchor:** “$e\in\bigwedge^4\mathcal A$”; “en car. 2”. **Context comparison:** supplies a concrete obstruction absent from Hua XII's reconstruction discussion; compatible with the source's later warning about division by two. **Register:** no direct row. **Confidence / reason:** high, from the matrix-unit counterexample recorded in the algebra verification.

Over a field of characteristic two, the adjugate map $\iota(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)I-X$ fixes $I$, preserves the determinant, and preserves every top-exterior orientation because its linear determinant $-1$ becomes 1. Nevertheless $\iota(E_{12})\iota(E_{21})=E_{11}$ while $\iota(E_{12}E_{21})=E_{22}$, so it is not an algebra automorphism. Thus the literal data $(\det,1,e)$ with $e$ an ordinary volume element cannot recover multiplication functorially in characteristic two. A stronger orientation or component datum could address another formulation, but it is not specified on these leaves.

### A05 — The symmetric triple trace identity is integral

**File / scan / location:** 83 / 184; boxed sum. **Kind:** own idea — verification. **Anchor:** “$\tau+\tau'=bt+b't'+b''t''-bb'b''$”. **Context comparison:** confirms Hua XII, page 183, without importing its notation into the transcription. **Register:** no direct row. **Confidence / reason:** high, from expansion of generic $2\times2$ matrices over $\mathbf Z$.

With $b=\operatorname{Tr}x$, $c=\det x$, $t=\operatorname{Tr}(yz)$ and cyclic primes, the displayed sum follows from polarizing the degree-two Cayley–Hamilton identity. Exact symbolic expansion confirms it as an integer polynomial identity, so it remains valid in characteristic two and after arbitrary base change. Its validity does not require the later normalization of $\Delta$.
