### 138-07 — Scans 141–142, ll. 79 and 3 — own idea

This adds a possible construction to `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 107–108, where the points of $D_d$ already remember a local branch germ, and to `LM_140-4_text.tex`, scans 242–245, where germs and their coverings are organized by filters. Confidence is medium: the local model is explicit, but effectiveness and presentation independence for the author's multiplicities still require proof, especially in light of scan 146's equivariant obstruction to choosing a normal-bundle parametrization.

### 138-08 — Scan 142, ll. 3–8, (235) — conjecture

### 138-09 — Scan 143, ll. 29–33, (237) — unpursued implication

Defining the fundamental groupoids “par revêtements et isomorphismes entre tels” requires a decision about which coverings are retained. A comparison must therefore state whether it concerns all topological coverings, finite coverings, or a completion, and must not silently replace the manuscript's discrete lattice kernels by profinite lattices.

### 138-18 — Scan 146, l. 6 — own idea

The transvection example can be checked directly if the uncertain upper-right matrix entry is the retained first reading $1$: the generator acts by $(x,y)\mapsto(x+y,y)$ and fixes the line $y=0$. The proposal adds a conditional repair to the quotient-germ programme of `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 78–80, and to the equivariance comparisons in `LM_140-4_text.tex`, scans 263–264; confidence is high for the metric construction under the stated hypotheses, but it does not establish descent for arbitrary multiplicities or simultaneous compatibility with every boundary branch.

### 138-25 — Scans 148 and 175–178, especially page-file 148, l. 7, and 178, l. 15 — own idea

A useful test of “un quotient de $T$” and “par l’image des $\pi_2$” is the zero-section divisor in the total space of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)$: its punctured normal bundle identifies with $\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\}$ by sending a nonzero vector in its tautological line to that vector. This total space retracts to $S^3$, so its fundamental group is zero, whereas the trivial punctured line bundle over $\mathbb P^1$ has fundamental group $\mathbb Z$; the contrast is the Hopf-bundle instance of the connecting map discussed by Serre (1949–1950, §§1, 5). This **adds** an explicit example to the normal-line torsors of `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 122–123, and the $\pi_2$ caveat on scan 130, and tests the models on current scans 175–178 as well as the earlier qualification on scan 148. Confidence is high in the calculation because the identification is explicit, and medium in its usefulness for the full diagram comparison because one piece does not settle canonical transition maps; no claim is made that this particular bundle occurs in the moduli application.
