# Notation table

This table records the visual conventions used throughout the 59 retained scans. The second visual audit covers every page; the final concordance was completed before opening the context archive. Alternative readings remain in the transcription wherever the pen strokes do not settle them. The first alternative records the preferred reading of the ink. The table identifies written forms and their LaTeX encodings; it does not supply identifications of mathematical objects that the pages leave unspecified.

| Doubled disk letter | `\mathbb D` | Scans 64–65; the disk in the preliminary map calculation. |

# Legibility

All 59 retained pages were read from the whole-page image and both supplied halves, then received a second comparison against the images. Small marks and difficult cancellations were enlarged again. No OCR was used, and no context text was opened before the transcription freeze. The displayed formulas and typed sheets are generally more secure than the compressed cursive prose; a legible large formula can still contain a doubtful tiny exponent, bar or arrowhead, which is marked locally.

The least secure passages are the dense diagonal ramification margin on scan 75, the long crossing proof margin on scan 78, the overwritten/deleted words on scans 93 and 98, the cancelled algebra on scans 103 and 105, the small marks and cancelled products on scans 107, 109 and 115, and some words in the Russian paragraph on scan 119. The G/S letter, starred spaces on scans 89–91, the prime/exclamation on scan 63, and the final Gamma diagram on scan 111 retain explicit alternatives after comparison. A single obliterated word after “Notons” on scan 116 is recorded as `\deleted{\illegible{1}}`; this count estimates one word and does not stand for missing mathematical notation.

The doubt table uses the exact page-file line as its locator. Medium marks give a preferred but insecure reading; low marks retain a competing reading or physically illegible ink. Reproduced drawings preserve topology, relative placement, arrow directions and labels as closely as the scans allow, rather than claiming facsimile geometry. The literal arithmetic, agreement, spellings, repeated numbering and unfinished sentences remain even where a more familiar expression would be tempting. The dates on Cornu's leaves, the administrative typed faces and Deligne's letter are local evidence for those sheets; they have not been propagated into dates for adjacent handwriting.
