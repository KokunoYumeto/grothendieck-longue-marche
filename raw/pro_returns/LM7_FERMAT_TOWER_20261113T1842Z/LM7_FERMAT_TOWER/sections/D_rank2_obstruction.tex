\subsection{The minimal solvable even bases: a complete obstruction and a corrected elliptic lift}

\begin{statement}{D.45}{Proved}{The recorded D.39 action has no full profinite lift; its elliptic rank-two projection does lift one layer farther}
The two minimal nonnilpotent solvable even bases of a
sign-preserving marked quotient of \(B=C_2*C_3\) have order
\(24\). Their groups and actual source markings can be taken as
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
       &s&r\\ \hline
 S_4&(1\,2)&(2\,3\,4)\\
 A_4\times C_2&((1\,2)(3\,4),1)&((1\,2\,3),0).
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{D.45.1}
\]
Both are already actual quotients of \(Q\).
Their full relative pro-\(2\) completions are respectively
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal P_F&=\widehat B/
          \ker(\widehat F\to F^{(2)}),&
 F&=\ker(B\to S_3),\\
 \mathcal P_E&=\widehat B/
          \ker(\widehat {B'}\to (B')^{(2)}),&
 B'&=\ker(B\to C_6).
 \end{aligned}                                                 \tag{D.45.2}
\]
The exact reductions of (D.45.1) are \(S_4/V_4=S_3\)
and \((A_4\times C_2)/V_4=C_6\), with these original
source maps. The maximal common marked quotient of the
two completions is \(C_2\). Consequently their actual
joint source image is
\[
       \mathcal P_F\times_{C_2}\mathcal P_E.                    \tag{D.45.3}
\]
Over the actual common base
\(Q_0=S_3\times_{C_2}C_6\), its kernel is
\(F^{(2)}\times(B')^{(2)}\).

The rank-four source \(K=\ker(B\to Q_0)\) maps onto
that product. In the recorded \(K\)-basis, and the
rank-two graph bases specified in the proof, its integral
homology matrix is
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}
 -1&-1&-2&1\\
 -1&-1&1&-2\\
 -2&1&-1&-1\\
 1&-2&-1&-1
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det P=-27.                              \tag{D.45.4}
\]
It is an isomorphism over \(\mathbb Z_2\). The kernel of
\[
                  K^{(2)}\twoheadrightarrow
                      F^{(2)}\times(B')^{(2)}                  \tag{D.45.5}
\]
is the closed normal subgroup generated by the four
mixed commutators of any lifts of the two free bases
of the two factors. The literal source map and both
directions of this kernel description are proved below.
The recorded D.39 homology action has the respective
blocks, modulo \(16\),
\[
                 I_2,\qquad
                    \begin{pmatrix}11&8\\8&3\end{pmatrix}.    \tag{D.45.6}
\]

Write \(D_nG=\prod_{i2^j\ge n}\gamma_i(G)^{2^j}\)
for the closed mod-two Zassenhaus filtration.
The recorded D.39 action has a canonical projection
to each of \(B/D_9F\) and \(B/D_9B'\):
\[
 \mathcal T\longrightarrow T_3=B/D_9K
                \longrightarrow B/D_9F,\ B/D_9B'.             \tag{D.45.7}
\]
The first projection, to the sphere source \(B/D_9F\),
\emph{has no peripheral lift with multiplier one to}
\(B/D_{10}F\). This excludes every conjugator pair
inducing the same lower action, not just the recorded
pair of words.

In particular there is no genuine full profinite
\(\widehat{\mathrm{GT}}_0\) element inducing the
entire recorded normalized D.39 action. The same
conclusion holds for the particular quotient
automorphisms D.40--D.44 extending that action.
The retained infinite homology forces the multiplier
to be exactly one; thus changing its higher
congruence classes cannot remove this obstruction.
This conclusion concerns those actual quotient
actions. It does not exclude another action
having the same prescribed pair of CM coordinates.

Moreover, the sphere obstruction persists for every two-adic
multiplier preserving the recorded lower action: the cusp has
order \(32\) in both successive sphere quotients. The actual
source inner map \(\operatorname{Int}(c_0^{258})\) transports
the original D.33 action to this obstruction. Consequently that
original action has no full relative pro-two peripheral lift,
and its particular outer fibre in (D.35.5a) is empty. The
supplement below proves the torsion conjugacy, quotient
preservation and full cusp-inner normalization used in this transfer.

The second projection has a different proved
outcome. There is an actual peripheral automorphism
of \(B/D_{10}B'\), with multiplier one, that retains
the entire recorded action on \(B/D_9B'\).
The certificate specifies and evaluates literal
corrected \(B\)-words \(A_{\rm new},\beta_{\rm new}\)
of lengths \(3386495,3109533\).
Their cusp equation holds at all six graph vertices
through degree nine, and their Frattini action is
the identity. This establishes that finite
elliptic lift, without asserting simultaneous
compatibility with the excluded sphere action or
with all D.39 homology coordinates.

The computations address Part D(e), section 51.16,
folio 734 verso, scan 174, and D-after-LM2(1),
section 51.15, folios 725--727, scans 156--160.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The minimal bases and their literal reductions.}
An even image is generated by the images of \(r,srs\),
both of order dividing three. A group of order less
than \(12\) generated this way is a \(3\)-group:
the only possible orders divisible by three are
\(3,6,9\), and its Sylow \(3\)-subgroup is normal
in the order-six case. At order \(12\), a
nonnilpotent such image has four Sylow
\(3\)-subgroups. Its action on them is faithful:
the kernel lies in a Sylow normalizer of order
three, and a nontrivial kernel would make that
Sylow subgroup normal. The image is the
order-twelve subgroup \(A_4\) of \(S_4\).
Thus the least sign-preserving order is \(24\).

Conjugation by \(s\) is an involution of
\(A_4\), whose automorphism group is \(S_4\)
under its action on the four Sylow
\(3\)-subgroups. The nontrivial involutions
are inner double transpositions and outer
transpositions. The trivial action would
give \(r=srs\) and would not generate
\(A_4\). The inner case is exactly
\(A_4\times C_2\): if the action is
\(\operatorname{Int}(h)\), the mutually
inverse marked maps are
\[
    l s^\epsilon\longmapsto(lh^\epsilon,\epsilon),
       \qquad
    (m,\epsilon)\longmapsto mh^{-\epsilon}s^\epsilon.
\]
The outer case is \(S_4\). The program
\texttt{d\_cm\_solvable\_even\_boundary.py}
enumerates the two actual markings in
(D.45.1), not just their group orders.
The first is the canonical mod-four
quotient, also checked in D.44. The
second is the actual mod-three
\(A_4=\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\)
coordinate together with the mod-two
sign; the program checks all \(144\)
products in the displayed mod-three
source isomorphism. Their normal \(V_4\) reductions
are precisely the indicated \(S_3,C_6\).

For a normal subgroup of finite \(2\)-power
index, the induced pro-\(2\) topology is
the full one. Indeed take a subgroup
normal of \(2\)-power index in the
smaller group and intersect its finitely
many conjugates; then take the finite
normal \(2\)-power core if necessary.
This gives a subgroup normal of
\(2\)-power index in the larger group.
Applying this to the actual \(V_4\)
kernel reductions identifies their
relative completions with (D.45.2)
in both directions.

Let \(J\) be a finite common quotient
of the two groups in (D.45.2).
The images of their pro-\(2\) kernels
are contained in \(O_2(J)\).
Thus \(J/O_2(J)\) is a marked common
quotient of \(S_3\) and \(C_6\), and
is a quotient of \(C_2\). The group
\(J\) itself is a \(2\)-group.
The original order-three element
\(r\) is then trivial; \(s\) generates
the image, so \(J\) is a quotient
of \(C_2\). The sign is retained
on both sides, proving that the
maximal common marked quotient
is exactly \(C_2\). Closed Goursat
proves (D.45.3).

\emph{The two graph maps and their exact joint kernel.}
The marked Bass--Serre graphs each
have six edges, three \(s\)-vertices,
and two \(r\)-vertices; their free
rank is two. The chosen free bases are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 f_1&=r^{-1}s r^{-1}s,& f_2&=rsrs
                                 &&\text{for }F,\\
 e_1&=rs r^{-1}s,& e_2&=r^{-1}srs
                                 &&\text{for }B'.
 \end{aligned}                                                 \tag{D.45.8}
\]
In the recorded \(K\)-basis \(k_1,\ldots,k_4\),
the literal graph maps are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &F&(B')\\ \hline
 k_1&f_1^{-1}f_2^{-1}&e_1^{-1}e_2e_1^{-1}\\
 k_2&f_2^{-1}f_1^{-1}&e_2^{-1}e_1e_2^{-1}\\
 k_3&f_1^{-1}f_2f_1^{-1}&e_1^{-1}e_2^{-1}\\
 k_4&f_2^{-1}f_1f_2^{-1}&e_2^{-1}e_1^{-1}.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{D.45.9}
\]
The certificate checks these words and
all eighteen original \(Q_0\) section
words. They give a bijection onto
the literal fibre product
\(S_3\times_{C_2}C_6\).
Abelianizing (D.45.9) gives (D.45.4).
Conjugating the recorded D.33/D.39
homology matrix by this \(P\) gives
the two blocks (D.45.6), with zero
off-diagonal blocks.

The kernel of (D.45.3) over \(Q_0\)
is exactly the product of the two
pro-\(2\) kernels. Thus the image of
the actual \(K\) is dense in that
product, and its map extends to
the surjection (D.45.5).
Choose lifts \(a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2\)
of \((f_1,1),(f_2,1),(1,e_1),(1,e_2)\)
in \(K^{(2)}\). Their Frattini classes
are a basis, by the odd determinant
in (D.45.4). They are a free pro-\(2\)
basis of \(K^{(2)}\).
Quotienting by the closed normal
subgroup generated by
\([a_i,b_j]\), \(1\le i,j\le2\),
gives the product of the free
pro-\(2\) groups on the \(a_i\)
and the \(b_j\): the maps from
the quotient to that product
and back send those four generators
to each other. They are inverse
continuous homomorphisms.
This proves the exact kernel
description, with the source
map fixed by (D.45.9).

Geometrically the first subgroup
has three width-two cusps and genus
zero; the second has one width-six
cusp and genus one. These statements
also follow directly from the
regular permutation cycles of
\(t=sr\) on the two six-point
marked quotients and Euler
characteristic \(-1\).

\emph{The actual lower action and its degree-nine target.}
Functoriality gives
\(D_9K\subset D_9F\cap D_9B'\).
This proves the two source maps
in (D.45.7), and hence the
normalization transfer for the
recorded action. The program
\texttt{d\_cm\_rank2\_boundary\_test.py}
reconstructs the exact D.33 words
from the saved integer certificates,
checks both original SHA256 hashes,
and applies the retained D.39 gauge
\(k=-438\). It evaluates their

of both graphs. The raw cusp
word contains \(12921196\)
letters of \(B\).
The C++ helper uses exact
noncommutative mod-two Magnus
series, through degree nine.
An independent tuple-monomial
Cauchy calculation checks
its output on \(108\) graph
paths, including inverse
letters and every starting
vertex.

Put \(X=f_1-1,\ Y=f_2-1\)
in the sphere computation,
and use \(e_1-1,e_2-1\)
in the elliptic computation.
The homogeneous dimensions
of the restricted free Lie
algebra in degrees \(1,\ldots,9\)
are
\[
                2,\ 3,\ 2,\ 6,\ 6,\ 11,\ 18,\ 36,\ 56.        \tag{D.45.10}
\]
The program constructs a basis
by \(2\)-powers of the ordinary
Lyndon commutators, and verifies
independence in the free
associative algebra. In degree
nine these are the \(56\)
ordinary Lyndon commutators.
The finite kernels have orders
\(\lvert F/D_9F\rvert
=\lvert B'/D_9B'\rvert=2^{84}\)
and the next kernels have
order \(2^{140}\).

Write the recorded action as
\[
 \alpha(s)=\beta s\beta^{-1},\qquad
 \alpha(r)=A rA^{-1}.
\]
Both recorded conjugators lie
in the indicated free rank-two
kernel. The defect
\(\alpha(t)t^{-1}\) vanishes
through degree eight in both
graphs. Its degree-nine terms
\(d_F,d_E\) lie in the
degree-nine Lie subspace.
Multiplying the two conjugators
on the left by arbitrary
degree-nine central elements
changes that target by
\[
       (1-s)c_\beta+s(1-r)c_A.                                 \tag{D.45.11}
\]
Here \(s,r\) act by the actual
conjugation substitutions on
the source free group.
The \(112\) exact columns
of (D.45.11) have ranks
\(47\) and \(38\) in the
sphere and elliptic cases.
Both recorded targets are
outside those initial images.
This calculation alone would
only exclude the recorded
conjugator images. The next
step accounts for all the
remaining same-action freedom.

\emph{Complete lower centralizers and every same-action correction.}
In each of the two sources

computes the entire subgroup
\(C_{G/D_9G}(s)\), not only
the \(s\)-fixed subspace of
the last graded layer.
It uses the actual free

\(s e_i s\) at every degree.
For
\(1\to L_n\to G/D_{n+1}G
\to G/D_nG\to1\),
the map from a lower
centralizer element to the
class of \(s g s^{-1}g^{-1}\)
in \(L_n/(1-s)L_n\)
is the exact obstruction
to lifting that element.
The program evaluates this
map on actual reduced words.
Its kernel supplies all
liftable lower elements;
the fixed subspace of
\(L_n\) supplies all new
elements in the kernel.

At every step the produced
subgroup is elementary
abelian. This is checked
in the full quotient:
each generator has depth
at least \(\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil\),
so products are additive,
squares and commutators
vanish, and the displayed
truncated Magnus vectors
are independent. Induction
using the exact central
extension therefore proves
completeness. The successive
ranks in \(G/D_jG\),
\(j=2,\ldots,9\), are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
 j&2&3&4&5&6&7&8&9\\ \hline
 F&1&2&3&5&8&13&22&38\\
 B'&2&3&4&7&10&17&28&50 .
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{D.45.12}
\]
The corresponding connecting

\((0,1,0,2,0,1,0,4)\)
and \((0,2,1,3,3,4,7,14)\).
The final generators all

Their words and the complete
recursive construction
are in the certificate.

Replacing \(\beta\) on the

\(s\)-centralizer element
preserves its conjugation

Its degree-nine defect

No omitted freedom from

enlarge this obstruction
space. The order-three

central \(2\)-group
extension. Every lower
\(r\)-fixed element lifts

\(1+r+r^2\) norm, and

central correction is
already among (D.45.11).
This is also the finite

literal coordinates.

Before concluding

all possible conjugators
of a proposed lift.
Its \(S_3\) action equals
the identity, as does
the recorded action.
The image of an

Thus (D.45.11) and

exhaust all lifts of
this lower sphere
