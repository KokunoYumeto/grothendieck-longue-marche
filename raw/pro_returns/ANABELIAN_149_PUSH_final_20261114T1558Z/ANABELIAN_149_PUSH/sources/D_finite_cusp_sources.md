## Harari and Voloch (2010)

- Title: The Brauer–Manin obstruction for integral points on curves.
- Journal: Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society 149(3), 413–421.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004110000381.
- Primary author PDF: https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~david.harari/articles/dhvol.pdf.
- Local inspected files: tmp/D_HarariVoloch2010.pdf and .txt.
- Opened: Theorem 1, Conjecture 2, Remark 2.5 and the proof of Theorem 1, author pp.4–7. Publisher metadata/DOI were also opened.
- Use: exact outside-S closure/descent comparison; the needed diagonal statement is retained as Conjecture 2. Remark 2.5 distinguishes its formulation from the weaker existence form of Skolem's conjecture.

## Helm and Voloch (2011)

- Title: Finite descent obstruction on curves and modularity.
- Journal: Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society 43(4), 805–810.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/bdr015.
- Primary author PDF: https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/voloch/Preprints/modularity.pdf.
- Local inspected files: tmp/D_HelmVoloch2011.pdf and .txt.
- Opened: Proposition 1, Theorems 2–5 and Proposition 6, author pp.3–8.
- Use: Theorem 4 retains the common finite set, all good local elliptic curves outside it, and the integral Tate-module hypotheses. Theorem 3 gives existence on the indicated modular twist; it does not identify every local coordinate of a section. An arbitrary finite étale neighbourhood is not asserted to be a congruence modular curve.

## Liu and Xu (2015)

- Title: Very strong approximation for certain algebraic varieties.
- Journal: Mathematische Annalen 363, 701–731.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-014-1107-6.
- Opened primary version: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.1988, arXiv:1405.1988v2.
- Opened: Definition 1.1 and Theorems 1.2–1.5, pp.2–3; Proposition 4.6, pp.15–16; Corollary 5.11, p.24.
- Use: distinguish the projection of an all-place Brauer–Manin set from a condition involving only places outside S. Theorem 1.4 is the function-field case; the indicated stronger conclusion over Q in Corollary 5.11 has S archimedean. The missing ordinary components at cuspidal places cannot be supplied by an unmentioned hypothesis.

## Focused follow-up search, 14 November 2026

Poonen's primary four-page author note, https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/siegel_for_Q.pdf, dated 23 December 2020, was opened in full, especially Propositions 2.1–2.2, Theorem 2.3 and Section 3. Its cyclic-cover and Skolem argument proves finiteness of actual S-integral points. It does not identify a prescribed completed S-unit tuple with a diagonal point; it is not used as a substitute for the missing diagonal assertion.

The current primary arXiv version https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.09462, Betts–Kumpitsch–Lüdtke v2 dated 11 April 2026, was opened at Definition 1.5, Theorem A and Theorem 2.3. Its S-Selmer definition requires ordinary points also at every omitted place, and therefore does not silently include the finite local cusps left here. The newly posted Li–Lüdtke arXiv:2608.20615 abstract was opened only at the official arXiv page; no uninspected theorem from that paper is used. No exhaustive literature or priority claim is made.
