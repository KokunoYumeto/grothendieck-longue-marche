# Root review of the Legendre finite-ramification criterion

Reviewed on 14 November 2026. Verdict: PASS in the exact stated scope. This is not a proof that a noncuspidal section is a rational point section.

We read the full candidate, checked its dependencies D.6 and D.25, and separately opened the passages listed below. A local cuspidal splitting at the coefficient prime need not come from a rational tangent. Its Legendre representation, after a finite quadratic twist, is an extension of Q_l by Q_l(1). Berger's explicit Kummer computation covers every such class, including the split extension; potential semistability therefore follows. The quadratic semisimple monodromy at infinity is retained.

The character lemma is valid with a finite coefficient extension E/Q_l. Compactness puts the image in O_E^*. Raising to a suitable power and using the dense direct sum of the factors of the cyclotomic abelianization proves that all factors away from l are killed in a torsion-free pro-l quotient. Serre's local algebraicity theorem applies to the underlying Q_l representation of the E-line. Its algebraic inertia action centralizes E; a Q_l-defined cocharacter of Res_{E/Q_l} G_m has one integer exponent. Thus a global Hodge–Tate character of G_Q is finite order times an integral power of the cyclotomic character. No finite-ramification assumption is smuggled into this lemma.

If a Legendre representation had an invariant line over an algebraic coefficient closure, that line and its quotient would be defined over a finite coefficient field and would have exponents 0 and 1. Their traces at primes splitting the finite character would be p+1. Positive-density Chebotarev splitting intersects the density-one good integral set from D.25, contradicting the elliptic Weil bound. This proves absolute irreducibility before finite ramification is assumed.

For one l≥5, finite ramification, odd determinant, irreducibility and distinct potentially semistable local weights are exactly Pan's hypotheses. The l=3 residual exception is deliberately avoided. Any twisting character in the modular conclusion is itself de Rham, as a subobject of Hom(V_f,rho), so the character lemma reduces it to a finite character and a Tate power. The two weights force weight two and zero Tate power; the finite twist can be absorbed into the eigenform. The determinant makes its nebentype trivial.

Outside a fixed finite set, an ordinary local point has good elliptic reduction by Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich. The displayed Legendre j-function has negative valuation at every nonintegral tripod parameter at an odd prime, so such a point must be integral. A cuspidal local representation has quadratic-twisted triangular form. Unramifiedness of the whole representation forces the quadratic character to be unramified there, and its trace is then ±(p+1), contradicting modular purity. This includes a zero local monodromy operator. Conversely integral local points outside one finite set make every coefficient-prime representation unramified away from that set and the coefficient prime. The three ramification/integrality conditions are therefore equivalent.

For the elliptic realization, equality of rational integer traces on a density-one set gives equality of the characters of f and each coefficient conjugate by Chebotarev and continuity. The away-from-level newform multiplicity-one statement in Stein §9.2 then gives a rational coefficient field. Eichler–Shimura supplies an elliptic curve; only existence up to isogeny is used. Comparing integer traces on the common good set and using semisimplicity identifies all rational Tate representations. This does not identify the local parameters or the full nonabelian section.

The original stronger prospective inference to a rational point was explicitly rejected during review: cofinite integrality allows finitely many cuspidal local sections, whereas D.5 and Stix's Corollary 6 require ordinary local points everywhere. That additional hypothesis is not established. Even the common elliptic realization concerns a quotient representation and cannot replace the missing full-section argument.

The separate cuspidal example is an actual continuous section: multiply the Kummer cocycle of 13 by the idempotent with 5-primary component one and all other components zero, and twist the standard splitting at t=0. At 13, the reference Tate unit has valuation zero and −1 is a square. Its extra monodromy has coefficient 2 at l=5 and zero at l=7; both semisimplifications have polynomial (T−1)(T−13). The exact finite certificate checks the compatible CRT residues and tame matrix relation. This refutes an unconditional strict cross-prime monodromy inference, not the noncuspidal section conjecture.
