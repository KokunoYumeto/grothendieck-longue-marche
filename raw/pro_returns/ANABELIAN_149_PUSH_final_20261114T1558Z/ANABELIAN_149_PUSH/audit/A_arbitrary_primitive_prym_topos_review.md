# Independent review: arbitrary odd primitive Pryms

**Reviewed:** audit/A_arbitrary_primitive_prym_candidate.md, Sections 1–9, 14 November 2026. This is the complete written candidate for A.C2.19.

**Conclusion:** the argument passes independent review. In particular, the controlled-field step, descent of the free action, and reduced two-orbit fibre argument do prove the advertised extension from two prime divisors to every odd conductor. No universal existence of the primitive-orbit theta condition is inferred.

## Controlled fields and the numerical bound

I reopened Poonen's primary author PDF and read Proposition 2.1, Lemma 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 on author page 3. The candidate reproduces the actual induction correctly. If \([F:M]=d/s\) and \(a=[M(h):M]\), then
\[
[F:M(h)]=d/(as),\qquad [M(h):k(h)]=as.
\]
The fields \(M,k(h)\) generate \(M(h)\), so these are precisely the two indices needed in Castelnuovo–Severi. The displayed algebraic rearrangement is correct, including its nonnegative correction term \(as(a-1)(s-1)\). There is no reduction of a many-generator problem to two original generators without proof.

For \(r\ge2\), the numerical inequality \(n>(2^r-1)^2\) is valid. The separate \(r=2\) case has minimum \(15>9\). For \(r\ge3\), the estimate \(n\ge3\cdot5^{r-1}>4^r\) is deliberately weaker than the product of the actual distinct smallest primes, but valid. Its base case is \(75>64\), and its ratio increases by \(5/4\).

## Collision support and separability

The product \(\prod_{\ell\mid n}(1-\sigma^{n/\ell})\) annihilates all nonprimitive character summands exactly, while the primitive summands have zero étale two-adic rank by the theta hypothesis. Integral odd-order idempotents therefore give annihilation of all geometric two-primary torsion. Duality only inverts indices and preserves primitivity.

The subset products of the prime-order elements are distinct because the corresponding subgroups have pairwise coprime orders. Freeness and distinct images of \(P',Q'\) on \(C\) make all \(2^{r+1}\) points distinct. Thus the positive and negative parts of the principal collision divisor each have degree \(d=2^r\), with coefficient one everywhere. Its function is separable: a characteristic-two square cannot have a simple zero or pole.

This last property also ensures that every intermediate extension of \(k(D)/k(f)\), including the map to the deck-translates field, is separable.

## Action on the controlled quotient

The field generated by every deck translate is stable under the deck group. Its index \(e\) divides \(d\), so it is a power of two. The genus bound excludes \(e=1\).

The induced action of the odd group \(G\) is faithful. Its kernel acts on the finite separable field extension \(k(D)/M\); its order divides the extension degree by the fixed-field degree formula, and also divides the odd number \(n\). It is therefore trivial.

More strongly the action on the quotient curve \(Z\) is free. A hypothetical stabilizer contains an odd prime-order element. Its action on the degree-\(e\) fibre upstairs is free, and preserves the ramification multiplicities. The fibre's total degree would be divisible by that odd prime, contradicting that \(e\) is a power of two. Merely counting the number of points would have been insufficient for a possibly ramified fibre; the candidate uses the correct multiplicity-weighted argument.

Riemann–Hurwitz for the resulting étale degree-\(n\) quotient \(Z\to Z/G\) gives
\[
g(Z)-1=n(g(Z/G)-1).
\]
Thus \(g(Z)\) is one or at least \(n+1\); genus zero of the quotient would give a negative genus for \(Z\). The controlled-field upper bound excludes the latter case, giving \(g(Z)=1\).

## Why the remaining map has degree two

This is the decisive new step, and it is valid. Since \(f\in M\), choose a zero of the induced nonconstant function on \(Z\). Every point of its complete inverse-image fibre is a zero of \(f\). Reducedness of the zero divisor forces the zero order downstairs and every ramification index above it to equal one. Since the field extension is separable and the constant field is algebraically closed, that fibre contains exactly \(e\) distinct geometric points.

Every one of those points lies in the union of the two original deck orbits. Each orbit maps injectively to \(Z\), since the action on \(Z\) is free and the map is equivariant. Therefore that fibre contains at most two points, so \(e\le2\). Combining this with \(e>1\) gives \(e=2\).

The quadratic map is separable, so its involution exists. Stability of \(M\) under \(G\) makes \(G\) normalize its order-two deck group, and hence centralize it. The candidate correctly avoids assuming a general uniqueness theorem for bielliptic involutions at the larger original pencil degree.

## Ramification and contradiction

The rest is the checked argument of A.C2.17. The nonempty ramification locus is a union of free \(n\)-orbits, while the total different degree is \(2n\) and every ramification point contributes at least two. There are exactly \(n\) ramification points. Crew's ramified Deuring–Shafarevich formula yields \(f(D)\ge n-1\).

The independent divisor proof retained in the candidate also gives this lower bound: the free elliptic action is by translations, the \(n[R_i-R_0]\) are two-torsion, and an invariant principal relation would descend and have even valuations, contradicting its odd coefficient \(n\). The primitive vanishing estimate gives \(f(D)\le n+1-\varphi(n)<n-1\), completing the contradiction.

The passage from equal point-section classes to the lifted two-primary divisor difference is exactly A.3. Finite-extension forms and open-subcurve consequences use no extra arithmetic hypothesis. The only remaining existence input is the explicitly retained primitive-orbit theta condition.
