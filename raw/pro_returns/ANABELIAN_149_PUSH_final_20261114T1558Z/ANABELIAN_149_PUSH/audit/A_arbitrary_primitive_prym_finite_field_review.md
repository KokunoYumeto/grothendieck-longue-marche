# Independent Part-A review of the arbitrary-conductor primitive Prym criterion

Status: **APPROVED**, 14 November 2026. Reviewer: the Part-A/finite-field agent.
Reviewed candidate: audit/A_arbitrary_primitive_prym_candidate.md. This review does not itself change the integrated claim or assert universal existence.

I read the entire proof and reopened Poonen's primary author PDF, *Gonality of modular curves in characteristic p*, https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/gonality.pdf, Section2, Proposition2.1, Lemma2.2 and Corollary2.3, authorp.3 (opened source turn694view0). The controlled-field corollary is exactly the many-generator statement used here. Its short Castelnuovo–Severi induction is reproduced with the correct indices; there is no assumption that two deck-translated functions already generate the entire field.

The operator product over every prime divisor of n is supported on 2^r distinct deck elements. Distinctness follows by projection to each prime-order factor in the cyclic group, including prime-power conductors. Applied to a difference of points lying over distinct base points, its positive and negative divisors are disjoint, reduced, and each has degree2^r. The resulting function is separable because it has simple zeros. The primitive character vanishing kills the integral two-adic summands, while every remaining character is annihilated by at least one operator factor; this correctly avoids inferring an integral endomorphism identity from reduction modulo two alone.

The subfield generated by all deck translates is stable under the deck group. Its index e divides 2^r. Any stabilizer on its curve Z would act freely on the invariant degree-e fibre divisor, so its odd order would divide e; therefore the action is free. The candidate's additional faithfulness argument is valid, but freeness also excludes a kernel directly. The controlled-field genus bound and n>(2^r−1)^2 force genusZ=1.

The key strengthening is the support argument. A zero fibre of the descended function on Z pulls back to a reduced divisor, so it contains e distinct points and has no hidden inseparable or ramification multiplicity. Because G acts freely on Z, each of the two original G-orbits maps injectively to Z. This fibre can therefore have at most two points. Hence e=2. The quadratic extension is separable, its involution is normalized and thus centralized by G, and the ramification set is nonempty by genus. Its size is a positive multiple of n and at most n by the total different2n and local lower bound2, so it has exactly n points. The Deuring–Shafarevich lower bound f(D)>=n−1 contradicts the primitive-vanishing bound. The supplied independent ramification two-torsion construction gives the same contradiction.

The inequality n>(2^r−1)^2 holds for r=2 by n>=15>9, and for r>=3 by n>=3*5^(r−1)>4^r. Prime-power n is already handled by A.C2.8. Thus all conductor cases are covered without changing the theta-orbit hypothesis.

The conclusion is conditional on every primitive doubling orbit meeting theta, or equivalently the stated primitive two-rank-zero condition. One arbitrary prime-to-two theta point, torsion density, or projection to a primary component does not give that condition. The candidate preserves this limitation and makes no universal A1 claim. I found no mathematical gap in its stated scope.
