# A uniform difference bound for cyclic theta packets

Status: root and topos approved; integrated as A.C2.21 in sections/A_char2_deck.tex with the complete proof and exact lower bounds.
Written by the root on14November2026. No priority is claimed.
Question locus:149, manuscriptpp.17–17v, scans35–36.

## 1. Statement

Let C be any smooth proper genus-two curve over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic two, let Theta be its symmetric
Raynaud theta curve, and let L have exact odd order n>1. Put

    S={i in Z/n : L^i belongs to Theta},
    b(n)=floor((1+sqrt(8n−7))/2),
    nu=ord_n(2).

Then

    |S|(|S|−1) ≤ 2(n−1),  hence |S|≤b(n).

For the cyclic étale cover D→C attached to L, write f_prim(D)
for the two-rank of its primitive conductor-n Jacobian factor.
One always has

    f_prim(D) ≥ max(0, phi(n)−nu*b(n)).

If −1 belongs to the subgroup generated by2 modulo n, symmetry
strengthens this to

    f_prim(D) ≥ max(0, phi(n)−nu*floor(b(n)/2)).

In particular every cyclic étale127-cover of every such C has
primitive two-rank at least14, and every cyclic étale257-cover has
primitive two-rank at least80. Neither conductor can provide a
zero-two-rank primitive Prym on a genus-two curve in characteristic
two. Since these conductors are prime, their deck actions on
geometric two-primary torsion are necessarily faithful.

These are obstructions to specified cover methods, not
counterexamples to point-section injectivity.

## 2. At most two ordered differences

Write the theta curve as the injective Abel image

    R maps to O(R−B_theta),

where B_theta is its theta characteristic. For any nonzero degree-zero
class T, there are at most two ordered pairs (P,Q) of points of C
with [P−Q]=T.

Indeed suppose (P,Q) and (P',Q') are distinct such pairs. They give

    P+Q' ~ P'+Q.

The two effective degree-two divisors cannot be equal as formal
divisors unless the pairs were equal or P=Q, the latter contradicting
T≠0. Their distinct linear equivalence therefore gives the unique
genus-two hyperelliptic pencil. If iota is its involution, one must
have Q'=iota(P) and P'=iota(Q). Thus a second ordered pair, if it
exists, is uniquely determined. This proof also covers ramification
points and uses no transversality assumption on theta intersections.

For each nonzero k in Z/n, an ordered pair of distinct indices
(i,j) in S with i−j=k gives such a pair with T=L^k. Exact order of L
makes T nonzero. There are at most two pairs for each k. Summing
over all n−1 nonzero differences gives the stated quadratic bound.
Solving that quadratic inequality gives b(n). This is equivalently
the set-theoretic consequence of Theta squared=2, but the divisor
argument proves exactly the cardinality assertion used here.

## 3. The primitive character lower bound

Each primitive doubling orbit modulo n has length nu, and there
are phi(n)/nu such orbits. By A.C2.5, a one-dimensional character
cycle on this genus-two cover is nilpotent precisely when it meets
theta; otherwise it contributes its full orbit length to the
stable Frobenius part. The primitive part therefore has two-rank

    nu * (number of primitive doubling orbits missing S).

Every killed primitive orbit requires at least one point of S in
that orbit. Distinct orbits require distinct indices. At most
b(n) primitive orbits can therefore be killed, proving the first
lower bound. Points of S at nonprimitive indices, including the
origin when it lies on theta, cannot help to kill a primitive orbit.

If −1 is a power of2 modulo n, negation preserves each primitive
doubling orbit. Symmetry of Theta then makes every killed primitive
orbit contain both i and−i. These are distinct because n is odd and
i is a unit. Each killed orbit now requires at least two distinct
indices of S, so at most floor(b(n)/2) primitive orbits can be killed.
This proves the strengthened bound. No choice of a representative
of a Frobenius orbit changes the count.

## 4. The two explicit forbidden conductors

At n=127, one has phi(n)=126 and nu=7, since2^7=1 modulo127
and7 is prime. Negation is outside this odd-order subgroup.
The quadratic bound gives b(127)=16. Thus

    f_prim(D) ≥ 126−7*16 =14.

There are18 primitive doubling cycles but at most16 theta points
in any cyclic subgroup. They cannot all be hit.

At n=257, one has phi(n)=256 and nu=16, with2^8=−1 modulo257.
Here b(257)=23, and negation preserves each primitive doubling
cycle. The stronger bound gives

    f_prim(D) ≥ 256−16*floor(23/2)=80.

At either prime conductor, a surviving nontrivial character is
faithful. The lower bounds therefore also imply faithful deck
action on the geometric two-torsion and the full étale two-adic
Tate module. They do not assert that any point-section collision
exists.

## 5. Scope and dependencies

The proof uses only the genus-two Abel embedding and unique
hyperelliptic pencil, plus the already checked character/Frobenius
calculation A.C2.5 and integral interpretation A.C2.8. No extra
prime-order existence theorem is used. The accompanying exact
integer program checks the displayed orders, cycle counts,
quadratic bounds and lower ranks. The argument for all n is the
proof above, not an extrapolation from those finite checks.

The result makes one limitation of A.C2.19–20 precise: primitive
vanishing is a substantive condition, and for some fixed odd
conductors it is impossible for every genus-two curve. A.C2.14
separately prevents choosing a single fixed finite set of primes
for all curves. Neither statement rules out finding another
suitable conductor for a particular curve.
