# A universal-tripod example for the small geometric Galois criterion

This note supplies a concrete example for the independently reviewed F.49 sufficient theorem. It is not integrated. Let
\[
 U=\mathbf P^1_{\bar{\mathbf Q}}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\},
 \qquad K_0=\bar{\mathbf Q}(t),\qquad D=\mathbf Q(t).
\]
Inside a fixed separable closure of \(D\), let \(k/K_0\) be the union of the function fields of all connected finite étale covers of \(U\), equivalently the maximal extension unramified over \(U\).

## 1. Normality and smallness

Finite étale covers are stable under fibre products, passage to normal closure and normalization. Hence \(k/K_0\) is Galois. Every automorphism over \(D\) preserves \(\bar{\mathbf Q}\), the coordinate \(t\), and the rational branch set \(\{0,1,\infty\}\). It therefore preserves the family of these unramified extensions. Thus \(k/D\) is algebraic Galois and contains \(\bar{\mathbf Q}\).

By geometric base-change invariance and Riemann existence,
\[
                 \operatorname{Gal}(k/K_0)
                    \simeq\pi_1(U)\simeq\widehat F_2.
 \tag{T.1}
\]
The exact primary statements were opened locally in the supplied complete SGA1 source:

* Exposé XII, Theorem5.1, printed p.333, identifies finite étale covers of a complex finite-type scheme with finite topological covers.
* Exposé XIII, 4.5.3, printed p.421, gives the arbitrary-base-change comparison for the pointed smooth curve with normal-crossings compactification. Apply it to the tripod over the algebraically closed fields \(\bar{\mathbf Q}\subset\mathbf C\), with all primes since the characteristic is zero.

The complex tripod deformation retracts onto a wedge of two circles, giving the free profinite group in (T.1). The local source is background/literature/SGA1/src_arXiv_math_0206203/smf_doc-math_3_01.tex; the two exact passages begin around lines20306 and24866. Existing reference E_SGA1 suffices.

A profinite group topologically generated by two elements is small. An open subgroup of index \(d\) gives a continuous coset representation into \(S_d\); there are only finitely many homomorphisms into this finite group, since the images of the two generators determine one. For each such action there are finitely many point stabilizers. Thus \(\widehat F_2\) has finitely many open subgroups of each fixed index. F.49 therefore gives reconstruction over \(k\), over every finitely generated extension of \(k\), and over every positive-transcendence subfield of such an extension.

## 2. It lies outside the full F.46 sufficient class

Every open subgroup \(V\subset\widehat F_2\) is free profinite of rank
\[
                         1+[\widehat F_2:V]\ge2.
\]
One can see this directly by taking the finite covering of the two-petal rose corresponding to \(V\cap F_2\): its fundamental group is free of that rank, and the full finite-index subgroup topology is induced by the ambient free group. This is also the Schreier calculation already proved and used in F.11.

For arbitrarily large odd \(n\), \(A_n\) is generated by the two elements \((1\,2\,\cdots\,n)\) and \((1\,2\,3)\). Conjugation supplies the adjacent3-cycles, which generate \(A_n\). Thus every such \(V\), and each of its open subgroups, maps onto \(A_n\) for arbitrarily large \(n\). No \(V\) is virtually pro-\(\mathcal C\) for a proper subclass \(\mathcal C\) of the finite groups that contains cyclic groups and is closed under subgroups, quotients and extensions: the elementary argument in F.44 excludes all sufficiently large \(A_n\) from any such \(\mathcal C\).

This excludes every alternative finitely generated algebraic model, not just \(D=\mathbf Q(t)\). Suppose \(F_0\subset k\) is finitely generated over \(\mathbf Q\), \(k/F_0\) is algebraic, and the geometric normal closure \(N/F_0\bar{\mathbf Q}\) has a virtually pro-\(\mathcal C\) Galois group. The field
\[
                    B=F_0\bar{\mathbf Q}\,\mathbf Q(t)
\]
lies in \(k\) and is finite over both \(F_0\bar{\mathbf Q}\) and \(K_0\). Indeed the two fields are finitely generated one-variable function fields over \(\bar{\mathbf Q}\), inside a common algebraic extension; adjoining their finite generating sets gives a finite extension in each direction.

The group \(\operatorname{Gal}(N/B)\) is open in the proposed virtually pro-\(\mathcal C\) group. Since \(k/K_0\) is Galois and \(K_0\subset B\subset k\), the extension \(k/B\) is Galois. Restriction gives a surjection
\[
                  \operatorname{Gal}(N/B)
                       \twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(k/B).
\]
Its target is an open subgroup of \(\widehat F_2\). Quotients and open subgroups preserve virtual pro-\(\mathcal C\), contradicting the preceding paragraph. Hence \(k\) lies outside F.46's entire sufficient embedding class, including its equivalent geometric-normal-closure formulation.

## 3. Why radical closedness must not be added to the hypotheses

All \(n\)-th roots of \(t\) and of \(1-t\) lie in \(k\), since their cyclic covers ramify only at the removed boundary points. However \(t-2\) is not a square in \(k\). At the ordinary place \(t=2\), every finite subextension of \(k/K_0\) is unramified. A coherent prolongation of this discrete valuation through the union therefore retains value group \(\mathbf Z\), with \(v(t-2)=1\). A square would have even value.

Thus the example is not radically closed. This is consistent with the weaker conclusion \(D(E^\times)\subset k^\times\) used in F.49, and shows why replacing that inclusion by the equality \(D(k^\times)=k^\times\) would incorrectly discard the example.

No property is inferred by taking a filtered union of reconstruction fields. F.49 proves the infinite field directly from normality and smallness. The henselian necessary obstructions F.47–F.48 do not apply to these uncompleted, nonhenselian function fields.
