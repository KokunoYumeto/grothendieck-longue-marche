# The remaining nonabelian existence gap

Status: a precise obstruction to an inference, not a counterexample to A1 and not a claim that the required covers do not exist. This note accompanies the proposed abelian descent theorem A.C2.23. No integrated claim is modified.

Question locus: 149; manuscript pp.17–17v; scans35–36. All deductions below belong to this audit.

## 1. What the opened existence theorem actually supplies

Raynaud (2000), Theorem 2, gives every genus-\(g\ge2\) curve a finite solvable prime-to-\(p\) étale cover and a finite representation having no theta divisor. Section 1 identifies nonordinariness with a nonzero kernel of the relevant Frobenius map on \(H^1\). Having no theta divisor gives nonzero kernels after every degree-zero line-bundle twist. Theorem 17 constructs the required higher-rank representation by a Heisenberg cover under its explicit polarization divisibility and slope conditions.

These statements assert neither Frobenius nilpotence nor zero \(p\)-rank of an entire nontrivial isotypic factor. The distinction matters: in genus two a nontrivial one-dimensional character has one-dimensional \(H^1\), but a nonabelian irreducible representation of rank \(r>1\) has a multiplicity space of dimension \(r(g-1)\).

For the source's universal genus-two construction in characteristic two, the proof of Theorem 2 first takes a cyclic cover of degree five, of genus six, and then uses \(n=3\) in Theorem 17. The Heisenberg representation has rank \(3^6\); the cohomological multiplicity space on that genus-six intermediate base has dimension \(5\cdot3^6\). The conclusion that a Frobenius map has a nonzero kernel does not make that space nilpotent.

## 2. A group-algebra annihilator sees support, not multiplicity

Let \(G\) be a finite group of order prime to \(p\), and let \(T\) be a characteristic-zero representation over a splitting field. Write

\[
T=\bigoplus_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}
W_\rho\otimes M_\rho.
\]

The group algebra acts on \(W_\rho\) and acts trivially on the multiplicity space \(M_\rho\). Its annihilator on \(T\) is therefore

\[
\operatorname{Ann}(T)
=\bigoplus_{\rho:\,M_\rho=0}
\operatorname{End}(W_\rho)
\]

under the Wedderburn decomposition. If \(M_\rho\ne0\), every nonzero matrix in that simple block acts nontrivially on \(W_\rho\otimes M_\rho\).

Thus decreasing a multiplicity from \(r(g-1)\) to a smaller positive integer does not create any group-algebra annihilator. A sparse integral operator that kills all geometric \(p\)-primary torsion is stronger still. If the rational Tate module retains every irreducible, its group-algebra annihilator is zero, so no such nonzero operator exists.

The same distinction holds for faithfulness of the deck action: losing copies of an irreducible does not change its kernel.

## 3. An exact Heisenberg profile demonstrating the algebraic issue

Let \(G\) be the Heisenberg group of order 27, generated by \(x,y,z\) with \(z\) central, all three generators of order three, and \(yx=zxy\). Over \(\mathbf F_4\), its irreducibles consist of nine linear characters and two three-dimensional representations, distinguished by the two nontrivial central characters.

The Frobenius automorphism of \(\mathbf F_4/\mathbf F_2\) pairs the eight nontrivial linear characters and pairs the two three-dimensional representations. Consequently

\[
\mathbf F_2[G]\simeq
\mathbf F_2\times\mathbf F_4^4\times M_3(\mathbf F_4).
\tag{RG.1}
\]

For a prime-to-characteristic étale \(G\)-cover of a genus-two curve, the coherent Chevalley–Weil module is

\[
\mathbf 1\oplus k[G].
\]

Here this identity can be checked directly: the bundle attached to a nontrivial irreducible \(W\) has degree zero, has no global sections because the connected cover has only constant global functions, and has \(h^1=\dim(W)(g-1)\) by Riemann–Roch. The trivial representation contributes \(g=2\). Semisimplicity reconstructs the displayed module.

Its dimension is 28. Consider the following **abstract** semilinear Frobenius profile on that module. It is bijective on the two trivial dimensions and all nontrivial linear-character blocks. On the multiplicity-three block of each three-dimensional representation, let it preserve two copies and kill the third, compatibly with the interchange of the two coefficient-conjugate representations.

The stable dimension is then

\[
2+8+2\cdot3\cdot2=22,
\]

so this profile is nonordinary. Nevertheless every irreducible representation occurs in its stable part. The resulting module over (RG.1) is faithful as a group-algebra module, and its annihilator is zero. The corresponding characteristic-zero odd-group representations lift using the third roots of unity in an unramified coefficient extension, with the same representation support.

This is not asserted to be the Frobenius of an actual curve, and it does not model the full family of twists in Raynaud's no-theta assertion. Its purpose is exact and limited: nonordinariness, the coherent multiplicity formula, semilinearity, and the deck action do not by themselves imply disappearance of a representation or a group-algebra annihilator.

The certificate code/BC_abelian_descent_and_support_checks.py constructs all 27 matrices on the 22-dimensional \(\mathbf F_2\)-space, checks all 729 multiplication identities, and verifies that their linear span has dimension 27. It also constructs the commuting idempotent on the 28-dimensional coherent module, of stable rank 22. The output explicitly records that no geometric realization is claimed.

## 4. Why merely repeating a singular map does not repair the gap

Even a uniformly singular family of maps need not have nilpotent products. On a vector space of dimension at least two, the constant projector

\[
\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,0)
\]

has a nonzero kernel at every step and remains unchanged under every positive power. More generally, a rank-\(r-1\) image can avoid the next one-dimensional kernel, so the product can keep the same positive rank.

This elementary example does not disprove a possible geometric theorem for Raynaud's bundles. It identifies what such a theorem must add: a relation forcing successive images into the subsequent kernels, or an equivalent vanishing of the complete stable multiplicity space. The opened existence statements do not provide that relation.

There is also no monotonicity principle for tensoring arbitrary finite representations: Raynaud's Proposition 1(6) explicitly produces a finite representation whose tensor product with a prescribed one is ordinary. A proposed iteration must therefore specify how its actual Frobenius subspaces are controlled.

## 5. An annihilator depending on one collision is a different problem

If a fixed vector lies in \(W_\rho\otimes M_\rho\) with \(\dim M_\rho<\dim W_\rho\), some nonzero matrix in \(\operatorname{End}(W_\rho)\) can annihilate that vector. This does not mean that the whole representation is absent.

For a \(p^a\)-torsion divisor class, an operator chosen in a \(p\)-adic group algebra can be approximated modulo \(p^a\) by an integral one. Its coefficients, however, can grow with \(p^a\), and its support need not be small. The controlled-field argument of A.C2.20 needs a divisor with a bounded degree determined by a sparse operator with controlled integer coefficients. A general matrix annihilator does not supply that bound. No uniform control of the torsion exponent or coefficient size has been proved here.

## 6. The exact missing existence assertion

A genuinely new nonabelian route must supply one of the following additional facts, with hypotheses that apply to the fixed curve:

1. an odd cyclic étale cover of some intermediate base whose full primitive \(p\)-rank is zero, with a transfer argument to the original curve;
2. disappearance of enough complete nonabelian isotypic factors to produce a specified sparse integral annihilator;
3. a direct sparse annihilator of every potential collision class, with coefficients and support bounded strongly enough for the controlled-field inequality.

The abelian-total-cover version of the first route is exactly equivalent to the original cyclic existence question by A.C2.23. The higher-rank no-theta theorem does not yet prove any of the three stronger conclusions.

For a fixed finite-field genus-two curve, prime-to-two torsion density on its theta curve remains insufficient on its own: A.C2.19 requires every primitive doubling orbit for one cyclic conductor to meet theta. Neither replacing an order by its prime-power part nor grouping characters after abelian base change preserves this condition automatically.
