# Root review of A.C2.19

Reviewed on 14 November 2026. Verdict: PASS for the stated sufficient criterion; no universal existence assertion is accepted.

We read the full candidate and the integrated source `sections/A_arbitrary_primitive_prym.tex`. The controlled-field argument is the exact multi-generator induction in Poonen, *Gonality of modular curves in characteristic p* (2007), Proposition 2.1, Lemma 2.2 and Corollary 2.3, author page 3, opened separately by the root. In the induction, adjoining a degree-d function to an intermediate field of index d/s gives degree a over that field and degree as over the new rational subfield. The Castelnuovo–Severi expression is

`a(s−1)^2+(a−1)(as−1)=(as−1)^2−as(a−1)(s−1)`.

All extensions involved in the eventual geometric application are separable: the original function has simple zeros and poles. The estimate therefore applies in the required characteristic-two setting without an omitted inseparability assumption.

The group-ring element has nonzero character scalar exactly at primitive conductor n. Splitting the odd cyclic representation over the unramified two-adic coefficient extension is integral; the argument kills all geometric two-primary torsion, not merely its mod-two quotient. The subset products are distinct because their factors lie in the independent prime-order subgroups. The resulting divisor has exactly 2^ω(n) simple zeros and the same number of simple poles in two disjoint free deck orbits.

The inequality n>(2^ω(n)−1)^2 holds for all odd n>1. For at least three prime divisors, the intentionally weaker estimate 3·5^(r−1)>4^r suffices. The field generated by all deck translates is stable. Its degree e divides a power of two; faithfulness and freeness of the induced odd-order action follow respectively from the kernel-degree argument and the fibre divisor multiplicities. Riemann–Hurwitz then forces the quotient curve to have genus one. A reduced zero fibre contains at most one point from each of the two original deck orbits, proving e=2.

The quadratic involution centralizes the deck action. Its nonempty ramification set is a union of free odd deck orbits, while total different is 2n and each wild quadratic ramification point contributes at least two. Thus there are exactly n such points. Crew (1984), Corollary 1.8, printed pages 35–36, was opened separately by the root and supplies the ramified Deuring–Shafarevich formula. It yields f(D)≥n−1, contradicting the primitive-character upper bound. The independent two-torsion argument retained in A.C2.17 also supports this last inequality.

The prime-power case is explicitly delegated to A.C2.8; all finite-extension, form and open-subcurve transfers use A.3 and the actual fundamental-group comparison. Nothing here asserts the existence of a suitable L for every curve. One theta point does not in general meet every primitive doubling orbit.
