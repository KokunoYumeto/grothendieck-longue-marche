# F.47 candidate: a henselization obstruction to reconstruction

**Status:** candidate for independent review; not integrated. This concerns the actual closed-point-preserving convention of CHW. It does not modify the literal-R27 counterexamples or resolve the intrinsic converse classification F.9.

## 1. Statement and exact scope

Let \(F\) be any characteristic-zero field, let \(t\) be an indeterminate, and put

\[
 A_0(F)=\{a\in F((t)):a\text{ is algebraic over }F(t)\}.
 \tag{47.1}
\]

Then \(A_0(F)\) is the fraction field of the henselization of \(F[t]_{(t)}\). Neither \(A_0(F)\) nor any algebraic extension of it satisfies closed-point-preserving étale reconstruction. Over \(A_0(F)\) itself there is an explicit nonalgebraic, nonconstant, pinned relative geometric automorphism of the full small étale topos of \(\mathbb G_m\), induced semilinearly by

\[
                         t\longmapsto t+t^2.
 \tag{47.2}
\]

The absolute Galois group has the usual split tame description

\[
 G_{A_0(F)}\simeq\widehat{\mathbf Z}(1)\rtimes G_F,
 \tag{47.3}
\]

with coefficientwise splitting and the cyclotomic conjugation action on inertia. The lift of (47.2) used here centralizes this entire group, including its inertia subgroup.

There is also an intrinsic necessary condition. Suppose \(K\) has characteristic zero and finite transcendence degree \(d\geq1\) over \(\mathbf Q\). If \(K\) admits a nontrivial henselian valuation with residue field \(\kappa\) such that

\[
 \operatorname{char}\kappa=0,
 \qquad \operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbf Q}\kappa=d-1,
 \tag{47.4}
\]

then \(K\) does not reconstruct. The valuation need not be discrete. In particular the conclusion applies to a henselian discrete valuation having the stated residue transcendence degree. No conclusion is asserted for an arbitrary transcendental extension of \(A_0(F)\), or for a henselian valuation failing the residue-degree hypothesis (47.4).

## 2. Identification with the ordinary henselization

Set \(R=F[t]_{(t)}\). The field \(F\) is excellent; localization of a finite-type algebra preserves excellence. Thus \(R\) is a Noetherian local domain and a G-ring. The current Stacks Project statements are Proposition 15.53.3 and Lemma 15.53.2, Tags 07QW and 07QU (the section number changed from 15.52 in older copies).

Stacks Example 16.13.3, Tag 0A1W, identifies \(R^h\subset\widehat R=F[[t]]\) with the elements of \(F[[t]]\) algebraic over \(F(t)\). Its map into the completion is injective and is the standard henselization embedding. Every element \(a\in A_0(F)\) becomes a power series after multiplication by some \(t^m\), and \(t^m a\) is still algebraic over \(F(t)\). Consequently

\[
                         A_0(F)=\operatorname{Frac}(R^h).
 \tag{47.5}
\]

This is the ordinary henselization, without adjoining all roots of \(t\). In particular its value group is \(\mathbf Z\), and its residue field is \(F\). Alternatively, these two facts follow immediately from \(F(t)\subset A_0(F)\subset F((t))\).

## 3. An explicit algebraic closure and its generators

Choose an algebraic closure \(\bar F\) and compatible symbols \(t^{1/n}\), so \((t^{1/mn})^m=t^{1/n}\). Use the algebraic closure from van den Dries–Ribenboim (1984), Proposition on p. 314:

\[
 \Omega=
 \bigcup_{\substack{F'/F\ \mathrm{finite},\ F'\subset\bar F\\n\geq1}}
             F'((t^{1/n})).
 \tag{47.6}
\]

Each element has all its coefficients in **one finite extension** of \(F\). We do not replace (47.6) by all Laurent series over \(\bar F\). Let

\[
 \Omega_0=\{a\in\Omega:a\text{ is algebraic over }F(t)\}.
\]

It is algebraically closed: any root in \(\Omega\) of a polynomial with coefficients in \(\Omega_0\) is algebraic over the field generated by finitely many such coefficients, hence algebraic over \(F(t)\). Since \(F(t)\subset A_0(F)\subset\Omega_0\), it is an algebraic closure of \(A_0(F)\).

We prove the precise field-generation statement

\[
 \Omega_0=
 \bigcup_{\substack{F'/F\ \mathrm{finite}\\n\geq1}}
                       A_0(F)F'(t^{1/n}).
 \tag{47.7}
\]

The right side is plainly contained in the left. For the reverse inclusion, take \(a\in\Omega_0\cap F'((u))\), \(u=t^{1/n}\), enlarging \(F'/F\) to be finite Galois and to contain \(\mu_n\). For \(0\leq j<n\), put

\[
 b_j(t)=\frac{u^{-j}}{n}
        \sum_{\zeta\in\mu_n}\zeta^{-j}a(\zeta u).
 \tag{47.8}
\]

Writing out the Laurent expansion shows that only exponents congruent to \(j\bmod n\) survive. Thus \(b_j(t)\in F'((t))\) and

\[
                              a=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^j b_j(t).
\]

Every substitution \(u\mapsto\zeta u\) fixes \(F'(t)\); therefore \(a(\zeta u)\), and hence \(b_j\), is algebraic over \(F(t)\). Choose an \(F\)-basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_s\) of \(F'\) and its trace-dual basis \(e_1^*,\ldots,e_s^*\). Then

\[
 b_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}_{F'((t))/F((t))}(e_i^*b_j)
          \in F((t)),\qquad
 b_j=\sum_i e_i b_{ij}.
 \tag{47.9}
\]

The trace is the finite sum of coefficientwise conjugates. These conjugates preserve \(F(t)\), so \(b_{ij}\) is algebraic over \(F(t)\). Hence \(b_{ij}\in A_0(F)\), proving (47.7). This finite Fourier/trace calculation supplies the generator statement directly; it does not require an additional assertion that all abstract field automorphisms preserve infinite series sums.

For completeness, it also proves (47.3). Coefficientwise \(G_F\) acts on \(\Omega_0\) and fixes \(A_0(F)\) and every chosen \(t^{1/n}\). An \(F\)-basis of a finite \(F'/F\) remains linearly independent over \(F((t))\): compare each Laurent coefficient in any proposed linear relation. Thus

\[
                  [A_0(F)F':A_0(F)]=[F':F].
\]

The field \(A_0(F)F'\subset F'((t))\) has value group \(\mathbf Z\). The polynomial \(X^n-t\) is Eisenstein for its valuation ring, so adjoining \(t^{1/n}\) has degree \(n\). After \(F'\) contains \(\mu_n\), these are cyclic inertia extensions. Formula (47.7) now identifies the kernel of restriction to \(G_F\) with the inverse limit of the groups \(\mu_n\), namely \(\widehat{\mathbf Z}(1)\). Restriction is surjective by the coefficientwise action, which is a continuous splitting. Conjugation sends an inertia root multiplier \(\zeta_n\) to \(g(\zeta_n)\), giving the cyclotomic action. All maps are continuous on finite subextensions, so the asserted isomorphism is topological.

## 4. A nonidentity automorphism invisible to the whole Galois group

The formal substitution \(t\mapsto t+t^2\) defines an \(F\)-automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(F((t))\). Its inverse substitutes

\[
                r(t)=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+4t}}2\in t\mathbf Q[[t]],
                 \qquad r(t)+r(t)^2=t.
 \tag{47.10}
\]

If \(a\in A_0(F)\), its substituted polynomial relation shows that \(\sigma(a)\) is algebraic over \(F(t+t^2)\subset F(t)\); it also lies in \(F((t))\). Thus \(\sigma(A_0(F))\subset A_0(F)\). Conversely, substitution by \(r(t)\) sends an algebraic element to an element algebraic over \(F(r(t))\), which is algebraic over \(F(t)\). It therefore preserves \(A_0(F)\) as well. Hence \(\sigma\) is an automorphism of \(A_0(F)\), and it is nonidentity because \(\sigma(t)=t+t^2\ne t\).

Extend it to \(\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Omega)\) by fixing \(\bar F\) and setting

\[
              \tau(t^{1/n})=t^{1/n}(1+t)^{1/n},
 \tag{47.11}
\]

where the second factor is the constant-term-one binomial series with rational coefficients. At each level \(F'((t^{1/n}))\), this substitutes a uniformizer with leading coefficient one. The unique constant-term-one roots make the substitutions compatible as the coefficient field and the root degree increase. The inverse uses the unique constant-term-one root of \(r(t)/t\). Thus these are mutually inverse automorphisms of the directed union \(\Omega\). Both preserve \(\Omega_0\), because the images of \(t\) under both substitutions are algebraic over \(F(t)\). We use the same symbol \(\tau\) for the induced automorphism of \(\Omega_0\).

Let \(g\in G_{A_0(F)}=\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega_0/A_0(F))\). The maps \(g\tau\) and \(\tau g\) agree on \(A_0(F)\), since \(g\) fixes it and \(\tau\) preserves it. They agree on \(\bar F\), because \(g\) preserves the elements algebraic over \(F\), while \(\tau\) fixes them all. Finally, write

\[
                    g(t^{1/n})=\zeta_n t^{1/n},
                        \qquad\zeta_n\in\bar F.
\]

The series \((1+t)^{1/n}\) belongs to \(A_0(F)\): it lies in \(\mathbf Q[[t]]\subset F((t))\) and is algebraic over \(F(t)\). Therefore

\[
 g\tau(t^{1/n})
   =\zeta_n t^{1/n}(1+t)^{1/n}
   =\tau g(t^{1/n}).
 \tag{47.12}
\]

The field generators in (47.7) prove \(g\tau=\tau g\) on all of \(\Omega_0\). No continuity of an arbitrary field automorphism for formal-series sums was used. Thus 

\[
  1\ne\tau\in
  C_{\operatorname{Aut}(\Omega_0)}(G_{A_0(F)}),
\]

and \(\sigma\) induces the identity outer automorphism of \(G_{A_0(F)}\).

## 5. The full-topos counterexample and algebraic extensions

Apply the already proved semilinear construction in F.40, with \(k=A_0(F)\). The absolute scheme automorphism defined by

\[
 k[T,T^{-1}]\longrightarrow k[T,T^{-1}],
                   \qquad c\mapsto\sigma(c),\quad T\mapsto T
\]

induces a geometric automorphism of the full small étale topos. The trivial outer Galois action supplies a structural isomorphism over \(BG_k\). This is an actual relative morphism under CHW's convention. It preserves closed points because its underlying scheme map is an isomorphism. It is genuinely nonconstant on points.

On \(k\)-rational points its value is \(a\mapsto\sigma^{-1}(a)\). If it came from a \(k\)-scheme map \(\mathbb G_m\to\mathbb G_m\), that map would be \(cT^m\) for \(c\in k^\times\) and \(m\in\mathbf Z\). Values at \(1\) and \(2\) give \(c=1\) and \(m=1\). The value at \(t\), however, is \(r(t)\ne t\), a contradiction. The relation \(r+r^2=t\) itself proves \(r\ne t\), since \(t\ne0\).

Now let \(E/A_0(F)\) be any algebraic extension. If \(E\) reconstructed, F.32's algebraic-extension descent would force \(A_0(F)\) to reconstruct. Therefore every such \(E\) fails. This last conclusion uses descent contrapositively; it does **not** assert that the particular substitution \(\sigma\) preserves an arbitrary chosen intermediate extension \(E\).

The definition of the old Puiseux field \(A(F)\) in F.42 gives \(A_0(F)\subset A(F)\); this extension is algebraic because both fields are algebraic over \(F(t)\). The present conclusion is stronger than merely excluding \(A(F)\): non-reconstruction of a larger algebraic field alone cannot imply non-reconstruction of the smaller field by F.32. Here the smaller field is excluded by its own explicit semilinear witness.

## 6. The intrinsic henselian-valuation obstruction

Let \(K,v,\kappa,d\) satisfy (47.4), with valuation ring \(\mathcal O_v\). A coefficient field \(F\subset\mathcal O_v\) mapping isomorphically to \(\kappa\) exists. For clarity, here is the complete elementary argument, also given by Aschenbrenner–van den Dries–van der Hoeven, Proposition 3.3.8 and Lemma 3.3.9, author-PDF pp. 117–118.

Since the residue characteristic is zero, \(\mathbf Q\subset\mathcal O_v\). By Zorn choose a maximal subfield \(F\subset\mathcal O_v\); reduction is injective on \(F\). If a residue element \(\alpha\in\kappa\) were transcendental over the residue image of \(F\), any lift \(a\in\mathcal O_v\) would satisfy \(P(a)\in\mathcal O_v^\times\) for every nonzero \(P\in F[X]\). Hence \(F(a)\subset\mathcal O_v\), contradicting maximality. The residue extension is therefore algebraic. If it were nontrivial, choose a missing algebraic residue element. Its minimal polynomial over the image of \(F\) is separable in characteristic zero. Lift its coefficients to \(F\); Hensel's property gives a root \(a\in\mathcal O_v\) with that residue. The lifted polynomial is irreducible, since its reduction is irreducible, so \(F[a]\) is a larger subfield of \(\mathcal O_v\), again a contradiction. Thus \(F\simeq\kappa\).

Choose any nonzero \(t\in\mathfrak m_v\). It is transcendental over \(F\): in a nonzero polynomial \(\sum c_i t^i\), all nonzero coefficients \(c_i\in F\) have valuation zero and the least nonzero exponent gives the unique term of least valuation. The same argument shows

\[
                 \mathcal O_v\cap F(t)=F[t]_{(t)}.
 \tag{47.13}
\]

This does not require \(v(K^\times)\) to be discrete. The field \(F(t)\) has transcendence degree \(d\) over \(\mathbf Q\) by (47.4), so \(K/F(t)\) is algebraic. The inclusion of the t-adic valued field \(F(t)\) into the henselian valued field \(K\) extends uniquely through its henselization. By (47.5), this gives a valued-field embedding \(A_0(F)\hookrightarrow K\) extending the chosen \(F(t)\hookrightarrow K\). The universal property is precisely the definition preceding Proposition 3.3.25 in the same author source, p. 125.

Since \(K/F(t)\) is algebraic and \(F(t)\subset A_0(F)\subset K\), the extension \(K/A_0(F)\) is algebraic. Section 5 excludes \(K\). Consequently a finite-transcendence-degree reconstruction field cannot admit a valuation satisfying (47.4).

This is a genuine necessary field condition, not a restatement of the CHW test for maps into \(\mathbb G_m\). It supplies no converse to the sufficient classes F.43–F.46 and leaves the intrinsic classification F.9 unresolved.

## 7. Exact finite certificate and what it checks

The existing program `code/F_Puiseux_checks.py` and its saved output `audit/F_Puiseux_checks.json` check 27 exact rational identities in \(\mathbf Q[t]/(t^{13})\): the two inverse substitutions, \(r+r^2=t\), the constant-term-one roots for \(n=1,2,3,4,6,12\), and their divisibility compatibilities. These are exactly the formal substitutions reused in (47.10)–(47.12).

This finite certificate does not establish the infinite field, Galois, or topos assertions; those are proved above. No new finite counterexample or unexecuted computation is being claimed.
