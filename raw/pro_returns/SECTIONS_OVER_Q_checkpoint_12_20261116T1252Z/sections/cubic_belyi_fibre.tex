% A.114: exact comparison; no obstructed completed source tuple is asserted.
\subsection{A cubic Belyi cover and its specified completed fibres}

\begin{statement}{A.114}{Proved}
{Quadratic-coset Kummer detection and the cubic source fibre}
Let $D\in\Q^\times/\Q^{\times2}$ be nontrivial, let
$K_D=\Q(\sqrt D)$, and let $\mathcal P_D$ be its
unramified nonsplit rational primes.
Put $M_\ell=\varprojlim_n\Q^\times/\Q^{\times\ell^n}$,
embedded in $\widehat{\Q^\times}$ by the primary
product map of A.97.
Define the following set of coefficient primes:
\[
 \mathcal E_D=\{\ell:K_D\subset\Q(\mu_{\ell^\infty})\}.
\tag{A.114.1}
\]
It is empty or a singleton.  Its nonempty cases,
with $D$ represented by a squarefree integer, are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
  D&\mathcal E_D\\ \hline
  -1,\ 2,\ -2&\{2\}\\
  (-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell,\quad\ell\text{ odd prime}&\{\ell\}.
 \end{array}
\tag{A.114.2}
\]
If a completed class $C$ is trivial in
$\widehat{\Q_p^\times}$ at every prime of
$\mathcal P_D$, or at all but finitely many of them,
then every primary component of $C$ outside
$\mathcal E_D$ is zero.
In particular this localization is injective
when $\mathcal E_D$ is empty.
The possible single primary component cannot
be removed from this assertion in general.

Now set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 V=\mathbf P^1_\Q\setminus
           \{0,1,\tfrac23,-\tfrac13,\infty\},\qquad
 U=\mathbf P^1_\Q\setminus\{0,1,\infty\},\\
 f:V\longrightarrow U,\qquad
                 f(T)=\frac{27}{4}T^2(1-T).
 \end{gathered}
\tag{A.114.3}
\]
This is an actual finite etale cover of degree three.
Let $A_0,A_1,A_{2/3},A_{-1/3}$ be completed global
classes with ordinary local centres $T_p\in V(\Q_p)$
at every finite prime:
\[
          (A_c)_p=\kappa_p(T_p-c)
               \qquad(c=0,1,\tfrac23,-\tfrac13).
\tag{A.114.4}
\]
Put
\[
 A=-\frac{27}{4}A_0^2 A_1,\qquad
 B=\frac{27}{4}A_{2/3}^2 A_{-1/3},\qquad
                       D=[3AB]\in\Q^\times/\Q^{\times2}.
\tag{A.114.5}
\]
Suppose $D\ne1$ and that either $\mathcal E_D$ is
empty or each finite local centre $T_p$ is rational.
Then pushforward through the displayed cover gives
an exact bijection of the specified Selmer-section fibres
\[
 \operatorname{Sec}_{\rm Sel}
       (V;A_0,A_1,A_{2/3},A_{-1/3})
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \operatorname{Sec}_{\rm Sel}(U;A,B).
\tag{A.114.6}
\]
These are full profinite, continuous sections up to
geometric conjugacy.  The real point-or-cusp condition
is retained on both sides.  No existence of an
obstructed completed tuple on this particular
five-punctured source is asserted here.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The exact quadratic-coset detection argument.}
Fix a coefficient prime $\ell$ outside
$\mathcal E_D$ and a finite exponent $m=\ell^n$.
Choose a rational representative $a$ of $C\bmod m$,
and form the actual splitting fields
\[
 F=\Q(\mu_m),\quad L=F(a^{1/m}),\quad
 N=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F),\quad \Omega=LK_D.
\tag{A.114.7}
\]
By the choice of $\ell$, $K_D\not\subset F$.
Each element of $N$ acts on the $m$ roots by
a Kummer translation, and every nonidentity
translation fixes none of them.

Suppose $N\ne1$.
If $K_D\not\subset L$, then
$L\cap K_D=\Q$.
Choose a nonidentity $\sigma\in N$ and the
nonidentity automorphism of $K_D$.
They define an actual automorphism of $\Omega$
whose restrictions are these two chosen maps.
If instead $K_D\subset L$, restriction
$N\to\operatorname{Gal}(K_D/\Q)$ is onto:
otherwise its fixed field would contain $K_D$,
contrary to $K_D\not\subset F$.
Choose $\sigma\in N$ with nonidentity restriction.
In either case we have produced an element of
$\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/\Q)$ that is nonsplit
on $K_D$ and has no fixed root of $Z^m-a$.
Both properties hold on its whole conjugacy class.
Chebotarev supplies primes in that class outside
any prescribed finite exceptional set
\cite[Theorem 8.31]{AgentMilneANT}.
At such a prime, separable reduction and Hensel's
lemma say that $Z^m-a$ has a local root exactly
when Frobenius fixes one of its geometric roots.
The assumed local triviality of $C\bmod m$
therefore contradicts the constructed class.
We conclude that
\[
                             N=1.
\tag{A.114.8}
\]

For odd $\ell$, replace $a$ by a positive
representative if necessary; multiplication by
$-1$ is an $m$th-power change.
The positive real root $\alpha=a^{1/m}$ lies
in $F$ by (A.114.8).
Its field is a real subfield of an abelian
Galois extension, hence is Galois and its only
possible conjugates are $\alpha,-\alpha$.
Thus $\alpha^2\in\Q$.
Since $m$ is odd and $\alpha^m\in\Q$,
Bezout's identity gives $\alpha\in\Q$.
Therefore $C\bmod\ell^n=1$ for every $n$.

For $\ell=2$, apply (A.114.8) at exponent
$m=2^{n+1}$.
If $a>0$, the same real-root argument gives
$a=(\alpha^2)^{2^n}$.
If $a<0$, divide one root by a primitive
$2m$th root of unity; the resulting roots of
$Z^m-(-a)$ all lie in the abelian field
$\Q(\mu_{2m})$.
Its positive real root $\beta$ has
$\beta^2\in\Q$, giving
$a=-(\beta^2)^{2^n}$.
Consequently the coordinate of $C$ modulo $2^n$
is either $1$ or $-1$ for every $n$.
These two classes are distinct at every such
level and their transition maps preserve their
signs.  Coherence makes the entire two-primary
component equal to one fixed global sign.
The negative sign is nontrivial in the
two-primary completion of $\Q_p^\times$ at
every odd prime $p$: the two-primary roots of
unity there have finite order, so $-1$ cannot
be divisible by every power of two.
One odd prime of $\mathcal P_D$ outside the
finite exceptional set excludes that sign.
The two-primary component is therefore trivial
as well.
This proves the stated primary containment.

\emph{The list of exceptions and its necessity.}
For odd $\ell$, every quadratic character of
$(\Z/\ell^n)^\times$ factors through
$\mathbf F_\ell^\times$, and that cyclic group
has one nontrivial quadratic character.
For its Legendre character $\chi$, the actual
Gauss sum $g=\sum_a\chi(a)\zeta_\ell^a$ satisfies
\[
 \sigma_b(g)=\chi(b)g,\qquad
 g\bar g=\ell,\qquad
 \bar g=\chi(-1)g,
 \qquad g^2=(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell.
\tag{A.114.9}
\]
The middle identity follows by substituting
$a=tb$ in the double sum: its inner sum over
$b\ne0$ is $\ell-1$ for $t=1$ and $-1$
otherwise.  These identities identify the
unique quadratic field, not just its degree.
For two, every quadratic character factors
modulo eight, since $1+8\Z_2$ consists of
squares.  The three fields are visibly
$\Q(i),\Q(\sqrt2),\Q(\sqrt{-2})$, using
$\sqrt2=\zeta_8+\zeta_8^{-1}$.
This proves (A.114.2) and also proves that
at most one coefficient prime occurs.

Let $e_\ell\in\widehat\Z$ be the idempotent
with component one at $\ell$ and zero elsewhere.
The exceptional containment is necessary.
For $D=(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell$, $\ell$ odd,
the nonzero class $\ell^{e_\ell}$ is trivial
at every prime of $\mathcal P_D$.
Indeed such a prime is different from $\ell$
and is not one modulo $\ell$, so its unit
group has zero pro-$\ell$ completion.
For $D=-1$ use $2^{2e_2}$: nonsplit odd
primes have $v_2(p-1)=1$.
For $D=2,-2$ use $2^{4e_2}$: their nonsplit
odd residue classes modulo eight have
$v_2(p-1)\le2$.
The only nonzero valuation coordinate of each
example is at its ramified coefficient prime,
which is absent from $\mathcal P_D$.
The displayed exponents kill all the remaining
finite unit components, proving these examples
by their actual localization maps.

\emph{The cubic morphism, its sign cover, and its Kummer map.}
The exact factorization and derivative are
\[
 1-f(T)=\frac{27}{4}(T-\tfrac23)^2(T+\tfrac13),
 \qquad f'(T)=\frac{27}{4}T(2-3T).
\tag{A.114.10}
\]
The ramification indices above zero are two
at $0$ and one at $1$; above one they are
two at $2/3$ and one at $-1/3$; infinity
has its one inverse image of index three.
Removing exactly these points gives the
finite etale degree-three map (A.114.3).
Its pullback on the named invertible functions
is precisely (A.114.5).
In particular the local pair is
$(\kappa(f(T_p)),\kappa(1-f(T_p)))$.

The fibre polynomial over $t\in U$ is
\[
           Z^3-Z^2+\frac{4t}{27}.
\tag{A.114.11}
\]
Its discriminant is
\[
          \frac{16}{27}t(1-t)
                   =\left(\frac49\right)^2 3t(1-t).
\tag{A.114.12}
\]
Thus the sign of the actual permutation
action on its three roots is the quadratic
Kummer character of $3t(1-t)$.
On the source it is the same character as
\[
                  (1-T)(1+3T)
                         =-3(T-1)(T+\tfrac13).
\tag{A.114.13}
\]
This also verifies the stated sign class
$D=[3AB]$ from the old completed coordinates.

\emph{The unique global fixed sheet.}
Take a section in the right side of
(A.114.6), represented by a continuous
$s:G_\Q\to E_U$.
At a finite prime its two Kummer classes
are those of the actual ordinary point
$t_p=f(T_p)$.
None of the three cusp packets can have
this pair: their respective regular
coordinates $1-t$, $t$, and $t/(t-1)$
have class one, which would contradict
full local Kummer injectivity for
$t_p\notin\{0,1\}$.
Hence the local section is the ordinary
point section at $t_p$.

The finite cover (A.114.3) gives an actual
three-element geometric fibre and a
continuous permutation representation
\[
                \theta_s:G_\Q\longrightarrow S_3.
\tag{A.114.14}
\]
At every finite prime its restricted
permutation action has a fixed letter,
because the actual fibre over $t_p$
contains $T_p\in V(\Q_p)$.
If any element of the global image were
a three-cycle, its Chebotarev class
would give an unramified local action
with no fixed letter.
The image therefore contains no
three-cycle.
The subgroups of $S_3$ with this property
are the identity and the subgroups
generated by one transposition:
two distinct transpositions already
generate a three-cycle.
By (A.114.12) its sign is the nontrivial
character $D$.
The image is consequently one such
order-two subgroup and has exactly
one fixed letter.

The finite-cover correspondence turns
that fixed letter into a global lift
of $s$ to $E_V$.
Explicitly, after choosing compatible
geometric base points at a rational
source point, $E_V$ is an
index-three subgroup of $E_U$.
A coset has a representative $g$
in the geometric group $\Pi$, since
$E_V\to G_\Q$ is onto.
A fixed coset $gE_V$ says
$s(G_\Q)\subset gE_Vg^{-1}$, and
$g^{-1}sg$ is the actual lifted section.
Changing its geometric representative
$g$ within that coset changes
the lift by geometric conjugacy in
$E_V$.
Any geometric centralizer of $s$ can
only permute its fixed cosets; here
there is just one.
Thus the lift is unique as a section
class over the given target class.

At each finite prime the chosen fixed
sheet gives an ordinary point
$U_p\in V(\Q_p)$ over $t_p$.
At an unramified nonsplit prime of
$K_D$, the local sheet action is the
same order-two group and has one
fixed sheet.  Hence
\[
                         U_p=T_p
                                   \qquad(p\in\mathcal P_D).
\tag{A.114.15}
\]
These assertions use the actual finite
etale fibre of (A.114.11), whose
$\Q_p$-points are its Galois-fixed
geometric letters.

The real Selmer condition also lifts.
For an ordinary real target point,
the chosen global fixed letter is
fixed by $G_\R$, hence is an actual
real point of its finite etale fibre.
When the cubic discriminant is
negative it is the unique real root;
when it is positive and a local
root exists, all three roots are
real, so the chosen letter is real
as well.
For a real cusp packet, inertia
orbits on the sheets index the
boundary points of the proper
normalization.  The chosen fixed
letter fixes its orbit, and the
intersection of the source subgroup
with that cusp decomposition group
is the decomposition group of its
boundary lift.
All boundary points in (A.114.10)
are rational.  Thus the lifted
local section is again cuspidal.
The global lift is a Selmer section.

\emph{Recovery of all four prescribed old coordinates.}
Let $C_c$ be the completed Kummer
coordinate $T-c$ of the lifted
section, for the four old punctures.
Its finite localization is
$\kappa_p(U_p-c)$.
Equation (A.114.15) makes each ratio
$C_c/A_c$ trivial at every prime of
$\mathcal P_D$.
The first part of the theorem
therefore kills every coefficient
outside $\mathcal E_D$.
If that set is empty, all four
coordinates are already the
prescribed ones.

In the remaining case write
$\mathcal E_D=\{\ell_0\}$ and use
the stated rationality of every
$T_p$.
At each physical prime $p\ne\ell_0$,
the global coefficient-$p$ components
of $C_0,C_1$ equal those of $A_0,A_1$.
Their localizations therefore give
the actual equalities
\[
 \begin{split}
  \kappa_p(U_p)_p&=\kappa_p(T_p)_p,\\
  \kappa_p(1-U_p)_p&=\kappa_p(1-T_p)_p.
 \end{split}
\tag{A.114.16}
\]
The rightmost subscript denotes the
coefficient-$p$ primary completion.
The own-coefficient theorem A.97,
applied to the rational reference
$T_p\in\Q\setminus\{0,1\}$ at
this one physical prime, gives
$U_p=T_p$.
Thus $C_c/A_c$ is trivial locally
at every finite prime except at
most $\ell_0$.
A.76 now gives $C_c=A_c$ globally
for all four punctures, and full
local Kummer injectivity also
gives $U_{\ell_0}=T_{\ell_0}$.

This proves surjectivity in
(A.114.6).
The unique-fixed-sheet argument
proves its injectivity.
Pushforward itself preserves
ordinary sections and cusp packets
through the displayed point and
boundary maps, completing the
proof of the exact Selmer-fibre
bijection.
\end{proof}

The case $D=1$ is excluded from
the fibre assertion.  In that
case the same Chebotarev argument
only makes (A.114.14) trivial,
leaving three global fixed sheets;
the proof above does not select
one prescribed completed old tuple.
For $D\ne1$, the theorem supplies
the exact comparison needed before
an obstruction on this particular
source could be transported to the
tripod.  It does not construct
that source obstruction.
