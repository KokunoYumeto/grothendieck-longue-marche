\subsection{An explicit radicand update with one successful norm representative}

\begin{statement}{A.144}{Proved}
{Every-depth norm tests in one changed global tower}
Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 K=\Q(\alpha),\qquad \alpha^3=3,\qquad
 q=511177,\qquad
 \gamma=-68+64\alpha+\alpha^2,\\
 U=\alpha^2-2,\qquad
 z=\alpha q^8,\qquad a'=3q^{24},\qquad
 \beta=(1-\alpha)U\,\frac{q}{\gamma^3}.
 \end{gathered}
\tag{A.144.1}
\]
Choose compatible roots in one algebraic closure,
and retain the actual fields and transition norms
\[
 \theta_0=z,\qquad \theta_{j+1}^3=\theta_j,\qquad
 E_j=K(\theta_j),\qquad
                       N_{E_{j+1}/E_j}.
\tag{A.144.2}
\]
Then
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 N_{K/\Q}(\gamma)=q,\qquad N_{K/\Q}(\beta)=-2,\\
 [E_j:K]=3^j,\qquad
 \beta\in N_{E_j/K}(E_j^\times)\quad\text{for every }j\ge1 .
 \end{gathered}
\tag{A.144.3}
\]
At every place \(v\) of \(K\), there is a compatible
family in the actual local etale algebras
\(E_j\otimes_K K_v\) whose norm at level zero is
the specified \(\beta\).
Every finite initial segment of the global
norm system likewise has a solution.

The relative norm-one element
\[
                         Uq/\gamma^3\in T_1(\Q)
\tag{A.144.4}
\]
has at \(q\), in the labeled mod-three
coordinates of \(T_1[3]\), tame and unramified
vectors
\[
                  t=(1,1,1),\qquad c=(0,2,1).
\tag{A.144.5}
\]
They span a two-dimensional local image.
Nevertheless the resulting \(\beta\) satisfies
the full stable norm equations at all three
places above \(q\).
This is an explicit successful sheared local
update, with the original three-adic unit
cofactor \(U\) of A.138.

The new rational coefficient has
\(a'/3=q^{24}\), so its class modulo twenty-four
is still that of \(3\).
The stated conclusions concern the particular
representative \(\beta\) and the single tower
(A.144.2).
They do not establish nonemptiness of the
global inverse limit of norm fibres, or
construct a completed ordinary ghost pair.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The exact prime, norm, and residue certificates.}
Trial division up to \(\sqrt q\) proves that
\(q=511177\) is prime.
It satisfies \(q\equiv49\pmod{72}\), and hence
\(v_3(q-1)=1\).
For \(a+b\alpha+c\alpha^2\) the literal norm is
\[
 N_{K/\Q}(a+b\alpha+c\alpha^2)
                  =a^3+3b^3+9c^3-9abc .
\tag{A.144.6}
\]
It gives \(N\gamma=q\).
The polynomial \(X^3-3\) has the three distinct
roots
\[
                         (r_0,r_1,r_2)=(13382,29914,467881)
\tag{A.144.7}
\]
in \(\F_q\).
The residue unit 5 is primitive, and
\(\zeta=5^{(q-1)/3}=472522\) satisfies
\(r_i=r_0\zeta^i\).
The residues of \(\gamma\) are
\((0,157366,353607)\).
Since \(\gamma\) is integral and has prime norm
\(q\), its divisor is the single degree-one
prime \(P_0\) with residue \(\alpha=r_0\).

Here are literal cube-root witnesses for the
two unit triples needed below:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrr}
 i&0&1&2\\ \hline
 1-r_i&497796&481264&43297\\
 \text{cube root}&421&110767&207713\\
 3-2r_i&484416&451352&86595\\
 \text{cube root}&384954&368127&368991 .
 \end{array}
\tag{A.144.8}
\]
All entries are nonzero modulo \(q\).
Their third powers give the stated rows.
They lift to actual cubes in \(\Q_q^\times\),
because three is a \(q\)-adic unit.
In addition \(162620^{24}\equiv-2\pmod q\).
Thus the new coefficient prime also has
the required unit point on the exponent-twenty-four
old cover: set \(X=1\) and lift a unit root of
\[
                         Y^{24}=(1-3q^{24})/(-2).
\tag{A.144.9}
\]
The same finite cover has the rational point
\((X,Y)=(q^{-1},1)\), which retains centre \(3\).
These assertions are finite-coordinate statements.

By A.138, \(U\) is an actual global unit of
norm one.
Also \(N(1-\alpha)=-2\).
Formula (A.144.6) therefore proves
\(N\beta=-2\).
Let \(P_2\) be the degree-one prime of \(K\)
over two, and let \(P_0,P_1,P_2'\) denote
the three labeled primes over \(q\).
The distinct notation \(P_2'\) avoids
confusing the last \(q\)-prime with \(P_2\).
The exact divisor of \(\beta\) is
\[
             \operatorname{div}(\beta)
                         =P_2-2P_0+P_1+P_2'.
\tag{A.144.10}
\]
Indeed \(1-\alpha\) has absolute norm two,
\(U\) is a unit, \((q)=P_0+P_1+P_2'\),
and \((\gamma)=P_0\).
In particular \(\beta\) is a unit at every
place away from two and \(q\).

\emph{The full norm condition at the new prime.}
At the three places above \(q\), the base
field is \(\Q_q\), the radicand is
\(z_i=\alpha_i q^8\), and its valuation is
the prime-to-three integer 8.
The local extensions of degree \(3^j\)
are therefore totally ramified.
The exact field identity
\[
 \beta z
   =(1-\alpha)(3-2\alpha)
                         \left(\frac{q^3}{\gamma}\right)^3
\tag{A.144.11}
\]
uses \(\alpha(\alpha^2-2)=3-2\alpha\).
The residue witnesses (A.144.8) make its
right side a cube at each of the three
places above \(q\).
Write there \(\beta_i z_i=w_i^3\).
For the first cubic extension,
\[
 N\bigl(\theta_1^{-1}w_i\bigr)
                           =z_i^{-1}w_i^3=\beta_i .
\]
Since \(v_3(q-1)=1\), the exact stable norm
formula A.131 identifies the norm group
at every higher pure-three depth with
this first cubic norm group.
Thus the same \(\beta_i\) is a norm at
every depth.
The compact fixed-valuation fibres in
A.131 supply a compatible actual local
family, without asserting surjectivity
of arbitrary choices of norm representatives
between depths.

The local image in (A.144.5) is also
explicit.
The norm-one element in (A.144.4) has
valuation vector \((-2,1,1)\), which
reduces to \(t=(1,1,1)\).
For the tame generator whose cyclotomic
unit is \(\zeta=472522\), the cubic
unit characters of \(\alpha_i\) are
\((0,1,2)\).
Choose the arithmetic Frobenius lift
which fixes the chosen cubic root of
the uniformizer \(q\).
Those of \(U_i=\alpha_i^2-2\) are
\((0,2,1)\); these equalities follow
by raising the residues to \((q-1)/3\).
The quotient \(q/\gamma_i^3\) contributes
no additional unit character.
Consequently the second vector is the
displayed \(c\).
It is not a scalar multiple of the
constant vector \(t\).
In the notation of A.131, the full
stable equations here read
\[
       8c_i-t_i a_i=0\quad\text{in }\Z/3,
           \qquad (a_0,a_1,a_2)=(0,1,2).
\tag{A.144.12}
\]
They hold since \(c_i=-a_i\) and \(t_i=1\)
modulo three.
This records the actual branch term
which permits the two-dimensional image.

\emph{All the other local places and their compatible algebras.}
At the unique place of \(K\) above three,
the normalized valuation of \(\alpha\)
is one and \(q\) is a unit.
The tower is exactly the A.140 tower
with base \(\Q_3(\alpha)\) and radicand
\(\alpha q^8\).
The target \(\beta\) is a unit there.
A.140 gives a compatible actual unit
norm family at every depth.

At two the polynomial has distinct
linear and quadratic residue factors:
\[
                 K\otimes_\Q\Q_2=\Q_2\times F_4,
\tag{A.144.13}
\]
where \(F_4/\Q_2\) is the unramified
quadratic extension.
On the linear factor the unit
\(\alpha q^8\) has a unique
\(3^j\)-th root in \(\Q_2\) for every
\(j\), with compatible roots.
This gives a compatible degree-one
factor of the local etale algebras;
it does not require all their factors
to have degree one.
Put the specified target on that
factor and one on every other factor.
Their algebra norms give a compatible
family, including the valuation-one
target from (A.144.10).
On the quadratic factor the target is
a unit.
All pure root algebras are unramified,
because the radicands are units and
the derivatives \(3^jX^{3^j-1}\) are
units at every root modulo two.
The product norms on the unit groups
of these unramified factors are onto.
They give compatible unit families
by compactness, or recursively by
the onto unit transition norms.

At a place away from \(2,3,q\), both
the radicand and target are units.
The same derivative argument makes
every factor of each local etale
algebra unramified.
Its unit norm is onto, and the
corresponding finite products have
onto unit transition norms.
This proves compatibility there.
At the real place the odd root
polynomial has a real degree-one
factor through every depth; at a
complex place every factor is
complex.
Use a compatible degree-one branch
for the target and one in the
other factors.
This proves the local assertion
at every place.

\emph{The full decomposition group at three.}
The radicand \(z=\alpha q^8\) has
normalized valuation one in
\(\Q_3(\alpha)\).
Thus \([E_j:K]=3^j\).
Its global Galois closure is
\[
 \Omega_j=K(\theta_j,\mu_{3^j}),\qquad
 G_j=C_{3^j}\rtimes(\Z/3^j)^\times,\qquad
 H_j=(\Z/3^j)^\times .
\tag{A.144.14}
\]
Here \(H_j\) is the actual stabilizer
of \(\theta_j\).
To verify the full unit factor,
the intersection
\(K\cap\Q(\mu_{3^j})\) is \(\Q\):
the only possible nontrivial
intersection has degree three and
would make the non-Galois cubic
field \(K\) a subfield of an
abelian extension.
The same argument locally gives
\(\Q_3(\alpha)\cap\Q_3(\mu_{3^j})=\Q_3\).
Over that local cyclotomic compositum,
the radicand is not a cube, by the
Eisenstein and non-Galois argument
of A.140.
Consequently the local Galois closure
has degree \(3^j\varphi(3^j)\), equal
to the full global degree in
(A.144.14).
Its decomposition group at three
is therefore literally \(G_j\).

\emph{The actual norm-torus lattice and the Hasse norm map.}
Let
\[
 T_j=\ker\!\left(
       R_{E_j/K}\mathbf G_m\longrightarrow\mathbf G_m\right),
 \qquad
 X_j=X^*(T_j)
      =\Z[G_j/H_j]\big/\Z\!\left(\sum_{gH_j}gH_j\right).
\tag{A.144.15}
\]
The defining norm sequence and Hilbert 90
give the exact global and local maps
\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 H^1(K,T_j)&\simeq&K^\times/N_{E_j/K}(E_j^\times)\\
 \downarrow&&\downarrow\\
 \displaystyle\prod_v H^1(K_v,T_j)&\simeq&
 \displaystyle\prod_v K_v^\times/
       N_{E_j\otimes_K K_v/K_v}
                     (E_j\otimes_K K_v)^\times .
 \end{array}
\tag{A.144.16}
\]
The local computations put the class of
\(\beta\) in the kernel of this
localization map.

The torus duality and finite-group
comparison used here are the general
Proposition 6.4 and Corollary 6.5 of
\cite[p.~35]{BIR_Oki2026Norm}.
Their statements apply to the displayed
norm torus at every degree \(3^j\).
They identify this kernel with the
dual of
\[
 \ker\!\left(
       H^2(G_j,X_j)\longrightarrow
                 \prod_v H^2(D_{j,v},X_j)\right),
\tag{A.144.17}
\]
where \(D_{j,v}\) are the actual
decomposition subgroups.
The factor at three is restriction
from \(G_j\) to \(G_j\), by (A.144.14).
It is the identity, so the displayed
kernel is zero.

For clarity, the underlying inflation
comparison also retains its maps.
A class of
\(\operatorname{Sha}^2(K,X_j)\) restricts
over \(\Omega_j\), where the lattice is
constant, to a tuple of continuous
\(\Q/\Z\)-valued Galois characters:
\[
 H^2(G_{\Omega_j},\Z^{\,3^j-1})
       =\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm cts}
                  (G_{\Omega_j},\Q/\Z)^{\,3^j-1}.
\tag{A.144.18}
\]
Local triviality and Chebotarev kill
these characters.
Also \(H^1(G_{\Omega_j},X_j)=0\),
since a continuous map from a compact
group to a discrete torsion-free
lattice has zero image.
Hochschild--Serre therefore makes the
class the inflation of a unique
element of \(H^2(G_j,X_j)\).
At the three-adic place, the analogous
local inflation is injective by the
same \(H^1=0\) argument, and the
decomposition group is \(G_j\).
The local restriction of the inflated
class is zero, so that finite-group
element is zero.
This gives exactly the comparison
used in (A.144.17).
Duality and (A.144.16) now give an
actual \(\beta_j\in E_j^\times\)
with \(N_{E_j/K}(\beta_j)=\beta\)
for every fixed \(j\).

\emph{The remaining global limit.}
For every fixed height \(J\), choose
such a \(\beta_J\) and define the
lower elements by their actual norms.
This gives a compatible global
family through height \(J\).
The global sets
\[
 \mathcal X_j=
       \{w\in E_j^\times:N_{E_j/K}(w)=\beta\},
       \qquad
 \mathcal X_{j+1}\xrightarrow{N_{E_{j+1}/E_j}}\mathcal X_j
\tag{A.144.19}
\]
are thus nonempty at every finite
height.
The local compactness arguments
above prove nonempty inverse limits
after localization.
They do not provide compact global
fibres or onto global transition
maps in (A.144.19).
Nonemptiness of that global inverse
limit, including its pro-three
completed version, remains the
compatibility question.
The coefficients \(a'\) and \(-2\)
are specified rational finite-stage
representatives, not a claimed
completed ordinary unit-equation pair.
This proves precisely the assertions
of the statement.
\end{proof}
