# Exact odd-prime descent filters for the complementary elliptic factor

This records the executed arithmetic attached to local_preprint's actual
Prym quotient.  The local calculations identify the two isogeny Selmer
groups exactly; they do not give a rank-zero conclusion.  The source and finite data are
`code/harmonic_prym_odd_filter.py`, its retained `_output.txt`, and
`_data.json`.  The independent dyadic filter is
`code/harmonic_prym_dyadic_filter.py` and its output/data.

## Field, model, and global classes

Put K=Q(sqrt(2)), epsilon=1+sqrt(2),
M=7*13*23*139*1119169=325596479663, and

    E_b: y^2=x^3+b*x,       b=1146*epsilon^2*M^2.

The dual E_{-4b} is K-isomorphic to E_{-b}: substitute
x=2X, y=2sqrt(2)Y.  This preserves the x-coordinate squareclass,
because 2=(sqrt(2))^2 in K.  The code therefore uses signs +1 and -1.

O_K=Z[sqrt(2)] is norm-Euclidean.  For any field element subtract
nearest integer coefficients to leave r+s sqrt(2), with |r|,|s|<=1/2;
the absolute norm |r^2-2s^2| is at most 1/2.  Thus its ideals are
principal.  Its units are generated by -1 and epsilon.  One elementary
verification of the fundamental unit is that a positive real unit strictly
between 1 and epsilon would have second integral coefficient 0 or1;
the norm equations exclude both.  Multiplication by powers of epsilon
then reduces every positive unit to this interval.  Epsilon has norm -1,
so the narrow class group is trivial as well.

The code takes pi_2=2+sqrt(2), which is totally positive and has norm2,
and positive generators for the other prime ideals.  The factorization

    b = pi_2^2 * 3 * (17+7sqrt(2))*(17-7sqrt(2)) * M^2

is checked as an equality of integer pairs, not only a squareclass identity.
There are twelve supporting prime ideals: one over2, one over3, two over191,
two over7, one over13, two over23, one over139, and two over1119169.
At3 and both primes over191 the valuation of b is1; at every other
supporting ideal it is2.  The integral generators, their chosen residue
roots, and the exact norm checks are in the JSON data.

For y^2=x(x^2+b), the x-coordinate class delta(P) is supported on these
prime ideals: outside them, a positive x-valuation occurs with even parity
from v(y^2)=v(x), and a negative one from v(y^2)=3v(x).  The usual chord
calculation proves the same homomorphism as in A143, over K verbatim.
Both real embeddings of b are positive, so a nonzero x on E_b is positive
in both.  All chosen prime generators are totally positive.  Among the
four unit squareclasses 1,-1,epsilon,-epsilon only1 is totally positive.
Consequently E_b has at most 2^12=4096 candidate classes.  For E_{-b}
we keep all four unit classes, giving 16384 candidates, without imposing
an unjustified sign exclusion.

## Actual residue fields and local quartic conditions

For a candidate d, its quartic is

    N^2=d U^4+(b/d)V^4.

A local rational point gives primitive integral U,V and integral N.
For an odd supporting prime ideal, write b=pi^e B, d=pi^delta d0,
where e is1 or2, delta is0 or1, and B,d0 are units.  The exact necessary
conditions used in the program are:

| e | delta | finite residue condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | d0 is a square |
| 1 | 1 | B/d0 is a square |
| 2 | 0 | d0 is a square or B/d0 is a square |
| 2 | 1 | -B/d0^2 is a fourth power |

For e=1 the unequal term valuations force the unique valuation-zero
coefficient to be a square.  For e=2, delta=0, either U or V is a unit;
the term valuations differ by2 modulo4, so the leading unit is one of
the two displayed ones.  For e=2, delta=1, if exactly one coordinate is
a unit the right side has odd valuation.  Thus both are units and the
two valuation-one terms must cancel modulo pi, giving the fourth-power
equation.  These tests are also sufficient at odd places: a square-unit
case has the point with U=0 or V=0 and Hensel lifting, while the
fourth-power case lifts its nonzero fourth root (derivative4 is a unit)
to a point with N=0.

For an inert rational prime p the code computes in
F_p[theta]/(theta^2-2), of cardinality p^2.  For a split prime with
generator A+B sqrt(2), it uses the actual root theta=-A/B modulo p.
After omitting the prime's own generator, multiplication of all the
other generators with their exact b-exponents gives B in that field;
the identical omission from d gives d0.  Every denominator used is
checked nonzero.  Square and fourth-power tests use the exact exponents
(q-1)/2 and (q-1)/gcd(4,q-1).

## Executed outcome and its precise implication

The odd filters leave64 classes on E_b and8 on E_{-b}.  Intersecting
the independent dyadic conditions modulo pi^13 leaves64 and4.
The four dual classes are exactly

    1, 1119169, -573, -573*1119169.

Every survivor is retained as its unit bits, prime-generator mask,
actual integer pair, and dyadic squareclass.  Local_preprint independently
audited the odd formulas, generators, residue embeddings, and code.
The complete 64-element first set is the squareclass span of

    3, 191, 23, 139(99+52sqrt(2)),
    7(1059+34sqrt(2)), 7(1059-34sqrt(2)).

The second set has basis 1119169 and -573.  The program verifies the
whole XOR spans, not just the two cardinalities.

The dyadic finite test is sufficient as well as necessary.  Write
pi=sqrt(2), v(pi)=1, and d=pi^delta*d0 with delta=0 or1.  The coefficient
b has valuation2.  In a primitive solution modulo pi^13, U or V is a
unit.  If U is a unit, the derivative of
N^2-dU^4-(b/d)V^4 in U has valuation4+delta, at most5.  If V is a unit,
its derivative has valuation6-delta, at most6.  The error valuation13
is strictly larger than twice either applicable derivative valuation.
One-variable Hensel lifting therefore gives a local point and preserves
the chosen unit coordinate.  This proves sufficiency for all eight
surviving local classes on E_b and both on E_{-b}.

At an odd prime outside the support, d and b/d are units.  The proper
quartic N^2=dU^4+(b/d)V^4 has smooth genus-one reduction: the binary
quartic has no repeated root since the residue characteristic is odd
and both coefficients are nonzero.  Hasse's bound gives at least
q+1-2sqrt(q)>0 residue-field points, which lift smoothly.  At the two
real places the positive curve requires and has d>0; for the negative
curve one of d and -b/d is positive, so every candidate has a real
point.  Thus the displayed 64 and4 classes are exactly the locally
soluble isogeny-covering classes, namely the two isogeny Selmer groups.
They are upper bounds on the actual images of the two global descent
maps and do not show that a remaining quartic has a global point.
The explicit dual isogeny argument of A143 works over K.  Since b and
-b are nonsquares in K, both rational two-torsion groups have order2,
and the nonzero kernel point of the dual isogeny is not in the first
isogeny's image.  Thus

    2^rank E_b(K) = |im delta_b| * |im delta_{-4b}| /4 <=64.

Equivalently rank E_b(K)<=6.  The finite-generation input follows by
the same finite-coset height descent over this number field; the explicit
descent kernels give a finite E_b(K)/2E_b(K).

There is an additional exact parity constraint.  Let sigma exchange
sqrt(2) and -sqrt(2).  The coefficient of the conjugate curve is
b*epsilon^{-4}.  The isomorphism E_b -> E_b^sigma scales x by
epsilon^{-2}, y by epsilon^{-3}.  Therefore the map on K-points

    J(x,y)=(epsilon^2*sigma(x), epsilon^3*sigma(y))

is a group homomorphism E_b(K)->E_b(K), and J^2=[-1], because
epsilon*sigma(epsilon)=-1.  The free rational Mordell--Weil space
is consequently a Q(i)-vector space.  The established possibilities
for its rank are0,2,4,6.  This calculation proves neither rank zero
nor a Tate--Shafarevich finiteness assertion, and it supplies no
unproved rational descent of the curve itself.

The next arithmetic step is to distinguish global points from the
remaining quartic coverings, or compute a sufficient analytic
nonvanishing certificate.  The first local tests have not supplied
the required finite-Mordell--Weil target.
