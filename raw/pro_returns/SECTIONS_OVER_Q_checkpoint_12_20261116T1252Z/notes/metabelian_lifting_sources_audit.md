# Exact comparison with the Hoshi and Saidi constructions

Audit completed 16 November 2026. The published primary PDFs and extracted text are saved as `work/sources/hoshi2010.*` and `work/sources/saidi2023.*`.

## Saidi: the projection to the prescribed abelian quotient is fixed

Source: Mohamed Saidi, *Further Examples of Non-geometric Sections of Arithmetic Fundamental Groups*, PRIMS 59 (2023), 571–586, DOI 10.4171/PRIMS/59-3-5, https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/41437. Read Condition (*) on pp.573–574, diagram(1.2), Fact1.3 and Lemma1.4 on pp.578–580.

In his notation, the geometric quotient has an exact sequence

\[
1\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow\Gamma\longrightarrow\Delta\longrightarrow1,
\]

and Condition(*)iii supplies the map \(\Delta\to\pi_1(\overline X)^{ab}\). Thus the actual composite \(H\to\Gamma\to\pi_1(\overline X)^{ab}\) is zero. Diagram(1.2) pulls the arithmetic extension back along a fixed point section \(s_1:G_k\to\widetilde\Pi\). Every constructed section is a section of that pullback; its image in \(\widetilde\Pi\), and therefore in the geometrically abelian arithmetic quotient, equals \(s_1\). Nonzero classes in \(H^1(G_k,H)\) distinguish the lifts, while this projection remains fixed. This gives the exact map responsible for the fixed abelian coordinate.

## Hoshi: an actual Jacobian-coordinate lift and its descent requirement

Source: Yuichiro Hoshi, *Existence of Nongeometric Pro-p Galois Sections of Hyperbolic Curves*, PRIMS 46 (2010), 829–848, DOI 10.2977/PRIMS/27, https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/41121. Read Lemmas3.2–3.4 and Theorem3.5 on pp.842–845, Corollary3.6 on pp.845–846, and their proofs.

For a regular prime \(p\), Hoshi uses the free pro-\(p\) quotient \(Q=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{un-p}/k)\), for his specified finite subfield \(k\) of the extension unramified away from \(p\). He constructs

\[
\Pi_U\simeq\Pi_U^Q\times_QG_k,
\qquad
\Pi_J\simeq\Pi_J^Q\times_QG_k.
\]

Lemma3.3 makes a compact-Jacobian section descend to \(\Pi_J^Q\). Theorem3.5 lifts it through \(\Pi_U^Q\twoheadrightarrow\Pi_J^Q\) using projectivity of \(Q\), then pulls back to \(G_k\). This genuinely permits changing the compact-Jacobian section. The descent proof kills inertia away from \(p\): its pro-\(p\) abelian quotient has Frobenius eigenvalue \(Nq\), whereas the good-reduction Jacobian Tate module has eigenvalues of complex absolute value \(\sqrt{Nq}\). An equivariant map between those modules is zero.

For the tripod torus, the relevant explicit map is instead

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Frob}_q}
       (\mathbb Z_p(1),\mathbb Z_p(1))=\mathbb Z_p.
\]

The inertia restriction of \(\kappa_p(a)\) is \(v_q(a)\,t_p\), where \(t_p\) is the tame pro-\(p\) character. This follows by writing \(a=\varpi^{v_q(a)}u\): adjoining compatible \(p\)-power roots of the unit is unramified at \(q\ne p\), while the compatible roots of the uniformizer give \(t_p\). Thus every nonzero ordinary integer valuation is nonzero in this inertia module. Such a coordinate class cannot descend through the displayed quotient \(G_k\to Q\).

## A general elementary barrier for free pro-p quotients

Let \(K\) be a nonarchimedean local field of residue characteristic \(q\ne p\), with residue cardinality \(Q_K\), and let \(f:G_K\to F\) be continuous, where \(F\) is free pro-\(p\). Then \(f(I_K)=1\).

Indeed the wild pro-\(q\) inertia maps trivially to a pro-\(p\) group. The remaining inertia image is generated by the image \(\tau\) of tame pro-\(p\) inertia. If \(\phi\) is the Frobenius image, the exact tame relation is

\[
\phi\tau\phi^{-1}=\tau^{Q_K}.
\]

The closed subgroup \(L=\overline{\langle\phi,\tau\rangle}\) is free pro-\(p\); the closed-subgroup theorem is also the input explicitly used in Hoshi's proof of Lemma3.2. Its abelianization is a finite-rank free \(\mathbb Z_p\)-module. Hence \((Q_K-1)[\tau]=0\) implies \([\tau]=0\), so \(\tau\in L'\).

The subgroup generated by \(\phi\) must equal \(L\). Otherwise a finite \(p\)-group quotient detects that it is proper, and a maximal subgroup containing its image has index \(p\). The resulting nonzero homomorphism \(L\to\mathbb Z/p\) kills \(\phi\), and kills \(\tau\in L'\), contradicting their generation of \(L\). Thus \(L\) is procyclic. The tame relation now gives \(\tau^{Q_K-1}=1\). A free pro-\(p\) group is torsion-free, so \(\tau=1\), proving the assertion.

Consequently every continuous map from a number-field Galois group to a free pro-\(p\) group kills all inertia of residue characteristic different from \(p\). Replacing Hoshi's quotient by another free pro-\(p\) quotient cannot retain the nonzero ordinary fresh-prime valuation coordinates of the constructed ghosts. This conclusion concerns this exact projective-quotient method; the full pro-\(p\) metabelian obstruction remains to be computed through its actual nonprojective extension.
