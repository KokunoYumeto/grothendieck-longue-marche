The ordered cocycle has h_m=1, h_u=w^-1 r, and h_(st)=alpha_s(h_t)h_s. Its actual graph section is H=<m,fv>=<m,r^-1wu>. The previously proved full centralizer and character argument gives

For clarity, the equality uses alpha_s(r)=h_s r h_s^-1 for every s, ordinary free centralizers, and the invariant character chi_x(r,bar_r,w)=(1,-1,1), which vanishes on EVERY h_s. Thus a source fixing r has h_s=1, not merely a cyclic correction.

## 2. An actual invariant quotient, with explicit inverse presentation

Factor T0 by the normal closure of

    r^2, bar_r^2, f^2, g^2.                                (2)

All four are actual displayed peripheral words, with the exact original f and g above. Set z=bar_r r. The resulting group is exactly

    P=<z,w,r | [z,w], r^2=1, r z r=z^-1, r w r=w^-1>
     =Z^2(z,w) semidirect C2(r),                           (3)

where r inverts both translations.

Here are both presentation directions. From r^2=bar_r^2=1 and g^2=(r w)^2=1 obtain r w r=w^-1. From f^2=(w^-1 bar_r)^2=1 obtain bar_r w bar_r=w^-1. With bar_r=z r, the latter gives [z,w]=1, while bar_r^2=1 gives r z r=z^-1. Conversely the formulas bar_r=z r, f=w^-1 bar_r and g=r w in (3) make all four squares (2) equal to one, with inverse maps on r,bar_r,w. In particular the translation subgroup in (3) is the genuine free abelian group Z^2; it is not a further unidentified quotient.

The actual source maps descend to P. This can be seen directly from the following automorphisms of (3), with their displayed inverse matrices and corrections; equivalently substitute all signed actual words into (3). Write every element uniquely as z^a w^b r^epsilon. In column-vector convention, a source acts by

    (n,epsilon) -> (A_s n+epsilon b_s,epsilon),

where the two positive matrices and r-corrections are

    M=A_m=[[1,-1],[0,1]],       b_m=(0,0),
    U=A_u=[[1,0],[4,1]],        b_u=(0,-2).                 (4)

Indeed m fixes r and bar_r, and sends w to w z^-1, so z is fixed. Also u sends r to w^-2 r and bar_r to w^2 bar_r; hence z goes to z w^4 and w is fixed. Every matrix is integral of determinant one, and (n,epsilon)->(A n+epsilon b,epsilon) is an automorphism of the semidirect presentation (3). The inverse is given by A^-1 and -A^-1 b. Thus (2) is invariant under BOTH signed source generators, and the quotient is genuine for the entire free source S.

## 3. The all-word affine correction is a rational coboundary

Let e1=(1,0)^T and q=e1/2 in Q^2. On both actual generators,

    b_s=(I-A_s)q.                                         (5)

The action composition law is b_(st)=A_s b_t+b_s. Therefore (5) holds for EVERY s in S, including all signed words; no induction bound occurs. The right side is integral for these actual matrices because it equals the actual integer correction.

If alpha_s(r)=r in the actual free fiber, its image in P is also r. Unique normal form in (3) then gives b_s=0. By (5),

    A_s e1=e1.                                           (6)

Every integral determinant-one matrix satisfying (6) has the form [[1,k],[0,1]] with k in Z. This is exactly M^-k. The only remaining point is faithfulness of the actual matrix representation S->SL2(Z); it cannot be replaced by an unproved monodromy assertion.

## 4. Complete faithful ping-pong proof

Conjugate both matrices by D=diag(2,1), viewed over Q. This gives

    A=D M D^-1=[[1,-2],[0,1]],
    B=D U D^-1=[[1,0],[2,1]].                              (7)

On the real projective line, use disjoint nonempty sets

    X={x real:|x|>1} union {infinity},
    Y={x real:|x|<1}.

For EVERY nonzero integer n,

    A^n(x)=x-2n,             A^n(Y) subset X;
    B^n(x)=x/(2n x+1),       B^n(X) subset Y.              (8)

For the first inclusion, |x-2n|>1 when |x|<1. For the second, when |x|>1 the denominator has absolute value at least 2|n||x|-1>|x|; infinity maps to 1/(2n), also in Y. The possible pole -1/(2n) lies in Y, so does not affect B^n(X).

For completeness this proves free-product faithfulness without invoking an external theorem. Any nontrivial reduced word in nonzero powers of A and B is conjugate to either a nonzero power of one generator or a mixed cyclically reduced word with at least two syllables. Nonzero powers are nonidentity from their displayed matrices. For a mixed cyclically reduced word, conjugate once more by a nonzero power of its last generator chosen so that the last syllable does not cancel: the resulting reduced word starts and ends in that generator. If it starts and ends in A it maps Y into X by (8); if it starts and ends in B it maps X into Y. Either contradicts being the identity because the sets are disjoint. Hence every nontrivial reduced word acts nontrivially on the projective line. In particular the representation F(m,u)->SL2(Z) in (4) is injective: a matrix in its kernel would act trivially on the projective line. This also excludes a nontrivial word mapping to -I.

Now (6) implies A_s=M^-k, and injectivity gives s=m^-k. Conversely every power of m fixes the original r. Combining with (1) proves the complete actual equality

    L=H intersect S=<m>.                                  (9)

This argument keeps both operators, all signed source words, and the entire cocycle. The pillowcase quotient is used only as a necessary condition whose faithful source matrices identify the stabilizer; no quotient-fixed element is lifted back by assumption.
