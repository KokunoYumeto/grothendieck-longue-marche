    J_e^alpha = s phi A_r       if e=+1,
                A_r s          if e=−1.

The involution on the actual pure centralizer T_R,e=C_(E_R)(R_e) is the restriction of

    kappa_e = Ad((J_e^alpha)^−1) alpha

on E_R'. A117 gives

    T_R,e = T2,e × T3,e × product_(p≥5) Tp,e,
    T2,e = (P')^(rho_e),
    T3,e = C_(G3')(J_e),  Tp,e = C_(Gp)(J_e),
    J_e = diag(1,e) J diag(1,e)^−1,
    J = [[0,−1],[1,−1]].

Write G_odd'=G3' × product_(p≥5) Gp and T_odd,e=T3,e × product_(p≥5) Tp,e. Then

    H1(C2,G_odd';kappa_e) = {*},

whereas A112/A117 give

Consequently the map induced by the ACTUAL inclusion of coefficient groups,

    H1(C2,T_R,e;kappa_e) → H1(C2,E_R';kappa_e),

identifies with

    H1(C2,T2,e;kappa_e) × product_(p odd) C2

    ([z2],epsilon_odd) ↦ inclusion_*([z2]).

These are continuous maps of pointed compact spaces. In particular the complete subspace with basepoint dyadic coordinate and arbitrary odd coordinates maps to the basepoint. The original coherent class need not have basepoint dyadic coordinate, and none of its coordinates is evaluated by this statement.

## 3. A full odd matrix cocycle lemma

Let O=Zp for an odd prime and let L be an invertible trace-zero two-by-two matrix with L²=lambda I, lambda in O×. Let kappa=Ad(L). Then

    H1(C2,SL2(O);kappa) = {*}.                         (1)

Indeed, a cocycle z satisfies z kappa(z)=I. Set M=zL. Then

    M²=lambda I,  det M=det L=−lambda.

The Cayley–Hamilton identity gives (tr M) M=0. Since M is invertible, tr M=0. Both M and L therefore have characteristic polynomial t²−lambda.

Here are complete integral conjugacy and determinant-adjustment arguments. If lambda is a square modulo p, it has a unit square root a in O, and the idempotents (I±M/a)/2 decompose O² into two rank-one direct summands. Both have rank one by the characteristic polynomial modulo p. They are free, since O is local, and similarly for L. Choosing their bases makes both matrices diag(a,−a), so M=g0^−1 L g0 for some g0 in GL2(O). The centralizer of L has matrices of any unit determinant in this basis, for example diag(t,1).

If lambda is a nonsquare modulo p, the reduction of t²−lambda is irreducible. For any primitive vector v, the pair (v,Mv) is a basis modulo p and hence over O; it gives the companion matrix of the same polynomial. The same construction for L proves GL2(O) conjugacy. The centralizer algebra is O[t]/(t²−lambda), the unramified quadratic algebra, and determinant is its norm. This norm is onto O×: its residue norm is onto because the finite-field multiplicative group is cyclic; after lifting a residue solution, the remaining unit lies in 1+pO and is the square of a scalar unit. The latter square root exists by successive lifting, because multiplication by two is invertible modulo p. The norm of that scalar unit is its square. Thus the centralizer again has every unit determinant.

Choose c in the centralizer of L with det c=(det g0)^−1 and put g=cg0. Then g belongs to SL2(O) and M=g^−1 Lg. Equivalently

    z = g^−1 kappa(g).

This proves (1) for every cocycle, not just for a finite selection of matrix values. In the actual application of section2, lambda=1, so only the split eigenspace argument is needed. The nonsplit paragraph shows that the conclusion does not rely on an unstated square choice.

## 4. The precise three-adic derived correction

The coefficient at three is G3', not the full G3. The actual quotient is

    chi:G3 → C3,  ker chi=G3',
    chi(X)=chi(Y)=1.

The original alpha sends X to X^−1 and Y to a conjugate of Y^−1. It therefore acts by −1 on chi. Conjugation by either original J_e^alpha acts trivially on chi: its pure factors act internally, while s swaps X and Y, which have the same chi value. Consequently

    chi(kappa_e(g)) = −chi(g)                          (2)

on the full G3. This is a statement about the actual marked action, not about an arbitrary matrix normalizer.

Let z be a kappa_e cocycle in G3'. Section3 supplies g in G3 with z=g^−1 kappa_e(g). Applying chi and (2) gives

    0=chi(z)=−2chi(g).

Multiplication by two is invertible on C3, so g lies in G3'. The SAME coboundary therefore exists inside the required derived coefficient group. We have proved

    H1(C2,G3';kappa_e) = {*}.                          (3)

No fixed lifting across a pro-two kernel and no finite-group census is involved.

The actual product decomposition of E_R' is componentwise kappa_e-equivariant, as in A112/A117. Cocycles, correcting conjugators, and equivalence are componentwise. ## 6. Meaning for the original coherent equation

For a positive factor frame h, the actual defect is

    w_h=(J_e^alpha)^−1 alpha(h)^−1 v h in T_R,e.

An allowed change h to ht preserves the positive factor equation exactly when t centralizes R_e. Coherence is therefore the norm equation inside T_R,e, not inside all of E_R'. Section5 proves that every odd class becomes an ambient coboundary if one drops this constraint. Such a correcting element need not centralize R_e, and then it changes the positive factor being framed. Its existence is not a coherent frame.

In particular the original three-adic CM coordinate can be nontrivial as a centralizer class while mapping to the basepoint in the larger coefficient group. The theorem does not claim that this occurs for the actual full-GT datum. It proves why the vanishing of the ambient cohomology image cannot decide that coordinate.
