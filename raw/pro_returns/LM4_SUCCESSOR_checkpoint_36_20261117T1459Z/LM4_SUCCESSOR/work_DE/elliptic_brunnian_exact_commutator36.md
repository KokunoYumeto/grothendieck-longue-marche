# Exact closed commutator description of the actual elliptic joint kernel

Checkpoint 36 research, separate from the frozen formal statements. The ordinary intersection proof below is elementary and complete. The passage to the actual compact kernel uses the separately proved full topology of one specific finitely generated fiber product; no general infinite-index completion assertion is made.

## 1. Actual marked input and ordinary model

The completely reviewed note `work_A/actual_elliptic_brunnian_two_retractions36.md` identifies the actual compact third-forgetting fiber as
\[
 F=\widehat F(h,k,m),\qquad H=\widehat F(h,k),\qquad w=[k^{-1},h^{-1}],
\]
with commutator convention \([a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}\). Its two retractions fix H and send m respectively to 1 and w. Write U and V for their actual closed kernels, equivalently the closed normal closures of m and \(mw^{-1}\) in F. The actual joint elliptic kernel is exactly
\[
 \mathcal B=U\cap V.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular no additional intersection with the ambient pure derived subgroup is missing: U already lies in that subgroup, by the actual meridian marking. The image of the pair of retractions is exactly
\[
 H\times_{H_{\rm ab}}H.
\tag{2}
\]

Let \(H_0=F(h,k)\), \(F_0=H_0*\langle m\rangle\), and let \(U_0,V_0\) be the corresponding ordinary normal closures. The ordinary retraction pair has image
\[
 D_0=H_0\times_{H_{0,\rm ab}}H_0
\]
and kernel \(U_0\cap V_0\). Surjectivity follows because its image contains the diagonal H0 and (1,w), and the normal closure of w in H0 is H0'. We prove
\[
 U_0\cap V_0=[U_0,V_0]
\tag{3}
\]
before making any completion comparison.

## 2. An explicit simultaneous free basis for H0'

Put \(u=k^{-1},v=h^{-1}\), so \(H_0=F(u,v)\) and \(w=[u,v]\). Use the transversal
\[
 t_{ij}=u^iv^j\quad(i,j\in\mathbb Z)
\]
for the abelianization kernel. Schreier rewriting gives a free basis of H0' consisting of
\[
 e_{ij}=u^iv^j u v^{-j}u^{-i-1}\quad(i\in\mathbb Z,\ j\ne0),
\qquad e_{i0}=1.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed all v-edge Schreier generators vanish; the u-edge generators are exactly (4), with the j=0 edges belonging to the chosen spanning tree. This is the usual spanning-tree basis for the integer-grid covering graph.

The literal conjugates of the single commutator satisfy
\[
 w_{ij}=t_{ij}wt_{ij}^{-1}=e_{ij}e_{i,j+1}^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
For each fixed i, (5) is an invertible change of the countable free basis. The inverse expressions are finite:
\[
 e_{ij}=
 \begin{cases}
 w_{i,j-1}^{-1}w_{i,j-2}^{-1}\cdots w_{i0}^{-1},&j>0,\\
 w_{ij}w_{i,j+1}\cdots w_{i,-1},&j<0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Substitution into (5) and (6) gives the identity in both directions. These formulas define inverse homomorphisms of the corresponding ordinary free groups; there are no infinite products. Consequently
\[
 \{w_{ij}:i,j\in\mathbb Z\}
 \text{ is a free basis of }H_0'.
\tag{7}
\]
The order and the transversal in (5) matter; arbitrary conjugates have not been claimed to form a basis.

## 3. The entire ordinary intersection

The kernel U0 of \(F_0\to H_0\), m↦1, has the simultaneous free basis
\[
 m_h=h m h^{-1}\quad(h\in H_0).
\tag{8}
\]
Set \(W_0=[U_0,V_0]\). Since both factors are normal in F0, W0 is normal in F0 and is contained in \(U_0\cap V_0\). In \(F_0/W_0\), the images of U0 and V0 commute elementwise.

For every h, the word \(m_h w_h^{-1}\), where \(w_h=hwh^{-1}\), belongs to V0. Hence on the image \(\overline U_0=U_0/W_0\) the two conjugations agree:
\[
 \operatorname{Ad}(w_h)|_{\overline U_0}
 =\operatorname{Ad}(m_h)|_{\overline U_0}.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular this holds for every \(h=t_{ij}\).

Every h has a unique form \(h=r t_{ij}\), with \(r=ht_{ij}^{-1}\in H_0'\). Express r as a finite word in the free basis (7). By (9), its conjugation action on \(\overline U_0\) is the inner action of the same word with each \(w_{ab}\) replaced by \(m_{t_{ab}}\). Thus
\[
 m_h=r m_{t_{ij}}r^{-1}
\]
lies in the subgroup of \(\overline U_0\) generated by all \(m_{t_{ab}}\). Since (8) generates U0, these latter elements generate the whole quotient.

Let Z be the ordinary free group on labels \(z_{ij}\). The maps just proved give a surjection
\[
 Z\longrightarrow \overline U_0,\qquad z_{ij}\longmapsto m_{t_{ij}}W_0.
\]
The second retraction m↦w factors through this quotient, and the composite to H0' sends \(z_{ij}\) to \(w_{ij}\). By (7), that composite is an isomorphism. Therefore the surjection to \(\overline U_0\) is itself injective and the induced map \(\overline U_0\to H_0'\) is an isomorphism. Its kernel is exactly \((U_0\cap V_0)/W_0\). This proves (3), and hence
\[
 F_0/[U_0,V_0]\cong D_0
\tag{10}
\]
with both retraction markings preserved.

## 4. Why this particular completion comparison is valid

The complete separate proof `work_A/elliptic_fiber_product_full_topology36.md` establishes that D0 has its full profinite topology induced from \(G_0=H_0\times H_0\). Here is its finite-target argument, included to make the exact dependency transparent.

D0 is finitely generated by diagonal h,k and (1,w). Given a finite-index normal K in D0, the G0-conjugacy orbit of K is finite: a finitely generated group has only finitely many subgroups of its fixed index. Its intersection K0 is normal in G0 and finite index in D0. Then \(G_0/K_0\) is a finitely generated extension of \(\mathbb Z^2\) by the finite group D0/K0. Centralize that finite kernel. The resulting finite-index group is a central extension of a finite-index subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^2\) by a finite group. Powers of two basis lifts commute and generate a finite-index central free abelian subgroup. Its normal core is still finite-index free abelian. The whole group is residually finite: quotient by that core separates elements outside it, and characteristic subgroups n times the core separate its nonzero elements. A single finite quotient can therefore be chosen injective on the finite D0/K0. Pullback gives a finite G0 target whose kernel on D0 lies in K.

This is finite-target refinement, not extension to the identical target. It proves that \(\widehat D_0\) embeds as its actual closure in \(\widehat H_0\times\widehat H_0\). Completion of the normal quotient \(G_0/D_0=\mathbb Z^2\) then identifies that closure with
\[
 \widehat D_0\cong H\times_{H_{\rm ab}}H.
\tag{11}
\]
No unrestricted completion of an infinitely generated orbit subgroup is involved.

## 5. Exact actual closed commutator theorem

Completing (10) and using (11) yields
\[
 F/\overline{[U_0,V_0]}
 \cong H\times_{H_{\rm ab}}H.
\tag{12}
\]
Here the bar is actual closure in F. The quotient identification follows directly from the universal property of full completion of an ordinary normal quotient. On the free generators h,k,m, its map is exactly the pair of actual retractions, so its kernel is U∩V by (1)–(2).

The ordinary U0 and V0 are dense in the actual U and V: each actual kernel is the closed normal closure of its displayed relator, and F0 is dense in F. Continuity of the commutator map therefore gives
\[
 \overline{[U_0,V_0]}=\overline{\langle [a,b]:a\in U,b\in V\rangle}.
\]
Consequently the entire actual elliptic joint kernel has the precise description
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal B=U\cap V=\overline{[U,V]}.}
\tag{13}
\]
The right side means the closed subgroup generated by all such commutators. It is normal in F because U and V are normal.

This does not identify either infinite-index ordinary subgroup completion with its actual closure. It does not assert that the joint kernel is free, trivial, or generated by one finite list. It supplies no value of the actual common word a_u, no vanishing of the specified Brunnian cyclic cocycle, and no elliptic boundary, full-hexagon, or CM conclusion. In particular it is compatible with the separately computed nontrivial whole space \(H^1(C_3,\mathcal B)\cong E'\).
