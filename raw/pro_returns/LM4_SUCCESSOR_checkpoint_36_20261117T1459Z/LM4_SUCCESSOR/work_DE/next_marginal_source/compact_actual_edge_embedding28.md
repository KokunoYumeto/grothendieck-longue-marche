## 1. Actual data, not a general subamalgam assumption

Write the actual proper amalgam and its proved ordinary subgroup as
\[
V=R_L*_{K}R_R,
\quad R_L=R^0,\quad R_R=p(R^0),
\]
\[
H_i=E_1*_{F}B_i,\qquad F=\langle f\rangle,
\quad E_1\le R_L,\quad B_i\le R_R.
\]
Here B136/B137 proves ordinary inclusion and
\[
E_1\cap K=B_i\cap K=F.
\]
There are actual identified-neck retractions
\[
r_L:R_L\to K,\qquad r_R:R_R\to K
\]
which fix the same embedded K, retaining the mirror eta in the right identification. They satisfy
\[
r_L(E_1)=F\subset E_1,
\qquad r_R(B_i)=F\subset B_i.
\tag{1}
\]
The left equality is the B136 color/retraction calculation. For the right one, B_i is generated by f and a^-1 p(k)a; the actual right retraction fixes f,a and kills p(k), so it kills the second generator.

The subgroup E1 is a retract of R_L (retain the color-one graph and H), hence has full induced profinite topology. The separate all-n virtually-free target proof establishes full topology of B_j in R_L; applying the actual theta gives full topology of B_i in R_R. These are the only vertex-topology inputs.

## 2. Refine an arbitrary finite target of the WHOLE H_i

Let psi:H_i->F0 be any homomorphism to a finite group. By full vertex topology, choose finite quotients
\[
q_L:R_L\to A_0,\qquad q_R:R_R\to B_0
\]
such that their restriction kernels on E1 and B_i are contained in the kernels of psi on those subgroups. Choose one finite-index normal subgroup
\[
M\triangleleft K,
\qquad M\subset\ker(q_L|_K)\cap\ker(q_R|_K).
\]
Replace the two quotients by their product maps
\[
q'_L=(q_L,r_L\bmod M),\qquad q'_R=(q_R,r_R\bmod M),
\]
and let A,B be their finite images. Their restrictions to K have exactly kernel M. Hence they identify the same finite edge C=K/M, and give a genuine quotient
\[
\pi:V\to G=A*_{C}B.
\tag{2}
\]
No normal subgroup of one vertex is silently identified with one in the other; the actual common K/M factor supplies the exact edge equality.

Put A1=q'_L(E1), B1=q'_R(B_i), and C1=q'_L(F)=q'_R(F), regarded in C. The crucial equalities are
\[
A_1\cap C=C_1=B_1\cap C.
\tag{3}
\]
For the left equality, suppose q'_L(e)=q'_L(x) with e in E1 and x in K. The second coordinate says xM=r_L(e)M. By (1), r_L(e)=f^n for some integer n. Since M is contained in the first coordinate's restriction kernel, q'_L(x)=q'_L(f^n). Thus the common element belongs to C1. The reverse containment is immediate. The right proof is identical with r_R(B_i)=F. This uses the entire retraction coordinate, not only equality of original edge kernels.

Ordinary amalgam normal form and (3) give an embedded subgroup
\[
J=\langle A_1,B_1\rangle
   =A_1*_{C_1}B_1\le G.
\tag{4}
\]
The restriction of pi to H_i surjects onto J. Both restrictions of psi factor through A1 and B1, by construction; they agree on C1 because they agree on the actual cyclic F. Therefore there is a homomorphism psi_J:J->F0 with
\[
\psi=\psi_J\circ\pi|_{H_i}.
\tag{5}
\]
This factors the arbitrary target of the whole subgroup, including mixed words in its two vertices; separate vertex separation would not suffice.

## 3. Finish finite-target extension

The amalgam G in (2) has finite vertex and edge groups. It is virtually free. An explicit proof is useful here: take sufficiently many copies of the two regular finite vertex actions on a common finite set. Their restrictions to C are free with equal orbit counts, so identify their C-orbits equivariantly. This gives a finite permutation quotient of G faithful on both vertices. Its kernel meets every conjugate of every finite vertex group trivially, acts freely on the ordinary Bass--Serre tree, and is therefore free. It has finite index and finite-rank quotient graph.

Every finitely generated subgroup of a virtually free group has full induced profinite topology, by the finite-core covering and finite-index argument written completely in `compact_vertex_overlap28.md` Section3. The group J is finitely generated, so there is a finite quotient omega:G->D whose kernel on J is contained in ker(psi_J). Compose omega with pi. Its restriction kernel on H_i is contained in ker(psi), by (5).

Since psi was arbitrary,
\[
\boxed{H_i\text{ has its full induced profinite topology in }V.}
\tag{6}
\]
Applying theta gives the same for H_j. Thus their actual closures in Vhat are their full completions. This proof uses the actual retractions and the proved B_i topology, not an unrestricted profinite subamalgam theorem.

## 4. Exact completed vertex and edge intersections

The same finite-target construction proves more than abstract full topology. In the actual completion Vhat,
\[
\overline{H_i}\cap\overline{R_L}=\overline{E_1},\qquad
\overline{H_i}\cap\overline{R_R}=\overline{B_i},\qquad
\overline{H_i}\cap\overline K=\overline F.
\tag{7}
\]
Here the regional vertex and edge completions are already proper by B136's actual compatible-kernel argument.

For example, take x in the first intersection and an arbitrary finite psi:H_i->F0. Form G,J,pi,psi_J exactly as above. In Ghat, the image of x lies both in Jbar and in the finite vertex A. The subgroup J is closed in the virtually free G; therefore
\[
\overline J\cap A=J\cap A=A_1.
\]
The last ordinary equality is the subamalgam normal form (4). Hence psi(x), computed using the continuous extension of (5), lies in psi(E1). This holds for every finite psi, so x lies in the closure of E1 in H_i_hat. Its actual image is exactly E1bar, proving the first equality. The second proof uses B. The third uses the finite edge C and JcapC=C1. Conversely all three indicated inclusions are immediate. No completion/intersection exchange was assumed.

The same argument, after theta, gives all the analogous completed intersections for H_j.

## 5. Actual compact subtrees, and the still separate intersection gate

By the completion universal property and (6), H_i_hat is the proper full profinite amalgam
\[
\widehat{E_1}\amalg_{\widehat F}\widehat{B_i}.
\]
The vertex groups and cyclic edge embed because they have their full topology already in H_i (indeed in V). Its standard compact coset tree maps continuously to the standard compact tree of Vhat. Equations (7) make each vertex-coset map and the edge-coset map injective. A continuous injection from these compact spaces into the Hausdorff ambient coset spaces is an embedding. Consequently the actual completed cyclic-edge subgroup has its expected embedded standard compact subtree. The same holds for H_j.

This closes the explicitly retained **compact cyclic-edge embedding/full-topology gate**. It does not by itself prove
