\statement{A.96}{Proved}{The actual sphere kernel and its relative pro-two embedding}
Remove only the original central full twist and put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 G=P_4/\langle\Delta_4^2\rangle,\quad P=def,
 \quad A=af^{-1},\quad C=cd^{-1},\\
 N=F(P,A,C),\qquad B=F(d,f).
\end{gathered}
\tag{A.96.1}
\]
Then \(G=N\rtimes B\), with the exact positive conjugation actions
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &P&A&C\\ \hline
 d&C^{-1}PC&C^{-1}P^{-1}CPA&C\\
 f&A^{-1}PA&A&CPA^{-1}P^{-1}A.
\end{array}
\tag{A.96.2}
\]
The map to B is the actual sphere retraction
\(a\mapsto f,c\mapsto d,e\mapsto(fd)^{-1}\), with d,f fixed.
Its full completion is \(\widehat N\rtimes\widehat B\).
For every finite quotient Q of B, writing \(H=\ker(B\to Q)\),
the actual relative quotient
\[
 \widehat G/O^2(\widehat N\rtimes\widehat H)
 \tag{A.96.3}
\]
retains an embedded free pro-two group \(N^{(2)}\) of rank three,
with P primitive and nontrivial. Here \(O^2\) denotes the kernel
of the maximal pro-two quotient.

\begin{proof}
The original identities give \([a,P]=[c,P]=1\),
\(Z=abc\), and \(\operatorname{Ad}(Z^{-1})=\operatorname{Ad}(P)\)
on the fiber. Thus the central element \(ZP=\Delta_4^2\) splits
off: recover \(P_4\) from G and an infinite central t by
\(Z=tP^{-1}\), \(b=a^{-1}Zc^{-1}\). These are inverse
presentation maps, so no boundary P is killed in G.

Substitute
\[
 a=Af,\qquad c=Cd,\qquad e=d^{-1}Pf^{-1},
 \qquad b=a^{-1}P^{-1}c^{-1}.
 \tag{A.96.4}
\]
The relations \([a,f]=[c,d]=1\) give \(f(A)=A,d(C)=C\).
The two boundary relations give \(f(P)=A^{-1}PA,d(P)=C^{-1}PC\).
The remaining original a-row is
\(a^{-1}da=Pf^{-1}dfP^{-1}\); with the preceding relations it
is equivalent to \([d,PA]=1\), hence to the displayed d(A).
The remaining c-row is
\(c^{-1}fc=d^{-1}PfP^{-1}d\); it is equivalent to
\(C^{-1}fC=PfP^{-1}\), hence to the displayed f(C).
Every substitution is reversible.

The resulting two maps are automorphisms independently of the
presentation argument. In the free basis \((P,C,R)\), \(R=PA\),
d conjugates P by \(C^{-1}\) and fixes C,R; its inverse conjugates
P by C. In the free basis \((P,A,S)\), \(S=CP\), f conjugates
P by \(A^{-1}\) and fixes A,S; its inverse conjugates P by A.
The semidirect product therefore exists, and (A.96.4) gives inverse
maps with the entire original presentation. Its kernel is exactly N.
Both actions are identity on \(N_{\rm ab}\). They preserve the
N-conjugacy classes of P,R,S and \(L=CPA=SP^{-1}R\), and fix L
literally; these assertions also follow directly from (A.96.2).

For the full topology, choose a characteristic finite-index
\(N_0\triangleleft N\). The B-action on \(N/N_0\) has finite
image. Intersect its kernel with any prescribed finite-index base
subgroup; the resulting finite semidirect quotient retains
\(N/N_0\). Such characteristic subgroups are cofinal because N is
finitely generated. This proves the full topology and the asserted
completed semidirect product.

We give the finite-group ingredient for the pro-two statement.
For a finite p-group J, every proper subgroup is properly contained
in its normalizer: use a central element outside it, or pass to the
quotient by the center and induct. Thus maximal subgroups are normal
of index p and
\(\Phi(J)=J^p[J,J]\). Every tuple lifting a basis of
\(J/\Phi(J)\) generates J, since otherwise it lies in a maximal
subgroup. The kernel of
\(\operatorname{Aut}(J)\to\operatorname{Aut}(J/\Phi(J))\)
acts freely on the set of all such lifts: fixing a generating tuple
forces identity. That set has p-power cardinality
\(|\Phi(J)|^{\dim J/\Phi(J)}\), so orbit--stabilizer makes the
automorphism kernel a p-group.

Apply this to every characteristic finite two-group quotient
\(N/N_0\). The action of B is trivial on its Frattini quotient,
because it is trivial on \(N_{\rm ab}/2\). Its action image is
therefore a two-group. Refining any finite two-group quotient of H
by this action kernel gives a finite two-group semidirect quotient
of \(N\rtimes H\) retaining \(N/N_0\). The inverse limits in both
directions give
\[
 (N\rtimes H)^{(2)}=N^{(2)}\rtimes H^{(2)}.
 \tag{A.96.5}
\]
Since \(N\rtimes H\) is finite index in G, its full completion is
\(\widehat N\rtimes\widehat H\), with the same maximal pro-two
quotient. This proves (A.96.3) and its embedded N-kernel. The quotient
by that kernel is the corresponding actual relative base quotient.
No completion of an infinite abstract relation kernel has been
identified with an ambient closure. The action of an original
canonical involution on these particular words requires its actual
marked peripheral input, supplied separately in A.97.
\end{proof}
