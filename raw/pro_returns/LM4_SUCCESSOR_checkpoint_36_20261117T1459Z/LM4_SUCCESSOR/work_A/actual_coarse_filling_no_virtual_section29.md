## 4. A self-contained restriction argument with the nontrivial module action

We prove precisely the finite-index implication needed here. Use homogeneous cochains: a two-cochain c(g0,g1,g2) is G-equivariant, and its differential is the alternating sum of omitted arguments. In the compact case every cochain is continuous. Choose representatives t for the right cosets H backslash G, and define the H-equivariant retraction rho:G to H by g=h t mapping to h. This is continuous for open H because its finitely many cosets are clopen.

Suppose c is a cocycle and its restriction is delta b on H, with b an H-equivariant one-cochain. Put b_rho(g0,g1)=b(rho g0,rho g1). Define the explicit prism cochain

\[
Kc(g_0,g_1)=c(\rho g_0,g_0,g_1)-c(\rho g_0,\rho g_1,g_1).
\]

Both cochains are H-equivariant, and direct expansion of the cocycle identity gives

\[
\delta Kc=c-\rho^*c,\qquad
\delta b_\rho=\rho^*c.
\]

Hence c=delta B as an H-equivariant cochain on G, where B=Kc+b_rho. Now choose representatives r for the left cosets G/H and average with the actual coefficient action:

\[

The expression is independent of each representative r: replacing it by r h cancels through H-equivariance of B. Reindexing the finite sum shows it is G-equivariant. It is continuous when appropriate. Differential commutes with the sum, and G-equivariance of c gives

\[

Since n is invertible in F_p, n^{-1}Av B is a G-equivariant coboundary for c. This proves the implication.
