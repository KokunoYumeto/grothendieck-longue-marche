## 2. The actual two-strand half-twist

Let F=widehat(P1,2)=Fhat(X,Y), with the same commutator convention, and let s denote the original two-strand half-twist in B1,2. The reduced total products and the mixed relation give

\[
X_2=sXs=X^{-1},\qquad
Y_2=s^{-1}Ys^{-1}=Y^{-1},\qquad s^2=K=[Y,X].
\]

Thus its actual conjugation action j on F is

\[
j(X)=X^{-1}K^{-1},\qquad j(Y)=K Y^{-1},\qquad
j(K)=K,\qquad j^2=\operatorname{Ad}(K).
\tag{2}
\]

The equality j(K)=K follows either from K=s^2 or by direct substitution, and j^2 is checked on both free generators. In particular j is not replaced by an order-two automorphism.

## 3. Specify the based deletion maps

Start with the actual geometric deletion of strand3,

\[
\pi_3:\widehat P_{1,3}\longrightarrow F.
\]

Use the inherited order of the two surviving strands and their original handle paths as the target marking. Deleting a third-strand handle path contracts it, while the n=2 total products identify the two remaining handle paths as inverses. Deleting a crossing involving the removed strand contracts that meridian. Consequently

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 &X&Y&X_2&Y_2&a&b\\ \hline
\pi_3&X&Y&X^{-1}&Y^{-1}&K&1 .
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]

The pure full twist C^3 is the ordinary three-strand full twist. Deleting strand3 gives the ordinary two-strand full twist s^2=K. Thus

\[
\pi_3\rho^3=\operatorname{Ad}(K)\pi_3=j^2\pi_3.
\tag{4}
\]

Define the other two *based* deletion maps by the explicit braid whiskers

\[
\pi_1=\pi_3\rho^{-1},\qquad
\pi_2=j^{-1}\pi_3\rho.
\tag{5}
\]

Conjugation by C permutes the three labels cyclically. Thus these are geometric deletion of strand1 and strand2, with the target basepaths in (5) stated as part of their definition. Formula (5), rather than an unbased permutation of three maps, specifies their normalization.

The following is a generating set of the actual pure group:
X,Y,X2,Y2,a,b. Indeed, forgetting strand3 has free rank-two base generated by the first-strand handle loops. Its punctured-torus fiber is generated by the two third-strand handle loops and one of the two pair meridians. Here X3=(XX2)^{-1}, Y3=(YY2)^{-1}, and that meridian may be A13=K a^{-1}. Thus all of these fiber and base generators belong to the displayed set. The same set topologically generates the full completion.

## 4. Complete conjugation and deletion rows

The exact conjugation rows on that generating set are

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
g&\rho(g)\\ \hline
X&X_2a^{-1}\\
Y&aY_2\\
X_2&X_3A_{13}^{-1}\\
Y_2&A_{13}Y_3\\
a&b\\
b&bA_{13}b^{-1}.
\end{array}
\tag{6}
\]

The X and X2 rows are already literal argument transformations in A121. The Y row follows from tau commuting with Y and Y2=sigma^{-1}Ysigma^{-1}. For Y2, use rho(sigma)=tau:

\[
\rho(Y_2)=\tau^{-1}(aY_2)\tau^{-1}
         =\tau^{-1}a\tau\,Y_3=A_{13}Y_3.
\]

The braid relation gives rho(a)=b and
rho(b)=tau sigma^2 tau^{-1}=b A13 b^{-1}.
The total products determine the X3,Y3 rows, so no additional generator is omitted.

Applying (3)–(5) to (6) gives the complete normalized deletion table

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 &X&Y&X_2&Y_2&a&b\\ \hline
\pi_1&1&1&X&Y&1&K\\
\pi_2&X&Y&1&1&1&1\\
\pi_3&X&Y&X^{-1}&Y^{-1}&K&1 .
\end{array}
\tag{7}
\]

Here pi1(A13)=1, pi2(A13)=K, pi3(A13)=1. To make the whisker verification explicit, (6) gives pi3 rho(X)=j(X), pi3 rho(Y)=j(Y), and pi3 rho(X2)=pi3 rho(Y2)=pi3 rho(a)=pi3 rho(b)=1. This proves the pi2 row. For pi1, use pi3 rho^{-1}=j^{-2}pi3 rho^2 from (4). The rho^2 images of X,Y,a have trivial pi3 image; those of X2,Y2,b have images KXK^{-1}, KYK^{-1}, K respectively. Applying j^{-2}=Ad(K^{-1}) gives exactly its row in (7).

It follows on the whole completed pure group that

\[
\boxed{\pi_1\rho=\pi_3,\qquad
       \pi_2\rho=j\pi_1,\qquad
       \pi_3\rho=j\pi_2.}
\tag{8}
\]

The first and third are immediate from (5); the second follows from (4). The table verifies every actual generating image and confirms the same equalities directly.

## 5. Project the unique cubic transporter

Let w be any profinite commutator word satisfying the original pentagon, and retain its uniquely normalized p_w from A124–A125. Put

\[
U=w(X,K),\quad
W=w(YX^{-1}Y^{-1},K)Yw(X,K)^{-1},\quad
B_w=w(a,b).
\]

The exact transporter equation is

\[
W B_w=\rho(p_w)\,Y\,p_w^{-1}.
\tag{9}
\]

The deletion images of the left side are (1,W,W). Indeed pi1 kills X,Y, and in each deletion the two arguments of B_w include the identity, so its commutator-word value is one. Write p_i=pi_i(p_w). Equations (7)–(9) give

\[
p_1=p_3=t,\qquad
W=j(t)Yp_2^{-1}=j(p_2)Yt^{-1}.
\tag{10}
\]

These are the actual based equations. In particular j cannot be omitted.

## 6. The exact remaining common word

Because j(X)=YX^{-1}Y^{-1} and j(K)=K,

\[
W=j(U)Y U^{-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

Define

\[
\theta=\operatorname{Ad}(Y^{-1})j.
\]

Then direct substitution gives

\[
\theta(X)=X^{-1},\quad
\theta(Y)=XY^{-1}X^{-1},\quad
\theta^2=1,\quad
j\theta=\operatorname{Ad}(Y).
\tag{12}
\]

For example theta^2 is the inner action of
Y^{-1}j(Y^{-1})K=1. In the free basis (X,YX^{-1}), theta inverts both generators; no action has been replaced without its actual basis change.

Set a_w=U^{-1}t. The first equality involving W in (10) gives
p2=U theta(a_w). The other equality is then automatic by
j theta=Ad(Y). Thus the full exact result is

\[
\boxed{\pi_1(p_w)=\pi_3(p_w)=U a_w,\qquad
       \pi_2(p_w)=U\theta(a_w),\qquad a_w\in F'.}
\tag{13}
\]

The membership a_w in F' follows from A125: p_w belongs to the actual pure derived subgroup, every homomorphism pi_i sends it into F', and U is a commutator-word evaluation. The word a_w is uniquely determined by the actual p_w and depends continuously on w. No equality a_w=1 is proved.

The identity j(W)=K W^{-1}, sometimes obtained from the two equations in (10), is already automatic from (11) and j^2=Ad(K). It is therefore not an additional canonical or pentagon restriction.

## 7. There is no hidden compatibility among the three derived images

There is an exact continuous surjection

\[
(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3):
(\widehat P_{1,3})'\longrightarrow (F')^3.
\tag{14}
\]

To prove it, A125 shows that all pure pair meridians lie in the pure derived subgroup: they are the meridian classes killed by the intersection calculation. This also follows directly for A13 once a,b are known, since A13=K a^{-1}. Their table is

\[
b\longmapsto(K,1,1),\quad
A_{13}\longmapsto(1,K,1),\quad
a\longmapsto(1,1,K).
\]

Each pi_i is onto, by (7). The image of the closed normal closure of the corresponding meridian under the whole pure group is therefore the complete closed normal closure of K in the corresponding copy of F, with the other two coordinates one. The closed normal closure of [Y,X] in the free profinite group on X,Y is exactly F': its quotient is the free profinite abelian group on those generators. Compactness ensures the images are closed. Thus the joint image contains each individual F' coordinate, and hence their product. The reverse containment is functoriality of the derived subgroup. This proves (14).

Accordingly every formal triple on the right side of (13), for any U,a_w in F', is the triple of images of some actual element of the pure derived subgroup. Such a lift need not solve the full transporter equation. This establishes the precise limitation of the three projections; it does not replace the unique actual transporter by an arbitrary lift, identify the joint kernel, or prove that a_w is unrestricted for actual pentagon words.

## 8. Scope and next exact gate

All identities above retain the original w, the actual p_w, and specified based maps. For canonical w=u, (13) is a new exact reduction. Its common word remains tied to the full equation (9), not merely to its projections. The original norm and the full elliptic involution may impose a further relation on this common word and on the joint-kernel part of p_u; neither is discarded.
