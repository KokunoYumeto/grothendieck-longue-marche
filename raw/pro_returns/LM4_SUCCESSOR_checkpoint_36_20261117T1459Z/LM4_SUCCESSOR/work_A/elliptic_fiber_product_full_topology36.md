# Full topology of the ordinary fiber product underlying the actual elliptic joint kernel

This note closes one particular completion comparison by a finite-extension argument; it does not invoke a general subgroup-completion theorem or an unproved intersection theorem.

Let H0=F(h,k) be the ordinary rank-two free group, let Q0=H0ab=Z² and put

\[
 G_0=H_0\times H_0,\qquad
 D_0=H_0\times_{Q_0}H_0.
\tag{1}
\]

The quotient map G0→Q0 is the difference of the two abelianization maps, so D0 is normal and G0/D0=Z². Let w=[k inverse,h inverse]. The diagonal h,k and the element(1,w) generate D0 as an ordinary group: the normal closure of w in H0 is H0′, and every pair with equal abelianization is diagonal times(1,H0′). In particular D0 is finitely generated.

## 1. Every finite D0 target extends to a finite product target

Let K be a finite-index normal subgroup of D0. A finitely generated group has only finitely many subgroups of a fixed finite index: each is a stabilizer in one of the finitely many homomorphisms to the corresponding symmetric group. Conjugation by G0 preserves D0 and the index of K. Therefore

\[
 K_0=\bigcap_{g\in G_0}gKg^{-1}
\]

is a finite intersection, is normal in G0 and has finite index in D0. Write A=D0/K0, a finite group. Then E0=G0/K0 is a finitely generated extension

\[
 1\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow E_0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2
 \longrightarrow1.
\tag{2}
\]

We verify directly that E0 is residually finite. Its subgroup E1=C_E0(A) has finite index, since A is finite. Its image Q1 in Z² has finite index, and its kernel is Z(A), central and finite. Choose lifts u,v of a basis of Q1. Then E1 is generated by u,v and that finite central kernel, so it has nilpotency class at most two and [u,v] has finite order. If n is the exponent of the finite kernel, u^n and v^n commute with all of E1. They generate a central free abelian subgroup of rank two and finite index in E1, hence of finite index in E0. Taking its finite-index core gives a normal finite-index free abelian subgroup B of E0. An element outside B is separated by E0/B. A nonidentity element of B is separated by E0/(mB) for some positive integer m, because mB is characteristic in B, normal in E0 and finite index. This proves residual finiteness without an unsupported finite-extension assertion.

Since A is finite, residual finiteness of E0 supplies one finite quotient injective on A, by intersecting finitely many separating kernels. Pulling its kernel back to G0 gives a finite-index normal subgroup N with

\[
 N\cap D_0\subseteq K_0\subseteq K.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus every finite target of D0 is detected by an actual finite quotient of the ambient product. The full profinite topology of D0 is induced from G0.

## 2. Exact completion, with the actual marking

The embedding above identifies D0hat with the closure of D0 in G0hat. The completion of a direct product is the product of completions: restrictions of a finite quotient to the two commuting factors give the required finite product target. Also quotienting G0hat by the closure of a normal subgroup gives the full completion of the corresponding ordinary quotient, by the universal property. Consequently

\[
 \widehat D_0
 =\ker\bigl(\widehat H_0\times\widehat H_0
                 \longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{2}\bigr)
 =\widehat H_0\times_{(\widehat H_0)_{\mathrm{ab}}}\widehat H_0.
\tag{4}
\]

These are actual topological identifications with the given diagonal generators and(1,w). Under h↦X inverse,k↦Y inverse, this is exactly the compact fiber product in the two-retraction note. Therefore that particular finitely generated ordinary fiber product has now been shown to have the required full induced topology; it was not presumed from the compact kernel calculation.

## 3. Precise remaining ordinary gate

In the ordinary free group F(h,k,m), let U0 and V0 be the normal closures of m and m w inverse. Its map to D0 has kernel U0 intersect V0. The quotient by both relators has the torus presentation after eliminating m. If one separately proves the ordinary identity

\[
 U_0\cap V_0=[U_0,V_0],
\tag{5}
\]
