The actual ordinary retraction is essential. Its restriction after
conjugation by the adapted cone is the endomorphism
`nu(a,B,d,e,f)=(1,1,1,1,f)`. Consequently an element of K which
centralizes C is fixed by nu and is exactly a power of f. In the full
completion the same conclusion is literal: the retraction embeds Khat,
the endomorphism has procyclic image, and the primitive f-exponent map
uniquely identifies its parameter with a profinite integer.

The proposed uniform wreath-product detector is correctly typed. Under
`C -> lamp_0`, `k -> shift_1` in `C2 wr C_n`, the image of `C k^-1`
has shift coordinate -1. Conjugation of a lamp by any element of the
wreath product depends only on its shift coordinate. Thus a power with
parameter m can commute with lamp_0 only when m is zero modulo n.
Applying every n proves m=0 both in Z and in Zhat. The central z
coordinate of f does not affect this computation. This is an exact
symbolic family, not an inference from a finite cutoff.

Conjugating the whole intersection by a is legitimate because a belongs
to K and the selected cone is exactly `c_gap=a C a^-1`. It yields
`C_R(c_gap) intersect K=1` and its completed analogue.

For the normalizer, put `S=<c_gap,k>` and `F_gap=a f a^-1`.
The actual coarse projection identifies S with `D=C2*Z` and sends K
to the diagonal cyclic subgroup generated by `(C k^-1,z)`.
The element F_gap normalizes S, as follows from f=(C k^-1)z and
centrality of z on D. If x in K normalizes S, its coarse parameter m
is removed by `n=x F_gap^-m`. The resulting n remains in K and in

`n s n^-1` and s are both in S and have the same coarse image;
injectivity on S makes them equal. The preceding cone-intersection
statement forces n=1. This proves the exact normalizer, not only one
inclusion. The same proof works for closed subgroups in full completion:
all maps are actual completed retractions, the parameter is unique in
Zhat, and F_gap^m is defined continuously.
