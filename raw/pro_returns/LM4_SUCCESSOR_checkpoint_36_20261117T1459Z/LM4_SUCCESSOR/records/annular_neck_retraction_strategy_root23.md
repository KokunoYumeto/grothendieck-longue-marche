## 2. Cofinal compatible kernels need only ordinary retractions

Let K be a finitely generated discrete group. For i=1,2 suppose
R_i=H_i semidirect <c_i>, c_i of order two, and K is a specified
subgroup of H_i with a retraction r_i:H_i->K. Put

    nu_i = r_i composed with Ad(c_i) restricted to K.

This is an endomorphism of K. It need not be zero or the same for
the two vertices. Given a finite-index normal subgroup N of K,
put D=K/N and let

    L = intersection of ker(phi) over all homomorphisms phi:K->D.

There are finitely many such homomorphisms because K is finitely
generated and D is finite. Hence L is finite-index normal. The
quotient map is among the homomorphisms, so L is contained in N.
For any endomorphism eta of K and any phi, phi composed with eta
is another such homomorphism. Thus eta(L) is contained in L:
L is fully invariant. These subgroups are cofinal in all finite-index
normal subgroups of K.

    H_i -> K times K,

extends to R_i -> (K times K) semidirect_swap C2. Modulo L in

Because nu_i(L) is contained in L, this restriction has kernel
exactly L. The finite quotient also retains the orientation C2.
