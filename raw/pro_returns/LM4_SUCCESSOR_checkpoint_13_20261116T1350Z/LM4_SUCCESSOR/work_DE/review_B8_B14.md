# Independent topology check of root B.8 and B.14

## B.8: exact preimages

The orbifold path extension of the icosahedral action on the twelve-punctured sphere is the full inverse image in the Birman extension with one extra distinguished point. Its quotient orbifold has signature(0;2,3;one cusp), hence group B=C2*C3. The kernel is the ordinary sphere-minus-twelve-points group. A primitive puncture loop in that kernel maps to a conjugate of the fifth power of the primitive orbifold cusp τ.

The precise cusp identification of R12 must retain the extra puncture. In B12, R12 is the last-strand loop about the other eleven strands. On collapsing the disk boundary to the extra distinguished puncture, its inverse represents point pushing of that distinguished puncture about the twelfth puncture (up to the stated basepath conjugacy and orientation convention). This can also be checked using the factorization of the full twist into the full twist on eleven strands and the last-strand loop. Thus the verified identity a^5=Z R12^{-1} gives a^5 conjugate to τ^5 after quotienting by Z.

The subgroup L generated by the order-two and order-three images of β2,β3 is a free product C2*C3. One proof uses the Bass–Serre tree of the ambient free product: nontrivial finite stabilizers of its two vertex types have distinct fixed vertices and the edge stabilizers are trivial; the usual tree ping-pong argument gives the free product. Since the centralizer of τ^5 is the infinite cyclic group generated by primitive τ, the equality a^5=wτ^5w^{-1} forces a=wτw^{-1}. (If the cusp orientation is inverted, the same argument gives τ^{-1}.)

There is a direct tree argument for equality L=B, avoiding a finite-area inference. The primitive cusp τ has translation length2 in the Bass–Serre tree of C2*C3. For nontrivial elliptics fixing distinct vertices u,v, and with trivial edge stabilizers, the translation length of their product is2d(u,v): concatenate the geodesic from u to v with its translates; no cancellation occurs at either endpoint because neither elliptic fixes the incident edge. Since a is conjugate to τ or its inverse, the unique fixed vertices of the order-two and order-three generators are adjacent. B acts transitively on edges, so these are conjugates of the two vertices of the fundamental edge. Each generator generates the full corresponding vertex stabilizer. They therefore generate a conjugate of the entire free product B, which is B itself. This rules out a proper subgroup of the full Birman preimage.

The Artin preimage is now generated by β2,β3 because their subgroup surjects onto B and contains the entire central kernel <Z>. The abstract group <u,v|u²=v³> maps onto it, induces the identity C2*C3 quotient, and maps its central infinite cyclic generator to the infinite-order Artin full twist. Hence its kernel is trivial. This proves the exact B3 identification, rather than just a quotient with the right two relations.

Removing the order-five exceptional fibre from the Poincaré Seifert manifold leaves a Seifert space over a disk with cone orders2,3, namely a trefoil exterior up to orientation. This matches the central extension and should be tied to the checked Kirby–Scharlemann description or the explicit boundary slope below.

## B.14: meridian and homotopy type

The algebraic change d=ab, a=db^{-1} is a Nielsen move. It changes the two relators to b³d^{-2} and (db^{-1})^5b^{-3}. The first one-relator complex is a homotopy spine of the trefoil exterior: split the torus-knot exterior into its two solid tori with annular intersection. Their homotopy pushout is the double mapping cylinder of maps S1→S1 of degrees3 and2; collapsing a joining arc gives the stated presentation complex.

It is NOT sufficient to identify μ=db^{-1} as a meridian by abelianization alone. An explicit verification is available. In the standard trefoil Wirtinger presentation <x,y|xyx=yxy>, set b=xy, d=xyx. Then b³=d² and

    μ=db^{-1}=b x b^{-1}.

Thus μ is conjugate to the Wirtinger meridian x, with a specified basepath. The regular Seifert fibre z=b³=d² is represented on the boundary by the torus framing of the (2,3)-knot; its linking number with the knot is6. Hence z=μ^6 λ for the preferred longitude λ. Since μ andλ commute,

    μ^5 z^{-1}=(μ λ)^{-1}.

This is the negative of the primitive meridian-plus-longitude slope. Attach the two-handle on that slope. Its boundary is a sphere; filling that sphere by the final three-handle yields the ±1 trefoil surgery identified with the Poincaré sphere (handedness changes orientation). Before the three-handle one has exactly that closed manifold minus an open ball.

At the level of homotopy type, attaching a two-handle adds a two-cell along its core attaching curve. Transporting that curve through the trefoil homotopy equivalence gives precisely the second relator. Homotopy invariance of cell attachment then proves the claimed equivalence of the balanced presentation complex with the punctured Poincaré sphere. No inference from fundamental group and Euler characteristic alone is needed or valid.
