                                             Abstract. This paper establishes a new characterization of simple closed curves on a closed
                                           orientable surface. Let Γ be the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface. We prove
                                           that if an element g ∈ Γ has the same possible images as a given simple closed curve γ ∈ Γ
                                           under epimorphisms from Γ to every finite group, then g belongs to the Aut(Γ)-orbit of γ,
                                           i.e. g is itself a simple closed curve with the same topological type as γ. Consequently, the
                                           set of simple closed curves in Γ is closed in the profinite topology of Γ; and we obtain a new
                                           algorithm to decide whether a given element in Γ can be represented by a simple closed curve.

                                                                               1. Introduction
                                             Given a finitely generated group Γ, properties of an element γ ∈ Γ can be reflected
                                           by the possible images of γ under epimorphisms from Γ to finite groups. For any finite
                                           group G, we define
                                                     EpiImγ (Γ, G) = {φ(γ) | φ : Γ → G is a surjective homomorphism}.
                                           It is natural to ask: to which extent is γ distinguished from elements in Γ by the data
                                           EpiImγ (Γ, G)?
                                           Definition 1.1. Let Γ be a finitely generated group. Two elements γ1 , γ2 ∈ Γ are
                                           profinitely equivalent in Γ if EpiImγ1 (Γ, G) = EpiImγ2 (Γ, G) for every finite group G.
                                             It is clear that if there exists an automorphism of Γ that sends γ1 to γ2 , then γ1
                                           and γ2 are profinitely equivalent. The converse need not be true. For instance, if Γ is
                                           not residually finite, then there exists a non-trivial element γ ∈ Γ that is profinitely
                                           equivalent to the identity element 1 ∈ Γ.
                                           Definition 1.2. Let Γ be a finitely generated group. An element γ ∈ Γ is said to be
                                           profinitely rigid in Γ if any element that is profinitely equivalent to γ in Γ belongs to
                                           the Aut(Γ)-orbit of γ.
                                             The main result of this paper is as follows.
                                           Theorem 1.3. Let Σ be a closed orientable surface, and let Γ = π1 (Σ, ∗) be its funda-
                                           mental group. Then, for any element γ ∈ Γ represented by a simple closed curve and
                                           for any d ∈ Z, γ d is profinitely rigid in Γ.
                                           Remark 1.4. Theorem 1.3 is not true when profinite is weakened to pro-p. For

                                           a simple closed curve. Then, for any n ∈ Z coprime with p, (n, 0) is pro-p equivalent
                                           to (1, 0), i.e. they have the same epimorphism images onto finite p-groups. However,

                2. Profinite groups and profinite completion
   In addition to the materials covered in this section, the readers are referred to [19]
for a standard reference on profinite groups.

2.1. Profinite groups.

Definition 2.1. A profinite group is the projective limit of finite groups indexed over
indexed over a directed partially ordered set.

   A profinite group is a topological group. It inherits its topology from the product
topology of the factors of the inverse limit, where each finite group appearing in the
inverse limit is equipped with discrete topology. As a consequence, any profinite group
is compact and Hausdorff, see [19, Theorem 1.1.12].
   By a homomorphism of profinite groups Φ : G → G′ , we mean a group homomor-
phism that is continuous with respect to their topologies assigned above. As such, by
an isomorphism Φ : G → G′ , we mean a group isomorphism that is simultaneously a
homeomorphism.

3.1. Algorithm for recognition problem.

Proposition 3.2. Suppose Γ is a finitely presented group, and γ ∈ Γ is profinitely
rigid in Γ. Then, there exists an algorithm that given an element δ ∈ Γ in terms of a
word in the finite presentaion of Γ, determines in a finite amount of time whether δ
belongs to the Aut(Γ)-orbit of γ.

Proof. Let Γ = ⟨S = {s1 , · · · , sk } | R = {r1 , · · · , rm }⟩ be a finite presentation. This
algorithm proceeds with two sub-algorithms simultaneously.
   On the n-th day, list all finite groups of order n in terms of their multiplication
tables, and then list all possible surjective homomorphisms from Γ to these finite
groups. Consequently, we obtain EpiImγ (Γ, G) and EpiImδ (Γ, G) for every finite group
of order n, and then compare whether EpiImγ (Γ, G) = EpiImδ (Γ, G). The algorithm
stops once one finds such a finite group G with EpiImγ (Γ, G) ̸= EpiImδ (Γ, G).
   On the n-th night, list all automorphisms of Γ up to complexity n, i.e. a total of
finitely many choices among pairs of homomorphisms ϕ : Γ → Γ and ψ : Γ → Γ such
that:
(1) each ϕ(si ) and ψ(si ) is presented by a word in S of length at most n;
(2) each ϕ(ri ) and ψ(ri ) can be obtained by at most n relators in R with at most n
    steps of concatenations, inverses, and conjugations by an element in S ∪ S −1 ;
(3) each ϕ(ψ(si ))s−1                −1

    at most n steps of concatenations, inverses, and conjugations by an element in

whether ϕ(γ)δ −1 can be obtained by at most n relators in R with at most n steps of
concatenations, inverses, and conjugations by an element in S ∪ S −1 . The algorithm

                        7. Distinguishing topological types
   Two (oriented) simple closed curves a, b on a closed orientable surface Σ have the
same topological type if there exists a homeomorphism h : Σ → Σ such that h(a) = b.
Suppose that the free homotopy classes of a and b represent the conjugacy classes of
α, β ∈ π1 (Σ). By Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem (see [5, Theorem 8.1]), a and b have
the same topological type if and only if there exists ϕ ∈ Out(π1 (Σ)) that sends the
conjugacy class of α to the conjugacy class of β.
Proposition 7.1. Let Σ be a closed orientable surface, and let Γ = π1 (Σ, ∗). Suppose
α, β ∈ Γ are represented by simple closed curves. If α and β are profinitely equivalent
in Γ, then α and β have the same topological type, i.e. there exists φ ∈ Aut(Γ) such
that φ(α) = β.
Proof. For brevity, we use the same notation α and β to denote two simple loops on
(Σ, ∗) that represent the pointed homotopy classes of α and β, see Proposition 6.1.

  We can now finish the proof of Theorem 1.3.

Theorem 1.3. Let Σ be a closed orientable surface, and let Γ = π1 (Σ, ∗) be its funda-
mental group. Then, for any element γ ∈ Γ represented by a simple closed curve and
for any d ∈ Z, γ d is profinitely rigid in Γ.

Proof. When Σ is a torus, the theorem has been proven in Proposition 3.4. Thus, we
may assume that the genus of Σ is at least 2. When γ = 1 or d = 0, the statement is
equivalent to the residual finiteness of Γ, which is known from Proposition 4.3. Thus,
it suffices to assume that γ ̸= 1 and d ̸= 0. According to Corollary 4.7, it suffices to
prove the theorem for d = 1.
   Suppose δ ∈ Γ is profinitely equivalent to γ in Γ. Then, according to Proposition 3.1,
there exists an isomorphism ϕ : Γ   b → Γ   b such that ϕ(γ) = δ. As a consequence,
ϕ(⟨γ⟩) = ⟨δ⟩, and ϕ(CΓb (γ)) = ϕ(CΓb (δ)). Since γ is a non-power element, Lemma 4.6 (1)
implies that CΓb (γ) = ⟨γ⟩. Hence, CΓb (δ) = ⟨δ⟩, and Lemma 4.6 (1) again implies that
δ is a non-power element. Then, we apply Proposition 6.6 to deduce that δ is also
represented by a simple closed curve, and we apply Proposition 7.1 to deduce that δ
has the same topological type as γ. To sum up, δ ∈ Aut(Γ) · γ. Hence, γ is profinitely
rigid in Γ.                                                                            □
