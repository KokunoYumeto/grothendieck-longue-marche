properties which are analogous to the Magnus property proved for Π in [5] and [6].
    For a given element x of a (profinite) group G, let us denote by hxi and hxiG , respec-
tively, the (closed) subgroup and the (closed) normal subgroup generated (topologically)
by x in G. The Magnus property for the discrete group Π says that if, for two given
elements x, y ∈ Π, the normal subgroup hxiΠ equals the normal subgroup hyiΠ, then x is
conjugated either to y or y −1 . This property cannot be transported literally to the profinite
case since Zb has more units than just {±1} and so the property would fail already for Z.          b
Moreover, even if we take this into account, there are counterexamples to the analogue
property which can be formulated for the profinite completion Π      bS .
    A counterexample for a free profinite group of finite rank is the following. Let us denote
by M(G) the intersection of all maximal normal subgroups of a group G. Let then U be
a normal subgroup of a finitely generated free profinite group F such that U/M(U) is a
direct product of non-abelian simple groups (for instance, let U be the kernel of the natural
epimorphism of F onto the maximal prosolvable quotient of F ).
    By Proposition 8.3.6 in Chapter 8 of [20], the subgroup U is the normal closure of
an element u ∈ U if and only if U/M(U) is the normal closure of u · M(U). Now, in
an infinite direct product of non-abelian simple groups, there are plenty of elements and
groups generated by them which are non-conjugate but normally generate U/M(U). For
instance, any element with non-trivial projection to every direct simple factor of U/M(U)
has this property. However, having different order in some of the projections, such elements
are not conjugate.
    A counterexample for the pro-p case is instead the following. Let F = F (x, y) be the
free pro-p group in two generators. Then, the normal closure of x has generators which
are not conjugated to powers of x. Indeed, this follows from the fact that hxiF , modulo
its Frattini subgroup, identifies with the completed group ring Fp [[hyi]] and this has more
units than just the powers of y.
    For this reason, the profinite analogue of Magnus property should be rather formulated
saying that, if x, y ∈ Π b S satisfy the stronger condition hxn iΠb S = hy n iΠb S , for all n which
are a product of primes in ΛS , then x is conjugated to a power y s , where s is a unit of
the standard pro-S cyclic group Z     b S , i.e. the pro-S completion of Z. The latter group is
more explicitly described as follows. Let ΛS be the set ofQprimes which occur as orders of
groups in S , there is then a natural isomorphism Z      bS ∼= p∈ΛS Zp .
