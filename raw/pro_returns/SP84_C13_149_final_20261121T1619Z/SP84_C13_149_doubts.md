## Legibility and limits

The larger central lines are generally more readable than the crowded interlinear and marginal writing. Nearby clear examples often help, but enlargement cannot restore ink hidden by cancellation, lost at the physical edge, or unresolved in the original pixels. Confidence is local to a reading, not a probability that a page, formula or argument is correct.

The least secure areas are overlapping correction layers, compressed oblique notes and tiny formula decorations. Examples include the overlaid Q/K forms on scan012; the crossed proof and interlinear replacement on scan013; the rounded Z and E-like alphabets on scans014–015 and021; and scan019’s pi relation, where not-equal, equality and a wavy equality remain alternatives. Scan022’s marginal notes retain doubts in reduced words and cancelled phrases; scan024 also has especially compressed and crossed marginal writing. A letter can be visible while its alphabet, prime, accent or live/deleted state remains uncertain. The corresponding page reports and crops explain each doubt; grammar and expected mathematics do not replace missing stroke evidence.

C and H are useful locators and competing readings; neither is a dependable default. C preserves `(pro-gr)` and `ss-groupe` on scan019 and `tt` on scan021 where H expands them. Conversely, scan007’s map combines H’s doubled B with C’s ordinary right arrow: neither entire witness reading stands. Scan020’s edge fragments support a closing parenthesis present in H and absent in C. Both witnesses can also fail together: both expand scan018’s short group as `ensemble`, and both turn the leader before `Notons` on scan011 into a parenthesis. These examples support local comparison, not an overall accuracy ranking. Sources: scan007 c07; scan011 c03; scan018 c07; scan019 c09/c12; scan020 c15; scan021 c03, in the corresponding review JSONs.

The reading process is visual: model readers used original scans, prepared halves and enlarged crops. No OCR or outside publications were used to derive readings. Enlargement, contrast, sharpening and rotation aid display without recovering absent detail. All first-crop coordinate gaps have now been closed; exact pixel reproduction verified the outstanding cases. The rectangles and processing evidence are in `documentation/first_crop_coordinates.tsv` and the coordinate-recovery notes.

The independent phase began after the first page readings were finalized. An assigned reader had not first-read that page and received its original image and neutral boxes without its witnesses, transcription, first box readings, report or notation decisions. Its TSV was written and frozen before comparison. These are separate model-agent readings, not human verification or statistical independence: readers share conventions and can know other pages. The boxes select places identified by the first pass, not a fresh full-page transcription or a random accuracy sample. A packet-parser error had exposed reader B to later witness rows during scan017 preparation; that reader was therefore excluded from independent readings of scans018–022. The incident and safeguard are disclosed in `documentation/packet_parser_correction.md`.

The independent notes distinguish physical edge loss from crop-boundary clipping and excluded neighbouring ink. Scan017 b11 clipped a raised cluster; a separately documented supplementary crop supplied its full extent for adjudication without rewriting the frozen second reading. Comparison also detects TeX-representation and target-scope differences, so its box and token-difference counts are not error rates. Visual adjudication remains a separate step; hashes document preserved inputs, not correct readings. See `documentation/audit_method.md`, `documentation/audit_scope_crops.tsv`, and the per-page independent notes and adjudications.
