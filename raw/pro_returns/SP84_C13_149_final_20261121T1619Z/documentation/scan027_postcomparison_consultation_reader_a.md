## Function labels

### b02

The first argument has a tall narrow stem, a closed descending lower loop, and a short oblique rightward return at the junction above that loop. Its high detached prime is clear. The left curved stroke is entangled with the opening parenthesis, so it should not by itself be counted as an independent crossbar. Nevertheless, the complete looped form gives strong support to `f'`. The other argument has a compact rounded upper bowl and a long stem ending in a leftward bottom hook; that bowl is a real distinction from the straight-stemmed j forms below.

### b05

The first argument has a long, nearly straight descender ending in a small dark point, with an oblique entry into the upper stem and a separate raised prime. There is no closed lower loop like b02's first argument. The second argument has a long straight stem and an open leftward bottom hook, with no separately identifiable upper bowl. The surrounding opening parenthesis is distinguishable from the letter. These features favor `j'` and `j`, respectively. The first letter is less complete as a conventional hooked j, but it does not acquire the distinctive lower closed loop seen in b02 or b14.

### Clear local comparison forms in the supplements

The named pair in the b12–b14 supplement consists of slender descending strokes; the first has a small open bottom hook, and the second has a separate raised prime. Neither has a closed lower loop or a separate transverse bar. In the b15 supplement, both letters in `j=j'` have long stems ending in broad, open leftward hooks; the right letter's prime is distinct. These four local j forms support the straight-stem/open-hook comparison without relying on the mathematical role of the letters.

### b14

Both disputed arguments have a **closed narrow lower loop**, produced by a descending stem and a returning stroke that crosses or meets the stem above the loop. The return rises to the right. This is materially closer to the looped b02 `f'` than to any of the four clear local j forms just described. The second argument has the raised prime.

There is, however, no securely separable horizontal f crossbar. The long cup-shaped stroke to the left is partly or wholly the opening parenthesis, and the rightward return can be described as the return of the loop itself. The loop distinction supports an f-first reading, but does not prove that a looped j variant is impossible in this hand.

**Consultative recommendation:** treat these as locally f-like but still ambiguous, rather than declaring certain authorial f slips or silently converting them to j from context. If alternatives are recorded, `\unsure{f|j}` and `\unsure{f'|j'}` express my stroke-based preference and its limit. This recommendation makes no claim about what the author intended mathematically.
