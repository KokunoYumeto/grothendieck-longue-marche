# Coherent unit cofactors in the fixed pure-three tower

This is a proved auxiliary construction for the ongoing norm-chain work. It concerns the single tower generated by compatible roots of the rational number 3. It does not assert that these choices extend after changing the completed coordinate A.

Put alpha_n = 3^(1/3^n), K_n = Q(alpha_n), and L_n = Q(alpha_n, mu_(3^n)), with the positive compatible roots. Put U = alpha_1^2 - 2. Then U is an algebraic integer unit of norm 1. There is a compatible family of completed global units U_n in the pro-three completion of O_(K_n)^times such that U_1 = U and Norm_(K_(n+1)/K_n)(U_(n+1)) = U_n. Consequently beta_n = (1-alpha_n) U_n is one coherent completed norm family of norm -2 in this same tower, with the same two-adic divisor as the canonical family 1-alpha_n.

## 1. The exact fields and their three-class groups

Each L_n/Q(mu_3) is a Galois three-extension, unramified at every finite prime except the unique prime above 3, and totally ramified there. The local proof used in A105 applies with radicand exactly 3: 3 cannot be a cube in any abelian cyclotomic extension of Q_3, because Q_3(cuberoot(3)) is Eisenstein of degree three and is not Galois. Hence its Kummer order over Q_3(mu_(3^n)) is exactly 3^n. The actual local Galois group is C_(3^n) semidirect (Z/3^n)^times. Its commutator contains the full translation subgroup, since the unit -1 acts by inversion. Its maximal abelian subfield is the cyclotomic one, which is totally ramified. Every unramified quotient is cyclic and therefore factors through that totally ramified field; it is trivial. The local and global degrees are equal, giving the claimed unique prime.

The ring Z[zeta_3] is norm Euclidean: rounding both coordinates in the basis (1,zeta_3) leaves errors u,v of absolute value at most 1/2, with norm u^2-uv+v^2 at most 3/4. Thus Q(mu_3) has class number one.

Here is the exact elementary class-group argument. Let F/k be any finite Galois three-extension with k=Q(mu_3), totally ramified at the only ramified finite prime. Let H/F be its Hilbert three-class field. This field is stable under Gal(F/k), so H/k is Galois. In

    1 -> A = Gal(H/F) -> G = Gal(H/k) -> P = Gal(F/k) -> 1,

inertia I at the prime above 3 maps isomorphically onto P: it is onto by total ramification below and has trivial kernel because H/F is unramified. Thus G = A semidirect I. The quotient by the normal closure of I is A_I, the coinvariants of the finite three-group A under the three-group I. This quotient defines an everywhere unramified three-extension of k. It is trivial because a nontrivial finite three-group has a nontrivial abelian quotient and k has class number one. A finite nonzero three-module under a finite three-group has nonzero coinvariants (apply the augmentation-ideal nilpotence to A/3A). Therefore A=0.

This proves Cl(L_n){3}=0, and the same proof applies to the intervening Galois field F_n=L_n(mu_(3^(n+1))).

## 2. The literal unit norm construction at a cyclic cubic step

Let E/F be either cyclic cubic step in

    L_n subset F_n subset L_(n+1).

These fields are totally imaginary; E/F is unramified outside its single prime above 3; and Cl(E){3}=0 by the preceding proof. Let epsilon be a global unit of F. At every finite place other than 3 it is a local norm, since the extension is unramified and unit norms of an unramified local extension are onto. The global cyclic-algebra reciprocity sum has only its three-adic term left, so that term is zero too. The Hasse norm theorem for the cyclic extension therefore gives beta in E^times with Norm beta = epsilon.

Write its principal divisor D = div(beta). Its ideal norm is zero. On each cyclic prime orbit, the ideal norm is the common residue degree times the sum of the coefficients. Consequently D=(sigma-1)J for a fractional ideal divisor J, including the fixed prime orbits, where both kernels are zero.

The order d of [J] in Cl(E) is prime to three, so choose gamma with div(gamma)=dJ. Then

    e' = beta^d * gamma / sigma(gamma)

is a global unit with Norm e' = epsilon^d. Choose integers a,b with ad+3b=1. The literal unit (e')^a * epsilon^b has norm epsilon. This proves surjectivity on actual global units, without an inverse-limit or unit-norm approximation assertion.

Primary source checked: J. S. Milne, Class Field Theory v4.03 (2020), VIII Theorem 3.1, printed p.234 / PDF p.242, is the cyclic Hasse norm theorem. The one-place reciprocity step uses the cyclic algebra class in the usual global Brauer exact sequence. The ideal manipulation above is proved here.

## 3. A single compatible family and all exact norms

Choose actual units eta_n in O_(L_n)^times recursively with eta_1=U and Norm_(L_(n+1)/L_n)(eta_(n+1))=eta_n; the two cyclic steps just proved supply every choice. Put

    v_n = Norm_(L_n/K_n)(eta_n).

Norm transitivity in the same field diagram gives Norm_(K_(n+1)/K_n)(v_(n+1))=v_n. At the first level, L_1/K_1 has degree two and U lies in K_1, so v_1=U^2. Also Norm_(K_n/Q)(v_n)=1.

The square map is an automorphism on the pro-three completion of a global unit group. Define U_n to be the unique completed half-power of v_n. This definition commutes with every norm. It gives U_1=U, the displayed compatible family, and Norm_(K_n/Q)(U_n)=1.

The inclusion of completed units into completed K_n^times is injective: an element of K_n whose 3^r power is a unit is already a unit. Finally Norm_(K_(n+1)/K_n)(1-alpha_(n+1))=1-alpha_n, so beta_n=(1-alpha_n)U_n is the asserted actual compatible completed norm family. Its unit cofactor has valuation zero at every finite prime, hence preserves the canonical two-adic divisor literally.
