## Decisive corrections and strengthened conclusions

**L4 confuses splittings with classes of splittings.** For a chosen reference splitting, an actual continuous splitting is a continuous 1-cocycle. Conjugation by inertia adds a coboundary. Therefore the splittings form a torsor under Z1, whereas their inertia-conjugacy classes form a torsor under H1. A nonzero coboundary at complex conjugation gives distinct actual splittings with identical cohomology class. The finite outer-inertia criterion, the root-of-uniformizer field and the twist formula survive with this correction. The finite C2-action calculation in the code illustrates the distinction.

**R13 needs a typed codomain.** The finite index mu occurs for the map from G_K to the fundamental group of the good total model, identified with the special-fibre arithmetic group. Its cusp inertia image is mu T, and its residue cocycle is mu times the splitting class plus the Kummer class of the leading unit. A generic section G_K→pi1(U_K) meets the geometric kernel trivially, so it cannot have finite index mu in a generic cusp decomposition group with infinite geometric inertia. This is a correction to an inherited formulation, not to the local Kummer multiplication calculation.

**The full residue-prime table is much more restrictive in mixed characteristic.** SGA 7 I, IX, Proposition 5.14(b) is stated for a trait, and gives infinite determinant inertia at p. Consequently full homological unramifiedness c, and hence full outer unramifiedness b, never hold for a hyperbolic curve over a mixed-characteristic henselian DVR. Full e also never holds: proper characteristic-zero and characteristic-p groups have incompatible p-homology ranks, while affine characteristic-p groups have infinite-dimensional Artin–Schreier H1 and are not finitely generated. C.3 records vacuous implications explicitly. This statement is not exported to equal characteristic p.

**R10/R11 are re-established without treating prior AI returns as authorities.** B.1 obtains the torus/abelian separation from Ribet's cyclotomic torsion theorem, Stix's exact formulation and nonzero closed-point specialization. B.2 gives the elementary centre-free extension reconstruction actually used. B.3 distinguishes regular Kummer charts, log purity, completion, relative geometric base change and closed-log-fibre equivalence. It supplies the saturated thickness-m node chart and its rank-one antidiagonal. B.4 writes the finite-extension pullback square and effective finite descent; the graph-weight and cusp-count arguments handle a putative realizing curve of a different type.

**The proper nonsemistable p-adic obligation can be reduced to the opened theorems.** B.11a goes beyond merely repeating AIK's semistable statement. Crystallinity of the unipotent representations gives crystalline H1, hence a good Jacobian by AIK1.2. The opened original Deligne–Mumford Theorem2.4 then gives a stable model of the curve over the same trait. Strict-henselian descent, regular semistable resolution and properness supply the model and integral base point required for AIK1.9. Thus the semistability assumption is removed for proper genus at least two, over the complete field with perfect residue specified there. B.11b separately settles type(1,1). B.11d now treats every marked type by finite-cover proper p-adic data, and B.11e passes to henselian perfect-residue traits; B.11f and B.12 now give a different finite-cover p-divisible-group criterion with no perfect-residue restriction.

**The finite-field realization criterion is proved through a separate prime-to-p cusp theorem.** C.4 adapts the finite-cover proof of Stix's theorem, using HM2011 Lemma1.5 for peripheral intersections and finite-field weights in place of the characteristic-zero torus theorem. A normalizer element lifting Frobenius to a positive power generates a continuous arithmetic section and descends the finite cover; the resulting nonzero map Q_l(1)→V_l Jac contradicts the distinct Frobenius absolute values. C.5 then treats graph loops, tree nodes, affine cusp counts, arbitrary residue splittings and wild stable-reduction extensions.

**C4's prime-to-p centralizers are determined, not merely bounded.** Under split stable reduction, the subgroup fixed by G_{K(mu_infinity)} is the full punctured normalized-component group at a smooth unmarked specialization, the edge group at a node, and the cusp group at a marked specialization. The proof uses the exact HM2011 Lemma3.6 centralizer and Proposition3.8 decomposition-group intersections. Contact slopes (a,m−a) and mu exclude vertex inertia in the latter two cases. This is different from the residue cyclotomic-kernel centralizer in B10: in C4 the cyclotomic kernel lies inside local inertia and retains an open tame image. It does not prove B10.

**B10 now has the full centralizer formula for every residue splitting.** B.15a proves uniqueness of a fixed lifted cusp for an arbitrary section over k(mu_infinity), without assuming that it extends to G_k. At any fixed vertex, the log vertex group is pi1(Y_v) x_{G_k} G_K; quotienting the vertical inertia gives precisely such a component section, and fixed incident edges are its fixed lifted cusps. Thus every fixed vertex has degree at most one. Zalesskii–Melnikov1989 Theorem2.8 gives a fixed profinite subtree for the arbitrary profinite group G_k°, and Lemma2.14 applies because the edge set is a finite union of compact coset spaces. Connectedness and degree at most one force the fixed subtree to be one vertex or one edge. The local R15 calculation then gives 1 at a smooth unmarked point, I_c at a mark, and Pi_e at a node. No edge collapse or unproved abstract-path connectedness is used; all contact multiplicities and splitting cocycles are retained.

**B11's proper case does not require arithmetic augmentation.** The closed inertia image is isomorphic to T when there is a node. Its unique pro-ell Sylow subgroups are characteristic. Mochizuki2007 Corollary2.7(iii), with all primes and the noncuspidal hypothesis, therefore reconstructs the proper graph and full node classes from the inertia image alone. With peripheral data, HM2011 Corollary4.2 supplies the affine analogue. Once the edge coordinates are known, the ideals m_e Zhat determine all prime valuations and hence the positive integral thicknesses. B.16u supplies the affine extraction without cusp data, using the independent full profinite JSJ theorem and the marked normal subgroup Pi in the constructed mapping-torus extension.

**B2's open-curve “node part” requires precision.** The image of monodromy has dimension equal to the graph's first Betti number and projects isomorphically to the dual graph quotient. It is not necessarily the span of all individual node boundary classes. In the rational four-puncture collision the graph is a tree and monodromy on H1 is zero, but the separating-node class a0+a_t is a nonzero cusp class. The stronger interpretation identifying these two subspaces is false. B.6 states the exact sequence and the valid interpretation explicitly.

The cusp-count predicate in B.10 is intrinsic to the residue action: properness is detected by H2; in the affine case the cusp number is b−a+1, where a is the eventual dimension of Hom(H1,Q_l) and b that of Hom(Q_l(1),H1) after finite residue extensions. B.1 and B.6 give a=t and b=t+nu−1. The criterion therefore does not require the original curve's cusp count as an extra input.

## General marked criterion via a tame finite cover (B.11d–e)

The requested marked criterion can be obtained without claiming a direct extension of AIK to the original open curve. Good marked reduction is equivalent, after a finite **unramified** extension, to the existence of a geometrically connected prime-to-p Galois cover ramified at every cusp, with proper compactification C of genus at least two and crystalline proper unipotent p-adic fundamental group at a point of the cover. This is p-adic data attached to Pi: an open normal subgroup is quotiented by its peripheral inertia, and its inherited arithmetic action is then completed unipotently over Qp. B.11d writes the quotient map explicitly.

Existence follows from a finite ell-group quotient with all cusp images nontrivial, for any ell≥5 distinct from p. For at least two cusps use the boundary augmentation module; for a single cusp map the first handle generators to the Heisenberg group, whose commutator survives. Taking the characteristic core preserves the ell-power degree. Riemann–Hurwitz gives proper genus at least two even in types(0,3),(0,4),(1,1). A good integral basepoint gives trivial actual inertia, and a further unramified extension kills the finite residue action. Kummer charts then give a smooth proper compactification.

Conversely proper AIK, together with B.11a's removal of semistability, gives a smooth model of C. Deck automorphisms extend faithfully by DM1.11. Every special tame stabilizer injects into the tangent multiplicative group and is cyclic. Hensel lifts its fixed point uniquely; averaging the parameter produces an eigenparameter w. The invariant local ring is R[[w^e]], giving both smooth quotient and an etale branch section. This excludes branch collisions and extra special branch points. The resulting branch divisor is exactly the original marked divisor. Tamagawa descends from the unramified extension.

B.11e extends this completed-field criterion to every henselian mixed-characteristic DVR with perfect residue by the opened Minamide–Tsujimura2022 Lemma3.1 (Galois groups unchanged by completion), characteristic-zero geometric comparison, and Tamagawa's general-DVR criterion. The criterion is evaluated on the completion; no descent of the auxiliary completed-field points is asserted. The arbitrary-residue scope is now treated by B.11f and B.12 through finite-cover p-divisible groups. The classical crystalline theorem is not imported into that setting, and the direct open-group comparison remains a distinct stronger formulation.

`code/BC_marked_cover_checks.py` adds six exact Heisenberg and small-type Riemann–Hurwitz checks. There are now 69 B/C computational checks overall. These calculations supplement, rather than replace, the complete local tame quotient proof.
