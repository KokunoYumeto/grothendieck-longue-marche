# The odd relation-module coprime gate

Research record, 2026-11-16. Not a formal statement in checkpoint13.
The finite computation below is independently reproducible. CE independently
checked the extension to the actual A79 finite head in the conversation.

## 1. The two kernels must be distinguished

Let Pi=C2 amalg C3, with source generators s,r and tau=sr. For an actual
finite quotient Pi→Q, put L=ker(Pi→Q), M=L/Phi_p(L), and
G=Pi/Phi_p(L), where Phi_p(L)=closure([L,L]L^p).

Even if p is odd and p does not divide |Q|, the fact that ker(G→Q)=M is
an elementary abelian p-group does NOT make the kernel of the relevant
automorphism map an odd group. That automorphism kernel contains module
automorphisms as well as derivations. The literal cusp constraint does not
remove all its even-order elements.

## 2. An actual order150 source example

Take Q=C6, p=5, and K=ker(Pi→C6). Open Kurosh gives rank(K)=2, hence
|Pi/Phi5(K)|=6·25=150. Put V=F5^2 and

    T = [[1,-1],[1,0]],       T^3=-I,       T^6=I.

In V semidirect C6, use multiplication

    (v,j)(w,k)=(v+T^j w,j+k).

Choose the actual source images

    s=(0,3),     r=(e1,4),     tau=(-e1,1).

They have orders2 and3 and generate all150 elements, as the exact script
checks. Thus this is the actual first mod5 relation quotient, not an
abstract target with an unproved source image.

Set k=(3,3), so (1-T)k=-2e1, and define

    phi(v,j)=(-v+(1-T^j)k,j).

Equivalently phi=Inn((k,0)) followed by minus identity on V and identity
on C6. It squares to the identity because minus identity sends k to -k.
It fixes the actual tau literally by the displayed equation, induces
identity on C6, and has exact order2. The s- and r-images are conjugate
to the original source elements by vectors in V; the script gives both
literal conjugators. Thus the normalized positive peripheral finite
automorphism kernel already contains an involution.

This does not assert that phi lifts to a full positive torsion
automorphism. That would be a different assertion and is unnecessary
for disproving the odd-kernel premise.

## 3. The same obstruction occurs over the A79 finite head

The actual Q of A79 has order 2^972·72·7^13 and has the source C6
abelian quotient. Thus 5 does not divide |Q|. Inflate the preceding
two-dimensional irreducible V from C6 to Q. Its endomorphism field is
F25, since X^2-X+1 is irreducible over F5.

For M=L/Phi5(L), coprime cohomology gives H1(Q,V)=H2(Q,V)=0. The
continuous five-term sequence therefore identifies

    Hom_Q(M,V) = H1(Pi,V).

The source cocycle space has F5-dimension4: both source norm operators
vanish on V. The inner cocycles have dimension2 because V^Pi=0.
Hence H1(Pi,V) has F25-dimension1, and the semisimple Q-module M
contains exactly one copy of V. Its V-isotypic summand is well defined;
choose minus identity there and identity on the other isotypic summands.

Choose a coprime splitting G=M semidirect Q, and write the actual cusp
as tau=(a,t). Let a_V be its V-coordinate. The map 1-t on V is
invertible. Choose k in this summand with (1-t)k=2a_V. Composing the
chosen module involution with Inn(k) now fixes tau literally and still
has square1. It induces identity on Q. Both finite source factors have
orders prime to5; coprime conjugacy of their lifts shows that this
automorphism preserves their source conjugacy classes, with conjugators
in M. Consequently the normalized peripheral automorphism kernel over
the actual A79 Q also has 2-torsion.

The argument uses an actual source quotient and the full relation
module. It claims neither full-source realization of this finite
automorphism nor compatibility with further central refinements.

## 4. Exact remaining finite gate

Suppose a prescribed lower negative automorphism b_Q has order2 and
b_Q(t)=t^-1. For p not dividing 2|Q|, select a splitting G=M semidirect Q.
An automorphism above b_Q has the form

    b(v,q)=(Bv+(1-b_Q(q))k,b_Q(q)),

where B is an invertible b_Q-semilinear map on M. If B^2=1, its square
is inner by k+Bk; thus b is involutory precisely when k+Bk belongs to
M^Q. Writing tau=(a,t), literal cusp inversion is the additional equation

    B a+(1-t^-1)k = -t^-1 a.

These are genuine finite coefficient and norm conditions. The source
s/r conjugacy classes follow from the specified lower classes and
coprime conjugacy of their lifts. This last assertion is only about
conjugacy classes: prescribed literal frames and their norm equations
must still be imposed separately.

One can conjugate the chosen splitting so that a is t-fixed, by
averaging over the cyclic group generated by t. Then the special
choice k=0 would require B a=-a. No existence of a semilinear
involution B with this condition has been proved for the A79 head.

### The remaining odd norm is removable once B has been chosen

More precisely, put m=ord(t), and let

    a_bar = (1/m) sum_{j=0}^{m-1} t^j a  in M^t.

There exists an involutory normalized negative automorphism of G above
b_Q if and only if there exists an invertible b_Q-semilinear B on M
such that

    B^2=1,                    B a_bar=-a_bar.

Necessity follows by restricting to M and applying the automorphism to
tau^m=m a_bar. For sufficiency, the right-hand side of the literal cusp
equation has zero projection onto M^t, precisely by the second condition.
Since m is prime to p, the image of 1-t^-1 is the kernel of this averaging
projection. Choose k solving the cusp equation. The resulting b0 has
square Inn(v), where v=k+Bk. It fixes the cusp after squaring, so v lies
in M^t; also Bv=v. The inner correction

    b = Inn(-v/2) b0

preserves literal cusp inversion and has square1. All source conjugacy
classes remain correct under this inner correction. This proves the
criterion, rather than merely identifying a necessary norm equation.

Suppose also that norm-one lower s- and r-frame elements have already
been prescribed, as in the retained-data application. They can then be
lifted while retaining their literal Q-values. Work in G semidirect <b>.
For A=<s> or <r>, the normalizer maps onto the normalizer of A_Q:
any lifted element conjugates A to another complement in the preimage
of A_Q, and coprime complement conjugacy corrects it by M. Its kernel
is C_M(A), an odd group. The preimage of the prescribed order-two
lower reflection is therefore an odd-by-C2 group and has an involution
above that reflection. Its action on A has the required sign because
A→A_Q is injective. This gives the required norm-one frame. For s the
normalizer is its centralizer. The s/r lifts are separate choices and
do not change the already selected automorphism or its literal cusp.

This argument requires p not dividing 6|Q|, which holds for p=5 and
the A79 Q. It does not presume a common transporter or assert any
additional simultaneous frame relations beyond the two prescribed
normalizer equations.

After a coefficient action B has actually been selected, differences
with that same action are derivations Q→M, an odd group; imposing a
literal cusp value cuts out the corresponding linear subspace. This
is the place where a genuine coprime averaging argument could apply.
It cannot be applied before coefficient actions are fixed: Section3
exhibits even-order module automorphisms in the actual normalized
kernel. Neither A79 nor the example here rules out a suitable smaller
odd-kernel lift group. The existence and stability of that group,
together with the literal frame constraints, remain to be established.
The preceding criterion isolates this to the semilinear coefficient
involution with its actual cusp vector; the subsequent odd norm and
the two individual retained normalizer frames have been accounted for.
