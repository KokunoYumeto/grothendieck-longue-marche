1. Finite action head and centralizer control.

For a finite coefficient module A with action quotient q0, the compactness
argument is exact. The closed sets of g satisfying [g,ci] in N decrease
to C_E(ci)=Ci as N runs over finite normal refinements. Intersecting them
with the closed set having q0-image outside q0(Ci) gives an empty total
intersection, hence an empty finite-stage intersection. Thus one obtains
a finite q with C_Q(q(ci)) projecting inside q0(Ci), simultaneously for
all three representatives. Pairwise separation of their conjugacy classes
uses disjoint compact conjugacy classes and the same finite-refinement
argument. Refining also by the orientation quotient keeps all involution
images nonidentity. No claim is made that a finite head centralizer itself
already has order two.

2. The allowed conjugacy classes give the desired H1 classes.

For a local cocycle a_i, the element (a_i,q(ci)) in A semidirect Q has
order two. The union of its three full conjugacy classes is a legitimate
allowed set under the precise primary definition. A real-projective
solution is a global cocycle d. Pairwise nonconjugate q(ci) force d(ci)
into the corresponding i-th allowed class. Its conjugator head centralizes
q(ci), and therefore acts on A as either 1 or ci. The conjugation formula
 d(ci)=k a_i+(1-ci)a
has the same H1(Ci,A) class as a_i: even in the second case,
ci a_i-a_i=(ci-1)a_i is a coboundary. Thus arbitrary three local classes
are realized simultaneously, for arbitrary finite abelian coefficients
and actions. This is the step that cannot be inferred merely from
high-degree localization.

3. Degree two and compact module projectivity.

A continuous finite-coefficient degree-two class factors through a
sufficiently fine finite action quotient. If its restrictions vanish,
the resulting finite extension has an involution lift above every ci.
Real projectivity solves that finite real embedding problem, splitting
the pullback extension over E. Hence the degree-two restriction is
injective. This argument works with nontrivial coefficient action.

For 0->R_p->P_p->Z_p->0, continuous Shapiro identifies the relevant
Ext terms of each permutation summand with H^j(Ci,A). The exact segment
therefore gives Ext^1(R_p,A)=0 for every finite p-primary E-module A.
These are the continuous Ext groups in the compact completed group-ring
category, not a calculation restricted to abstract modules or trivial A.

The transition from finite coefficients to compact projectivity is valid.
R_p is topologically finitely generated because its restriction to F is
Lambda_F^4. For an epimorphism X->Y of compact modules and a map R_p->Y,
each finite quotient epimorphism admits a lift by the finite Ext vanishing.
Each lift set is finite, since a finite list of generators determines a
map to a finite module. Any finite collection of compatibility conditions
is realized at a common finer quotient. Compactness of the product of
these finite sets gives a compatible system, hence a continuous lift to X.
Thus R_p is genuinely projective among compact Lambda_E-modules.

4. Induction, reduction modulo two, and actual freeness.

The free index-two subgroup F acts regularly on every E/Ci. The restricted
augmentation kernel is Lambda_F^2 plus I_F. For the full free profinite
group of rank two, its completed p-adic augmentation ideal is free of
rank two, giving Res_F R_p=Lambda_F^4. The tensor-induction identity gives
Ind Res R_p=R_p tensor Z_p[E/F]. Tensoring the augmentation sequence of
the order-two permutation module produces
 0 -> R_p tensor epsilon -> Ind Res R_p -> R_p -> 0.
The quotient is projective, so this particular sequence splits for every
p. At p=2 no sign-idempotent splitting of Z_2[C2] is used or available.

Modulo two the twist is trivial. For every finite quotient Q of E, the
corresponding finite module P_Q satisfies P_Q plus P_Q=F2[Q]^4. Finite
Krull-Schmidt gives P_Q=F2[Q]^2 by equality of all indecomposable
multiplicities. The finite sets of bases have the inverse-limit finite
intersection property: a basis at a common finer quotient induces
compatible bases at any specified finite set of quotient levels. One
does not need to assert that arbitrary independently prechosen bases at
those levels can be retained. This gives R_p/2R_p=F2[[E]]^2 at p=2.

Lift that basis to a map Lambda_E^2->R_2. Its compact cokernel has zero
reduction modulo two, hence is zero: any nonzero compact pro-two abelian
group has a nonzero finite quotient, and a finite two-group cannot have
surjective multiplication by two. Projectivity splits the map. Its
compact kernel is then a direct summand with zero reduction, so the same
argument kills it. This proves the actual isomorphism
 R_2 = Z_2[[E]]^2.

5. Exact scope.

For odd p the proof gives the projective module and the relation
R_p plus R_p tensor epsilon = Lambda_E^4, not untwisted rank-two freeness.
At p=2 the freeness concerns the actual completed group algebra of E;
it is not merely a statement about its maximal pro-two quotient.
Neither statement supplies a finite quotient graph of groups, a
profinite tree realization with the needed edge data, or a marked
reflection generating triple. Those recognition and peripheral gates
remain separate.

ADDENDUM: THE FINITE-HEAD TWISTED-DUALITY IDENTITY (14:43 UTC)

A's subsequent finite-head identity also passes, at its stated scope.
Let K be an orientation-faithful open normal subgroup, Q=E/K, p odd,
and let Rbar be the corresponding finite reduction of R_p. The actual
coinvariant sequence gives
 [Rbar]=[M]+[A]-[1],
where M=H1(K,Fp) and A is the sum of the three section permutation
modules. If C is the sum of cusp permutation modules and H is compact
surface homology, actual oriented boundary duality gives
 [M]=[H]+epsilon([C]-[1]),  H^*=H tensor epsilon.
Consequently
 [Rbar^*]-[Rbar tensor epsilon]=(1-epsilon)([A]-[C]).

For a finite dihedral cusp P with its two endpoint sections C_+,C_-,
the identity
 (1-epsilon)(Ind_(C_+)^P 1+Ind_(C_-)^P 1)=2(1-epsilon)[1]
holds. At a reflection the two permutation characters sum to two; at
rotations the factor 1-epsilon is zero. This also covers coincident
endpoints when the rotation image has order one. The ordinary character
identity gives the same Grothendieck identity in odd characteristic
by restricting to prime-to-p elements. Induce to Q and sum over the
cusps. The actual triangle incidence uses each section twice, so twice
(1-epsilon)([A]-[C]) is zero. G0 is free abelian on simple classes, hence
the difference itself is zero.

When p does not divide |Q|, semisimplicity upgrades this to an actual
isomorphism Rbar^*=Rbar tensor epsilon. Together with
Rbar plus Rbar tensor epsilon = Fp[Q]^4, it forces each selfdual simple
block to have twice its multiplicity in the regular module. Over a
nonsplitting finite field that regular multiplicity is
dim_(End_Q(S)) S, not necessarily dim_Fp S. Nonselfdual paired blocks
retain the antisymmetric multiplicity ambiguity. This is neither a
full compact duality assertion nor modular-head freeness.
