General wreath quotients and exact boundary of the unit-head argument
16 November 2026. Research notes after C44; no new formal09 statement.

Let L be a finite group, and let a_1,...,a_k be elements of pairwise
coprime orders which invariably generate L: every collection of their
independent conjugates generates L. Put E=L^n semidirect C_n for n>=k,
with the cyclic regular permutation of factors, and put
 z=(a_1,...,a_k,1,...,1).
Every subgroup H of E containing z and an element g whose C_n image is
a generator equals E. Powers of z isolate each individual a_i in its
one factor, by the Chinese remainder theorem. Conjugating these isolated
elements by powers of g transports a conjugate of each a_i into each
factor. Invariable generation therefore puts an entire L factor in H,
separately for every coordinate. Then H contains L^n and its generator
head, hence E. The same proof works for a factor-twisted action if the
chosen classes invariably generate under all automorphisms occurring in
that action.

For L=A5, an element of order3 and an element of order5 invariably
generate. A subgroup containing both has order divisible by15; orders15
and30 are impossible (order15 groups are cyclic, and order30 gives a
normal index-two subgroup of simple A5). Hence it is A5. This proves the
claim for every E_n=A5^n semidirect C_n, z=(a3,b5,1,...), n>=2. The
original pair x=cyclic shift,z already generates, so these are actual
quotients of the free pants group with the stated primitive cusp z.
C44 gives a full unit for the canonical g's cyclic head, so g,z generate
every one of these particular nonsolvable quotients, without requiring
a computation of the residual coordinates of g.

The head-unit implication has a precise limitation even among actual
quotients of that same free group. Take E=A5^2 semidirect C2, let
 z=(a3,a3), and x=(t5,1)swap. Then x^2=(t5,t5), so z and x^2 generate
the diagonal A5. Its conjugate by x is a distinct diagonal, since t5 is
not central. A subdirect subgroup of A5^2 is either the product or a
graph of an automorphism; a graph containing the diagonal is the diagonal
itself. Thus x,z generate E, giving an actual quotient. Nevertheless the
element g0=swap has generator head and <g0,z>=C2 times C3, a proper
subgroup. Therefore no argument using the unit head alone can prove
surjectivity on every finite quotient. This is not a claim that the
canonical g takes value g0, nor that this quotient supports the prescribed
negative action or all full-word constraints.

For a pair of coprime orders in the two-factor case, the uniform
head-generator conclusion is equivalent to invariable generation of the
pair. Sufficiency is the proof above. If independent conjugates a,b lie
in a proper subgroup M<L, conjugating the standard swap and M^2 back to
the original coordinates gives a proper subgroup containing the original
z and some element over the nontrivial C2 head. Thus the missing condition
in the simple unit-head proof is exact, rather than a topology issue.
