# Reader brief v004 — first used on scan 007

You are one of three independent readers of exactly ONE scan. Do not see or seek the other readers' work. You start with only this page's images, its C/H texts and C doubt/audit/notes, relevant S rows, these conventions, and the current notation table. Do not read any other page's image or text. Finish and return; do not continue to another page. The main reader makes every final decision.

## Required reading, in this order

a. Read meta.json and the page packet. Look at the whole image ONCE for layout. Then look at each supplied full-resolution half ONCE, in order, with C and H beside it. Use tools.view_image and forward its image with image(result.image_url). No OCR, text-recognition software, PDF text layer, internet or outside research. Reading a text witness never counts as looking at ink.

b. Read every line, word, accent, abbreviation, formula, index, sign, arrow, underline, deletion, insertion, diagram, drawing and margin. Every agreed C/H reading needs the ink too. Follow the mathematical meaning; if something does not parse or an index/sign contradicts it, inspect the ink, NEVER silently repair it. Explicitly check every C doubt. Treat S rows as further draft readings to confirm or overturn; distinguish a mathematical correction from what was actually written. H macro differences alone are not substantive differences. H's notes and drawn assets may contain descriptions rather than transcription: inspect and draw the actual ink.

c. The halves settle what is clear. List each place still open: unresolved C/H disagreement, C doubt, crucial small sign, slanted/struck/crowded writing. Make ONE tight crop per unresolved place, with a little line context, using PIL only for cropping/enlarging, autocontrast, sharpening and rotation. Upscale with LANCZOS to about 2400–3500 pixels across and rotate slanted text upright when necessary. Do not enlarge a whole half again; do not crop something the halves settled; do not crop a decided place again. Save crops under your own reader directory; log source image, pixel box, rotation, scale and what the crop decided. Use only your own subdirectory. If the ink remains undecidable after that crop, retain explicit uncertainty.

d. Return a COMPLETE page reading in our LaTeX conventions, and a thorough list of EVERY place it differs from either C or H, plus all C doubts (resolved or retained), S decisions, remaining doubts, inventory, notes and notation observations. If C is supported you may copy its syntax, but only after actually reading all of it in the ink. Save reading.tex and report.json in the assigned directory. Keep the report factual about what you did; do not claim an image or crop was viewed if it was not. Final message gives paths and highlights, not a substitute for the files.

## Our conventions

Use PREAMBLE.tex macros only, plus standard LaTeX/amsmath/amssymb/mathrsfs/ulem/TikZ/tikz-cd. One \cotefile{71} at combined start; page starts \scanpage{N}; own page number \mspage{n}; archive pencil number comment only. Preserve his language, spelling, accents, punctuation, abbreviations, paragraphing and numbering. Do not expand abbreviations or correct mathematics. Join words hyphenated only by a line ending. Two backticks/two apostrophes for his double quotes; guillemets only where drawn.

\deleted{...} for legible struck text; \deleted{\illegible{n}} for physically unreadable struck words; live overwrite after the old deleted form if readable. \inserted{...} at the precise caret/arrow insertion point. \margin{...} for independent marginal notes, with sideways/slanted comment. \uline and \uuline faithfully. \handwritten only for hand additions on a typed/printed page. Typescripts are not bold; double-struck type is \mathbb. Alphabets and every accent/index/prime/^! read from actual ink.

\diagram{\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&] ... \end{tikzcd}}: objects, arrows, labels and cancellations; no verbal replacement. \figure{\begin{tikzpicture}...\end{tikzpicture}}: reproduce every curve, region and label with relative positions. Best approximation plus % crop-candidate: scan N only if too complex. Displays \[...\], aligned for multiline; labels \tag*{...}.

\unsure{reading} or \unsure{reading|alternative}: first candidate is the ink's best reading, never the mathematically expected one. Include every ink-supported alternative, not hypothetical ones. Do not retain a doubt the strokes settle. Recurrent ambiguous glyph: first occurrence on each page marked, later same glyph unmarked, notation table records alternatives. \illegible{n} is for physically unreadable words at highest zoom, NEVER a mathematical symbol; give a marked candidate for the latter. A doubt is never permission to omit.

## Clarifications added after scan 003

- Do NOT compile TeX or install/build a TeX runtime. The supplied source prompt explicitly requests static brace, math-delimiter and environment balance checks only. Two first-page readers tried compilation and found no pdflatex.fmt; that did not verify rendering and is not repeated.
- Before returning, ensure every final reading in report.json agrees with reading.tex. A later judgment must update both. A disagreement between your report and TeX was observed on scan 003’s circlet.
- A compact formula or drawing with related unresolved marks may use one shared tight crop. Record the distinct questions it answers. A table without arrows may use an array inside the diagram macro. No arrows are invented.
- A repeated uncertain glyph can be unmarked after its first occurrence, but its report and doubts entry must still say unresolved. Absence of the later marker is not increased confidence.
- Compare H’s actual supplied drawing asset as well as its description. Its comments may acknowledge an undrawn mark; do not claim an omission for a label that its drawing does include.
- Do not spend report space reproducing full TikZ figures repeatedly. Name exact affected paths/labels with concise snippets and point to your complete reading. Distinguish added/missing strokes from a purely cosmetic reparameterization of curves.
- Keep notes to the requested page content, mathematical triggers, self-doubts, dates and titles. Put process history in the report’s images/crops/feedback, not in mathematical notes.
- The full comparison remains mandatory. Concise evidence is useful: name the actual visible strokes and the decisive half/crop, without repeating generic warnings.
