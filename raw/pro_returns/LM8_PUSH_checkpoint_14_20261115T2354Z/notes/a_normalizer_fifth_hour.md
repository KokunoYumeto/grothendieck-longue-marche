# Full-normalizer attempts during the fifth hour

These attempts address A.2/A.7 and yield no additional claimed theorem. The exact surviving configuration remains A.17: N0=P semidirect B, geometric kernel P times C, P=Z_l, C pro-prime-to-l, and B onto G_L. The actual quotient N0/C splits; lifting that section through C is the unresolved step.

## Cohomological dimension and local duality

A local full arithmetic group of an affine curve has geometric cohomological dimension one and arithmetic dimension two. A normalizer with a geometric procyclic l-component can therefore still have l-dimension three; dimension alone does not contradict the embedding. Proper compactification gives a duality group of dimension four, where a dimension-three subgroup is also not excluded. An infinite-index dimension drop available for certain Poincaré duality groups cannot be applied to the affine free-geometric extension as if it were a Poincaré duality group. No useful top-degree restriction map has been proved injective.

## Splitting over smaller Galois groups

Restricting to the abelian-closure Galois group, or to a suitable local l-Sylow subgroup, can remove a prime-complement obstruction and produce a section over an infinite algebraic extension. It does not produce a section over a finite local number field. The known local section input cannot be invoked for the former as if it were the latter. Likewise A.1 concerns a specified cusp packet; a newly constructed arbitrary section over Delta has not been shown to belong to such a packet.

## Geometric centralizers and Hall arguments

A free profinite group need not have procyclic centralizers of arbitrary pro-l elements. Groups of the form Z_l times a free pro-q group, q different from l, are compatible with projectivity; the special procyclic centralizer theorem for actual cusp inertia cannot be used for an unidentified P. Nor does the discrete Hall free-factor theorem automatically give a finite-index free factor containing an arbitrary closed profinite procyclic subgroup. We have supplied no proof of such a reduction.

## Primary-source search

The renewed searches for procyclic arithmetic normalizers, cuspidal recognition, and Nakamura's weight criterion led again to the cyclotomic-normalizer recognition theorem and its exposition in Stix, not to a theorem that every arithmetic normalizer acts cyclotomically. A.16 already isolates the remaining CM weight-one possibility. The arithmetic weight-one detection in finite proper covers is not sufficient to annul the prime-complement extension in the actual full group.

These failures have not been converted into counterexamples: an abstract nonsplit extension remains distinct from a subgroup embedded in the given arithmetic fundamental group.

## Sixth-hour check against the stronger local formation theorem

D.54/D.56 do not remove the prime-complement embedding problem. Projecting to one coefficient prime kills the complementary geometric kernel but gives a one-prime quotient, to which the two-prime local theorem does not apply. Retaining the residue prime in a full formation also retains a potentially nontrivial part of that kernel; the actual section has still not been constructed. Restriction to a local pro-ell Sylow at residue characteristic p different from ell does satisfy the inertia-surjection and open cyclotomic-image hypotheses of Mochizuki--Tsujimura Theorem F(i), reopened at printedpp7--8. It supplies a unique compatible Berkovich point, as already recorded above. The fixed constant field is then an infinite algebraic extension of a local field, however, and D.56 and Hoshi's local birational proposition have not been proved in that enlarged scope. Thus this revisited route still stops at a vertical stabilizer, without a horizontal point or a finite-local-field section.
