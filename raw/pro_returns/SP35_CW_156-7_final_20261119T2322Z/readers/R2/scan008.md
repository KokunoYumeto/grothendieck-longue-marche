## Mathematics and limits

**summary, not proof check**. Defines disjoint figures by compatibility and empty intersection, reduces disjointness to strata, and expresses the stratum relation as compatibility plus absence of a common lower bound. The NB discusses absence of a common refinement. A prospective spaciousness axiom and corollary, together with the marginal definition/example, are cancelled.

**targeted proof check** (R2-008-M1). The displayed all-strata disjointness criterion follows from the prior bounded-sup/strata axioms and At3. Forward: compatibility descends to strata, and any common lower stratum of X≤F and Y≤G would lie below F∩G, contradicting emptiness. Reverse: pairwise stratum compatibility gives F compatible G by At3. If F∩G were nonempty, At2 would supply a stratum Z below both; taking X=Y=Z contradicts the assumed disjointness of every pair. The proof uses that strata are nonempty strictly irreducible figures and the empty figure is bottom. It does not establish any new spaciousness axiom.

**targeted local check** (R2-008-M2). Using the already-read AtM0, ≤ implies ≪, absence of a common refinement implies absence of a common lower stratum: W≤X,Y would give W≪X,Y. This checks only the non-minoration component of the NB reciprocal. It does not establish X compatible Y, so it does not prove the full disjointness conclusion without an additional hypothesis.

**conditional targeted proof check** (R2-008-M3). For the NB forward direction, under the minimal/minimum interpretation of the revised AtM4(a) on scan007: from Z≪X and Z≪Y choose minimal A≤X and B≤Y among the respective strata refined by Z. Their minimality makes Z an internal refinement of A and B. If X and Y are compatible, heredity gives A compatible B; the already-read AtM4(b) yields A=B, a common lower stratum. Thus compatible strata without a common lower bound have no common refinement, under that stated existence/minimality reading. This is conditional because R2’s scan007 inserted adjective remained minimal|minimum|privilégié; it does not settle that earlier ink or certify a formulation based only on an arbitrary distinguished member.
