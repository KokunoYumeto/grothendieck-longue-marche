## Supporting calculations: correspondences, operators, and lettering

### L2. The ring/category relationship as actual maps

The point of departure in C 16/3–4 is a family of orthogonal idempotents, summing to one, inside an endomorphism ring. The formula

\[
\mathcal E^{(\nu)}=\bigoplus_i\pi_{i+\nu}\mathcal E\pi_i,
\qquad u\longmapsto\sum_i\pi_{i+\nu}u\pi_i
\]

gives its homogeneous part. C 16/16 makes the images of the projectors part of its proposed homomorphism from the universal operator ring; 16/17 rejects uniqueness from $L$ alone; 16/18 gives the correspondence-ring name. These are precise statements in the supplied first-pass text, including its editorial alterations; they should not be silently strengthened into a construction of every motivic structure.

Here is the comparison in the concrete rational Chow category, with the triple convention written in [J92, pp.447–448]. For smooth projective $X,Y$ and $d_X=\dim X$, set

\[
\operatorname{Corr}^{r}(X,Y)=\mathrm{CH}^{d_X+r}(X\times Y)_{\mathbf Q}.
\]

For $f\in\operatorname{Corr}^{r}(X,Y)$, $g\in\operatorname{Corr}^{s}(Y,Z)$, the composition is

\[
g\circ f=(p_{XZ})_*\bigl(p_{XY}^*f\cdot p_{YZ}^*g\bigr)
\in\operatorname{Corr}^{r+s}(X,Z).
\]

The idempotent-completed category has objects $(X,p,m)$ and

\[
\operatorname{Hom}\bigl((X,p,m),(Y,q,n)\bigr)
=q\,\mathrm{CH}^{d_X-m+n}(X\times Y)_{\mathbf Q}\,p.
\]

In particular, the total correspondence ring is related to the category by

\[
\operatorname{Corr}^{r}(X,X)
=\operatorname{Hom}(h(X),h(X)(r)).
\]

An algebraic cycle $z$ acts on cohomology by the concrete formula

\[
z_*(a)=(p_2)_*\bigl(p_1^*a\cup\operatorname{cl}(z)\bigr),
\qquad H^i(X)\longrightarrow H^{i+2r}(X)(r).
\]

Composition of cycles is carried to composition of these maps. Choosing a basis of the realized Tate line permits writing their direct sum as ordinary matrices; the preceding formula retains the twist that the matrix notation suppresses. Thus an operator of cohomological degree (2r) is represented by a morphism to the $r$-th Tate twist, and an idempotent $p$ is represented by the summand ($X,p,0$).

The off-diagonal blocks are also explicit. In the additive category, put $M=h(X)\oplus h(Y)$ and let $e_X,e_Y$ be its two projectors; then the identification

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(h(X),h(Y))\longrightarrow e_Y\operatorname{End}(M)e_X,
\quad f\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\f&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

has inverse given by restriction to the $X$-summand followed by projection to the $Y$-summand. This is the precise way a family of correspondence rings and its corner maps encodes morphisms between objects. None of these explicit maps establishes the textual history of the absent cotes 12–13.

The available cote 14 register excerpts are narrower evidence. R’s **MPL_14_002-044 M01** cites “Structure d’effectivité” on 14/002–003 and describes an already existing $M$; **§2** and **§107** summarize its correspondence/grading comparison; **MPL_14_087-132 M01** points to the notation on 14/117. All four are first-pass leads. Current 15/227 adds a visible question about multiplicative functors and places, rather than supplying the missing construction pages.

### L3. Operator normalizations, group morphisms, and the Tate test

#### L3.1 A concrete string calculation

Fix the vector space with basis $v_0,\ldots,v_m$, and define actual matrices by

\[
Ev_j=v_{j+1},\quad Uv_j=v_{j-1},\quad
Fv_j=j(m-j+1)v_{j-1},\quad Hv_j=(2j-m)v_j,
\]

where the values outside the basis range are zero. Direct calculation gives

\[
(EF-FE)v_j=
\bigl[j(m-j+1)-(j+1)(m-j)\bigr]v_j
=(2j-m)v_j,
\]

and hence $[E,F]=H$, $[H,E]=2E$, $[H,F]=-2F$, including both endpoints. The exact conversion is $F=U\,a(J)$, with $Jv_j=jv_j$ and $a(J)=J(m+1-J)$. Writing the conversion this way records every scalar.

For a Lefschetz string beginning in degree $i$ in total cohomology of dimension $n$, put $m=n-i$ and $Dv_j=(i+2j)v_j$. Then

\[
H=D-nI,\qquad
w(s)v_j=s^{i+2j}v_j,\qquad
h(s)v_j=s^{2j-m}v_j=s^{-n}w(s)v_j.
\]

This calculation supplies the precise comparison between centered Lefschetz weights and cohomological weights. N R2 and S3 already proposed the corresponding normalization issue; this memo verifies the displayed conversion rather than relying on their status.

For the integral exterior model of C 16/14, let $L$ be wedge multiplication by $\xi=e_1\wedge f_1+e_2\wedge f_2$. Its square on $1$ is $2\,\mathrm{vol}$. The contraction operator $\iota_{f_1^*}\iota_{e_1^*}+\iota_{f_2^*}\iota_{e_2^*}$, with each $e_a\wedge f_a$ contracting to $1$, sends $\xi$ to $2$; the unit-step matrix sends $L1$ to $1$. These are exact values of the two operators, and rational rescaling on each string is the comparison map above. No modification to C’s doubtful formula (6.5) follows from this editorial calculation.

#### L3.2 A primary-source correspondence morphism

For a polarized abelian variety $A$ of positive dimension $g$, write $\theta$ for its symmetric polarization class, $d=\theta^g/g!$, $\delta(x,y)=y-x$, and $\pi_i$ for its rational Chow–Künneth projectors. Beauville’s Theorem 3.3 constructs [B08]

\[
\varphi:\mathrm{SL}_{2,\mathbf Q}\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Corr}^{*}(A),\qquad
\operatorname{Corr}(A)=\mathrm{CH}^{*}(A\times A)_{\mathbf Q},
\]

with the correspondence-composition multiplication. Its differential is

\[
d\varphi(e)=\Delta_*\theta,\qquad
d\varphi(f)=\frac{\delta^*(\theta^{g-1})}{d(g-1)!},\qquad
d\varphi(h)=\sum_i(i-g)\pi_i.
\]

Its torus is exactly

\[
\varphi\!\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\0&t^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
=t^{-g}\sum_i t^i\pi_i.
\]

The further map $z\mapsto[z_*:a\mapsto(p_2)_*(z\cdot p_1^*a)]$ is the ring action on Chow groups. These formulas verify a later relationship between the rational Lefschetz ring, the centered grading, and Fourier correspondences. Künnemann’s introduction gives the abelian-scheme Chow-motive setting and its motivic Lefschetz isomorphism [Ku93]; this supports R’s B16/M24a/M10 bridge at rational coefficients. This memo makes no claim to have computed the integral order in WG2 S28.

#### L3.3 The complete projective-line calculation

Work over $\mathbf C$ with rational Chow motives and their Betti realization. In $\mathrm{CH}^1(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1)$, the diagonal has class $[\{p\}\times\mathbf P^1]+[\mathbf P^1\times\{p\}]$; the two correspondence projectors give

\[
M=h(\mathbf P^1)=\mathbf1\oplus T,\qquad T=\mathbf Q(-1).
\]

For integer $a,b$, the correspondence formula on $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C$ gives

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(T^a,T^b)=
\begin{cases}\mathbf Q&a=b,\\0&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\]

The rigid additive category generated by $T$ is therefore the category of finite-dimensional, finitely supported $\mathbf Z$-graded rational vector spaces, with the degree-$a$ line sent to $T^a$. A tensor automorphism of its forgetful fiber functor is determined by a scalar $t\in R^\times$ on $T\otimes R$; tensor compatibility gives $t^a$ on $T^a$, and the unit has scalar $1$. This constructs the isomorphism of group functors $G\simeq\mathbb G_m$, not just its rational points.

On the basis $e_0,e_2$ of $H^0\oplus H^2$, the two actions are

\[
\rho(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&t\end{pmatrix},\quad
E=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
H=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The displayed $E,F,H$ satisfy the three brackets above, while the differential of $\rho$ has image $\{\operatorname{diag}(0,a):a\in\mathbf Q\}$. For a Lie homomorphism $u:\mathfrak{sl}_2\to\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbb G_m)$, the abelian target gives $u(H)=u([E,F])=0$, $2u(E)=u([H,E])=0$, and $-2u(F)=u([H,F])=0$. Thus this concrete Lefschetz action cannot be the differential of a subgroup inclusion into the Tate motivic group. This directly verifies the two-dimensional test named, but only excerpted, in R’s **MPL_14_087-132 M04**.

The relationship is retained by conjugation:

\[
\rho(t)E\rho(t)^{-1}=tE,
\qquad\rho(t)F\rho(t)^{-1}=t^{-1}F,
\qquad\rho(t)H\rho(t)^{-1}=H.
\]

Equivalently, the raising correspondence is a morphism $M\to M(1)$; the twist has character $t^{-1}$, so the tensor-naturality equation is

\[
(t^{-1}\rho(t))E=E\rho(t).
\]

This is the explicit map relating the graded correspondence to tensor automorphisms. The cocharacters, with the convention that $T$ has Hodge index $1$ and cohomological weight $2$, are

\[
j(t)=\operatorname{diag}(1,t),\qquad
w(s)=j(s^2)=\operatorname{diag}(1,s^2),\qquad
h(s)=s^{-1}w(s)=\operatorname{diag}(s^{-1},s).
\]

The first coordinate of $h(s)$ checks its action on the unit. In this example $B'=L'=G=\mathbb G_m$, $U'=1$, and the Tate character $\varepsilon$ is the identity, giving $\varepsilon j=\mathrm{id}$. This is a concrete comparison with the formulas on current 18/011, not an assertion that the page assigns these exponents in every motive.

### L5. Semisimplicity and singular varieties: the available evidence

#### L5.1 The numerical comparison

Jannsen’s Theorem 1 proves that the category of pure motives for numerical equivalence is semisimple abelian, for characteristic-zero coefficients [J92]. The concrete comparison on correspondences is

\[
q_X:A^d_{\rm hom}(X\times X)\twoheadrightarrow
A^d_{\rm num}(X\times X),\qquad[z]_{\rm hom}\mapsto[z]_{\rm num}.
\]

This is well defined because a homologically zero cycle has zero intersection degree with every complementary cycle: the cycle-class map carries the intersection to the zero top-degree class. Its kernel is exactly the classes of numerically trivial cycles modulo homological equivalence. Thus there is a specific morphism relating the manuscript’s ring and the numerical ring.

For a direct algebraic check of what quotient semisimplicity alone says, the quotient

\[
\mathbf Q[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\longrightarrow\mathbf Q,
\qquad a+b\epsilon\longmapsto a
\]

has semisimple target and nonzero nilpotent kernel $\mathbf Q\epsilon$. In its two-dimensional regular representation, multiplication by $\epsilon$ is $\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. This is a calculation about that explicit algebra, not an identification of it with a motivic correspondence ring. R’s M09 and N S15–S16 already flag the comparison; the present memo verifies the numerical theorem and writes the quotient and its exact kernel without asserting a new geometric vanishing result.

### L6. Supplementary matrix-ring verification

This calculation verifies the string-algebra model used in the unchecked WG2 §2.3 #2 and gives an explicit morphism between the two operator algebras discussed in N S3. Its scope is this completely specified representation. It does not identify the manuscript’s integral exterior-algebra order with the model or supply the full proof of every manuscript formula requested by WG2 §6. #9.

Let $V_n=\bigoplus_{m=0}^n\bigoplus_{j=0}^m\mathbf Qv_{m,j}$, and let $E,U,F,H$ act on each summand by §L3.1. Set $e=1-EU$. The integral polynomial

\[
e_m=eU^mE^me-eU^{m+1}E^{m+1}e
\]

projects onto $\mathbf Qv_{m,0}$: its first term selects string bottoms of length at least $m+1$, and its second selects the strictly longer strings. Thus

\[
E_m^{ba}=E^be_mU^a
\]

sends $v_{m,a}$ to $v_{m,b}$ and every other basis vector to zero. This proves by the displayed map that

\[
\mathbf Q[E,U]\simeq\prod_{m=0}^n\operatorname{Mat}_{m+1}(\mathbf Q).
\]

For the conversion to the sl₂ algebra, the element $C=H^2+2H+4FE$ acts on the $m$-th string by $m(m+2)$. Define

\[
q_m(C)=\prod_{r\ne m}\frac{C-r(r+2)}{m(m+2)-r(r+2)},
\quad
p_{m,j}=q_m(C)\prod_{0\leq k\leq m,\ k\ne j}
\frac{H-(2k-m)}{2(j-k)}.
\]

Here $p_{m,j}$ is the projector onto the basis line $v_{m,j}$, so

\[
U=\sum_{m=0}^n\sum_{j=1}^m
\frac{1}{j(m-j+1)}Fp_{m,j}.
\]

Conversely $F=\sum_{m,j}j(m-j+1)Up_{m,j}$, using the already explicit matrix units in $\mathbf Q[E,U]$. Consequently $\mathbf Q[E,F]=\mathbf Q[E,U]$, and the image of $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ in this representation is exactly the displayed product. This is the explicit rational morphism; its denominators and the exterior calculation $\xi^2=2\mathrm{vol}$ are retained.

The identities, projectors, Casimir eigenvalues, and conversion for $n=0,1,2,3,4$ were also checked with exact rational matrices. The representation dimensions were $1,3,6,10,15$, and the algebra ranks were $1,5,14,30,55$. This finite check supports the direct basis proof above; it is not the integral-order computation requested by WG2 §6. #9.

## Calculation P-A — Explicit matrix calculation and its source-scope limit

This is an own calculation in a specified realization, not an insertion into the manuscript's definitions.

### P-A.1 A fixed elliptic curve

Take
\[
E/\mathbf Q:\quad y^2=x^3-x+1.
\]
Its discriminant is \(-16(4(-1)^3+27)=-368\ne0\), and
\[
j(E)=1728\,\frac{4(-1)^3}{4(-1)^3+27}
=-\frac{6912}{23}.
\]
CM elliptic \(j\)-invariants are algebraic integers (DanielsLozano, Theorem 2.1(1)); this rational noninteger therefore gives a non-CM example. Let \(V_{dR}=H^1_{dR}(E/\mathbf Q)\), \(V_B=H^1(E(\mathbf C),\mathbf Q)\), choose a rational de Rham basis \((\omega,\eta)\) with \(F^1V_{dR}=\mathbf Q\omega\), and choose any rational Betti basis dual to homology cycles \((\gamma_1,\gamma_2)\).

### P-A.2 Comparison convention and conjugation

Use the de Rham-to-Betti matrix
\[
\Pi=
\begin{pmatrix}
\int_{\gamma_1}\omega&\int_{\gamma_1}\eta\\
\int_{\gamma_2}\omega&\int_{\gamma_2}\eta
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A=\Pi^{-1}.
\]
Thus \(A\) has the direction of the manuscript's \(\alpha:T_B\to T_{DR}\). Write \(C_h\) for the rational matrix of geometric complex conjugation on homology and \(C=C_h^{\,t}\) for its pullback on Betti cohomology; \(C^2=I\).

Because the chosen differentials are defined over the real field \(\mathbf Q\), integration gives, entry by entry,
\[
\overline{\int_{\gamma_i}\omega_j}
=\int_{\overline{\gamma_i}}\omega_j
=\sum_r(C_h)_{ri}\int_{\gamma_r}\omega_j.
\]
Consequently
\[
\overline{\Pi}=C\Pi,\qquad
\overline A=AC,\qquad
\mathbf Q(\overline A)=\mathbf Q(A).
\]
This directly verifies the relevant real-Frobenius convention. It agrees with the compatibility diagram and matrix construction in Brown–Dupont, §2.1.1 and §2.4; their single-valued matrix is \(\Pi^{-1}C\Pi\). The frozen 15/201 already distinguishes the integral Betti involution from the corresponding semilinear action.

### P-A.3 Canonical opposite-Hodge splitting

The Hodge line in Betti coordinates is \(\Pi\mathbf C e_1\). Coefficientwise complex conjugation carries it to \(\overline\Pi\mathbf C e_1=C\Pi\mathbf C e_1\), and transport back to de Rham gives
\[
H^{0,1}_{dR}
=A C A^{-1}\mathbf C e_1.
\]
Write \(v=A C A^{-1}e_1=(v_1,v_2)^t\). The direct Hodge decomposition gives \(v_2\ne0\), and \(t=v_1/v_2\) belongs to \(\mathbf Q(A)\). Therefore
\[
H^{0,1}_{dR}=\mathbf C(t\omega+\eta).
\]

In the explicit model
\[
V_{Hdg}=\operatorname{Gr}^1_F V_{dR}\oplus
\operatorname{Gr}^0_F V_{dR},
\]
the splitting from \(V_{Hdg}\) to \(V_{dR}\), and its inverse, are
\[
s=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=s^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here \(B\) has the direction of the manuscript's \(\beta\). Set \(D=(BA)^{-1}\), with the direction of \(\gamma\); then
\[
BA=D^{-1},\qquad DB=A^{-1},\qquad AD=B^{-1}
\]
and
\[
\mathbf Q(A,B)=\mathbf Q(A)
=\mathbf Q(A,D)=\mathbf Q(B,D).
\]
The identities reproduce the cyclic comparison relations, with the Hodge splitting determined by \(A\), not supplied as an additional free coordinate.

## Calculation P-B — From the translated locus to a generated torsor

Work over \(F=\overline{\mathbf Q}\) with the matrix realization of \(H^1(E)\). Changing between the source's Betti-to-de Rham convention and modern de Rham-to-Betti conventions uses the algebraic isomorphism \(p\mapsto p^{-1}\) between the corresponding isomorphism schemes. No transcendence-degree information changes under this map.

The modern theorem is stated for \(h(E)\); the projectors supplied by the origin of \(E\) give \(h(E)=\mathbf1\oplus h^1(E)\oplus\mathbf Q(-1)\), and \(\bigwedge^2h^1(E)=\mathbf Q(-1)\). Hence each of \(h(E)\) and \(h^1(E)\) belongs to the tensor category generated by the other, and the restriction maps on their tensor automorphism groups are inverse; this is the precise passage to the page's rank-two matrices (also Kawabe26, Proposition 2.13).

Use the source's direction and write \(P=P_{\mathrm{mot}}=\operatorname{Isom}^{\otimes}_F(\omega_B,\omega_{dR})\), with left acting group \(G_{dR,F}=\operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}_F(\omega_{dR})\) and right acting group \(G_{B,F}=\operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}_F(\omega_B)\). Let \(Z\subset P\) be the closed \(F\)-locus of the comparison point and choose \(a\in Z(F)\). Such a point exists because \(Z\) is a nonempty finite-type affine scheme over the algebraically closed field \(F\). The right translation \(z\mapsto za^{-1}\) identifies \(P\) with \(G_{dR,F}\) and sends \(a\) to the identity; let \(H\subset G_{dR,F}\) be the smallest closed algebraic subgroup containing \(Za^{-1}\). The isomorphism \(G_{B,F}\to G_{dR,F}\), \(g\mapsto aga^{-1}\), explicitly identifies the two acting groups.

The closed set \(Ha\) contains \(Z\) and is closed under the torsor operation
\[
(x,y,z)\longmapsto xy^{-1}z.
\]
Conversely, for any closed subtorsor \(S\subset P\) containing \(Z\), the translate \(Sa^{-1}\) contains the identity and is closed under products and inverses:
\[
(ua^{-1})(va^{-1})=(ua^{-1}v)a^{-1},\qquad
(ua^{-1})^{-1}=(au^{-1}a)a^{-1}.
\]
Thus \(Sa^{-1}\) is a closed subgroup containing \(Za^{-1}\), so it contains \(H\). The generated torsor is exactly
\[
\Omega=Ha.
\]

For the de Rham–Betti realization \(M=h(E)\), Kreutz–Shen–Vial's Proposition 3.1 identifies the generated torsor with \(\Omega^{\mathrm{dRB}}_{M\otimes_{\mathbf Q}F}\). Their Theorem 5.13(ii), applied to one elliptic curve and \(L=F=\overline{\mathbf Q}\), then gives \(\Omega=P_{\mathrm{mot}}\); one elliptic curve involves at most one CM field, so the theorem's stated coefficient restriction is fulfilled in both elliptic cases on 18/013. These statements transport to the source convention by inversion, and the generated torsor lies in \(P_{\mathrm{mot}}\) because \(P_{\mathrm{mot}}\) itself is a closed subtorsor containing the comparison point. Translation therefore gives \(H=G_{dR,F}\), and conjugation by \(a\) gives the same equality in \(G_{B,F}\), exactly the group-generation question on that page. The closed immersion \(Z\hookrightarrow\Omega\) remains present: an equality after forming the generated torsor does not calculate the dimension of \(Z\).

## Supporting calculations: the four comparisons and Oda

### S1. Hom maps and the genus-two image calculation

Let $K$ be a number field and $X,Y/K$ smooth proper geometrically connected curves with rational basepoints. The covariant Albanese maps give the commutative chain

\[
f:X\longrightarrow Y\quad\mapsto\quad
\pi_1(f)\quad\mapsto\quad
\pi_1(\overline X)^{\mathrm{ab},\ell}\longrightarrow
\pi_1(\overline Y)^{\mathrm{ab},\ell}
\quad=\quad T_\ell\operatorname{Alb}(f):T_\ell J_X\longrightarrow T_\ell J_Y.
\]

Changing basepoints changes the fundamental-group homomorphism by inner conjugation; abelianization removes that conjugation. Using $f^*:J_Y\to J_X$ instead would require reversing the variance, so the displayed comparison uses $\operatorname{Alb}(f)$.

The source scopes are precise: [T66], Main Theorem, p. 134, treats abelian varieties over a finite field, with $\ell$ different from its characteristic. [F83], Satz 3–4, p. 360, treats abelian varieties over a number field: rational Tate-module semisimplicity and the integral endomorphism comparison; the Hom comparison follows by taking the off-diagonal block of $\operatorname{End}(A\times B)$. [M-Hom], Theorem A, author PDF p. 3, uses a sub-$p$-adic field $K$, a smooth pro-variety $X$, and a hyperbolic pro-curve $Y$: dominant $K$-morphisms correspond to open augmentation-compatible group homomorphisms, modulo inner conjugation by the geometric target group, for the profinite and geometric pro-$p$ versions stated there.

Now fix $X/\mathbf Q:y^2=x^5-x$ with its smooth projective completion and its rational point at infinity. The polynomial has five distinct roots; together with infinity these give six branch points of the degree-two map to $\mathbf P^1$, so $g(X)=2$. For a nonconstant self-map, Riemann–Hurwitz is

\[
2=2\deg(f)+\sum_x(e_x-1).
\]

Thus $\deg(f)=1$ and $f$ is an automorphism; its Albanese map has an inverse. In contrast, $[2]:J_X\to J_X$ has degree $2^{2g}=16$. Equivalently, for the canonical principal polarization $\lambda$, an induced curve automorphism has $f_*^\vee\lambda f_*=\lambda$, whereas $[2]^\vee\lambda[2]=4\lambda$. The actual Tate-module homomorphism $2I$ therefore exists and is Galois equivariant; these calculations identify why it is outside this curve-morphism image.

### S2. Period conjugations and the polarization matrices

Write the integration comparison in real bases as $I(w)=Pw$. Geometric complex conjugation has a real Betti matrix $R$; for a differential defined over $\mathbf R$,

\[
\int_{c\gamma}\omega=\overline{\int_\gamma\omega},
\qquad RP=\overline P,\qquad R^2=I.
\]

Consequently $R\overline P=P$. Coefficient conjugation on Betti cohomology sends $z\mapsto\bar z$, while the transported de Rham conjugation is $Pw\mapsto P\bar w$; hence $c_{\mathrm{dR}}=R c_B$. This proves the product identity in B04, including commutativity of the two semilinear involutions. [DK02] §5.1 records the same infinite-Frobenius factorization, but its introduction explicitly describes the text as Kedlaya's transcription of Deligne's lectures; the calculation above is supplied independently.

For the square elliptic torus, take the lattice basis $1,i$ and $Q(u,v)=u^tJv$. Direct multiplication gives

\[
JC=I,\quad C^2=-I,\quad c^2=I,\quad
c^tJc=-J,\quad cCc^{-1}=-C,
\qquad Q(u,Cv)=u^tv.
\]

For $g\in SL_2$, $g^tJg=J$ yields $CgC^{-1}=(g^t)^{-1}$. Its real fixed group is ${g:g^tg=I,\det g=1}=SO_2$; the semilinear involution $g\mapsto C\bar gC^{-1}=(\bar g^t)^{-1}$ fixes $SU_2$. The Galois-equivariant Weil pairing $T_\ell E\times T_\ell E\to\mathbf Z_\ell(1)$ gives $e_\ell(cx,cy)=\chi_\ell(c)e_\ell(x,y)=-e_\ell(x,y)$, agreeing with the computed multiplier.

[Mil94], Lemma 1.5, relates a $C$-polarization on a faithful real representation to the Cartan involution $\operatorname{Int}(C)$. Proposition 1.6 applies this to a polarizable rational Hodge structure, with $C=h(i)$, on $(G/w_h(\mathbf G_m))_{\mathbf R}$; retaining that quotient avoids silently imposing a compactness assertion on the weight torus.

### S3. Marked curves, integral weights, and explicit dual arrows

For the current Hodge comparison, work over a characteristic-zero field $K$ with a fixed embedding into $\mathbf C$. Fix a smooth proper curve $C/K$ with $r>0$ rational marked points $D$, $J=\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$, and generalized Jacobian $J_D$. Rigidifications of a trivial line bundle at the marked points, modulo a common scalar, give the exact torus

\[
T=(\mathbf G_m^D)/\mathbf G_m,\qquad
X^*(T)=\ker(\mathbf Z^D\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbf Z)=\operatorname{Div}^0_D,
\qquad T\simeq\mathbf G_m^{r-1}.
\]

With overbars denoting base change to an algebraic closure of $K$, and $\ell$ invertible in $K$, the realization sequence and its cohomological dual are

\[
0\to\mathbf Z_\ell(1)^{r-1}\xrightarrow{i}T_\ell J_D\xrightarrow{p}T_\ell J\to0,
\]
\[
0\to H^1(\overline C,\mathbf Q_\ell)\xrightarrow{p^\vee}
H^1(\overline{C\setminus D},\mathbf Q_\ell)
\xrightarrow{i^\vee}\operatorname{Div}^0_D\otimes\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)\to0.
\]

Here $V_\ell=T_\ell\otimes\mathbf Q_\ell$, and the cohomological identification is
$H^1(\overline{C\setminus D},\mathbf Q_\ell)
=\operatorname{Hom}(V_\ell J_D,\mathbf Q_\ell)$.
Thus $p^\vee(\varphi)=\varphi\circ p$ and $i^\vee(\psi)=\psi\circ i$; the toric dual is
$\operatorname{Hom}(V_\ell T,\mathbf Q_\ell)
=X^*(T)\otimes\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)$.
In the mixed-Hodge homological convention, with the matching weight filtration on the étale realization, the toric and abelian pieces have weights $-2,-1$; after the displayed duality their cohomological weights are $2,1$, respectively. This is the explicit weight and variance comparison with the Tate twist on current P18/009.

Deligne's 1-motive $M=[\operatorname{Div}^0_D\to J]$ has Cartier dual $M^\vee=[0\to J_D]$ through the Poincaré biextension and the canonical Jacobian polarization. Its pairing is
\[
T_\ell M\times T_\ell M^\vee\longrightarrow\mathbf Z_\ell(1),\qquad
y\longmapsto\bigl(x\longmapsto\langle x,y\rangle\bigr),
\]
identifying $T_\ell M^\vee$ with
$\operatorname{Hom}(T_\ell M,\mathbf Z_\ell(1))$.
These are the realization and duality constructions specified in [D74] and reported by LM7 C.1 [Known, return's claim].

For the marked elliptic example and its splitting calculation, cite LM7 C.8 [Refuted, return's claim] for the reported rank-two toric tower and elliptic generalized Jacobian with three nonzero two-torsion marked points, and LM7 C.5 [Proved, return's claim] for its reported extension-order, pullback-splitting and degree results. The present comparison adds the current page's twist/weight realization; it makes no independent proof claim about the unavailable LM7 source and does not repeat the reported extension-order or degree derivation.

### S4. Kummer conjugation and the punctured elliptic bracket

Choose compatible roots $t_n^{\ell^n}=t$ and $\zeta_{\ell^{n+1}}^\ell=\zeta_{\ell^n}$. An inertia generator sends $t_n\mapsto\zeta_{\ell^n}t_n$; an arithmetic lift fixing the chosen $t_n$ sends $\zeta_{\ell^n}\mapsto\zeta_{\ell^n}^{\chi_\ell(\sigma)}$. Evaluating the conjugate on $t_n$ proves the formula in B07 at every level and hence in the inverse limit. A different lift has the same conjugation action because $I_\ell$ is abelian.

For a once-punctured elliptic curve, the topological presentation is

\[
\pi_1(U(\mathbf C))=\langle a,b,\tau\mid[a,b]\tau=1\rangle.
\]

Eliminating $\tau$ gives the free group on $a,b$, and its maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient is free pro-$\ell$ on the same generators. Its second lower-central graded piece is generated by the alternating bracket $[a,b]$, identified with $a\wedge b$ in $\bigwedge^2(T_\ell E)$. An automorphism with matrix $M$ on first abelianization acts there by $\det M$; the Weil pairing gives $\det M=\chi_\ell(\sigma)$ for arithmetic action. Thus the peripheral element has zero first image but a nonzero cyclotomic image in degree two. This calculation concerns those specified quotients; the full common kernel $R$ in LM6 A.22 remains outside the supplied statement.

### S5. Oda's exact comparison and passage to isogenies

Oda's Dieudonné ring has generators $W(k),F,V$, with

\[
FV=VF=p,\qquad F\lambda=\lambda^\sigma F,
\qquad \lambda V=V\lambda^\sigma.
\]

Corollary 3.16 is the finite-group contravariant equivalence, Definition 3.17 takes the inverse limit on the module side, and Proposition 3.18 makes the module of a $p$-divisible group free over $W(k)$. Apply exactness to

\[
0\to X[p]\to X[p^\infty]\xrightarrow{p}X[p^\infty]\to0
\]

to obtain

\[
0\to D\xrightarrow{p}D\to M(X[p])\to0,
\qquad
D/pD\xrightarrow[\text{Oda 5.11}]{\sim}H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k).
\]

The associated Hodge sequence is

\[
0\to \operatorname{im}(V:D/pD\to D/pD)=\ker(F:D/pD\to D/pD)
\to D/pD\to H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)\to0,
\]

where the first term is identified with $H^0(X,\Omega^1_{X/k})$. More invariantly, the image $V(D/pD)$ is meant in the displayed first term; $pD\subset VD$ follows from $VF=p$. Definition 5.8 also gives $\operatorname{im}F=\ker V$ in the abelian/proper-curve cases treated there. The separate map $D\hookrightarrow D\otimes_{W(k)}W(k)[1/p]$ is injective because $D$ is free; on its target $F$ is invertible with $F^{-1}=p^{-1}V$.

For general smooth proper $X$, Oda defines Cartier iteration on successive domains. Theorem 5.10 and Corollary 5.12 identify the Picard-module image with the intersection of those domains expressed as $\bigcap_n\ker(dV^n)$. On p. 123 he illustrates the need to retain the domains with

\[
\omega=x^{p^n}\frac{dy}{y},\quad
V^j\omega=x^{p^{n-j}}\frac{dy}{y},\quad
dV^n\omega=\frac{dx\wedge dy}{y}\ne0.
\]

For $j<n$, the derivative is zero in characteristic $p$. This displayed computation supplies a concrete check on repeated Cartier operations; it is not asserted to be a global regular form on an arbitrary proper $X$.

For the local Tate-curve example O04, [BC09] uses the contravariant convention $D^*_{\mathrm{st}}(V)=\operatorname{Hom}_{G_K}(V,B_{\mathrm{st}})$. The Kummer class yields a matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\chi_p&\eta_p\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; its period construction gives the two basis vectors used in O04. The calculation $N\varphi(e_2)=pe_1=p\varphi N(e_2)$ verifies $N\varphi=p\varphi N$, while $\ker N=\mathbf Q_p e_1$ has dimension one. Fontaine's §5.1.6 supplies the exact crystalline-kernel identification. This is the precise map for the fixed example, with the logarithm choice stated.
