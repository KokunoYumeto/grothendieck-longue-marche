## Appendix A — Explicit matrix calculation and its source-scope limit

### A.1 A fixed elliptic curve

Take
\[
E/\mathbf Q:\quad y^2=x^3-x+1.
\]
Its discriminant is \(-16(4(-1)^3+27)=-368\ne0\), and
\[
j(E)=1728\,\frac{4(-1)^3}{4(-1)^3+27}
=-\frac{6912}{23}.
\]
CM elliptic \(j\)-invariants are algebraic integers (R7, Theorem 2.1(1)); this rational noninteger therefore gives a non-CM example. Let \(V_{dR}=H^1_{dR}(E/\mathbf Q)\), \(V_B=H^1(E(\mathbf C),\mathbf Q)\), choose a rational de Rham basis \((\omega,\eta)\) with \(F^1V_{dR}=\mathbf Q\omega\), and choose any rational Betti basis dual to homology cycles \((\gamma_1,\gamma_2)\).

### A.2 Comparison convention and conjugation

Use the de Rham-to-Betti matrix
\[
\Pi=
\begin{pmatrix}
\int_{\gamma_1}\omega&\int_{\gamma_1}\eta\\
\int_{\gamma_2}\omega&\int_{\gamma_2}\eta
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A=\Pi^{-1}.
\]
Thus \(A\) has the direction of the manuscript's \(\alpha:T_B\to T_{DR}\). Write \(C_h\) for the rational matrix of geometric complex conjugation on homology and \(C=C_h^{\,t}\) for its pullback on Betti cohomology; \(C^2=I\).

Because the chosen differentials are defined over the real field \(\mathbf Q\), integration gives, entry by entry,
\[
\overline{\int_{\gamma_i}\omega_j}
=\int_{\overline{\gamma_i}}\omega_j
=\sum_r(C_h)_{ri}\int_{\gamma_r}\omega_j.
\]
Consequently
\[
\overline{\Pi}=C\Pi,\qquad
\overline A=AC,\qquad
\mathbf Q(\overline A)=\mathbf Q(A).
\]
This directly verifies the relevant real-Frobenius convention. It agrees with the compatibility diagram and matrix construction in Brown–Dupont, §2.1.1 and §2.4; their single-valued matrix is \(\Pi^{-1}C\Pi\). The frozen 15/201 already distinguishes the integral Betti involution from the corresponding semilinear action.

### A.3 Canonical opposite-Hodge splitting

The Hodge line in Betti coordinates is \(\Pi\mathbf C e_1\). Coefficientwise complex conjugation carries it to \(\overline\Pi\mathbf C e_1=C\Pi\mathbf C e_1\), and transport back to de Rham gives
\[
H^{0,1}_{dR}
=A C A^{-1}\mathbf C e_1.
\]
Write \(v=A C A^{-1}e_1=(v_1,v_2)^t\). The direct Hodge decomposition gives \(v_2\ne0\), and \(t=v_1/v_2\) belongs to \(\mathbf Q(A)\). Therefore
\[
H^{0,1}_{dR}=\mathbf C(t\omega+\eta).
\]

In the explicit model
\[
V_{Hdg}=\operatorname{Gr}^1_F V_{dR}\oplus
\operatorname{Gr}^0_F V_{dR},
\]
the splitting from \(V_{Hdg}\) to \(V_{dR}\), and its inverse, are
\[
s=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=s^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here \(B\) has the direction of the manuscript's \(\beta\). Set \(D=(BA)^{-1}\), with the direction of \(\gamma\); then
\[
BA=D^{-1},\qquad DB=A^{-1},\qquad AD=B^{-1}
\]
and
\[
\mathbf Q(A,B)=\mathbf Q(A)
=\mathbf Q(A,D)=\mathbf Q(B,D).
\]
The identities reproduce the cyclic comparison relations, with the Hodge splitting determined by \(A\), not supplied as an additional free coordinate.

### A.4 Dimension and the precise unresolved identification

For the displayed \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) realization, the stabilizer of the line \(\mathbf Q\omega\) is the upper triangular subgroup, its unipotent radical is
\[
U=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\right\},
\]
and the dimensions are \(g=4,\ b=3,\ \ell=2,\ u=1\). A two-by-two comparison matrix contributes four entries, and adjoining its inverse contributes only rational functions, so the computed field has transcendence degree at most \(4\). The value \(g+u=5\) is incompatible with this concrete canonical-Hodge model.

The source itself displays \(K\subset\mathbf Q(k(\alpha),\overline{k(\alpha)})\). For the calculated field \(F=\mathbf Q(A)\), the complex-conjugate compositum is \(\mathbf Q(F,c(F))=F\), while the compositum with an algebraic closure is \(\mathbf Q(F,F^{\mathrm{alg}})=F^{\mathrm{alg}}\) and has the same transcendence degree as \(F\). Thus the complex-conjugate-field reading of the displayed inclusion agrees with the equality calculated above, and the algebraic-closure reading gives the same degree upper bound; the matrix proof itself requires neither choice of source convention.

The retained source names a torsor of Levi subgroups but does not spell out \(T_{Hdg}=\operatorname{Gr}_F T_{DR}\) or the selection rule for \(\beta\). The universal splitting torsor of the line has coordinate ring \(\mathbf Q[t]\), and its product with the comparison torsor has the projection corresponding to \(\mathcal O(P)\to\mathcal O(P)[t]\); the canonical point above evaluates \(t\) to the rational function \(v_1/v_2\) in \(A\). This construction identifies exactly the missing scope question: whether the manuscript uses that evaluation or another previously defined selection. Confidence is high in A.1–A.4's calculation and medium in the proposed identification with the unspecified selection on 18/011.

## Appendix B — From the translated locus to a generated torsor

Work over \(F=\overline{\mathbf Q}\) with the matrix realization of \(H^1(E)\). Changing between the source's Betti-to-de Rham convention and modern de Rham-to-Betti conventions uses the algebraic isomorphism \(p\mapsto p^{-1}\) between the corresponding isomorphism schemes. No transcendence-degree information changes under this map.

The modern theorem is stated for \(h(E)\); the projectors supplied by the origin of \(E\) give \(h(E)=\mathbf1\oplus h^1(E)\oplus\mathbf Q(-1)\), and \(\bigwedge^2h^1(E)=\mathbf Q(-1)\). Hence each of \(h(E)\) and \(h^1(E)\) belongs to the tensor category generated by the other, and the restriction maps on their tensor automorphism groups are inverse; this is the precise passage to the page's rank-two matrices (also R4, Proposition 2.13).

Use the source's direction and write \(P=P_{\mathrm{mot}}=\operatorname{Isom}^{\otimes}_F(\omega_B,\omega_{dR})\), with left acting group \(G_{dR,F}=\operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}_F(\omega_{dR})\) and right acting group \(G_{B,F}=\operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}_F(\omega_B)\). Let \(Z\subset P\) be the closed \(F\)-locus of the comparison point and choose \(a\in Z(F)\). Such a point exists because \(Z\) is a nonempty finite-type affine scheme over the algebraically closed field \(F\). The right translation \(z\mapsto za^{-1}\) identifies \(P\) with \(G_{dR,F}\) and sends \(a\) to the identity; let \(H\subset G_{dR,F}\) be the smallest closed algebraic subgroup containing \(Za^{-1}\). The isomorphism \(G_{B,F}\to G_{dR,F}\), \(g\mapsto aga^{-1}\), explicitly identifies the two acting groups.

The closed set \(Ha\) contains \(Z\) and is closed under the torsor operation
\[
(x,y,z)\longmapsto xy^{-1}z.
\]
Conversely, for any closed subtorsor \(S\subset P\) containing \(Z\), the translate \(Sa^{-1}\) contains the identity and is closed under products and inverses:
\[
(ua^{-1})(va^{-1})=(ua^{-1}v)a^{-1},\qquad
(ua^{-1})^{-1}=(au^{-1}a)a^{-1}.
\]
Thus \(Sa^{-1}\) is a closed subgroup containing \(Za^{-1}\), so it contains \(H\). The generated torsor is exactly
\[
\Omega=Ha.
\]

For the de Rham–Betti realization \(M=h(E)\), Kreutz–Shen–Vial's Proposition 3.1 identifies the generated torsor with \(\Omega^{\mathrm{dRB}}_{M\otimes_{\mathbf Q}F}\). Their Theorem 5.13(ii), applied to one elliptic curve and \(L=F=\overline{\mathbf Q}\), then gives \(\Omega=P_{\mathrm{mot}}\); one elliptic curve involves at most one CM field, so the theorem's stated coefficient restriction is fulfilled in both elliptic cases on 18/013. These statements transport to the source convention by inversion, and the generated torsor lies in \(P_{\mathrm{mot}}\) because \(P_{\mathrm{mot}}\) itself is a closed subtorsor containing the comparison point. Translation therefore gives \(H=G_{dR,F}\), and conjugation by \(a\) gives the same equality in \(G_{B,F}\), exactly the group-generation question on that page. The closed immersion \(Z\hookrightarrow\Omega\) remains present: an equality after forming the generated torsor does not calculate the dimension of \(Z\).

## Appendix C — Arithmetic exactness and S15: construction record

For the Deligne–Milne absolute-Hodge category, write
\[
f^*:\mathcal M_{\mathbf Q}^{AH}\to
\mathcal M_{\overline{\mathbf Q}}^{AH}
\]
for base change and \(\mathcal A_{\mathbf Q}\hookrightarrow\mathcal M_{\mathbf Q}^{AH}\) for the Artin-motive inclusion. Their Betti functors, with the fixed embedding, yield the maps \(i\) and \(p\) in PG16 by tensor-automorphism restriction. Over a finite Galois extension \(L/\mathbf Q\), the Artin motive \(h(\operatorname{Spec}L)\) realizes the permutation action on embeddings, and passage over all such \(L\) yields the proconstant \(\underline\Gamma_{\mathbf Q}\).

Kahn gives a descent proof of the exact sequence, including the rational Nori setting in §9. For a finite Galois extension with group \(\Gamma\), equip \(M\) with descent data \(b_\gamma:\gamma^*M\xrightarrow{\sim}M\) satisfying the descent cocycle identity; these data induce the automorphisms \([\gamma]\) of \(f_*M\) defined in his §1.2 and Lemma 1.3. Proposition 1.11(b) then constructs the projector
\[
e_\Gamma=\frac1{|\Gamma|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}[\gamma]
\quad\text{on }f_*M.
\]
Its image is the descended object, with the comparison \(f^*(\operatorname{im}e_\Gamma)\xrightarrow{\sim}M\) induced by the adjunction counit. The resulting base-change and Artin-restriction maps specify the relationship requested by C-README. They are external comparison constructions and do not fill the missing \(\pi\)-identification on 18/006.

For S15, the verified scope is: tensor automorphism groups and comparison torsors in the specified realized categories; the arithmetic exact sequence for rational Nori motives via the cited descent theorem; and the canonical Nori/Ayoub group isomorphism via explicitly inverse Hopf maps. The algebraicity of all correspondences in an earlier proposed motive category and injectivity of the numerical period evaluation are not proved by these cited maps. No conclusion from M23 or M28a about pure semisimplicity has been added here without checking its missing full source.
