## Findings for integration

### D1 — The precise ring and filtration

**Witness:** 56/036, paragraphs 1–4 and the group-ring display. **Kind:** unpursued implication / exact comparison. Delzant's group completion $Q(A)$ maps to $GW(A)$ by sending the class of each nondegenerate quadratic space to that same class; the inverse sends the same generators back, and both defining direct-sum and tensor-product relations agree. Write $J=\ker(\operatorname{rk}:Q(A)\to\mathbb Z)$ and $J_D=\ker(\mathbb Z[D(A)]\to\mathbb Z)$: the displayed surjection sends $J_D$ onto $J$, since diagonalization expresses $J$ as generated by $d_a=\langle a\rangle-1$, so its induced filtration has terms $J^r$. This supplies the exact source of the degree-two graded map queried on 56/038.

### D2 — Exact passage to the Witt filtration

**Witness:** 56/036 and 038. **Kind:** own calculation, confidence high; the inverse is explicit below. Put $h=\langle1\rangle+\langle-1\rangle$, $W(A)=GW(A)/\mathbb Zh$, and $I=\ker(W(A)\to\mathbb Z/2)$; the quotient map $p$ restricts to $p:J\xrightarrow{\sim}I$, with inverse $s(w)=q-\frac{\operatorname{rk}(q)}2h$ for any representative $q$ of $w\in I$. Indeed $h q=\operatorname{rk}(q)h$, $h^2=2h$, and $\operatorname{rk}(kh)=2k$, which prove representative-independence, injectivity, and $s(ww')=s(w)s(w')$ directly. Consequently the induced morphism $J^r/J^{r+1}\xrightarrow{\sim}I^r/I^{r+1}$ preserves the products and gives $\operatorname{grad}^2Q(A)=J^2/J^3\xrightarrow{\sim}I^2/I^3$.

The identity $h\langle a\rangle=h$ used here has an elementary isometry: in the plane with diagonal form $\langle a,-a\rangle$, the basis $(e_1+e_2)/(2a),e_1-e_2$ has Gram matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Applying the same construction at $a=1$ identifies both planes. Thus the equality is supported by an explicit change of basis.

### D3 — Both questions on 56/038 now have affirmative answers

**Witness:** 56/038, final “Remarque.” **Kind:** unpursued implication / later result. For the field $A$ of characteristic different from 2 used by Delzant, Milnor's Theorem 4.1 supplies the isomorphism

\[
s_2:K_2^M(A)/2\longrightarrow I^2/I^3,\qquad
\{a,b\}\longmapsto(\langle a\rangle-1)(\langle b\rangle-1).
\]

The norm-residue map $h_2:K_2^M(A)/2\to\operatorname{Br}(A)[2]$ sends $\{a,b\}$ to $[(a,b)_A]$. Write $\kappa:H^2(A,\mathbb Z/2)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Br}(A)[2]$ for the Kummer comparison specified below. D4 checks the directed equality

\[
\overline w^{\,2}
=\kappa^{-1}\circ h_2\circ s_2^{-1}\circ\operatorname{gr}^2(p):
J^2/J^3\longrightarrow H^2(A,\mathbb Z/2).
\]

Merkurjev's isomorphism theorem therefore proves both injectivity of Delzant's graded map and generation of all Brauer 2-torsion by these quaternion classes. The theorem was originally published in 1981; the primary proof actually checked here is Merkurjev's 2006 paper, Theorem 2.2, which explicitly attributes the original result to its reference [5]. [Milnor 1970, §3 and Theorem 4.1, pp. 328–332; Merkurjev 2006, Theorem 2.2, PDF p. 2.]

### D4 — Verification that the arrow is Delzant's arrow

**Witness:** 56/037, Whitney formula, and 038, graded-map display. **Kind:** own calculation, confidence high; degree-two coefficients determine the comparison. Put $c=\operatorname{pr}_2\circ w$, so $w(q)=(\operatorname{rk}(q),c(q))$ and $c(q)$ is the total-class series. With $u=(a),v=(b)\in H^1(A,\mathbb Z/2)$, the classes of $d_a d_b=\langle ab\rangle-\langle a\rangle-\langle b\rangle+1$ satisfy

\[
c(d_a d_b)=\frac{1+u+v}{(1+u)(1+v)},\qquad
w^1(d_a d_b)=0,\quad w^2(d_a d_b)=uv.
\]

The Whitney identity makes $w^1$ additive, so its vanishing on these additive generators gives $w^1|_{J^2}=0$. The same identity then makes $w^2$ additive on $J^2$. Expansion on triple products, which additively generate $J^3$, gives total-class series with zero degree-one and degree-two coefficients. Thus $w^2|_{J^3}=0$, and $w^2$ induces $\overline w^{\,2}$ on $J^2/J^3$. On the displayed generators its image under $\kappa$ is $[(a,b)_A]=h_2(\{a,b\})$, proving the directed equality in D3. This checks the arrow and normalization directly from the formula printed on 56/037.

A further normalization check is available for an even-rank representative $q$ of $\xi\in I^2$: writing $\operatorname{rk}(q)=2m$ and $z=(-1)$, the rank-zero lift is $s(\xi)=q-mh$, so $c(s(\xi))=c(q)/(1+z)^m$. Its first class is zero, giving $w^1(q)=mz$, and the second coefficient is $w^2(s(\xi))=w^2(q)+\binom m2 z^2$. This records the exact rank correction relevant to comparison with a Clifford invariant.

### D5 — The filtered target retains rank

**Witness:** 56/037, definition of $\widetilde H$, final paragraph, and 038, real place calculation. **Kind:** reading question / unpursued implication. The reference on 56/038 is Grothendieck, *La théorie des classes de Chern*, BSMF 86 (1958), §4.2, pp. 147–149: it equips $\mathbb Z\times(1+\widehat H^+)$ with addition $(r,c)+(s,d)=(r+s,cd)$, unit $(1,1)$, and the universal product characterized on line classes by $(1,1+x)(1,1+y)=(1,1+x+y)$. Thus Delzant's map has the explicit value $q\mapsto(\operatorname{rk}q,\prod_j(1+(a_j)))$ on a diagonal form, while his final paragraph and the next page print an untilded $H$. For the real forms $\langle1\rangle$ and $\langle1,1\rangle$, all positive-degree classes coincide and equal zero, while their ranks are 1 and 2; retaining the displayed integer factor resolves the rank information in the surrounding number-field argument without changing the frozen target notation. [Grothendieck 1958, §4.2, formulas (8)–(11).]

### D6 — What the note leaves for its references

**Witness:** 56/036 “Proposition”; 037 “Corollaire” and Clifford paragraph. **Kind:** unproved claim. The proposition on 036 asserts generation of the kernel by binary relations without giving the reduction from arbitrary diagonal isometries. The Witt citation immediately before the corollary on 037 supplies the invariant criterion for those binary relations; its printed placement does not explicitly assign the proposition's relation-generation proof to that reference. The Clifford paragraph separately cites Springer for the construction of the relative class $u(q)$. The proof obligations are the relation-generation step, compatibility of the filtered ring operations with those relations, and comparison of $u(q)$ with the displayed $w^2(q)$. D1–D4 verify the filtration and degree-two comparison needed for the final questions, while leaving the frozen presentation and its attribution intact.

For the Brauer link, the finite-level comparison is the étale Kummer sequence on a scheme $X/\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[1/2]$:
\[
0\to\operatorname{Pic}(X)/2\to H^2_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X,\mu_2)
\to\operatorname{Br}'(X)[2]\to0,
\]
with the invertibility requirement verified in Stacks, Lemma 59.28.1. Delzant's field has characteristic different from 2, so $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ lies over this base and $\operatorname{Pic}(A)=0$. The coefficient isomorphism $\mathbb Z/2\xrightarrow{\sim}\mu_2$ sends $1$ to $-1$; the induced identification $\mu_2\otimes\mu_2\xrightarrow{\sim}\mu_2$ sends $(-1)\otimes(-1)$ to $-1$. The resulting composite is

\[
\kappa:H^2(A,\mathbb Z/2)
\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(\operatorname{Spec}A,\mu_2)
\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Br}(A)[2],
\qquad
\kappa((a)\cup(b))=[(a,b)_A].
\]

The first arrow includes the field's Galois-to-étale cohomology comparison and the stated coefficient map; the second comes from Kummer and $\operatorname{Pic}(A)=0$. This is the precise common Kummer construction underlying the comparison with context 16/021–023. The latter passage takes its inverse limit under its printed Picard/Néron–Severi and finiteness assumptions; this comparison leaves the earlier runs' statuses intact.
