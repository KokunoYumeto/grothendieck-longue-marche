# Seventh-hour attack on Lead 15: an actual motivic extension

The target remains the rationality of the embedded degree-eight slope lambda, not merely the one-dimensionality of its 5-adic arithmetic image. No additional proved claim is registered from this attempt.

## Proposed route and its exact missing step

The two coordinates of the balanced degree-eight plane have the same CM character. A diagonal change of the two CM homology generators therefore rescales both coordinates by the same factor. At a first nonzero associated-graded layer, a unipotent comparison change has no effect. Thus a genuine motivic subquotient with a rational one-dimensional image in this plane could imply rationality of the ratio; a comparison-torsor ambiguity alone is not a sufficient reason to reject that route.

One possible construction would identify the primitive balanced degree-eight image with a pair of extensions in the established category of mixed Tate motives over Q(i). Its Ext1 in Tate weight4 has rational dimension1, so two such extension coordinates would be rational multiples of a common generator. What remains unproved is that this particular nonmetabelian elliptic subquotient is an object of that established category, with exactly the required coordinate comparison and primitive quotient. The known one-dimensional 5-adic image does not supply a rational motivic subobject. Nor may the arithmetic vanishing of lower-character brackets be substituted for the corresponding motivic vanishing without proof.

## Primary sources checked

1. Hain--Matsumoto, *Universal Mixed Elliptic Motives*, supplied source arXiv:1512.03975, introduction and the subsection **Motivic Sheaves**. The category is constructed from compatible realizations. That subsection explicitly asks whether its objects are realizations of motivic sheaves; it explains that an affirmative answer would give actual motives after specialization to an elliptic curve over a number field. The node/Tate-fibre results do not alone identify the CM fibre used in G.1.

2. Huber--Müller-Stach, *Periods and Nori Motives*, author's final Version6, Proposition10.3.3 and Remark10.3.4, printedp240 (PDFp262), https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/arithgeom/preprints/buch-alt/buch-v6.pdf. The standard mixed-Tate category embeds fully faithfully into mixed Tate Nori motives, with image closed under subquotients. This statement does not say that every Tate-filtered Nori extension lies in that image. Merely constructing a Nori fundamental-group object would therefore leave an essential-image question.
