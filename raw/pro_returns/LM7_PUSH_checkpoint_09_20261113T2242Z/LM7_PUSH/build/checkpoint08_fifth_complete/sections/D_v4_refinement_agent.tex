\subsection{The two-character surface invariant and an actual two-group refinement}

\begin{statement}{D.49}{Proved}{Complete chief fibres after a specified elementary-two refinement}
Let \(S\) be a finite nonabelian simple group with a fixed identification
\(\operatorname{Out}(S)=O=\mathbf F_2^2\); in particular, take the actual
\(A_6\) model of D.31. Let
\[
 1\longrightarrow A=S^\Omega\longrightarrow E\xrightarrow{\pi}D
       \longrightarrow1,\qquad
 q_E:\widehat F(x_1,x_2)\twoheadrightarrow E
 \tag{D.49.1}
\]
be an actual finite chief extension and an epimorphism.
Put \(x_3=(x_1x_2)^{-1}\), \(X_i=q_E(x_i)\),
\(q_0=\pi q_E\), and \(d_i=q_0(x_i)\).
The construction below gives an actual finite quotient
\[
 q^\sharp:\widehat F\twoheadrightarrow D^\sharp,
 \qquad p:D^\sharp\twoheadrightarrow D,
 \qquad pq^\sharp=q_0,
 \qquad\ker p\text{ elementary abelian of exponent two}.
 \tag{D.49.2}
\]
Set \(R=E\times_DD^\sharp\). The joint reference map
\((q_E,q^\sharp):\widehat F\twoheadrightarrow R\) is onto.
For every peripheral epimorphism
\(q_1^\sharp:\widehat F\twoheadrightarrow D^\sharp\), and every specified
\(r_i\in D^\sharp\) with
\[
 q_1^\sharp(x_i)=r_iq^\sharp(x_i)r_i^{-1},
 \tag{D.49.3}
\]
choose any \(H_i\in E\) above \(p(r_i)\). The complete literal set of
epimorphisms \(f:\widehat F\twoheadrightarrow R\) projecting to
\(q_1^\sharp\), with
\[
 f(x_i)\in
 \left\{a(H_i,r_i)(X_i,q^\sharp(x_i))(H_i,r_i)^{-1}a^{-1}:a\in A\right\},
 \tag{D.49.4}
\]
has the same positive cardinality as the corresponding reference set.
Every ordinary conjugator choice is included. The complete ordinary fibre
has the explicit orbit count in \textup{(D.49.26)}.

There is also a comparison retaining a whole relative pro-two source.
Let \(q_P:\widehat F\twoheadrightarrow P_n\) be the D.37 quotient,
\(K=\ker q_P\), and \(\mathcal P=\widehat F/O^2(K)\), with its actual
source map. Let \(D\) be any finite quotient of \(\mathcal P\) over
\(P_n\), and let \textup{(D.49.1)} project to that same quotient of
\(\widehat F\). Every multiplier-one peripheral automorphism
\(\alpha\) of \(\mathcal P\) admits a generating peripheral lift to
\(E\times_D\mathcal P\) retaining its entire \(\mathcal P\)-coordinate.
This applies to every D.43 lift of every specified D.37 \(\beta_Q\).
The maximal pro-five quotient of this actual joint source is precisely
\(P_n\), with the prescribed induced map.
\end{statement}

\begin{proof}
We give the surface invariant, its complete source maps, its behaviour
under every intermediate cover, and the actual finite refinement.

\paragraph{The fixed central extension and the framed surface invariant.}
Write an element of \(O\) as \(j=(j_1,j_2)\), and put
\(j\wedge k=j_1k_2+j_2k_1\in\mathbf F_2\).
Use the actual group
\[
 U=\{(a,b,z):a,b,z\in\mathbf F_2\},\qquad
 (a,b,z)(u,v,w)=(a+u,b+v,z+w+av),
 \qquad \sigma(a,b)=(a,b,0).
 \tag{D.49.5}
\]
Its central kernel over \(O\) is generated by \(z_0=(0,0,1)\).
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 [\sigma(j),\sigma(k)]=z_0^{j\wedge k},\qquad
 \sigma(j)^2=z_0^{j_1j_2}.
 \tag{D.49.6}
\]
Let \(\Pi\) be the fundamental group of an oriented surface of genus
\(g\) with \(b\geq2\) boundary components, with specified positive
based boundary words \(c_1,\ldots,c_b\). It is free after eliminating
one boundary from its surface presentation. Thus every
\(\chi:\Pi\to O\) has an actual lift \(\rho:\Pi\to U\): choose lifts
of a free basis. Write
\(j_i=\chi(c_i)\) and
\(\rho(c_i)=z_0^{\kappa_i}\sigma(j_i)\), and define
\[
             I(\chi;c_1,\ldots,c_b)=\sum_i\kappa_i\in\mathbf F_2.
 \tag{D.49.7}
\]
Two such lifts differ by a central homomorphism
\(\Pi\to\mathbf F_2\). Its boundary values sum to zero because
\(\sum_i[c_i]=0\) in \(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}\). This proves independence
of the chosen lift, while retaining the based boundary words.
For a standard surface presentation,
\[
 I(\chi)=\sum_{h=1}^g\chi(a_h)\wedge\chi(b_h)
               +\sum_{i<j}(j_i)_1(j_j)_2.
 \tag{D.49.8}
\]
Indeed evaluate \(\prod_h[a_h,b_h]\prod_i c_i=1\) in the displayed
group \(U\). This also gives a finite word computation of the invariant
in any actual Schreier basis.

If an oriented surface isomorphism \(\theta:\Pi_1\to\Pi_0\) satisfies
\(\theta(c_{1,i})=u_ic_{0,i}u_i^{-1}\), then applying \(\rho_0\theta\)
and \textup{(D.49.6)} gives the exact transport equation
\[
 I(\chi_0\theta;c_{1,i})
       =I(\chi_0;c_{0,i})+\sum_i j_i\wedge\chi_0(u_i).
 \tag{D.49.9}
\]
Changing a conjugator by a boundary power changes its character by an
element of \(\langle j_i\rangle\) and leaves the right side unchanged.
The boundary is primitive: eliminate a different boundary in the free
presentation. Its free-group centralizer is its cyclic subgroup, by
reduced-word cancellation in a basis containing that boundary.
Consequently \([\chi_0(u_i)]\in O/\langle j_i\rangle\) is exactly
the effective based-path phase.

\paragraph{The complete image of the source phase map.}
For a surjective \(\chi:\Pi\twoheadrightarrow O\), the character-preserving
positive-boundary automorphisms have phase image exactly
\[
 \ker\left(
       \bigoplus_{i=1}^b O/\langle j_i\rangle
          \xrightarrow{\ \Omega\ }\mathbf F_2\right),
 \qquad \Omega((k_i))=\sum_i j_i\wedge k_i.
 \tag{D.49.10}
\]
Necessity is \textup{(D.49.9)}. We prove surjectivity with actual
automorphisms, including their inverse maps.

For a handle and a boundary retain the D.45.7 map
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_c(a)&=c^{-1}ac,&
 P_c(b)&=c^{-1}a^{-1}cab c,&
 P_c(c)&=c^{-1}a^{-1}cac.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{D.49.11}
\]
It fixes \([a,b]c\) literally; D.45.8--9 give its Nielsen inverse.
It changes the \(b\)-character by \(j=\chi(c)\), and its phase at
\(c\) is \(\chi(a)+j\). Every other boundary is fixed.
The exact positive Hurwitz map and its inverse are
\[
 J(c)=cdc^{-1},\quad J(d)=c,
 \qquad J^{-1}(c)=d,\quad J^{-1}(d)=d^{-1}cd.
 \tag{D.49.12}
\]
Its square has phases \(\chi(c)+\chi(d)\) at \(c\) and
\(\chi(c)\) at \(d\), preserving every character.
For two boundaries with the same value \(j\), put
\(P_d=JP_cJ^{-1}\) and use
\(P_cP_d^{-1}\), with inverse \(P_dP_c^{-1}\).
The literal conjugating words at \(c,d\) are respectively
\[
 c^{-1}a^{-1},\qquad
 c^{-1}a^{-1}c^{-1}acac.
 \tag{D.49.13}
\]
The homology map fixes \(a,c,d\) and takes \(b\) to \(b+c+d\).
Thus it preserves the character and gives effective phase
\([\chi(a)]\) at each of \(c,d\), with zero phase at every other
boundary. The identities in \textup{(D.49.11)--(D.49.13)} follow by
the displayed free-word substitutions. Positive boundary swaps,
including a swap with the omitted boundary, and the handle-block swaps
of D.45 put any chosen handle and boundaries in these positions.
The exact change \(a\mapsto ab,\ b\mapsto b\), with inverse
\(a\mapsto ab^{-1}\), makes a \(b\)-value available as a handle
supplier when needed. Pullback by the actual inverse coordinate change
retains the effective phases; its two path contributions cancel.

Let \(B=\langle j_1,\ldots,j_b\rangle\leq O\).
If \(B=0\), the handle values span \(O\). Single pushes at each
boundary supply every phase there, independently, and preserve \(\chi\).
If \(B=\langle j\rangle\) has dimension one, a handle value lies
outside \(B\). At a zero boundary, a single push supplies that outside
value and a Hurwitz square with a nonzero boundary supplies \(j\);
the supplier has zero effective phase. At the nonzero boundaries the
only effective coordinate is one bit. Their number is even, because
\(\sum_i j_i=0\). Paired pushes \textup{(D.49.13)} supply all pairs
of these bits, and hence every even-parity tuple required by
\textup{(D.49.10)}.
If \(B=O\), the nonzero boundary colours include at least two distinct
values. A Hurwitz square between two different colours toggles exactly
their two effective bits. The complete multipartite graph on the
occurring colours is connected, so its edge vectors span all even
tuples: along a path the sum of edge vectors is the vector on its two
endpoints. Zero boundaries receive every phase independently from two
linearly independent boundary suppliers. These constructions prove
\textup{(D.49.10)} in every genus and for every number of boundaries
that occurs here.

\paragraph{The complete two-character source comparison.}
Suppose two oriented surfaces have the same genus and matched positive
boundary values \(j_i\), and their characters \(\chi_0,\chi_1\) are
onto \(O\). For prescribed effective phases \(k_i\), there is an
actual oriented source isomorphism with
\(\chi_0\theta=\chi_1\) and those phases precisely when
\[
              I(\chi_1)+I(\chi_0)=\sum_i j_i\wedge k_i.
 \tag{D.49.14}
\]
We have proved necessity. To match the characters first, use actual
positive boundary swaps to put the matched boundaries in the same
order. If \(B=O\), boundary bounding-pair translations change each
handle-character coordinate by every element of \(O\), independently.
If \(\dim B=1\), the handle characters modulo \(B\) are nonzero
covectors. The actual elementary symplectic maps in D.41 carry one to
the other; the remaining difference in each handle coordinate lies
in \(B\) and is corrected by a boundary bounding-pair translation.
If \(B=0\), the two characters are ordered independent pairs of
covectors on the handle symplectic space. The only orbit datum is their
symplectic pairing. For pairing one, the two corresponding vectors are
a hyperbolic pair and extend to a symplectic basis. For pairing zero,
they are independent isotropic vectors; choose dual vectors using
nondegeneracy, subtract their mutual pairing in one dual vector, and
extend the resulting two hyperbolic pairs to a symplectic basis.
Mapping these bases gives the required symplectic map.
The integral elementary surface twists from D.41 realize its reduction
over \(\mathbf F_2\), fixing the positive boundary classes.
Equation \textup{(D.49.8)} identifies the required pairing with \(I\)
when \(B=0\). Thus \textup{(D.49.14)} is exactly the condition needed
in this last case as well.

Let \(\theta_0\) be the resulting character-matching isomorphism,
and let \(k_i^0\) be its actual path phases.
Equations \textup{(D.49.9)} and \textup{(D.49.14)} give
\(\Omega((k_i-k_i^0))=0\). Precompose \(\theta_0\) by the
character-preserving automorphism constructed in
\textup{(D.49.10)} with these phases. Its explicit inverse supplies
the inverse source map. This proves the complete comparison.
For a cyclic or trivial image in \(O\), use the actual subgroup
\(\epsilon^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\chi)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(S)\)
and the already proved cyclic source maps D.47.5--11.

These maps compare epimorphisms as well as homomorphisms.
If \(T_i\) is any \(S\)-conjugacy invariant subset of the outer coset
\(j_i\), precomposition by \(\theta\) replaces it by
\(L(k_i)T_iL(k_i)^{-1}\), for any actual representative
\(L(k_i)\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)\).
Indeed the image of the source conjugator differs from \(L(k_i)\)
by an element of \(S\). A phase in \(\langle j_i\rangle\) acts
trivially on every such subset: if \(k=vj_i\) and \(a\) has outer
coordinate \(j_i\), then \(L(k)a^{-v}\in S\) and
\(L(k)aL(k)^{-1}=(L(k)a^{-v})a(L(k)a^{-v})^{-1}\).
Both the full subset and its possible emptiness are retained.

\paragraph{Every intermediate cover and the dyadic power term.}
For an actual oriented cover of degree \(e\), write each lifted
positive based boundary under the inclusion as
\(c'_\delta=h_\delta c_i^{m_\delta}h_\delta^{-1}\).
Put \(j_i=\chi(c_i)\) and \(k_\delta=\chi(h_\delta)\).
Restricting the same lift \(\rho\) to the covering group gives
\[
 I(\chi|_{\Pi'})
 =eI(\chi)
  +\sum_\delta {m_\delta\choose2}(j_i)_1(j_i)_2
  +\sum_\delta m_\delta\,k_\delta\wedge j_i
                 \quad\text{in }\mathbf F_2.
 \tag{D.49.15}
\]
For the proof, \((z_0^\kappa\sigma(j))^m\) has central coordinate
\(m\kappa+\binom m2j_1j_2\), and conjugation adds
\(m\chi(h)\wedge j\). For each downstairs boundary the sum of
its local degrees is \(e\), which gives the first term.
This is the exact restriction map for every intermediate cover.
In particular the even-degree correction in the middle term is
retained, together with every based path.

\paragraph{The actual chief coordinates and all extended characters.}
Let \(D_*\) be a factor stabilizer and identify
\(\Omega=D_*\backslash D\) with its right action.
Choose actual representatives \(L(a)\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)\) of
the four outer values, with \(L(0)=1\).
Centerlessness identifies \(E\) with
\(\operatorname{Aut}(A)\times_{\operatorname{Out}(A)}D\), by
\(e\mapsto(\operatorname{Ad}_e,\pi(e))\); a lift of the base value,
followed by the required inner correction, is the inverse construction.
Use the actual gauge in D.47.13--14, with the just chosen \(L(a)\),
to obtain a character \(\epsilon_*:D_*\to O\) and the model
\[
 E_*=
 \{(z,d)\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)^{D_*\backslash D}\rtimes D:
       \epsilon(z(\omega))=\epsilon_*(t_\omega d t_{\omega d}^{-1})\}.
 \tag{D.49.16}
\]
Here \(t_{\omega_0}=1\). The gauge sends a coordinate to
\(L(s_\omega)z(\omega)L(s_{\omega d})^{-1}\), where
\(s_\omega\) is the old outer coordinate of \(t_\omega\) at the
base factor. Adjacent actual inverses cancel in products, proving the
map and its inverse. No section of the outer extension is imposed.

For every \(D_*\leq C\leq D\) retain every homomorphism
\(\epsilon_C:C\to O\) extending \(\epsilon_*\).
Choose representatives \(R_\nu\) of \(C\backslash D\), with
\(R_C=1\), and put
\[
 k_C(\nu,d)=R_\nu dR_{\nu d}^{-1},\qquad
 b_C(d,\nu)=\epsilon_C(k_C(\nu,d)).
 \tag{D.49.17}
\]
They obey \(b_C(de,\nu)=b_C(d,\nu)+b_C(e,\nu d)\).
There is an actual induced group with elementary-two kernel,
with the displayed permutation action
\[
 \mathcal U_{C,\epsilon_C}=
 \{(u,d)\in U^{C\backslash D}\rtimes D:
             \bar u(\nu)=b_C(d,\nu)\},\qquad
 (u,d)(v,e)=(u(\nu)v(\nu d),de).
 \tag{D.49.18}
\]
Its kernel is \(\langle z_0\rangle^{C\backslash D}\).
Define the actual free-source homomorphism \(q_{C,\epsilon_C}\)
by sending \(x_1,x_2\) to the elements above \(d_1,d_2\) whose
coordinates are \(\sigma(b_C(d_i,\nu))\); its value on \(x_3\)
is their inverse product. Take \(D^\sharp\) to be the actual joint
image of \(q_0\) and all these maps in their fibre product over \(D\).
This defines \(q^\sharp\) and \(p\) in \textup{(D.49.2)}.
The kernel embeds in the product of the displayed elementary-two
kernels, proving the claimed type and the finite bound
\[
 |D^\sharp|\ \text{divides}\ |D|\,2^N,
 \qquad
 N=\sum_{\substack{D_*\leq C\leq D\\
                  \epsilon_C|_{D_*}=\epsilon_*}}[D:C].
 \tag{D.49.19}
\]
Subgroups, their homomorphisms, the four-element outer coordinates,
and the subgroup generated by the two specified joint elements are
finite enumerations, so this is an effective finite construction from
the given group tables and maps.

\paragraph{What the refinement forces on every intermediate surface.}
Fix \(q_1^\sharp,r_i\) as in the statement and put
\(q_1=pq_1^\sharp\), \(h_i=p(r_i)\).
Projection to \textup{(D.49.18)} gives actual maps
\(q^U_0,q^U_1:\widehat F\to\mathcal U_{C,\epsilon_C}\) and actual
conjugators \(r_i^U\) with
\(q^U_1(x_i)=r_i^Uq^U_0(x_i)(r_i^U)^{-1}\).
Let \(H_{j,C}=q_j^{-1}(C)\) and
\(\chi_j=\epsilon_Cq_j|_{H_{j,C}}\).
These are the completions of the actual finite-index subgroups of
the discrete pants group under the restricted \(q_j\).
Their full profinite topology is the induced one: every finite-index
subgroup of such a subgroup still has finite index in the discrete
pants group. Thus their completions identify with the displayed
open subgroups of \(\widehat F\), and the positive surface maps
used above give actual continuous maps on these groups.
Evaluation of the \(U\)-coordinate at \(C\) on this subgroup is
an actual homomorphism \(\rho_j:H_{j,C}\to U\) lifting \(\chi_j\).

Choose actual source words \(T^j_\nu\) with
\(q_j(T^j_\nu)=R_\nu\).
The \(C\)-coordinate of \(q^U_j(T^j_\nu)\) has outer value
\(\epsilon_C(R_CR_\nu R_\nu^{-1})=0\), so it lies in the center
of \(U\). Therefore evaluating the based boundary
\(T^j_\nu x_i^e(T^j_\nu)^{-1}\) gives exactly the permutation-cycle
product of \(q^U_j(x_i)\) at \(\nu\); the path conjugator cancels
because it is central.
Match the two boundary cycles by the actual map
\[
 C d\langle q_1(x_i)\rangle
           \longmapsto C d h_i\langle d_i\rangle.
 \tag{D.49.20}
\]
It preserves cycle lengths and every relative degree in finer covers.
For a new initial coordinate \(\nu'\), choose the old one to be
\(\nu'h_i\). The exact cycle-product conjugator is the single
component \(r_i^U(\nu')\), whose outer value is
\(b_C(h_i,\nu')\). Thus \textup{(D.49.6)} gives
\[
 I(\chi_1)+I(\chi_0)
       =\sum_{\delta'} j_{\delta'}\wedge b_C(h_i,\nu'_{\delta'}).
 \tag{D.49.21}
\]
The invariants here use these matched actual based boundaries.
If one instead keeps previously fixed cycle origins, the literal
path conjugators between the origins add their wedge contributions
on both sides, exactly as in \textup{(D.49.9)} and
\textup{(D.49.15)}. Consequently \textup{(D.49.21)} is an actual
source comparison, with every path and power accounted for.
Neither \(q^U_j\) nor \(\rho_j\) is required to be onto its whole
displayed auxiliary group for this argument.

\paragraph{All boundary root sets and all epimorphism counts.}
For each \((C,\epsilon_C)\), let \(E_C\) be
\textup{(D.49.16)} with \(C\backslash D\) and outer cocycle \(b_C\).
Put \(p_C(\omega)=Ct_\omega\) and
\(g_\omega=\epsilon_C(t_\omega R_{p_C(\omega)}^{-1})\).
The actual injection over \(D\) is
\[
 j_C:E_C\hookrightarrow E_*,\qquad
 j_C(z,d)(\omega)
    =L(g_\omega)z(p_C(\omega))L(g_{\omega d})^{-1}.
 \tag{D.49.22}
\]
Its outer-coordinate identity follows by multiplying the three
displayed elements of \(C\); adjacent actual inverses prove the
homomorphism identity. Reading one coordinate over each coarse
coset proves injectivity. Also \(g_{\omega_0}=0\).

Choose every fine cycle origin \(\omega\) above the chosen coarse
origin \(\nu\) of its boundary. Every fine cycle meets that fibre;
then its cycle product is literally the conjugated power displayed
below, with no additional rotation path.
For a coarse boundary \(\delta\), the complete subset
\(T_{j,C,\delta}\subset\epsilon^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\epsilon_C)\)
is the intersection of its required outer coset with all conditions
\[
       L(g_\omega)a^{m_\gamma}L(g_\omega)^{-1}
                       \in\mathcal C_{j,\gamma}.
 \tag{D.49.23}
\]
Here \(\gamma\) ranges over every fine boundary above \(\delta\),
\(m_\gamma\) is its actual relative degree, and
\(\mathcal C_{j,\gamma}\) is the \(S\)-class of the fine cycle
product of \(X_i\), or of \(H_iX_iH_i^{-1}\) on the new side.
The coordinate conjugator equations in a permuted product show that
two elements above the same base element are \(A\)-conjugate
precisely when every such cycle product is \(S\)-conjugate:
solve successively for the coordinate conjugators, and the last
equation is exactly the cycle-product condition.
Thus \textup{(D.49.23)} retains every root and every empty
intersection.

The element of \(E_C\) above \(h_i\) with components
\(L(b_C(h_i,\nu))\) maps under \(j_C\) to an element differing
from \(H_i\) by an element of \(A\). Conjugation by this actual
element, and then its inverse, give the equality
\[
 T_{1,C,\delta'}=
 L(b_C(h_i,\nu'))T_{0,C,\delta}L(b_C(h_i,\nu'))^{-1}.
 \tag{D.49.24}
\]
For image \(O\), equation \textup{(D.49.21)} is precisely the
necessary and sufficient source equation \textup{(D.49.14)} for
these phases. The resulting actual source isomorphism therefore
gives a bijection of epimorphisms
\(H_{j,C}\twoheadrightarrow\epsilon^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\epsilon_C)\)
with their exact outer character and all sets
\textup{(D.49.23)}. Cyclic and trivial images use the corresponding
D.47 source maps as explained above. This proves equality of these
counts for every intermediate \(C\) and every extending character.

We retain the complete generation comparison. Put \(K_j=\ker q_j\).
For one of the just counted epimorphisms, its \(K_j\)-image is
normal in \(S\), hence is \(1\) or \(S\). In the first case it
is exactly \(\Phi q_j\) for a finite-base epimorphism
\(\Phi:C\twoheadrightarrow\epsilon^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\epsilon_C)\)

has components
\(s_\Phi(d)(\nu)=\Phi(R_\nu dR_{\nu d}^{-1})\).
Since \(q_1(x_i)=h_id_ih_i^{-1}\), the two finite-base source maps

Subtract these equal sets. This retains precisely the maps whose
\(K_j\)-image is \(S\).

For such a map \(\varphi\) on \(H_{j,D_*}\), set
\(N_\varphi=\ker(\varphi|_{K_j})\).
Its normalizer is \(H_{j,C}\) for one \(C\geq D_*\).
Conjugation on \(K_j/N_\varphi\simeq S\) gives its unique extension

Consequently the summed retained count \(M_{j,C}\) counts precisely

normalizer is exactly \(H_{j,D_*}\):
\[
 e_j=\sum_{D_*\leq C\leq D}\mu(D_*,C)M_{j,C},
 \qquad e_0=e_1,
 \qquad N_0:=|S|^{|\Omega|-1}e_0>0.
 \tag{D.49.25}
\]
The actual Shapiro map sends a surface map to the source coordinates
\(z_\varphi(g)(\omega)=
 \varphi(T_\omega gT_{\omega q_j(g)}^{-1})\).
Conversely evaluate at \(\omega_0\) on \(H_{j,D_*}\).
The gauge vector \(v(\omega)=z(T_\omega)(\omega_0)\) belongs to
\(S^\Omega\), has \(v(\omega_0)=1\), and gives exactly

\textup{(D.49.22)} restricts to the same surface map, since
\(g_{\omega_0}=0\). These are the actual maps for the factor in
\textup{(D.49.25)}.
Minimal normalizer means that the coordinate kernels on \(K_j\)
are pairwise distinct. A subdirect product of nonabelian simple
groups with distinct coordinate kernels is the full product:
induct on the number of factors using the two-factor Goursat
comparison; a proper relation identifies two simple coordinates
and their kernels. Thus the counted maps are exactly the
epimorphisms onto \(E\). The given \(q_E\) proves positivity.

\paragraph{The joint map and its entire ordinary conjugator union.}
The joint reference source in \textup{(D.49.1)--(D.49.2)} projects
onto both \(E\) and \(D^\sharp\). On \(\ker q_0\) it is a
subdirect product of \(A\) and \(\ker p\).
Their common quotient in the exact Goursat comparison is a quotient
of the perfect group \(A\) and the elementary abelian group
\(\ker p\); it is trivial. Hence the joint image is all \(R\).
The same argument applies to every counted map \(f_E\) and the
given \(q_1^\sharp\). The literal correspondence
\(f_E\mapsto(f_E,q_1^\sharp)\) therefore identifies their complete
generating fibres, with exactly the boundary conditions
\textup{(D.49.4)}. Their cardinality is \(N_0\).

For an exact ordinary-fibre count, let \(t_i\) be the size of the
orbit of the \(A\)-class of \((X_i,q^\sharp(x_i))\) under
\(C_{D^\sharp}(q^\sharp(x_i))\), acting by any lift in \(R\).
Two lifts differ by \(A\), so this action on \(A\)-classes is
well-defined. Equivalently it is the action of the actual image of
this centralizer in \(C_D(d_i)\) on the \(A\)-class of \(X_i\).
Conjugation by \((H_i,r_i)\) transports this orbit to all ordinary
