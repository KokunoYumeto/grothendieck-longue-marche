\paragraph{Retaining both central covers in the same target.}
We also retain the triple-cover target of E.60 through its actual
projection to the old signed $A_6$ target. Write
\[
 T_0=T_{L,A_6},\qquad T_2=T_{L,U_2},\qquad
 T_3=T_{L,U_3},\qquad
 \Sigma=S_L\times\operatorname{ASL}_2(\mathbf F_3).
\]
Here $U_3$ and $T_{L,U_3}$ are precisely the cover and target called
$U$ and $T_{L,U}$ in E.60. Let $K_i=\ker(T_i\to T_0)$ for
$i=2,3$, and let
\[
 C_0=\ker(T_0\to S_L),\quad
 C_2=\ker(T_2\to S_L),\quad
 C_3=\ker(T_3\to\Sigma).
\]
All three $C_i$ are perfect by E.54, E.60 and the target proof
above. Their maps are the specified actual braid-evaluation maps.
Both maps $C_i\to C_0$ are onto. Indeed their images are normal
in $T_0$, and each quotient of $T_0$ by such an image is a
quotient of the corresponding solvable group $T_i/C_i$.
The remaining quotient of the perfect group $C_0$ is therefore
both perfect and solvable, hence trivial.

The affine-three coordinate restricts to an epimorphism on $K_3$.
To prove this, take an element of $T_3$ with any prescribed
$\Sigma$ value $(1,a)$. Its $T_0$ value lies in $C_0$.
Multiplication by the inverse of a lift in $C_3$ kills that value
without changing $(1,a)$. Thus the exact restricted sequence is
\begin{equation}
 1\longrightarrow K_3\cap C_3\longrightarrow K_3
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{ASL}_2(\mathbf F_3)
 \longrightarrow1,\qquad |K_3\cap C_3|=3^{105}.
 \tag{E.63.36}\label{eq:E63-both-cover-kernel}
\end{equation}
The order follows from $|K_3|=2^3 3^{108}$ and
$|\operatorname{ASL}_2(\mathbf F_3)|=216$.
The exact derived orders in E.60.21 and E.63.32 show that the
abelianization of this affine group has order three:
its derived group has order $3456/48=72$.
Every homomorphism from $K_3$ to a finite two-group kills the
normal subgroup of order $3^{105}$ and factors through that
affine group. A nontrivial finite two-group has a quotient $C_2$,
while this affine group's abelianization has order three.
Thus every such homomorphism is trivial.

Apply Goursat to the actual image of $B_4$ in $T_2\times T_3$.
Its common quotient maps onto $T_0$, since the two displayed
projections agree on every braid generator. The kernel of this
common-quotient map is a quotient of both $K_2$ and $K_3$.
It is a two-group because $K_2\simeq\mathbf F_2^{151}$,
and the preceding paragraph makes it trivial. Consequently
the actual common image and its order are
\begin{equation}
 T_{2,3}:=\operatorname{im}(B_4\to T_2\times T_3)
       =T_2\times_{T_0}T_3,\qquad
 |T_{2,3}|=2^{557}3^{271}5^{117}7^8 11^3 13^2.
 \tag{E.63.37}\label{eq:E63-both-cover-target}
\end{equation}
This identifies the common quotient by its two actual maps;
the order is $|T_3|\,2^{151}$.

The map $T_{2,3}\to\Sigma$ through $T_3$ has kernel
$C_2\times_{C_0}C_3$. Its projection onto $C_3$ has kernel
$K_2$, so its abelianization is a two-group. Its projection
onto $C_2$ has kernel $K_3\cap C_3$, so the same abelianization
is a three-group. Both conclusions use surjective projections
and the perfection of their targets. Their common abelianization
is therefore trivial. This proves that this entire kernel is
perfect and that $\Sigma$ is the specified maximal solvable
quotient of $T_{2,3}$.

The actual E.60 arrow $T_{23}\twoheadrightarrow\Sigma$ agrees
with the new arrow on the braid generators. Applying Goursat
once more, a common quotient with the solvable group $T_{23}$
kills the perfect kernel just proved. The actual joint target is
\begin{equation}
             T_{23}\times_{\Sigma}T_{2,3}.
 \tag{E.63.38}\label{eq:E63-both-cover-T23}
\end{equation}
The uniform root-stabilizer and Hurwitz factors of E.60 are
retained as well. Their actual product $V_T$ is perfect, and
the old full target is $T_3\times V_T$. On the kernel of the
old $T_3$ map, the actual new joint image projects onto $K_2$
and $V_T$. Every common quotient is both a two-group and
perfect, hence trivial. Its image is the full product.
Thus the enlarged target is $T_{2,3}\times V_T$, and adjoining
$T_{23}$ gives \eqref{eq:E63-both-cover-T23} times $V_T$.
These statements hold through the retained finite-level maps;
the nonempty compact fibres give the same inverse-limit
comparison. This target computation retains the literal source
maps and does not itself assign a simultaneous corrected
source word beyond the source statements proved above.
