\subsection{A prime-to-five central obstruction that survives every five-adic unit power}

\begin{statement}{D.25}{Proved}{An order-five peripheral cycle obstructed by a central kernel of order two}
There are finite groups
\[
 E\xrightarrow{\pi}G=A_8^5,\qquad |\ker\pi|=2,
 \tag{D.25.1}
\]
an epimorphism $q_E:\widehat F\twoheadrightarrow E$, and a
multiplier-one, labelled peripheral automorphism
$\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ of order five, such that
none of $\alpha,\alpha^2,\alpha^3,\alpha^4$ has a weak
peripheral lift through $\pi$.  The kernel is central and its
obstruction group in D.23 is exactly $\mathbf F_2$.
Consequently taking a $5$-adic unit power cannot remove this
obstruction.  In particular the order of the obstruction group
does not, by itself, bound the exponent of a power that removes
a pointed peripheral lifting obstruction.

Both groups in \textup{(D.25.1)} are perfect, and their maximal
pro-five quotients are trivial.  Thus this counterexample
corrects a proposed lifting argument; it imposes no restriction
on a nonzero pro-five leading coordinate in D.1.
\end{statement}

\begin{proof}
We construct the double cover used in the example rather than
invoke a group database.  Let $\mathcal C$ be the real Clifford
algebra on $e_0,\ldots,e_7$, with $e_i^2=1$ and
$e_ie_j=-e_je_i$ for $i\ne j$.  The unit vector
\[
 u_{ij}=\frac{e_i-e_j}{\sqrt2}
 \tag{D.25.2}
\]
acts by the reflection $v\mapsto-u_{ij}vu_{ij}^{-1}$ that
interchanges $e_i,e_j$.  Products of an even number of these
vectors therefore act by conjugation as even permutations of
the eight vectors.  Let $\widetilde S$ be their group.  This
gives an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow\{1,-1\}\longrightarrow\widetilde S
 \longrightarrow S=A_8\longrightarrow1.
 \tag{D.25.3}
\]
Indeed, an even Clifford element that commutes with every $e_i$
is a scalar, as is seen on the monomial basis $e_{i_1}\cdots
e_{i_r}$; the reversion norm of a product of unit vectors is
one, so a scalar in the kernel is $1$ or $-1$.  The element
$u_{01}u_{23}$ squares to $-1$, showing that both occur.
Surjectivity follows by writing even permutations as an even
number of transpositions.  Hence $|\widetilde S|=2|A_8|=40320$.
Both lifts of the double transposition $(01)(23)$ have square
$-1$, so \textup{(D.25.3)} has no group section.

For a seven-cycle $g=(i_0\ i_1\ \cdots\ i_6)$ with $i_0$
the least member of its support, put
\[
 v_g=\prod_{k=6,5,\ldots,1}u_{i_0i_k},\qquad
 \widetilde g=\varepsilon_gv_g,\qquad
 v_g^7=\varepsilon_g\in\{1,-1\}.
 \tag{D.25.4}
\]
This is the unique lift of $g$ of order seven.  The other lift
has order fourteen.  Formula \textup{(D.25.4)} has rational
Clifford coefficients, with denominator a power of two, so all
the following calculations use exact integers.

Write permutations in one-line notation: the entry in column
$i$ is the image of $i$, with columns numbered $0,\ldots,7$.
Let
\[
 a=(1,2,3,4,5,6,0,7)
\]
and take the following five pairs, where $c_j=(ab_j)^{-1}$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|r}
 j&b_j&c_j&
  s_j=\widetilde a\,\widetilde b_j\,\widetilde c_j\\\hline
 0&(0,2,5,1,6,4,7,3)&(4,0,3,1,7,5,2,6)&-1\\
 1&(0,2,4,1,7,3,5,6)&(7,0,3,1,5,2,6,4)&1\\
 2&(0,2,4,7,3,6,1,5)&(5,0,6,1,4,2,7,3)&1\\
 3&(0,2,6,5,3,1,7,4)&(2,0,5,1,4,7,3,6)&1\\
 4&(0,2,7,4,1,3,5,6)&(7,0,4,1,5,3,6,2)&1.
\end{array}
 \tag{D.25.5}
\]
Every entry $a,b_j,c_j$ belongs to the same one of the two
seven-cycle conjugacy classes in $A_8$.  An explicit conjugator
from $a$ to any entry $g$ is the permutation that maps
$0,\ldots,6$ in order to the successive members of its
seven-cycle, starting with its least member, and maps $7$ to
its fixed point.  Each of the ten displayed conjugators is
even; this is checked directly and saved in the certificate.

Each pair $(a,b_j)$ generates $S$.  Furthermore no automorphism
of $S$ takes $(a,b_i)$ to $(a,b_j)$ for $i\ne j$.  The finite
certificate for these two assertions is described below and
contains a literal distinguishing relator for each of the ten
pairs of components.  It follows that the two tuples
\[
 g_1=(a,a,a,a,a),\qquad
 g_2=(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)
 \tag{D.25.6}
\]
generate the entire group $G=S^5$.  Here is the exact
subdirect-product argument.  A subdirect subgroup of a product
of copies of a nonabelian simple group is a product of diagonal
blocks.  Inductively, after projecting to the first $r-1$
factors, its kernel in the last factor is either the full
simple group or trivial.  In the latter case the subgroup is
the graph of an epimorphism $S^{r-1}\to S$.  The commuting
normal images of the factors show that exactly one factor
maps isomorphically to $S$.  This would identify two of the
component epimorphisms up to an automorphism of $S$, which
the distinguishing relators exclude.  The certificate also
proves simplicity of this particular $S$, so the argument
uses no unverified finite-group recognition.

Set $E=\widetilde S\times S^4$, and define the source triple
\[
 \begin{split}
 X_1&=(\widetilde a,a,a,a,a),\\
 X_2&=(\widetilde b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4),\\
 X_3&=(-\widetilde c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4).
 \end{split}
 \tag{D.25.7}
\]
Its product is one by the first spin in \textup{(D.25.5)}.
It generates $E$.  In fact $S$ is perfect by simplicity, and
the derived subgroup of $\widetilde S$ maps onto $S$.
If that derived subgroup missed the central element $-1$,
it would be a complement, contradicting the nonsplitting
proved above.  Thus $\widetilde S$ and $E$ are perfect.
The image generated by $X_1,X_2$ already maps onto $G$; if it
missed $\ker\pi$, it would have index two in $E$, giving a
nontrivial abelian quotient of a perfect group.  This proves
the claimed epimorphism $q_E$.

Let
\[
 \alpha(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4)=(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4,v_0).
 \tag{D.25.8}
\]
It has order five and preserves the three labelled conjugacy
classes of $g_i=\pi(X_i)$ because all five entries in each
coordinate triple are in the same class of $S$.
For $0\leq k\leq4$, a conjugate of $X_1$ above
$\alpha^k(g_1)$ must have first coordinate $\widetilde a$;
a conjugate of $X_2$ above $\alpha^k(g_2)$ must have first
coordinate $\widetilde b_k$.  These assertions follow from
their odd orders.  A conjugate of $X_3$ has order fourteen,
so its unique possible first coordinate above
$\alpha^k(g_3)$ is $-\widetilde c_k$.
The corresponding first-coordinate product is
\[
 \widetilde a\,\widetilde b_k(-\widetilde c_k)
       =-s_k
       =\begin{cases}1,&k=0,\\-1,&1\leq k\leq4.
         \end{cases}
 \tag{D.25.9}
\]
The other coordinates multiply to one.  Each of the individually
prescribed elements is a conjugate of its original $X_i$:
lift a conjugator in $S$ and use uniqueness of the order-seven
lift, also multiplying by $-1$ for the third coordinate.
Thus \textup{(D.25.9)} is the complete obstruction, after all
conjugators have been allowed.

One can also check the base-centralizer quotient directly.
The centralizer in $S$ of a seven-cycle with one fixed point
is its cyclic group of order seven.  Hence
$C_G(g_i)\simeq C_7^5$ for all three indices, and every map
$\kappa_i:C_G(g_i)\to C_2$ in D.23 is zero.  Consequently
the obstruction group is $C_2$ and the four nonzero products
in \textup{(D.25.9)} survive it.  An element
$u\in\Z_5^\times$ gives $\alpha^u=\alpha^k$ for a unique
$k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, proving the assertion about unit powers.
The perfectness already proved gives the trivial maximal
pro-five quotient maps exactly as in D.24.

For reproducibility, \texttt{a\_peripheral\_cycle.py} constructs
all $20160$ even permutations of eight letters and uses the
Clifford multiplication rule
\[
 e_Ie_J=(-1)^{\#\{(i,j)\in I\times J:i>j\}}e_{I\triangle J}
 \tag{D.25.10}
\]
to evaluate \textup{(D.25.4)}.  The selected triples, their full
rational Clifford coefficients, their explicit conjugators,
and the four negative products are retained in
\texttt{a\_peripheral\_cycle\_a8.json}.
Closure under the two literal generators gives $20160$
elements for each selected pair.  For each pair of components,
the program propagates the proposed generator map along these
words; two words with the same source value and different
target values give the saved relator witness.  These witnesses
exclude every possible automorphism carrying one pair to the
other, without a classification of $\operatorname{Aut}(A_8)$.
Finally it partitions the nonidentity elements into all
thirteen conjugacy classes and verifies that the normal
closure of a representative of each has order $20160$.
Every nontrivial normal subgroup contains such a representative,
which proves simplicity.  Their class sizes are
\[
 112,210,1344,2520,1120,1680,2880,2880,
 105,3360,1344,1344,1260;
\]
their sum is $20159$.  The certificate also verifies
$(u_{01}u_{23})^2=-1$.  The executed output ends with
\texttt{PASS\_ORDER\_FIVE\_WITH\_CENTRAL\_TWO\_OBSTRUCTION}.
The earlier seven-letter search, with eight inequivalent
generating triples all having spin $-1$ in its fixed profile,
is retained under the unsuffixed filenames and is not used
as an exclusion outside that search domain.
\end{proof}
