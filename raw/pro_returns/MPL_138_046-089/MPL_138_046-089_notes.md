# Research notes — MPL 138, scans 046–089

## Scope, evidence, and the answer to bridge B6

These notes concern **42 supplied scans of `138.pdf`, 046–089 excluding 079 and 088**. The page files and combined diplomatic transcription were frozen before the context was opened; the hashes are recorded in `progress.log` and `frozen_sha256.txt`. Every alternative reading below is a research question, not a change to that transcription. In source locators, “138/085, L22” means scan 085 of `138.pdf`, line 22 of `pages/MPL_138_046-089_138_scan085.tex`; these are reproducible TeX line numbers, not a claim to preserve the manuscript's line breaks.

The strongest evidence in this selection is a progression from **explicit algebraic covers and flag permutations to peripheral profinite groups and an arithmetic outer action**. It does not contain a located explicit statement that every smooth, projective, connected curve over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) arises from maps, or that the absolute Galois action on maps is faithful. Those are the curve hypotheses understood in the universal-converse question below. This is a bounded finding about this selection, supported by reading all the pages and by the searches recorded below; it is not a claim about the whole cote, everything Grothendieck knew, or everything he had already said elsewhere.

### N02 — A concrete three-point cover, not a universal assertion

**Source:** 138/047 L21–47 and the ramification diagram L48–52. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context/relation:** Hua §5, “La tour diédrale,” L948–967; *Esquisse* §3, scan 119/13 L549–571 — instantiates the later dictionary. **Register:** README B6; bears on the examples behind **EXIT_TOWER E.24 [Open, return's claim]**, not its image calculation.

The formulas “\(x_n^n=x_1\)” and \(u=\tfrac12-\tfrac14(z+z^{-1})\), with the explicitly recorded ramification above \(0,1,\infty\), exhibit an algebraic family whose composite has the familiar dihedral symmetry. Hua derives the composite \(u=\tfrac12-\tfrac14(x_n^n+x_n^{-n})\) and the type \((2,2,n)\), crediting the source formulas rather than a general theorem. This is strong evidence for the cover side of B6, but it does not say that every finite map, or every algebraic curve, arises by this particular construction.

### N03 — What is the limit denoted by \(\mu_\infty\)?

**Source:** 138/046 L8–12; /047 L45–47; /081 L33–36. **Kind:** reading question. **Context/relation:** Hua §5 L951–967; LM140-4 scan 97 L5654 onward, Fermat-tower discussion; the explicit inverse-limit Tate module on 138/081 — differs in how the limiting object is specified. **Register:** the Fermat-tower orientation in `RESULTS_STATE.md` L19; **LM6 A.22 [Open, return's claim]** is a later full-group question, not decided by this notation.

Is \(\mu_\infty\) here a notation for the compatible inverse system of finite deck groups, or for the union of roots of unity? The distinction matters because the inverse limit with power transition maps is the profinite Tate module explicitly written on scan 081, whereas the torsion union is a different group; Hua repeats \(\mu_\infty\) and does not resolve this convention.

### N04 — The cubic identity is an identity on the conic

**Source:** 138/049 L4–18; /055 L33–42. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context/relation:** Hua §5, “Les endomorphismes de la conique,” L974–989 — restates his explicit algebraic explanation. **Register:** no matching result row; local calculation within README B6's examples.

The contribution of the diplomatic page is the actual sequence of trials and corrections, not a new discovery of that identity.

### N05 — Why the power is odd: a conditional descent calculation

**Source:** 138/051 L63–70, especially “\(n\) impair”; /049 L4–10; /055 L27–31. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the stated calculation, conditional on using the displayed conic and coordinate. **Context/relation:** Hua §5 L972–997 gives the \(\mathbb Q(i)\)-coordinate but does not explicitly give this parity test — adds a descent explanation. **Register:** no matching settled row; this local test does not determine **LM7_PUSH E.3 [Open, return's claim]**.

For \(u=(y-ix)/z\) on \(x^2+y^2+z^2=0\), coefficient conjugation over \(\mathbb Q(i)/\mathbb Q\) gives \(\sigma(u)=-u^{-1}\). For a positive integer \(n\), the power pullback \(f_n^*(u)=u^n\) commutes with this semilinear involution exactly when \((-1)^nu^{-n}=-u^{-n}\), hence when \(n\) is odd. This supplies a concrete reason for the “\(n\) impair” in the diagram and for the cubic example, without asserting that the manuscript itself gives the descent proof or that the observation is new.

### N06 — The two conic equations must remain distinct

**Source:** 138/051 L22,36,48–60. **Kind:** reading question. **Context/relation:** Hua §5 L992–997 — differs by explicitly changing coordinates. **Register:** no matching result row.

Hua reconciles them over \(\mathbb Q(i)\) by \(z\mapsto iz\), but that reconciliation is his explanation and is not written at the corresponding place on the page. The question is whether the working notes are switching conic models without announcing it; replacing the plus sign would conceal the issue.

### N07 — The critical-value elimination leaves boundary cases to check

**Source:** 138/049 L40–60. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the finite-coordinate observation, because differentiating \(p/q\) gives the displayed numerator. **Context/relation:** Hua §5 L1003–1004 explicitly notices the same calculation; *Esquisse* §3, scan 119/15 L652–654, uses rational-map coefficients — adds a verification task, not a new formula. **Register:** README B6; no matching settled row.

The equations “\(p-\lambda q=0\),” “\(p'-\lambda q'=0\)” lead to \(p'q-q'p=0\), a useful test for finite critical points where the quotient is defined. To turn this scratch calculation into a certificate of a three-point cover, one would also check poles, the point at infinity, common factors, and the actual critical values. That would connect the calculation to the branch-value condition used in B6; the page does not carry out those remaining checks.

## IV. Special profinite groups and the arithmetic outer action
