### Q3d — Local membership and global o-minimality cannot be conflated

**Source:** 150/038–039; additional 120/017 and 121/004–005. **Kind:** own idea / diagnostic example. **Quote:** “stratification cylindrique locale.” **Context:** MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scans 182–184; adds a test of the local/global distinction. **Register:** WG2 B15 (§1.2) [unchecked plan]. **Confidence:** high, directly from the definitions.

The set \(\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb R\) is locally semialgebraic at each ambient point but cannot be globally definable in an o-minimal expansion of the ordered real line, since it has infinitely many isolated components. Thus a local geometric category and its associated global o-minimal structure need not contain the same subsets of \(\mathbb R\) before the comparison construction. This is consistent with Schürmann's dictionary, not a counterexample to it. Merely finding a locality axiom in Grothendieck is insufficient to identify his theory with “locally o-minimal” or “weakly o-minimal.”

### Q4b — Shiota's compact Hauptvermutung is a genuine partial match

**Source:** 150/037–039, conditional on a specified tame category. **Kind:** unpursued implication / literature comparison. **Quote:** “catégorie topologique modérée.” **Context:** MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scan 190; adds a precise triangulation comparison absent there. **Register:** WG2 B15 (§1.2) [unchecked plan].

Shiota's clean 1997 \(\mathcal X\)-Hauptvermutung is Corollary III.1.4, p. 271: a \(\mathcal X\)-homeomorphism between compact real polyhedra entails a PL homeomorphism, with compatible compact triangulation addressed by Theorem II, p. 96. ### Q4c — The isotopy refinements are substantially closer to the programme

**Source:** 150/038–043. **Kind:** unpursued implication / literature comparison. **Quote:** “la même théorie” [uncertain final noun on 038]. **Context:** MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scan 190; adds a modern relative comparison. **Register:** WG2 B15 (§1.2) [unchecked plan].

Shiota (2016, printed p. 8) recalls the Supplement on p. 227 of his 2014 paper: definable homeomorphisms of finite compact-polyhedron families are definably isotopic to PL homeomorphisms through homeomorphisms, explicitly correcting the omitted qualification in the earlier statement. His 2010 preprint also gives the straightening complement on p. 17 and, on p. 46, a relative injectivity argument through homeomorphisms fixing prescribed subsets. These stronger results must be considered before judging the comparison; bare object-level Hauptvermutung understates what Shiota proves. The published Supplement's wording here is verified through Shiota's own later restatement and correction, not by an independent reading of the inaccessible publisher page.

### Q4d — A component comparison follows in the compact polyhedral setting

**Source:** 150/039–043; additional Esquisse comparison in Q4a. **Kind:** own deduction from the checked relative isotopy results. **Quote:** “Isom” in the later comparison statement. **Context:** MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scans 188–190; adds a restricted modern realization of the desired morphism comparison. **Register:** WG2 B15 (§1.2) [unchecked plan]. **Confidence:** high for fixed compact polyhedral families and the stated definable isotopies.

Straightening gives surjectivity of PL homeomorphism classes onto definable homeomorphism classes modulo admissible isotopy, and the relative injectivity argument gives injectivity in the compact setting (Shiota, 2010, Complement to Theorem 2.1 and p. 46). This is a component-level deduction with the chosen isotopy convention, not a theorem about arbitrary compact-open continuous families. Likewise, an equivalence on isotopy classes does not make the literal inclusion of categories of PL maps full: \(x\mapsto x^2\) on \([0,1]\) is semialgebraic but not PL.

### Q4e — The full mapping-space claim still needs a parameter-preserving comparison

**Source:** 150/037–043; additional Esquisse comparison in Q4a. **Kind:** own proposed verification problem. **Quote:** “équivalence d'homotopie” in the later statement. **Context:** MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scans 188–190; adds an explicit higher-parameter test. **Register:** WG2 B15 (§1.2) [unchecked plan]; no supplied register settles this comparison. **Confidence:** high about the required test; no claim that it is unknown in the literature.

For the chosen semisimplicial family model, a sufficient comparison lemma would deform a larger-theory homeomorphism \(F:X\times\Delta^n\to Y\times\Delta^n\) to a smaller-theory one through homeomorphisms over \(\Delta^n\), relative to a boundary family already in the smaller theory and with the specified marked subsets. Straightening a total-space homeomorphism need not preserve its parameter projection, and preserving finitely many faces does not by itself preserve every fibre. Such all-dimensional relative control would support a weak-equivalence comparison; passing to an actual homotopy equivalence also requires the chosen realization or model, for example CW realizations. The checked results establish substantial compact isotopy-class comparison without this review establishing Grothendieck's entire claim across his proposed theories, map types, and systems.

## I. Stratifications, orders, and the opening deployment programme

### R06 — The closure test for openness uses continuity

**Source:** 150/005, opening display. **Kind:** own idea / hypothesis check. **Quote:** “\(\varphi^{-1}(\overline B)=\overline{\varphi^{-1}(B)}\).” **Context:** MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scan 182, restriction to open subspaces; adds a hypothesis audit. **Register:** WG2 §3.1 #2 [unchecked plan]. **Confidence:** high, by a two-point computation.

## II. Cones, collars, germs, and simultaneous compatibility

### R11 — The absolute cone keeps an attaching map of actual spaces

**Source:** 150/012, (1)–(4). **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “identifie \(\bar T\) au cône de l'application.” **Context:** MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scans 140–142 and 177–180; adds an explicit topological model to neighbourhood germs and groupoid comparisons. **Register:** WG2 §1.8 #5 [unchecked glossary plan]; EXIT_ONE_TYPE P1d1 [Proved, return claim].

The defining pushout is \(Y\amalg_{\{0\}\times\dot T}(I\times\dot T)\), with \(f(x)=0.x\), so it retains the link space \(\dot T\), its map to \(Y\), and the quotient topology. In common modern terminology this is a mapping cylinder of \(f\), called a cone in the manuscript; when \(Y\) is a point it is the ordinary cone on \(\dot T\).
