## Principal decisions

The three relevant topoi are different objects. A shrinking full neighbourhood is a pro-object of Grothendieck topoi. Its covering germ is a classifying topos. A fixed stratification gives a further constructible topos. A finite or ordinary bicategorical colimit after taking covering reflections need not recover the geometric shape. This is established by the universal properties and by the span Set ← BZ → Set, whose ordinary topos pushout is Set while its space pushout is S2. The proof of geometric gluing uses full sheaf descent, all intersections, and full flag coherence. For a locally finite chart system the radii are synchronized by a continuous positive minorant. Proper étale groupoids supply the same finite local control. The further extension to arbitrary nonproper infinite-isotropy atlases is withdrawn: F.51 supplies an explicit failure of common-stage synchronization. Intrinsic actual-stack-neighbourhood pro-objects remain distinct from objectwise atlas germs.

The distinction is already visible for covering categories. Let Z act on P1 by z↦z/(1+z), fixing the boundary point0. Every orbit accumulates there, so the only invariant neighbourhood is all of P1. Its punctured quotient is C/Z=C*, with fundamental group Z. The derivative at0 is1, so the punctured normal-bundle stack has group Z²; the comparison sends the normal meridian to0. F.51 proves this counterexample and the corrected proper-stack scope. The sharp depth theorem still concerns the actual oriented-blow-up boundary; identifying that boundary with intrinsic punctured germs is a separate step. The arithmetic scheme results and the finite quotient cusp model are unaffected.

The triple crossing verifies the manuscript's actual error in formula (246): the displayed depth-one/two hexagon has fundamental group Z4, while the full coordinate link has Z3. The missing rank-three flags impose the extra relation. Relative general position in the boundary simplex proves the sharp bound depth k+2 for the k-type. It does not justify truncating the full topoi.

The arithmetic sequence uses the open stratum and a profinite Chern transgression. It need not start with zero. The line bundle O(1) on P1 gives a surjective transgression; a conic in P2 gives the degree-four quotient. A finite root stack kills finite powers of global inertia, so its kernel is the inverse image of the corresponding closed normal subgroup, not the original kernel. The infinite root/log construction and a stable-reduction quotient have different universal properties. Three explicit blow-ups resolve the cusp; the exceptional tails of self-intersection −3 and−2 impose h=b³ and h=a², with exact node kernels Z(−3,1), Z(−2,1) and their profinite analogues. The stable quotient t=s6, x=s2X, y=s3Y has injective vertical meridian. Its marked projective completion also has injective rank-two corner lattice, proved through the infinite puncture commutator and finite Heisenberg quotients of every modulus.

For stable towers F.39 supplies the missing profinite argument: a finitely generated free normal subgroup embeds after completion in every group extension, by conjugation into Aut of its free profinite completion. Cyclic subgroups of free groups acquire their full profinite topology by explicit finite graph coverings. The vertical lattice proof then uses the essential stable vanishing cycle and the toroidal contact matrix. These arguments do not assume that a discrete injection survives completion without proof. Smooth finite-type bases, the analytic marked-bundle condition, compatible toroidal stable completions, and the arithmetic residue-group hypothesis are stated explicitly.

For Mbar0,5, the ten meridian vectors are written in H1(M0,5,Z)=Z5. Every one of the fifteen incident pairs spans a primitive rank-two sublattice. This proves full meridian injection both discretely and profinitely. Free-by-free and link extensions establish goodness; compact-context congruences give finite category quotients; bounded coskeletal models establish the finite-homotopy representation statement. Thus the result is stronger than a finite-set exodromy assertion and is not obtained merely by citing an exodromy theorem.

The reconstruction part retains the base commutation isomorphism. This is why Frobenius does not refute faithfulness over the base topos. Reduced finite-presentation schemes give a faithful functor in every characteristic. Fullness needs a restriction on topos morphisms: a generic-point section already defeats the unrestricted statement. Even pinned fullness fails over finite fields, because the distinct relative twists (id,a), indexed by Zhat, cannot all arise from the countably many finite zigzags of finite-type scheme maps. F.49 extends this obstruction to every algebraic extension of a finite field; it also proves failure for every infinite perfect field with nontrivial absolute-Galois centre and every algebraically closed field. The witnesses are explicit pinned endomorphisms of the affine-line étale topos. Their framed geometric-point functions distinguish the relative maps and rule out realization by perfect-polynomial maps. The verified positive results distinguish pinned maps over fields from chivalrous maps over general bases.

F.47 adds a positive field range beyond the cited reconstruction theorems: every algebraic subfield of FQab for a number field F. Its proof first uses two-point Chinese-remainder evaluations, multiplicative independence, and the free abelian group M*/mu-infinity to force integral Kummer exponents on punctured P1. Transfer along a finite curve map isolates each boundary residue. The abelian-variety Kummer theorem kills the resulting infinitely divisible divisor class, giving an actual unit. All translates then force the full geometric-point function to be rational, including every finite exceptional point. Smooth compactification, transverse test curves, and a Picard/Neron–Severi argument carry this to arbitrary dimension. Finally CHW Theorem4.18 is applied in its all-regular-source form, which supplies the required compatibilities on every sliced étale object. Each of those passages is written in full; the final assertion is not inferred from point equality for one morphism.

F.50 broadens that theorem to every subfield of a finitely generated extension of Qab. Four additional arguments are proved. Divisors on a proper normal model reduce the free multiplicative quotient to the finite algebraic constant field. The abelian-variety divisibility theorem extends to finitely generated fields by spreading every division witness on one fixed normal model through finite étale multiplication fibres; no repeated shrinking is assumed. Smooth arithmetic models and Chinese remainders at distinct vertical primes give independent evaluation ratios even when the punctures are transcendental. Finally, regularity over the finite algebraic constant field supplies geometric points of unbounded residue degree for the quasi-rational argument with transcendental coefficients. These close the exact gaps between F.47 and F.50. All three other agents independently audited the complete extension.

### F.44: an arithmetic test beyond the verified reconstruction ranges

The former test over Q(mu_2-infinity) is settled. The integer-exponent calculation was strengthened in F.46 to pointwise algebraicity, in F.47 to full reconstruction over every algebraic subfield of FQab, and in F.50 to every subfield of a finitely generated extension of Qab. The general reduction in CHW4.18 is verified and derived explicitly.

The new concrete test not decided in this return is a nonconstant pinned endomorphism of Gm_et over Q^solv, the maximal solvable algebraic extension of Q. Its injectivity side is F.4. Dimension-zero finite-type schemes are finite étale after the specified localization, so this is a first positive-dimensional test; no literature-wide smallest-field or openness claim is made. The attempted extension of F.47/F.50 fails at two precise places. Q^solv is radically closed, so D(k*)=k*, and the height bound needed to reduce ambiguities to roots of unity cannot hold. Its algebraicity and its elements 2^(1/n) also exclude embedding in any finitely generated extension of Qab, whose algebraic constant field is finite over Qab. Moreover every elliptic 2-power division field is solvable because GL2(Z/2^r) is solvable; hence E[2-infinity] lies over this field and the AVKF/Picard Tate input fails. All these assertions are proved in F.44. The directly checked Mochizuki (2006) Theorem2.5 concerns solvably closed quotient groups and compatible isomorphisms; it does not supply the absolute-kernel-group, noninvertible Hom assertion here. Finite scheme-model descent does not imply descent of an arbitrary geometric morphism of complete sites. The next approach would have to use nonsolvable finite-cover compatibilities beyond the Kummer invariants; no example satisfying those compatibilities has been constructed and no full realization argument has been proved here.

The recovered CYCLOTOMIC row B2 is also checked against Jarden (2011), Proposition2.5, and Haran–Jarden (1991), Corollary2.5. Their exact hypotheses prove that the centralizer of G_Qsolv in G_Q is trivial. F.44 supplies the complete normal-subgroup argument, a concrete S5 polynomial certifying that the subgroup is nontrivial, and the resulting exclusion of both central comparison twists and nonidentity coefficient-semilinear automorphisms becoming relative. Thus neither elementary construction from F.49 settles this residual test.

A further attempted reduction through a proper finite extension is analyzed explicitly. Such extensions are Hilbertian by the Weissauer result invoked in the opened proof of Jarden's Proposition2.5. Pinned reconstruction in characteristic zero does descend through a finite extension: apply CHW's unit criterion, compare the two pullbacks of the representing unit by their geometric-point values, and descend the unit faithfully flatly. But every algebraic extension of Q^solv still contains its non-torsion divisible multiplicative subgroup and the same elliptic 2-primary divisible subgroup. It also has no nontrivial discrete valuation, since any Z-valued valuation kills the divisible base and then every algebraic element. Thus Hilbertianity does not restore any of the multiplicative, Picard, or valuation inputs used in the proved reconstruction range. This is an explicit failed route, not a claimed counterexample.
