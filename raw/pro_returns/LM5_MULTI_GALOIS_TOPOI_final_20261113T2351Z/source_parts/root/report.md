# Parts B and C: proof and source report

## Main correction to the mixed-topos question

The topos defined by the triples on scans 123–127 is the ordinary Artin gluing of the global covering topos and the residue covering topoi. It is not the Kummer-étale topos. Its derived gluing term is controlled by the **effective image** `K_s = im(H_s -> G × Q_s)`. The geometric punctured neighborhood instead remembers the whole local group `H_s`. Statement B.3 identifies the exact comparison cone, and B.4 gives the smallest counterexample to the unqualified proposition on scan 129: `(P¹,{∞})` in characteristic zero. The open affine line is a K(π,1), but the mixed topos is the arrow topos and has no positive constant cohomology; the projective line has nonzero degree-two cohomology.

This correction is compatible with the checked three-puncture calculation in LM3b. Three punctures have effective inertia. The extension proposed in the prompt from that example to every nonempty boundary is false. The proposition on scan 129 cannot be proved as requested without changing its hypotheses. The local equivalence with the henselian trait topos is valid; the invalid inference is that restriction from the global mixed topos preserves the injectives needed for derived support comparison.

B.6 proves local inertia injectivity for the full geometric fundamental group of every affine smooth curve in positive characteristic. The proof first realizes cyclic prime-to-characteristic extensions by divisors and Kummer theory, including a one-cusp positive-genus construction through finite Heisenberg groups. Artin–Schreier principal parts and surjectivity of `F-1` on `H¹(O_X)` realize cyclic p-extensions. For the affine line in characteristic p, an initial Artin–Schreier cover gives positive genus. The general local extension is handled through a fixed finite composition series, base-changing each next cyclic step. This fixed series is essential: it establishes termination rather than merely describing an iterative construction. The initial covers were checked for p=2 and p=3 as well as p=5. Two independent proof reviews checked this argument.

B.7 gives the full comparison list. The geometric exceptions are a proper rational curve with empty boundary, and in characteristic zero a proper rational curve with one puncture. The coefficientwise defect, including arithmetic descent, is stated as a derived Galois cohomology complex. Thus a geometric degree-two defect need not first appear in arithmetic degree two: the real anisotropic conic with F₂ coefficients first fails in degree three. Prime-to-p tame comparison has the extra one-cusp affine-line failure in every characteristic. Full wild comparison does not have that additional positive-characteristic failure. The Artin–Schreier cover `y^p-y=x` proves that tame groups cannot replace full groups for p-torsion.

B.9 constructs the comparison with logarithmic topoi and its prime-to-characteristic cohomology cone. The logarithmic statement that cohomology agrees with the open complement applies to logarithmic local systems. It must not be applied to an arbitrary specialization triple pulled back from the mixed topos without checking local constancy. B.10 separates filled-group orientation, local support, and the full relative peripheral complex. The latter retains the affine-line orientation even when the ordinary mixed topos has lost it. B.11 constructs the requested trace by dualizing restriction; scan 131 explicitly supplies the orientation-module isomorphism that the construction needs. B.12–B.13 give the complete mixed-map and twisted-splitting formulas and the exact meaning of uniqueness.

## Hole filling and exterior functoriality

C.1 proves the two pseudopullback squares in all Grothendieck topoi. The upper triangle has only a noninvertible specialization cell. C.2 proves more than the restricted multi-Galois assertion: because each boundary projection `H_s -> Q_s` is surjective, the hole-filling quotient is a pseudopushout in all ordinary topoi, including for profinite groups. This special surjectivity hypothesis is decisive. The generic profinite free-product span in C.3 is not a counterexample to C.2; it shows why an unrestricted profinite pushout theorem needs a smaller category. The other counterexample in C.3 distinguishes the ordinary-topos pushout `Set <- BZ -> Set`, which is Set, from the homotopy pushout of classifying spaces, which is S².

C.5 gives the exact acyclicity table. In particular the one-cusp characteristic-zero mixed-to-filled map is acyclic, even though the curve-to-mixed map misses the fundamental class. C.6–C.8 retain coherent group actions, the component factor, and the distinction between discrete torsors and profinite protorsors. For profinite arithmetic data, C.7 starts from an actual extension of profinite groups and proves the quotient base-change assertion by finite quotient diagrams and coherent inverse limits.

C.9 finds a finite obstruction to the proposed general functoriality for exterior maps: maps with trivial centralizer are not closed under composition. The explicit groups have orders 216, 1296 and 6, and all centralizer calculations were executed. One must retain the full bicategory or restrict to a composition-closed family. C.10 constructs the transporter square with its exact comparison cell. Direct inspection of scans 107–108 confirms the written order `Lambda = u Lambda-prime d`; it also exposes a reversed arrow in the prose and an unprimed symbol in an ill-typed composite. C.11 proves that this order, together with injectivity and self-centrality, forces the integer scalar to be invertible. The degree- and label-uniqueness margins both fail, and the exact scalar-orbit criterion is proved. C.12 supplies the operator construction left unfinished on scan 109.

## Primary sources actually opened

The sources used for these parts were opened in full or at the stated primary-source passages. The bibliography records this separately for each entry.

- Artin, Grothendieck, and Verdier, SGA 4, tome I, IV 9.5.4 and its proof: the accessible left-exact gluing theorem. The scholarly 2015 French re-edition at the CMLS host was used after a different host failed to respond.
- SGA 4, tome II, VI 8.7.1, 8.7.3, 8.7.7 and 8.7.8: coherent inverse limits and cohomological continuity. These hypotheses were checked for the finite-triple presentation in B.3; continuity was not invoked without a site-level explanation.

- Schmidt and Stix (2016), Lemma 2.7(a): the étale K(π,1) theorem for curves. B.5 includes the reduction and the coefficient and base-field extensions required here.
- Stacks Project tags 03SB, 03RQ and 0A3J: affine/proper curve cohomology, Kummer degree, and Artin–Schreier surjectivity.
- Illusie (2002), §§4.7, 5.2–5.4, 5.9 and Theorem 7.4: logarithmic local inertia, higher direct images, projection formula, Kato–Nakayama comparison, and logarithmic purity. Theorem 7.4, rather than the superficially similar Betti statement in §5.7, is the relevant open-complement comparison.
- Deligne (1977), Arcata lectures, VI Theorem 2.2 and its specialization proof, in Milne's scholarly translation: the normalized curve duality used for the abstract trace.
