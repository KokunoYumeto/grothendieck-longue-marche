# Legibility and witnesses

The ordinary typewritten baselines are generally legible. The difficult material is concentrated in overtyped letters, small handwritten additions, and words crossed by one or several cancellation strokes. The enhanced source images and saved enlarged crops permit a reading of much of this material, but enlargement does not restore ink hidden by an opaque stroke. An uncertainty is therefore retained even when one candidate would make better French, English, or mathematics.

The least secure readings are the erased or overtyped old letters in cote 123; the tiny superscripts and the crossed small formula in the lower part of 162-1/003; several names in the two loan registers; the cancelled date on 162-1/003; and the faint parenthetical notation on the cover. Those places carry in-text doubts and entries above. The whole-paragraph cancellation on 123/028 is distinct from its local deletions. The latter are preserved as nested states and can be less certain than the otherwise readable typewritten sentence.

C is usually the more complete diplomatic witness on these sheets, particularly for the typescript body and local revision layers. H often gives a cleaner running reading but sometimes expands shorthand, supplies a plausible familiar phrase, or omits typewritten passages behind an editorial account of the additions. H also supplies useful competing readings: neither witness was treated as an authority. Among the material disagreements reviewed here are names, pp mistaken for W in Rapport, manuscript reference numerals, operators and indices, live versus cancelled text, and the attachment or geometry of annotations. The numerical comparison in the changes log counts adjudicated places, not error probabilities or accuracy rates for either model.

The source’s contradictions and slips remain visible. Examples include the typed h where the surrounding statement calls for n on 123/002, the target g(-2) in 123/016, and that sheet’s e=0 alongside u≠1. The mathematical checks recorded in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv are narrowly scoped; a visual reading or a bibliographical title is not counted as proof of the mathematical work named. No text-recognition software, external bibliographical source, or TeX compilation was used.

Blind second-reading comparisons are kept separately for every selected box. Equivalent macro choices, a reader including more surrounding text, and genuinely different readings are all retained in the token comparison and adjudication records. Only decisions that alter the consolidated reading are listed as second-reading changes in the changes log; the original first and second TSV readings are preserved unchanged.
