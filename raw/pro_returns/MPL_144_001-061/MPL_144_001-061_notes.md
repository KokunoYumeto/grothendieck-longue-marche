## Scope, evidence, and status

These notes concern the 59 supplied scans of cote 144: 001–061, excluding the omitted blank scans 012 and 041. The diplomatic transcription was completed, visually audited page by page, compared for recurring notation, and frozen by SHA-256 before the context archive was opened; the chronology and hashes are in progress.log. The principal evidence is the frozen page file for each cited scan, named pages/MPL_144_001-061_144_scanNNN.tex. Line numbers below refer to those individual LaTeX files, not to hypothetical numbered manuscript lines; a displayed formula, heading, or short quotation usually provides the more useful locator. A question mark in the source and an editorial \(\mathrm{unsure}\) reading have different evidentiary status: the former may mark Grothendieck's uncertainty, whereas the latter records uncertainty in this transcription.

According to the supplied README.md, LM_140-1_text.tex through LM_140-4_text.tex are second-pass transcriptions checked against their scans. They are used as primary contextual text, with their surviving doubts and cancellations retained. The nine neighbouring-cote *_notes.md files and the LM research notes, overview, and section descriptions are earlier editorial work; their quotations, identifications, and conclusions are **unverified leads**, not established manuscript readings or mathematical results. In particular, an unavailable ARCH_147_*_text.tex cited inside an earlier note is not treated as a source supplied with this task.

Each numbered research item identifies its kind, source, short anchor, contextual relationship, and remaining issue. “Restates,” “adds,” “differs,” and “contradicts” describe the comparison, not a chronology of composition: physical order and a shared archival cote do not establish which thought came first. ## Answers to the five specified questions

### Q1. Is the \(M_{0,5}\) condition or order-five relation written as an equation, and dated?

**No identifiable \(M_{0,5}\) pentagon equation occurs in the supplied 59 pages.** The conspicuous five-factor equation on scans 037 and 040, \(\rho\varepsilon _0\rho^{-1}\varepsilon _0\rho=1\), is equivalent to the two-generator braid relation under the definitions on 036–040; the verification is N037.1 below. The 1983 date, with the month tentatively read “Juin” on 036 and the year independently written on 040, dates this braid-formula sequence, not a pentagon occurrence. The 1981 and 1982 formula headings and the cubic condition on 035 supply no date for an absent five-point equation.

In the GT/associator formulation, the pentagon compares several substitutions of one two-variable element in a larger configuration/pure-braid group; merely counting five factors, finding a three-cycle, or seeing a pentagonal picture is insufficient. Furusho (2010, Theorem 2, equation 4, and Remark 4) supplies a precise published comparator, while the checked LM_140-3_text.tex, scans 121–122 and 133, distinguishes cyclic cocycle and two-/three-cycle conditions. The neighbouring leads MPL_142_094-122_notes.md, R25 at scan 100, and MPL_147_048-137_notes.md, scans 056–069, concern relevant low-dimensional moduli, but the former retains the unresolved subscript \(5|3\) and neither can supply a missing equation here. This negative answer is limited to the supplied packet.

### Q2. Which \(\Gamma\) does each occurrence mean?

There is no single package-wide \(\Gamma_{g,\nu}\). Scans 004 and 006 concern a discrete Teichmüller/mapping class group; 007 uses \(\Gamma\) for the binary icosahedral group of order 120; 010 uses it for \((\mathbb Z/3)^2\); and 011 uses it for a finite subgroup of a Teichmüller group. On 013–028 the symbol alternates among discrete and completed geometric mapping class groups, their images, geometric moduli fundamental groups based at \(X\), absolute Galois groups, and—at 019, line 8—a groupoid of sections.

The full occurrence concordance below records every LaTeX line containing an actual \(\Gamma\) token, including doubtful and cancelled occurrences, and explicitly lists all pages without one. The particularly consequential changes are the definition of the no-plus completed group as a complex-conjugation preimage on 013, its later use as a geometric comparison group on 019–024, the genuine image prime on 014, and the permutation-kernel exponent \(!\) on 013. The Latin \(G_{0,3}\), \(T_{11}\), \(ST_{11}\), and \(G_2\) on the later sheets are not silently renamed \(\Gamma\).

### N005.8 — An explicit local Kummer check

**Source:** cote 144, scan 005, boundary-map program; contextual question about cote 149 scan 078. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for this local assertion.

For a strictly henselian discrete valuation ring \(R\), a uniformizer \(\pi\), and a pulled-back boundary parameter \(f^*t=u\pi^\mu\) with \(u\in R^\times\), \(\mu>0\), the integer \(\mu=\operatorname{length}_R R/(f^*t)\) is the contact multiplicity in this situation. Pulling back \(z^n=t\), for \(n\) invertible in \(R\), and extracting an \(n\)th root of \(u\), shows that a source inertia generator acts on the pulled-back root by \(\zeta^\mu\), hence multiplication by \(\mu\) on the tame Tate module, or on the full Tate module in residue characteristic zero (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d.-c, n.d.-d). **Restates the local mechanism:** LM_140-2_text.tex, scans 033, 045, and 075–077; scan 054 also records the cyclotomic factor contributed by arithmetic conjugation. The reason for confidence is the explicit finite-cover calculation, whereas embedding its target as the chosen global \(L_i\) and proving specialization compatibility remain separate obligations.
