## Isotopy, completion, and the initial groupoid: scans 001–024

### N003.1 — Homotopy invariance supplies the missing existence argument

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 003, paragraph after (3), through (5).  
**Brief source quotation:** « est pl. fidèle »; « il doit exister un rev. fini étale ».

Full faithfulness of the product functor on coverings gives uniqueness of a trivialization once it exists, whereas existence requires essential surjectivity as well. For the surfaces and interval in the manuscript, homotopy lifting and covering classification make restriction to \(Y\times\{0\}\) an inverse up to natural isomorphism to \(Y'\mapsto Y'\times I\), thereby supplying the omitted argument. The opening identification with paths in the space of finite covering maps also needs the chosen mapping-space topology and the corresponding parametrized lifting statement; it should not be inferred from full faithfulness alone (Hatcher, 2002, §1.3).

**Context comparison:** Adds the explicit product-with-an-interval argument to `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 092–093, which requests a careful isotopy theory for arrows beyond homeomorphisms; scan 222 explicitly restricts its existing isotopy category to homeomorphisms. No matching proof of essential surjectivity was found in those contextual passages.

### N004.1 — The orbit description depends on a continuous family of lifts

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 004, (6)–(9) and Proposition 1.  
**Brief source quotation:** « il f. et s. qu'ils soient … sous le groupe des autom. de \(X\) isotopes à l'identité ».

This proves the orbit statement once the parametrized trivialization of N003.1 is established, including continuity of the inverse family. It is the passage from a family of covering maps to a family of homeomorphisms, rather than a pointwise factorization, that carries the argument.

### N005.1 — Finite-order rigidity needs the hyperbolic hypothesis

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 005, Proposition 2 and the preceding examples.  
**Brief source quotation:** « tout autom. … d'ordre fini … isotope à l'identité est l'identité ».

The asserted rigidity concerns each connected finite-type orientable surface with \(\chi<0\), and is exactly what makes isotopy forget no deck transformations later. The examples \(\mathbb C^*\) and \(\mathbb U\times\mathbb U\) show why the excluded annulus and torus cannot simply be carried through the argument. A proof must either realize the finite action conformally and use rigidity, or supply the topological periodic-map argument requested on 007; contractibility of the identity component by itself is not a proof of torsion-freeness of that group.

**Context comparison:** Restates almost verbatim the claim in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 137–138, where preservation of a conformal structure is also an explicit input. ### N005.2 — The homology strengthening requires separate treatment of punctures

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 005, final paragraph, continued on 007.  
**Brief source quotation:** « il suffit que \(\sigma\) opère trivialement sur la homologie »; « \(n\geq3\) ».

For a compact curve, the level-\(n\) rigidity invoked here is grounded in Serre's theorem for finite-order automorphisms of an abelian variety acting trivially on its \(n\)-torsion, with \(n\geq3\), together with the curve-to-Jacobian argument (Serre, 1961, appendix; Grothendieck, 1961, §3). For a punctured curve, one must additionally control the permutation of the ends and the toric part of the generalized Jacobian; the compact theorem alone does not supply those steps. The level-two restriction is material, since inversion acts trivially on two-torsion.

**Context comparison:** Adds a homological sufficient condition to `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 137–138, and restates the level-\(n\) versus level-two distinction discussed there on scans 538 and 547. Those latter passages concern elliptic moduli and their residual involution, rather than a proof for every open surface.

### N005.3 — Removing fixed points does not automatically preserve the given isotopy

**Kind:** own idea. **Source:** scan 005, opening paragraph, completing 006's last sentence.  
**Brief source quotation:** « en remplaçant \(Y'\) par \(Y'\setminus S'\) ».

Even if the finite ramification set \(S'\), including fixed points of nonidentity powers, is invariant under \(\sigma\), an isotopy from \(\sigma\) to the identity on \(Y'\) need not preserve \(S'\) at intermediate times, so its restriction to \(Y'\setminus S'\) is not automatic. The proposed reduction to a free action therefore needs an isotopy relative to the added punctures, or an independent proof of rigidity such as N007.6. This is a logical gap in that particular reduction, not a counterexample to Proposition 2 itself; relative-isotopy results explicitly impose additional hypotheses (Yagasaki, 2000, §3).

**Context comparison:** Differs from the shorter conformal-action route in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 137–138, by exposing a new relative-isotopy requirement in the puncturing argument. The context's isolated-fixed-point discussion does not itself provide the needed relative isotopy.

**Own-idea confidence and reason:** high for the proof obligation: preservation by the endpoint maps does not imply preservation by the intervening isotopy; no claim is made that the desired relative isotopy is impossible here.

### N007.1 — Invariant complex structure is a substantive input

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 007, first three lines.

### N007.3 — A translation stabilizing the Abel image must be excluded

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 007, middle paragraph.

### N007.4 — A possible purely topological proof uses fixed-point indices

**Kind:** own idea. **Source:** scan 007, bracketed request.  
**Brief source quotation:** « trouver une démonstration purement topologique ».

After compactifying the ends, trivial action on ordinary integral homology forces the punctures to be fixed individually and makes the compact-surface Lefschetz number \(2-2g\); a nontrivial orientation-preserving periodic map has isolated fixed points of index \(+1\), by its local rotation model. For \(g\geq2\) the negative Lefschetz number is impossible, for \(g=1\) a puncture supplies a fixed point despite Lefschetz number zero, and for \(g=0\) hyperbolicity supplies at least three fixed punctures although a nontrivial periodic sphere rotation has only two. This is a proof strategy for the integral-homology version, conditional on spelling out end extension and the local rotation theorem; the mod-\(n\) version additionally needs the torsion-freeness of the principal congruence kernel at level \(n\geq3\) (Constantin & Kolev, 1994; Serre, 1961).

**Context comparison:** Adds the requested topological route to the conformal/Jacobian discussion in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 137–138, rather than claiming that the supplied context contains this proof. Its exceptional surfaces agree with the list on scans 215–218.

**Own-idea confidence and reason:** medium: the fixed-point count is decisive once the listed topological and congruence inputs are supplied, but those inputs are not reproved here.

### N007.6 — Transfer provides another topological rigidity argument

**Kind:** own idea. **Source:** scan 007, request for a topological proof; scan 005, puncturing reduction.  
**Brief source quotation:** « démonstration purement topologique ».

For a connected orientable surface \(X\) of finite type with \(\chi(X)<0\), start with a nontrivial finite-order orientation-preserving map acting trivially on \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\), replace it by a nonidentity power of prime order \(p\), and remove its finite fixed-point set, so the resulting \(C_p\)-action on \(V\) is free. In the homology exact sequence for deleting those points, the old homology and the new peripheral kernel are fixed, hence the finite-order action on their extension is trivial over \(\mathbb Q\), and then over torsion-free integral first homology. Transfer identifies the quotient's rational first homology with the invariants, so source and quotient have equal first Betti numbers and equal Euler characteristics because they are simultaneously compact or noncompact; multiplicativity \(\chi(V)=p\chi(V/C_p)<0\) is then impossible. This repairs the reduction without a relative-isotopy assertion, with finiteness of the fixed set obtained by extending the periodic map over the finite-puncture compactification and applying the local periodic-map classification there (Constantin & Kolev, 1994).

**Context comparison:** Adds a homological proof strategy to the conformal-action route in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 137–138, and addresses the hyperbolic exceptions listed on scans 215–218. No matching transfer argument was found in those passages.

**Own-idea confidence and reason:** high conditional on the explicit local classification and ordinary-homology convention: finite-order unipotent representations over characteristic zero are trivial, and transfer plus Euler-characteristic multiplicativity gives the contradiction.

### N008.1 — Contractibility of a mapping component needs more than a set-theoretic torsor

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 008, Corollaire 2.  
**Brief source quotation:** « Les composantes connexes … sont contractiles ».

For finite-type hyperbolic surfaces, the identity component of the homeomorphism group is contractible; a precise modern statement covering the noncompact cases and recalling the compact cases is Yagasaki (2000, Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 2.1). To transfer that result to the component of a covering map, the orbit bijection \(u\mapsto f\circ u\) must be shown to be a homeomorphism, or at least an appropriate homotopy equivalence, using parametrized covering lifts. The manuscript establishes freeness and transitivity but does not write this topological step.

### N008.2 — The finite stabilizer is the deck group, not an arbitrary realization group

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 008, Corollaire 3 and following paragraph.  
**Brief source quotation:** « le stabilisateur … s'identifie … [au] groupe des \(Y\)-autom. ».

### N008.3 — A genus-two test disproves the unrestricted marginal stabilizer assertion

**Kind:** own idea. **Source:** scan 008, upper marginal NB.  
**Brief source quotation:** « Tout sous-groupe fini … peut s'obtenir … comme stabilisateur ».

For a closed orientable genus-two surface \(X\), the hyperelliptic mapping class generates a subgroup of order two, but no unramified covering of that surface onto a closed orientable target can have a deck group containing an involution: the resulting free involution would give \(-2=2\chi(X/C_2)\), hence \(\chi(X/C_2)=-1\), impossible for a closed orientable quotient. Thus the NB is too strong for the finite étale arrows actually defined here, even before considering orientation-reversing mapping classes. Nielsen realization provides finite isometry actions, which may have fixed points; orbifold quotients or removal of the fixed points would change the category (Kerckhoff, 1980, Theorem 1; 1983).

**Context comparison:** Contradicts the unrestricted stabilizer formulation if read literally, while `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 102, asks only for realization by a finite action on a surface. That contextual formulation lacks the freeness condition needed for the present covering stabilizers.

**Own-idea confidence and reason:** high: the obstruction is the Euler-characteristic formula for a free involution on a closed orientable genus-two surface; it is an elementary test, not a novelty claim.

### N008.4 — Finiteness of the orbit set has a short degree-and-monodromy proof

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 008, lower marginal NB.  
**Brief source quotation:** « cet ens d'orbites est fini ».

For fixed connected hyperbolic \(X,Y\), a finite covering must have degree \(d=\chi(X)/\chi(Y)\), so either no positive integer degree is possible or one degree is fixed. A finitely generated \(\pi_1(Y)\) has only finitely many homomorphisms to \(S_d\), and hence only finitely many transitive permutation representations and isomorphism classes of connected degree-\(d\) covers. Restricting to those homeomorphic to \(X\) gives the asserted finite set.

The finite-type group presentations on scans 318–319 supply the corresponding finite-generation framework, but no identical degree argument was located there.

### N009.1 — Orbitwise completion is concrete in the surface case

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 009, (12) and its topological annotation.  
**Brief source quotation:** « complété profini »; « somme d'espaces compacts ».

### N010.1 — Closed arrow stabilizers alone do not make composition continuous

**Kind:** own idea. **Source:** scan 010, definition of orbit topologies and (14).  
**Brief source quotation:** « est alors … une application continue »; « Mérite d'être faite avec plus de soin ! ».

Take three objects \(X,Y,Z\), with \(\operatorname{Aut}(Y)=P\) an infinite profinite group and the other automorphism groups trivial, and set \(\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)=P\), \(\operatorname{Hom}(Y,Z)=P\), \(\operatorname{Hom}(X,Z)=P\), with the evident middle regular actions and composition \((q,p)\mapsto qp\). All arrow stabilizers are closed, but the manuscript's orbit topology makes the first two Hom sets profinite and the third discrete, so composition is not continuous. The image \(H_u\) for \(u:X\to Y\) is trivial and not open, showing exactly why the added finite-index hypothesis on 011 matters.

**Context comparison:** Adds a counterexample to the bare axiom on 010; no matching categorical example was found. The closest relevant warning is `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan 320, about topology on group-orbit quotients, which concerns a different failure of the initial axioms.

**Own-idea confidence and reason:** high: associativity follows from multiplication in \(P\), stabilizers are explicit, and a map from \(P\times P\) onto infinite discrete \(P\) cannot be continuous.

### N011.1 — The finite-index hypothesis repairs the stated continuity argument

**Kind:** unproved claim. **Source:** scan 011, both occurrences of (16) and the first NB.  
**Brief source quotation:** « il est ouvert s'il est d'indice fini ».

Because \(G_u\) is compact and the target automorphism group is Hausdorff, its image \(H_u\) is closed and carries the quotient topology from \(G_u\). Finite index then makes \(H_u\) open, and the equation \(v\alpha_i\varphi u=v\alpha_i u\psi\) gives continuity on each of the finitely many open cosets \(\alpha_iH_u\). This proves the desired joint composition continuity for a category already equipped with the stipulated closed stabilizers, while leaving construction of the completed category itself to 012–014.

**Context comparison:** Adds an explicit sufficient axiom to the profinite quotient constructions in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 318–320. No matching general categorical axiom was found in the context; its automorphism-group setting does not require this extra argument.
