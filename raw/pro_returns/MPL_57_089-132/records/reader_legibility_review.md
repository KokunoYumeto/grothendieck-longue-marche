## Reader's final legibility assessment

The whole pages and both supplied halves were read in the first pass, with additional crops for cramped or cancelled passages; every page then received a second visual audit against its saved text. This does not eliminate the risk of plausible but mistaken readings in the fast handwriting. The least secure passages are the crowded revisions on 091–094, the small marginal word on 101, the DVR and pseudo-section proof revisions on 106–109, and especially the incidence argument and its warning on 122–124; the overwritten descent hypotheses and cancelled proof on 126–129 and the lower edge of 131 also warrant close checking.

The reused typescripts are generally more legible, but their black cancellations, added mathematical glyphs, typewriter letter/number ambiguities and incomplete formula slots remain risks. Scan 132 is particularly important: missing signs and unclosed visible punctuation are intentional features of this diplomatic text, not completed mathematics. The reading doubts are local confidence judgments, not a measured error rate; absence of a doubt mark should not be taken as a guarantee of correctness.

Nontextual figures, including marginal strokes and doodles, are manually drawn TikZ approximations of the visible geometry. No scan image, OCR output, or context-derived correction is embedded in the transcription. Mechanical brace and environment checks were performed; a full LaTeX compilation was not available because the installed TeX programs lacked their format/package data, as documented in `records/tex_environment_check.md`.
