## A. Cote 156-1: evolving axioms, order, and proof obligations

### A01 — The cancelled two-segment expectation is not a theorem

**Source:** 156-1/003, cancelled NB 2; revisit at 025–027. **Kind:** conjecture / withdrawn in the same text. **Quote:** “Je présume”; “au plus deux segments”; “un ... cercle.”

In the cancelled first proposal he expects at most two segments with a given adjacent endpoint pair, with two precisely for a circle component. The later consecutive-segment discussion is compatible with his continuing interest in this expectation, but its last argument breaks off before a classification is completed. It should be indexed as an abandoned formulation and continuing question, not cited as a proved circle theorem for the final network axioms.

**Context:** search: cercle|R29|réseau|segments; MPL_148_029-072 R29 is a first-pass lead at 148/058:4–11,059:4–6,061:36–44 about marked-circle cutting: the present cancelled expectation adds a distinct segment-count question rather than settles that earlier scope issue.

### A02 — Support determines a segment only after an asserted corollary

**Source:** 156-1/003–004 and 007. **Kind:** self-flagged / unproved claim. **Quote:** “Je ne sais”; “J'ignore”; “Si Ĩ=J̃ alors I=J.”

The draft first asks whether a segment is determined by its support and endpoints, then asserts the stronger support-only conclusion as Corollary 4 on scan 007 and records the resulting identification S⊂P(L) in the 7 June margin of scan 004. A proof must recover the endpoints from support using F1–F2 and the internal-pair analysis, rather than assume injectivity in the initial data. The chronology matters because an extra injectivity axiom and a derived injectivity theorem give different presentations of the theory.

**Context:** search: support|incidence|R121-08|segments; MPL_121_049-073+153+155 R121-08, first-pass lead at 121/050–052 with comparisons to 147/179–180,185–190, concerns missing stratified attachment data; these pages add a local support-recovery assertion but do not establish an analogous recovery of a whole space.

### A05 — A segment is not required to be the union of its two subsegments

**Source:** 156-1/005 margin; 008 F4. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “je ne dis pas que”; “on n'affirme pas égalité!”

The explicit refusals of a union equality permit a lieu in I to be incomparable with an internal cutting lieu z in the later partial-order interpretation. Such a lieu is neither in [x,z] nor in [z,y], even though it remains in [x,y]. This is a substantive departure from an ordinary linearly ordered interval and explains why familiar interval arguments must be checked rather than imported.

**Context:** search: ordre|incidence|R121-08|recollement; MPL_121_049-073+153+155 R121-08 is a first-pass lead at 121/050–052: these pages add a concrete reason why an incidence-like model can have internal information beyond an elementary binary union.

### A06 — Divisibility forces infinite supports and infinitely many subsegments

**Source:** 156-1/005–006; revision at 024–025. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “Ĩ° est infini”; “du fait de la condition de divisibilité.”

Repeated subdivision of a nondegenerate segment under the original nonempty-interior condition gives infinitely many internal lieux and subsegments. That makes the original divisible segment model intrinsically infinite, even before any family of finite cuttings is considered. The statement does not apply to the reduced-graph revision, whose segments have exactly two points and empty interior.

**Context:** search: finite poset|finite category|divisible|B15; WG2 B15 (§1.2) and WG2 §3.1 #2 are unchecked plans; this distinction changes the interpretation of their hoped-for finite categorical bridge without contradicting the separately recorded finite-poset realization results.

### A07 — The internal endpoint-pair criterion deserves a proof on its own

**Source:** 156-1/006 Corollary 3 and 007 continuation. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “Cette application est injective”; “conditions équivalentes.”

He asserts that δ:SΙ→P2(Ĩ) is injective, that its image contains every pair meeting δI, and that an entirely internal pair {u,v} belongs to the image precisely when either lieu lies on the appropriate endpoint subsegment of the other. Proving these equivalences is the step that converts a support-and-endpoint system into a partial order, and it must respect the exclusions and uncertain marginal extension to arbitrary u,v. It does not say all pairs are admissible, which would improperly turn that partial order into a total order.

**Context:** search: ordre|pairs|poset|STRATA; STRATA A.4 and A.12 are return-level “Proved” exit-realization statements, with no corresponding internal-pair lemma: the present criterion adds a separate algebraic prerequisite before any such comparison.

### A08 — Branch equivalence needs transitivity, not just a dichotomy

**Source:** 156-1/007 and added clause at 013. **Kind:** self-flagged / unproved claim. **Quote:** “relation d'équivalence”; “Il manque”; “compléter F3.”

The common-initial-segment relation is declared an equivalence relation without a full transitivity proof on the page. The added intersection or local-filtering condition is exactly the sort of control needed to pass from I sharing a germ with J and J sharing one with K to a common germ of I and K. A formal axiomatization should state which version of F3 is used at every later appeal to branches, including the modification when divisibility is dropped.

**Context:** search: branche|germe|filtrant|Q1b; MPL_150_001-043 Q1b is a first-pass lead at 150/012–015,022–023 compared with 147/140–142 on conical germs: this page adds an abstract segment-germ equivalence, not the same germ construction.

### A09 — Extension through a regular endpoint is local

**Source:** 156-1/008 Corollary 1 and 009 Corollary 2. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “prolongement ... à une extrémité régulière.”

The argument first uses subdivision to find at least two branches at a regular endpoint, then the branch axiom to choose an opposite short segment and F4 to glue it on. Applying this at both regular endpoints gives a larger segment whose interior contains the original support. This supplies the later regular-end repair of F6, but it does not extend an arbitrary singular endpoint or prove a global common upper bound.

**Context:** search: extension|branches|filtrant|B15; no direct prolongation result is registered; WG2 B15 (§1.2), unchecked, remains a prospective global bridge, while this lemma provides only a local step.

### A10 — Topological arcs illustrate why F5 is a real restriction

**Source:** 156-1/009, 7 June margin. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “parties modérées ... isom. à [0,1]”; “F1):F4)”; uncertain continuation naming F5 or F6.

The margin proposes tame embedded arcs in a tame space as satisfying the early segment axioms, while flagging the later axioms as problematic. At an interior point of several arcs in a plane, arbitrarily many distinct germ directions may occur, so the order-two condition is not a generic property of all such arcs. This separates the intended one-dimensional regular network geometry from the class of all interval-like subsets of an arbitrary tame ambient space.

**Context:** search: modérée|Q9|Q1b|branches; MPL_150_044-084 Q9, first-pass lead at 150/073–076, concerns unsettled tame axioms, and Q1b concerns conical germs at 150/012–015,022–023: the margin adds a specific limitation, without choosing or proving a complete tame category.

### A11 — Arbitrary restriction is asserted but not proved

**Source:** 156-1/009 cancelled question and 010 assertion. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “partie quelc.”; “les axiomes F1-F5 sont satisfaits.”

For any L′⊂L the manuscript retains all original segments supported in L′ and asserts that F1–F5 still hold, upgrading the cancelled question on scan 009 to an affirmative statement. Subdivision is inherited, but F4 may force a glued segment containing extra lieux outside L′, since its support is only required to contain the union of the two given pieces. Therefore this claimed inheritance needs an additional closure condition or a narrower class of restrictions.

**Context:** search: sous|proper|D.11|H.1; BISITE D.11 and RESIDUE H.1 are return-level “Proved” statements about proper maps of finite posets with explicit lifting conditions, not arbitrary subsets: the present unrestricted assertion differs in hypothesis and is not supplied by those results.

### A12 — A counterexample to arbitrary restriction

**Source:** test of 156-1/010; warning at 008. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “on n'affirme pas égalité!” **Confidence:** high; the interval-by-interval check is given in records/segment_counterexamples_memo.md.

Take the ordered set L=(Q∩[0,1)) ⊔ ((Q∩(1,2))×{0,1}) ⊔ (Q∩(2,3]), with the first block below both mutually incomparable middle chains and both below the last block, and let S consist of all nondegenerate order intervals. It satisfies the intended F1–F5, the strengthened local F3, and both versions of F6, while for z=(3/2,0) the subset L′=[0,z]∪[z,3] omits the other middle chain. The retained segments [0,z] and [z,3] meet at the still-regular point z, but their unique original glued segment with endpoints 0,3 is all of L and is not retained, so F4 fails after restriction.

**Context:** search: sous|proper|D.11|H.1|contre; no matching counterexample is registered; BISITE D.11 and RESIDUE H.1, return-level “Proved” under properness conditions, are not contradicted, while this example contradicts the literal arbitrary-restriction assertion on 156-1/010.

### A13 — A plausible repair is closure under required gluings

**Source:** response to 156-1/010 and F4 at 008. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “les axiomes F1-F5 sont satisfaits.” **Confidence:** medium; necessary for the displayed counterexample, but a full inheritance theorem still requires all axiom checks.

A natural repair is to require L′ to contain the unique ambient F4 composite whenever two retained segments meet at a joint that is regular in the restricted model. Convexity in a fixed ordered segment model is a stronger sufficient-looking condition because every interval between retained comparable endpoints then stays inside L′. Neither repair is asserted to solve all restriction questions for general networks, particularly where branch equivalence or regularity changes under passage to the subset.

**Context:** search: proper|lifting|D.11|H.1; BISITE D.11 and RESIDUE H.1, return-level “Proved,” already emphasize explicit lifting conditions for a different comparison: the proposed closure condition is an analogous proof requirement, not a rederivation or application of their theorems.

### A14 — The empty-segment case changes after the revision

**Source:** 156-1/010 Remark 1; 024–025. **Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “S=∅ (i.e. si Lr=∅)”; “graphe réduit.”

Under the original divisibility condition, S empty is equivalent to Lr empty and the structure reduces to an arbitrary set of singular lieux. Once empty-interior segments are admitted, a nonempty reduced graph has Lr empty while S need not be empty. The earlier equivalence is therefore version-dependent, and retaining it as a universal consequence would collapse the very reduced networks introduced later.

**Context:** search: réduit|graph|finite|B15; no direct version distinction occurs in the register; WG2 B15 (§1.2), unchecked, receives a clarification that even the discrete case means a graph rather than necessarily a zero-dimensional form.

### A18 — Orientation consistency can be formulated as a transport problem

**Source:** 156-1/016–018, endpoint and branch identifications. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “syst. transitif”; “exactement deux classes.” **Confidence:** medium; this is a proof strategy, not an exhibited counterexample or completed theorem.

One can organize the local two-element sets and prescribed bijections into a transport system and ask whether every closed chain of transitions acts trivially. ### A19 — “Linear” does not here mean totally ordered

**Source:** 156-1/018–019. **Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “cas linéaire”; “relation ... transitive”; “cas circulaire.”

The source defines the linear case by transitivity of the oriented relation x<y determined by segment endpoints, and the circular case by its failure. Thus “linear” in this classification should not be paraphrased as an ordinary line or linearly ordered set.

### A23 — F6 is introduced to repair a genuine global failure

**Source:** 156-1/023 following 021–022. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “filtrant croissant pour l'inclusion.”

F6 imposes common enlargements within a fixed branch at x, precisely the behavior missing in the fork example whose two long segments share an initial germ but end in incomparable upper branches. It is therefore an additional axiom, not a consequence of F1–F5 as previously formulated. Its purpose is global extension within a branch, and that remains distinct from a category of arbitrary topological cuttings.

### A24 — Singular endpoints separate the two F6 formulations

**Source:** test of 156-1/023, first formulation, margin, and “Ou encore” formulation. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “On n'exige pas”; “I∪J⊂K.” **Confidence:** high; a complete verification is in records/segment_counterexamples_memo.md.

### A25 — Regularity of the other endpoints repairs this implication

**Source:** 156-1/023 margin; prolongation at 008–009. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “celles ... qui sont régulières”; “propriété d'extension” (alternative uncertain reading). **Confidence:** high, conditional on the stated regular-end prolongation corollary and interior-F6.

### A26 — Reduced graphs require the nondivisible version of the branch rules

**Source:** 156-1/007 margin; 024–025. **Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “u∈I∖{x}”; “si on n'admet pas condition de divisibilité”; “les axiomes ... sont vides!”
