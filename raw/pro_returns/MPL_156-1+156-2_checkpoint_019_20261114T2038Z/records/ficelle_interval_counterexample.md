# Own-analysis check: a nonstandard ficelle on an interval

**Status:** independently verified counterexample to the unrestricted interval classification in 156-2/011:8; **confidence: high**, from explicit checks of Fic a–d at 004:5–14. This is research after freezing, not an alteration of any source reading.

## Example and verification

Let X=[0,1], A={0,1}, and B={1/2}. Both endpoints in the sense of the ficelle definition are closed, nonopen, nonempty and disjoint, but A is disconnected. The quotient X/A is a circle, and its identified point has a closed conic neighborhood homeomorphic to an interval, with two-point compact base and open interior; X/B is simply X, with the same sort of conic neighborhood at its midpoint. Thus both subsets are regularly immersed in the manuscript's precise sense.

The first connectivity quotient is

\[
(X\setminus A)/B=(0,1)/\{1/2\}\cong(0,1),
\]

because contracting a singleton makes no change. The other is

\[
(X\setminus B)/A
=\bigl([0,1/2)\amalg(1/2,1]\bigr)/(0\sim1)
\cong(-1/2,1/2).
\]

For the latter homeomorphism, map t in [0,1/2) to t and t in (1/2,1] to t−1: the only equal images from the two pieces are 0 and 1, both sent to 0, and the quotient topology is the ordinary topology of the open interval. Hence Fic c holds, and Fic a–d are all satisfied. The two components of U=(0,1/2)∪(1/2,1) each have closure meeting both A and B, so c' holds as well.

This ficelle is not of the marginally asserted form A=[0,α], B=[β,1], even after exchanging A and B: neither a disconnected pair of endpoints nor an interior singleton can be the required opposite terminal subintervals. It is a second, independent effect of allowing disconnected endpoint sets, beyond the disconnected-ambient-space example in `ficelle_disconnected_counterexample.md`.

## Connected endpoints recover the asserted form

Suppose instead that both A and B are connected. Being nonempty closed subsets of [0,1], they are intervals; after exchanging them, write A=[u,v], B=[w,z], with v<w. If u>0, the nonempty component [0,u) of X minus A is disjoint from B and remains separated after B is collapsed, contradicting Fic c; hence u=0. The other connectivity condition similarly forces z=1, yielding precisely A=[0,v], B=[w,1]. These pairs satisfy the axioms when 0≤v<w≤1.

Thus connectedness of both endpoints repairs this classification, but it is a further condition only suggested as “raisonnable” at 011:16, not part of the earlier definition. This analysis does not decide the equivalence relation generated by endpoint inclusions or undo either explicit “Faux” annotation on the same page.

## Four-sentence note suitable for the research register

**Source:** 156-2/011:8, with 004:5–14 and 011:16. **Kind:** own idea / counterexample. **Confidence:** high; both punctured quotients are explicitly open intervals and both contracted endpoints have conic neighborhoods.

The marginal claim “les seules … ficelles sur [0,1]” is too strong for the unrestricted definition: ([0,1],{{0,1},{1/2}}) is a ficelle whose first endpoint set is disconnected. Its contraction at {0,1} gives a circle, contraction at {1/2} leaves an interval, and both connectivity quotients in Fic c are open intervals. If both endpoint sets are required to be connected, writing them as disjoint closed intervals and applying Fic c forces them to be opposite terminal subintervals, recovering the stated classification. This is an analyst's counterexample and conditional repair, not a proposed transcription correction.
