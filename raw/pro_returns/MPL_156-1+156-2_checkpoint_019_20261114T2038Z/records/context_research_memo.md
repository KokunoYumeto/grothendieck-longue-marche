## An order-theoretic consequence relevant to the planning guess

If finite cutting means the finite mutually disjoint collection of 156-2/010:19, it means a finite chain in the lieu poset. With refinement by inclusion, singleton systems {C} and {D} have a common refinement only when C,D can be in the same chain. Thus incomparable lieux prevent directedness of that literal refinement poset; this is a conditional own deduction with **high confidence**, from the stated disjointness/comparability relation at 010:15, not a claim that every enriched notion of refinement must fail.

One concrete test is the reduced ficelle (S¹,{a,b}). Let C and D each have one point on each of the two a-to-b arcs, ordered a–C–D–b on one arc and a–D–C–b on the other. Each is a conic two-point cut locus, but D occupies both sides after cutting along C, so the loci are set-disjoint without being compatible/comparable in the manuscript's sense. This supplies singleton cut systems without a common chain refinement by inclusion; the construction is the analyst's, not an example attributed to the manuscript.
