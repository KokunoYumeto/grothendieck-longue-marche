## Additional recurrent patterns

No additional broad error class is needed beyond the supplied ten categories.
The following recurring combinations deserve more explicit attention in future
runs; their counts are already included under the primary categories above.

1. **A numeral's common horizontal strokes can be mistaken for a completion
   hat.** Compare the distinctly hatted II on 027 with the simply framed III on
   028–029 and IV on 030. The final comparison removed a hat supplied by both
   witnesses on 028. Later graph labels on 033–040 also have shared upper/lower
   strokes, while the V at the right of the inclusion diagram on 039 is
   unframed. Reading “all graph names alike” would erase that exception.
2. **An author's revision can alter only one accent or one position in a
   formula.** The primes in 026 (19), the inverse exponents on 036–039, and the
   prime-before-omega versus prime-after-omega forms on 040 must be examined
   locally. A revision is neither a global substitution rule nor permission to
   restore a cleaner previous state.
3. **Overwritten and reused equation numbers coexist with genuine
   cross-references.** Scan 028 uses (27) twice; 040–041 repeat (75); 041 has
   (75′) and a final overwritten (79); 042 refers to these actual numbers.
   Scan 045 repeats (4), and the margin and main display on 051 both carry (22).
   The conspicuous (75bis) on 036 is retained despite the likely reference to
   (25bis). Sequential numbering cannot decide the ink.
4. **A slanted note may borrow neither its neighbour's words nor its
   grammatical completion.** The beta and lambda clauses on 027 are separate;
   the marginal proposal and following starred text on 028 have different
   deletion states; the left notes on 048 and 050 run beside independent
   main-column phrases. H sometimes merged these streams or replaced them
   with a plausible continuous sentence.
5. **Source inconsistency is especially easy to lose where both witnesses
   understand the mathematics.** Examples include i in omega on 030, psi_0
   on 034, missing tildes on 031 and 040, I in place of the previously used J
   on 046, the written genus convention on 047, and epsilon/omega plus the
   final endpoint list on 051. These are preserved and discussed in the notes,
   rather than silently repaired.
6. **A rough drawing can mix directed, reciprocal and headless paths.** The
   reversed infinity arcs on 028, the individually corrected arcs on 038, the
   headless generator on 040, and reciprocal quarter-circles on 050 show why
   enforcing a symmetric or algebraically expected pattern is unsafe. The
   headless connector in 051 (25) is retained for the same reason.
7. **A connected letter flourish and a detached modifier are different.**
   The upper flourish of G_J on 026–028 and 044–051 belongs to G; the separate
   tilde on G_3 on 029 is real. Likewise, the underlined cyclic-order omega is
   distinguished from the barred and underlined surface-orientation omega
   introduced on 044. The overwritten second occurrence on 050 remains
   doubtful rather than being supplied by that distinction alone.

For counting, related components can share one existing passage or drawing row
when the evidence and divergence are recorded together. The table counts those
recorded locations, not every character or arrow separately. A row's primary
category does not exclude secondary features: a cancelled abbreviated word can
involve cancellation, spelling and placement at once.
