## 1. Scans 048–070: Contractions, small dimensions, and boundary examples

### N048-01 — Stability of a contraction fibre

**Location:** scan 048, upper and lower paragraphs, after the connected-component isomorphism. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: maquette, générisation, fibre, rigidité MD.

The assertion “est stable … ssi les … le sont” needs the half-edges leaving each contracted fibre to become marked flags on that fibre; omitting those flags changes the stability inequality. A proof should sum \(2g_v-2+n_v>0\) over each connected fibre, identify its new genus as the sum of vertex genera plus its first Betti number, and then check the quotient vertex inequality. Scans 093–099 later develop the flag and fibre bookkeeping, so this is a point elaborated within the supplied pages, although the stability calculation is not written here.

### N048-02 — Connected components and what contraction forgets

**Location:** scan 048, first display and following definition of \(G'_\alpha\). **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: pi_0, composante connexe, genre, graphe quotient.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>\pi_0(G')\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\pi_0(G)</code>

The displayed \(\pi_0(G')\simeq\pi_0(G)\) concerns connectedness, whereas the genus carried by a cycle inside a collapsed fibre must move into the weight of the quotient vertex. One can therefore test a proposed definition of contraction on a loop: connected components stay unchanged, the topological cycle disappears, and the total arithmetic genus should remain unchanged only after the vertex weight is increased. This distinguishes the decorated contraction used here from an unweighted graph quotient and anticipates the reconstruction issues on scans 099–101.

### N048-03 — Which subset names the contracted edges?

**Location:** scan 048, opening sentence and fibre definition; compare scan 049, definition of écart. **Kind:** reading question. **Context:** C0; intended terms: B, B prime, C prime, arêtes contractées, générisation.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>tel que $B$ soit l'ens des</code>

The opening identifies \(B\) as the edges that contract to a point, while the subsequent fibre graph uses \(C'\) and describes the complementary branches through \(B'=A'\setminus C'\); scan 049 then defines the contraction rank by \(|C'|\). The relationships among these letters need to be checked before treating every subset symbol as the same contraction datum. Because scan 048 begins mid-argument and the supplied context contains no preceding material, this remains a notation question rather than a justified correction.

### N049-01 — Boolean quotients require a fixed source

**Location:** scan 049, first paragraph, “le nb des générisations … est \(2^{\mu'}\)”. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: maquettes quotients, parties, générisations, isomorphisme.

The Boolean count is literal for subsets of the edge set of a fixed source together with their contraction maps; it need not count abstract isomorphism classes of target graphs. For example, contracting either loop of a genus-zero vertex with two loops gives isomorphic one-loop, genus-one targets, although the two subsets of the original edge set are distinct. Scans 094–100 retain more morphism data, and the endpoint-versus-morphism distinction reappears abstractly on scans 114–118.

### N049-02 — Rank and composition of contractions

**Location:** scan 049, lower paragraph defining “écart”. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: écart, codimension modulaire, composé, parties.

Once “écart” is the number of contracted edges, composable contractions should have additive rank, with the second contracted set pulled back to the surviving edges of the first source. Establishing this explicitly would separate the rank function on the contraction category from the numerical modular dimension, which later uses the same letter \(\delta\) on scan 056. It would also supply the local grading needed by the weighted Boolean categories on scans 114–118.

### N050-01 — The factorial counts labelled contraction orders

**Location:** scan 050, opening paragraph, “égal à \(\delta!\)”. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: factorise, écart 1, delta!, isom près.

The \(\delta!\) assertion follows by ordering the contracted edges when the source and final contraction remain fixed and intermediate isomorphisms commute with those maps. Quotienting additionally by automorphisms of the source can identify different orders, so “à isom. près” needs that precise relative interpretation. This gives a useful test of the chain categories subsequently encoded by the nerve on scans 128–132.

### N050-02 — Local branches versus global stratum closures

**Location:** scan 050, final paragraph, “correspondance biunivoque”. **Kind:** reading question. **Context:** C0; intended terms: sous-multiplicités, fermées, lisses, générisations, normalisation.

The claimed correspondence between \(2^\mu\) contractions and closed smooth submultiplicities requires deciding whether these are local branches, normalized closures, or their images in the global moduli stack. Global images can identify branches and acquire self-intersections, precisely the issue exhibited in scans 069–073 and revisited on scans 134–137. The statement is therefore safest to investigate first in a branch-labelled local chart, without changing the manuscript's use of “inclusion”.

### N050-03 — Label stabilizers and descent

**Location:** scan 050, equation (59). **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: Gamma!, Ker, points marqués, épinglage.

The definition “\(\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{Aut}(\underline G)\to\mathfrak S_I)\)” separates automorphisms fixing individual markings from permutations of the marking set. Any comparison of the two quotient constructions in equations (57)–(58) should retain this kernel before passing to coarse spaces, because its elements may still act on branches or remain as ineffective stabilizers. The elliptic and four-point examples on scans 058–065 provide explicit tests of these two possibilities.

### N051-01 — The deferred normal-crossing argument

**Location:** scan 051, entire paragraph, “on y reviendra par la suite”. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: schémas modulaires formels, croisements normaux, déformation.

The deferred proof needs independent smoothing parameters for the nodes, unobstructed deformation of the pointed stable curve, and a description of how its automorphisms permute those parameters. Deligne and Mumford (1969, Theorem 5.2) establish the smooth-stack and normal-crossing-boundary statement in their stable-curve setting; the manuscript still needs to connect that local geometry to its particular graph-indexed objects and morphisms. Scans 106–137 pursue the corresponding abstract branch formalism, rather than supplying this deformation calculation.

### N052-01 — Zero-dimensional factors and the stability hypothesis

**Location:** scan 052, conditions(a)–(d). **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: dimension modulaire nulle, genre 0, poids marqué, maximal.

The equivalence rests on nonnegativity of every stable vertex contribution \(3g_v-3+n_v\): a zero sum forces each vertex to be rational with three total special flags. This argument fails without stability, so the preceding extension from MD-graphs to arbitrary “maquettes” on scan 049 must not be silently carried into this classification. It also explains why the zero-dimensional factors can be removed from the product decomposition on scans 056–060.

### N052-02 — A point as a stack can retain torsors

**Location:** scan 052, equations (60)–(61), “à isom unique près”. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: courbe standart, G-épinglée, multiplicité classifiante, torsor.

Uniqueness of the graph-pinned object over a base \(S\) does not imply uniqueness after the pinning is forgotten: the classifying object \(B\Gamma\) retains \(\Gamma\)-torsors and their automorphisms. A proof of equation (61) should identify descent of pinnings with that torsor groupoid over arbitrary bases, rather than merely count geometric curves. Scan 055 gives the automorphism statement for an already pinned curve, which supplies the local model but not by itself the full descent argument.

### N053-01 — Exhaustiveness of the rational tree list

**Location:** scan 053, paragraph before figure(64). **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: arbre, bouts, dimension ambiante, nu.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>a au moins trois bouts</code>

The claim that a tree other than a point or segment has at least three ends is correct for the underlying topological tree, whereas a combinatorial path may have several subdivision vertices. The three-vertex path actually drawn for \(\nu=5\) makes this interpretation necessary. An exhaustive proof should combine the leaf bound with the trivalence relation including marked flags, so that subdivision vertices consume the remaining markings.

### N053-02 — Two notions of dimension

**Location:** scan 053, equation (63), “dimension modulaire ambiante”. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: dimension ambiante, dim tot, h_1, codimension.

Here a stratum of modular dimension zero can lie in an ambient moduli space of dimension one or two; the drawings record the difference through the number of edges. Keeping both dimensions in a separate graph invariant would prevent the later small-dimensional classification from being read as a bound on the genus of every graph occurring in a boundary stratum. Scan 060's product cases illustrate why small stratum dimension and small ambient dimension should be tested independently.

### N054-01 — Completeness of the one-cycle enumeration

**Location:** scan 054, both paragraphs and figure(65). **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: h_1=1, cercle, bout, poids marqué.

“On voit” compresses a useful classification argument: with one topological cycle, total marking weight at most two, and rational trivalent decorated vertices, an attached tree consumes at least two markings at its terminal vertex. This leaves either the loop with one marked vertex, the two-vertex cycle, or the loop with a terminal rational component shown on the page. The argument should count loop half-edges twice and distinguish topological type from combinatorial subdivision, as in scan 053.

### N055-01 — Automorphism groups and dihedral conventions

**Location:** scan 055, tables(65)–(66). **Kind:** reading question. **Context:** C0; intended terms: D_4, D_2, Gamma, automorphismes.

The labels “\(D_4\)” and “\(D_2\)” should be checked against the actual permutations of flags, since competing conventions index a dihedral group by its order or by the number of polygon vertices. The first two-ended rational graph has \((\mathfrak S_2\times\mathfrak S_2)\rtimes\mathfrak S_2\) of order eight, whereas the two-edge cycle has independent vertex and edge swaps, giving order four. These calculations supply a convention check while leaving the written group labels intact.

### N055-02 — Canonicity after forgetting a pinning

**Location:** scan 055, final paragraph, “can. isom. à \(\Gamma\)”. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: base connexe, G-épinglée, canoniquement, automorphismes.

A chosen graph pinning identifies the automorphism group of the underlying standard curve with \(\Gamma\), but a different pinning conjugates that identification. For a curve obtained by nontrivial descent, the automorphism group scheme may instead be an inner form of the constant group, with its sections depending on the torsor. The precise claim should therefore be proved for the pinned object stated here and distinguished from the arbitrary object of \(B\Gamma\) considered on scan 052.

### N057-02 — Trivial graph action does not remove elliptic inertia

**Location:** scan 057, figure(70), elliptic row. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: Gamma=Gamma!, M_1,1, automorphismes, groupe trivial.

The elementary one-pointed genus-one graph has trivial graph automorphism group, while the elliptic curve it parametrizes still has automorphisms fixing its origin. Thus “\(\Gamma=\Gamma^!=\{e\}\)” concerns combinatorics and cannot identify \(\widehat M_{1,1}\) with its coarse \(j\)-line. ### N058-01 — The proposed realization of all subgroups of \(\mathfrak S_4\)

**Location:** scan 058, opening paragraph, “n'importe quel sous-groupe … sauf erreur”. **Kind:** conjecture. **Context:** C0; intended terms: sous-groupe S_4, image Gamma, sauf erreur, sommet distingué.

The expectation asks which permutation groups on the four flags at the unique four-valent moving vertex can occur as images of automorphism groups of stable decorated graphs. A proof must respect the requirement that all other vertices have modular dimension zero, as well as the permitted permutations of external markings. The three one-edge examples on scans 058–059 realize only particular subgroups and do not settle the general assertion.

### N058-02 — A construction strategy for prescribed flag symmetries

**Location:** scan 058, same expectation. **Kind:** own idea. **Context:** C0; intended terms: réalisation, automorphismes de graphes, quatre drapeaux, sous-groupes.

My proposed test is to attach finite rigid graph gadgets to a four-flag core and encode an \(H\)-invariant relation on those flags, then check that its full induced automorphism group is exactly the chosen \(H\leq\mathfrak S_4\). Confidence is medium because attaching four individually distinguished trees realizes some obvious stabilizers, but a general subgroup may require correlations between the attachments and trivalent replacement gadgets. This is a construction problem suggested by “doit pouvoir”, not a claim of a proved realization theorem or of novelty.

### N058-03 — Permutation image versus effective action

**Location:** scan 058, map(72). **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: S_4, action, U_0,3, Klein.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>elle se fait à travers</code>

Even a full \(\mathfrak S_4\) image on the four labels acts on the cross-ratio parameter through \(\mathfrak S_3\), with the Klein four group acting trivially on the base. Consequently the realization question and the effective-action question are different, and the quotient stack retains information lost by the latter. The factor-of-four tension in the Euler-characteristic calculation on scans 062–063 is a concrete consequence.

### N059-02 — Five products do not determine five quotient stacks

**Location:** scan 059, final paragraph and lower margin. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: trois cas, produits, groupe modulaire, dimension 2.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>Cela fait donc trois cas</code>

The five possibilities classify products of moving moduli factors before quotienting by the graph automorphism group. ### N060-01 — A factor-swap counterexample to the last action row

**Location:** scan 060, equation (74), final row “\(\Gamma\) opère trivialement”. **Kind:** own idea. **Context:** C0; intended terms: M_1,1 produit, échange des facteurs, Gamma, graphe symétrique.

My counterexample to the unrestricted last-row claim is the stable graph with two genus-one vertices joined by one edge and no external legs: both vertices have type \((1,1)\), and its graph involution exchanges the two \(\widehat M_{1,1}\) factors. For a pair of nonisomorphic elliptic curves this interchange is not naturally the identity on the product moduli stack, although each individual factor has only one attaching label. Confidence is high for \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) as defined on scan 050; an unstated restriction to automorphisms fixing both moving vertices would change the claim.

### N060-02 — Wreath products retain the block structure

**Location:** scan 060, NB below equation (74). **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: produit demi-direct, S_8, blocs, S_4.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>\text{prod. }\frac12\text{ direct}</code>

The factorization through \((\mathfrak S_4\times\mathfrak S_4)\rtimes\mathfrak S_2\) correctly retains the two blocks of four flags inside \(\mathfrak S_8\). ### N060-03 — The four elementary cases as a reconstruction expectation

**Location:** scan 060, final paragraph, “J'ai l'impression”. **Kind:** conjecture. **Context:** C0; intended terms: quatre cas élémentaires, pi_1 discrets, cas général, Teichmüller.

The expectation is that understanding the discrete fundamental groups in the four cases of modular dimension at most two gives the key to all genera. A precise version needs objects, gluing maps, generators, and coherence relations, rather than just four abstract group presentations. Bakalov and Kirillov (2000, Theorems 4.24 and 5.1) provide connected, simply connected marking complexes, while their introduction explicitly leaves the full tower-reconstruction implication to additional work; scans 075–105 develop related manuscript-specific functorial data.

### N060-04 — A concrete coherence test for the expectation

**Location:** scan 060, same final paragraph. **Kind:** own idea. **Context:** C0; intended terms: relations, recollement, dimension 2, cohérence.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>donnerait la clef</code>

My proposed test is to build the graph-pinned groupoid first, use one-dimensional strata for elementary moves, and attach two-dimensional relations while retaining stabilizers and the interchange of disjoint moves. Confidence is medium because the manuscript supplies the small-dimensional pieces but has not yet proved that the resulting comparison functor is fully faithful and essentially surjective. Hatcher and Thurston (1980, Theorem 1.1) give a relevant cut-system comparison, not a proof of this particular construction or its arithmetic analogue.

### N061-02 — Three partitions and a coordinate-free cross-ratio

**Location:** scan 061, diagram (76) and equation (77). **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: partitions(2,2), trois points, P^1, U_0,3.

The “trois partitions” of a four-element marking set give the boundary points without choosing a cross-ratio coordinate. ### N062-01 — The universal four-pointed curve left unwritten

**Location:** scan 062, opening paragraph, “Je vais pas le faire ici”. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: courbe modulaire, quatre sections, S_4, X chapeau.

The omitted family can be tested by starting with \(\mathbf P^1_x\times\mathbf P^1_\lambda\), taking the sections \(0,1,\infty,\lambda\), and blowing up their three collision points over \(\lambda=0,1,\infty\). The resulting fibres have two rational components with three special points each, so the remaining task is to verify the lifted label permutations and their compatibility with the base action. This explicit construction would also check the quotient-family assertion, which is stronger than identifying its coarse parameter line.

### N062-02 — An effective versus ineffective Euler-characteristic mismatch

**Location:** scan 062, last display and NB in the margin. **Kind:** reading question. **Context:** C0; intended terms: caractéristique EP, -1/24, -1/6, S_4.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>On peut écrire directement</code>

The visible summands \(-1+1/3+1/2\) equal \(-1/6\), whereas the margin explicitly gives \(-1/24\) by division by \(|\mathfrak S_4|\). Over the complex numbers these are respectively the stabilizer-weighted Euler characteristics for the effective \(\mathfrak S_3\) quotient and for the full \(\mathfrak S_4\) quotient with its generic Klein-four inertia. Thus a missing factor \(1/4\), rather than merely an arithmetic slip in the final denominator, is a concrete explanation to test; scan 063 uses the latter convention.

### N062-03 — One boundary orbit still has a stabilizer

**Location:** scan 062, paragraph after diagram (78), “un seul pt à l'infini”. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Context:** C0; intended terms: point à l'infini, trajectoire, stabilisateur, D_4.

Transitivity on the three partitions gives one boundary orbit, but its stabilizer in \(\mathfrak S_4\) has order eight. Consequently the stack boundary has the form \(B D_4\) with the order-eight convention, rather than a point with trivial inertia, and its weighted contribution \(1/8\) matches scan 063. ### N063-01 — What is the “suitable” Grothendieck group?

**Location:** scan 063, first paragraph and equation (79), “groupe de Grothendieck convenable”. **Kind:** reading question. **Context:** C0; intended terms: groupe de Grothendieck, augmentation, Tate, classes de stacks.

The coefficients \(1/4,1/12,1/8\) presuppose a realization that weights finite stabilizers by reciprocal order; that is additional structure, not an identity in every Grothendieck ring of stacks. Ekedahl (2009a) shows, for example, that classes of symmetric-group classifying stacks are trivial in the ring he studies, which distinguishes that ring from the manuscript's fractional rule. One should specify the target group, scissors relation, permitted denominators, and augmentation before testing the integral corrections on scan 065; Ekedahl (2009b) supplies a comparison framework, not an identification.

### N063-02 — The smooth elliptic gerbe and its band

**Location:** scan 063, equation (78) and following paragraph. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Context:** C0; intended terms: gerbe, groupe constant, plus ou moins 1, quatre points.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>une gerbe</code>

On the smooth locus with \(2\) invertible, the suggested construction is to quotient an elliptic curve by inversion, retaining the image of the origin as the distinguished one of four branch points. A proof of the gerbe assertion should identify the automorphism kernel, show local existence of the double cover, and verify descent, without assuming a globally chosen Legendre equation. Fulton and Olsson (2007, §5.1) give a directly checked characteristic-three local Legendre model with the corresponding order-two kernel, while the compactified assertion needs the separate test in N063-03.

### N063-03 — A boundary obstruction to the compactified gerbe assertion

**Location:** scan 063, the hats in equation (78), compared with scan 064, equation (79). **Kind:** own idea. **Context:** C0; intended terms: gerbe compactifiée, point à l'infini, automorphismes, admissible.

**Brief manuscript quote:** <code>qui fait de $\widehat M_{1,1}$ une gerbe</code>

My diagnostic is that a geometric cusp of the usual \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,1}\) has automorphism group of order two, while a boundary point of \([\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,4}/\mathfrak S_3]\) also has stabilizer of order two; a gerbe with order-two band over the latter would instead require an order-four stabilizer upstairs. The same tension appears numerically over \(\mathbf C\): scan 064 gives \(5/12\) for the compactified elliptic weighted Euler characteristic, whereas half of \(2/6\) is \(1/6\). Confidence is high under the ordinary stack meanings of the hats; the open gerbe can still be correct, but extension to the boundary needs a changed target, a root construction, or a restricted assertion.

### N064-01 — The exceptional elliptic automorphism orders

### N064-02 — The two exceptional sections meet nonreducedly
