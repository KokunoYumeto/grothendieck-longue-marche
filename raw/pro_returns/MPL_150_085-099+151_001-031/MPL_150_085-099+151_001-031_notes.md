Confidence: **high**, because the parameterized map, its endpoint values, and the monotonicity calculation are explicit.

**Context comparison:** **adds an elementary map-level test** — C147, scan 190, and present 150 scan 088 discuss isotopy and local triviality without this example. **Register:** WG2 B15 (unchecked); no novelty is claimed.

### K06 — Shiota II, Theorem 4, p. 13, and Statement 9, p. 19 — unpursued implication

[Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.5489v3#page=13) uses standard semilinear representatives for noncompact finite marked families; [Statement 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.5489v3#page=19) isotopes their definable homeomorphisms to semilinear ones. This representative change precedes the last comparison, and K07 gives an explicit example.

### K07 — Shiota II, §4; an explicit noncompact representative change — own idea

The semialgebraic homeomorphism
\[
u:\mathbb R\longrightarrow(0,1),\qquad
u(x)=\frac{1+x/(1+|x|)}2,\qquad
u^{-1}(y)=\frac{2y-1}{1-|2y-1|}
\]
gives the representative change explicitly. A finite piecewise-affine map on `ℝ` is affine on its last ray, and boundedness forces that affine slope to vanish, so no such bounded map can remain injective on that ray. Thus these two representatives are related by the displayed semialgebraic homeomorphism, while a semilinear homeomorphism between the very same representatives is obstructed by the ray calculation. Confidence: **high**, because the inverse formula and the final-ray argument prove both assertions directly.

**Context comparison:** **adds** — C147, scan 190, and present 150 scan 088 supply the isotopy-comparison question; the explicit representative map makes the noncompact scope of K06 concrete. **Register:** WG2 B15 (unchecked).
