## 1. Actual groups and the correct finite cover

Use the marked B125 group

`A=R *_K Q`,  `Q=K semidirect_eta C2(j)`.

Here R is the entire regional orbifold group, K is the actual common neck, and eta is the certified based reflection. Let `mu:A->C2` kill R and send j to one. This is the mirror character, distinct from the original ordinary character. Its kernel is

`D=R *_K R'`,  `R'=jRj`,

where `j u j=eta(u)` for every u in K.

This is an exact discrete presentation. The two cosets `{1,j}` give two R vertices and one Q-kernel vertex K. The latter is joined to both R vertices with an isomorphism onto its edge group, so collapsing it gives the displayed amalgam. Equivalently, start with two copies R,R', impose their actual neck identification, and let j exchange them using eta; the resulting semidirect product by C2 has exactly the original presentation of A. Thus D has index two in A. There is no extra HNN stable letter in this mirror-only cover. Such a letter occurs after additionally restricting the R vertices to the original ordinary cover, which is a different presentation.

## 2. Proper full completion of this cover

B125 proves full induced topology of R0 in A0. Since A0 has finite index in A, R0 also has its full topology in A. This implies full topology of the entire R in A: given a finite-index normal subgroup N of R, first refine N intersect R0 by an ambient finite kernel, and intersect that kernel with the kernel of the original parity character. Its intersection with R is then contained in N. The same statement holds for R'=jRj. The actual K has full topology in R by the established retraction and finite-index comparison.

D has finite index in A, so D has its full topology in A. Consequently the two maps from the completions of R and R' to the completion of D are injective. The finite-target universal property of the discrete amalgam identifies

`Dhat = Rhat amalg_(Khat) Rhat'`

as a proper full profinite amalgam. This conclusion uses the actual cofinal vertex topology of B125. It is not inferred merely from a drawing of a subgraph or from a discrete subgroup intersection.

## 4. Applicable primary subgroup theorem

Lopes and Zalesskii (2025, Corollary 2.18) prove that, in a proper free profinite amalgam, the closed group generated by one subgroup of each vertex is their free profinite product when both subgroups have trivial intersection with the amalgamated edge. The complete preceding Lemma 2.17 and the corollary proofs were opened and read in the publisher's full text for this application.

Apply that result to the two closed groups `<k>bar` and `j Shat j` in Section 2. It gives

`closure<k,jSj> = Zhat amalg Fhat5 = Fhat6`.

The discrete group generated by these same factors is `<k>*jSj=F6` by the already proved ordinary amalgam normal form. The marked map from its completion is exactly the free profinite product map just proved injective. Its image is its actual closure. Hence this actual pre-cross F6 has its full induced topology in D and A, with the six specified source words.

The earlier carrier application of the same corollary was correctly rejected because its smaller edge was the nontrivial procyclic group <P5>. That obstruction is absent here: the two present source factors both meet Khat trivially. No general profinite Kurosh theorem is invoked.
