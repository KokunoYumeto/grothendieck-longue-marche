\statement{B.88}{Refuted}{Source membership of the diagonal-normalized zero word, refuted by an actual quotient of order 64}
Use the actual source-adapted marking of B.83--B.86. In particular, z is
the actual source meridian at \(v=0,w=2\), while a and b are the
transported primitive meridians of \(v=1\) and \(vw=1\), respectively.
The proposed membership assertion
\[
                  Z'=a^{-1}b^{-1}zba\in H
\tag{B.88.1}
\]
is false. The same actual word does not belong to the actual compact
source image \(\overline H\) in the full completion.
There is an actual epimorphism onto
\(\operatorname{UT}_3(\mathbb F_2)\times
  \operatorname{UT}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), of order 64, which sends H onto
the diagonal subgroup and sends \(Z'\) outside that diagonal.
This refutes only the indicated source-word membership used in the
possible zero-direction extension of B.86. It does not refute the
marginal kernel equality.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Two actual based maps agreeing on the source.}\par
Put \(M=\mathbb P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}\). The maps
\[
 f_1(v,w)=v,\qquad f_2(v,w)=\frac{v(w-1)}{1-v}
\tag{B.88.2}
\]
are defined from the actual both-family filled surface \(Y_b\) of
B.87 to M, and hence also from the original Y. Indeed their possible
zeros and poles are among the removed divisors, and
\[
                 f_2-1=\frac{vw-1}{1-v}.
\tag{B.88.3}
\]
At the literal base point \(P=(399/200,1/200)\), both maps take the
value \(399/200\). On the source \(v+w=2\), they are identically
equal. Thus the two induced based homomorphisms agree on the entire
actual source image, not merely on its peripheral conjugacy classes.
The map from the source to M given by v is the inclusion of M with
finitely many further punctures removed, so it is surjective on
fundamental groups.

Choose the usual based free peripheral generators \(x_0,x_1\) of
\(\pi_1(M,399/200)\), and let
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 D=\operatorname{UT}_3(\mathbb F_2)
   =\{(r,s,t):r,s,t\in\mathbb F_2\},\\
 (r,s,t)(r',s',t')=(r+r',s+s',t+t'+rs').
 \end{gathered}
\tag{B.88.4}
\]
Map \(x_0\) to \((1,0,0)\) and \(x_1\) to \((0,1,0)\).
Write \(q_i\) for the resulting actual homomorphism induced by
\(f_i\), and \(q=(q_1,q_2)\). Then
\[
                         q(H)=\operatorname{diag}(D).
\tag{B.88.5}
\]
The first two coordinates of D are the mod-two winding numbers about
0 and 1. All unspecified dependence on whiskers is in the third
coordinate.

\emph{The actual peripheral coordinates.}\par
A primitive meridian of \(v=1\) maps under \(f_1\) to a 1-meridian.
Under \(f_2\) it maps to a meridian about infinity: both \(f_2\)
and \(f_2-1\) have a simple pole. A meridian of \(vw=1\) is killed
by \(f_1\), which extends across that divisor locally into M, whereas
\(f_2-1\) has a simple zero there. Finally z maps under both based
maps to the same conjugate of a primitive 0-meridian. Consequently the
following table is exact in the abelianization of D:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\operatorname{ab}(q_1)&\operatorname{ab}(q_2)\\ \hline
 a&(0,1)&(1,1)\\
 b&(0,0)&(0,1)\\
 z&(1,0)&(1,0).
\end{array}
\tag{B.88.6}
\]
Moreover \(q_1(b)=1\) literally, and \(q_1(z)=q_2(z)\) literally.
Changing a primitive peripheral orientation has no effect on the table,
since the coefficients are in \(\mathbb F_2\). Ambient transporting
paths change only the allowed third coordinates. This is why no
unrecorded common-whisker choice enters the detector.

For completeness, q is onto the stated order 64 group. It contains the
whole diagonal by (B.88.5). Multiplying \(q(a)\) by a suitable diagonal
element gives an element of \(\{1\}\times D\) with second-factor
abelian coordinates \((1,0)\). The element \(q(b)\) belongs to
\(\{1\}\times D\) and has second-factor coordinates \((0,1)\).
Any two such elements generate D: their commutator is its nontrivial
central element. Hence \(\{1\}\times D\subseteq q(\pi_1(Y))\),
and the diagonal then gives the full direct product.

\emph{The strict separation.}\par
Write \(g=ba\) and \(Z=q_1(z)=q_2(z)=(1,0,t_0)\).
Table (B.88.6) gives
\[
 \operatorname{ab}(q_1(g))=(0,1),\qquad
 \operatorname{ab}(q_2(g))=(1,0).
\tag{B.88.7}
\]
In D, conjugating \((1,0,t_0)\) by \((r,s,t)\), with the
convention \(h^{-1}Zh\), gives \((1,0,t_0+s)\).
Therefore
\[
 q_1(Z')=(1,0,t_0+1),\qquad
 q_2(Z')=(1,0,t_0).
\tag{B.88.8}
\]
The two coordinates differ by the nonidentity central element of D.
Thus \(q(Z')\notin\operatorname{diag}(D)=q(H)\), proving the
discrete nonmembership. The actual finite homomorphism q extends
continuously to the full profinite completion; the image of the compact
source is still exactly the finite diagonal. The same certificate
therefore proves \(Z'\notin\overline H\), without any assumption
about induced subgroup topology or the completion of an intersection.

Equivalently, before reducing modulo 2, the1-winding of
\(f_{2*}(ba)f_{1*}(ba)^{-1}\) is
\(\epsilon_b-2\epsilon_a\), where the primitive signed meridian
orientations \(\epsilon_a,\epsilon_b\) are each \(1\) or \(-1\).
It is always odd. The finite calculation above detects this directly
and avoids any need to import a full profinite centralizer theorem.

Both maps (B.88.2) factor through \(Y_b\). The resulting detector

The negative conclusion concerns exactly (B.88.1) and its actual
compact counterpart.
\end{proof}
