\emph{B.175.7. A finite separator without an efficiency assumption.}
Use \(\overline K\) from \eqref{eq:B175-finite-edge-separation} and form
\[

       (\overline K,\overline S,\mathrm{id};D),
 \qquad

\]

\(\overline c\notin\overline L\), including the prefactors in
\eqref{eq:B175-actual-prefactors}.
The retraction \(\overline B\to\overline K\), with \(D\mapsto1\),
is injective on its finite vertex group. Its kernel acts freely on
the Bass--Serre tree, so is a free normal subgroup of finite index.
It is finitely generated. The subgroup \(\overline L\) is finitely
generated as well, since its two displayed vertex subgroups are finite.

Here the needed subgroup separation can be proved directly.
In a finitely generated free group, take the finite folded core of the
Schreier covering of a finitely generated subgroup and add the path of
a prescribed nonmember. Its endpoint differs from the basepoint.
For each generator, pair the missing incoming and outgoing edges;
their numbers agree. This completes the finite graph to a covering
without identifying its existing vertices. The resulting finite-index
subgroup contains the original subgroup and excludes the nonmember.
For a group \(B\) with a finite-index normal free subgroup \(F_0\)
and a finitely generated subgroup \(A\), first use \(B/F_0\) if the
element is outside \(AF_0\). Otherwise write it as \(af\), where
\(f\in F_0\setminus(A\cap F_0)\).
The intersection is finitely generated because it has finite index
in \(A\). Choose a finite-index subgroup \(V_0\le F_0\) containing
\(A\cap F_0\) but not \(f\), by the preceding construction.
The core of \(V_0\) in \(B\) gives a finite quotient separating
\(af\) from \(A\): an equality \(af=a'n\), with \(n\) in that
core, would put \(f\) in \(V_0\).

Apply this argument to obtain a finite quotient \(Q_0\) of
\(\overline B\) in which the image \(c_0\) lies outside the image
\(L_0\) of \(\overline L\). In the finite-vertex amalgam
\[
                    Z_0=Q_0*_{L_0}(L_0\times C_{2,R}),
\]
the word \([c_0,R]\) is reduced and nonidentity.
The map \(Z_0\to Q_0\times C_2\) is injective on both finite
vertices, so its kernel is again finite-index free. A nontrivial
element survives a finite quotient: use this finite quotient if its
image is nontrivial, and otherwise separate it in the free kernel
by the preceding covering construction and take the ambient core.
Thus some finite quotient of \(Z_0\), and hence of \(G_\square\),
has nontrivial image of \(w_*\).

All source relations and all node translates hold in this finite
target. This argument does not assert residual finiteness or full
subgroup topology for the original infinite centralizer extensions.
It does not enumerate the generally much larger characteristic

Pull its kernel back to \(\Gamma _0^*\) and take its core in
\(\Gamma _0\). Since \(\Gamma _0^*\) has index two, this gives an
ambient finite quotient still detecting \(w_*\). This proves the
