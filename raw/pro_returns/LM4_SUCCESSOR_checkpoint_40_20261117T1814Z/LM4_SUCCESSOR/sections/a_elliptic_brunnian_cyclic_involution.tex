\statement{A.127}{Proved}{The entire elliptic joint-kernel obstruction and its actual corrected involution}
At the actual full-GT negative-involution scope of A.121--A.126,
let \(\mathcal B\) be the full closed kernel of all three based
forgettings on the pure derived group. The whole cyclic obstruction
space is canonically a pointed compact copy of the full two-generator
derived group. The actual elliptic involution induces the corrected
reflection printed below. The specified canonical cyclic defect has
parameter \(b_0^{-1}\beta_2(a_u^{-1})a_u\), whose corrected
\(C_2\)-class is \([b_0^{-1}]\). These statements retain the entire
joint kernel and the original unique derived transporters; none
asserts that the specified parameter, boundary defect or original CM
class vanishes.
\begin{proof}
\emph{A.127.1. Actual groups, conventions and the joint quotient}

Let G be the full completion of the actual reduced three-strand elliptic braid group, and P its pure subgroup. Write \(P'\) for its closed derived subgroup. In the retained marking put

\[
 K=[Y,X]=\sigma\tau^2\sigma,\quad C=\sigma\tau,\quad
 L=C^{-1},\quad\Pi_0=LY,\quad
 \rho=\operatorname{Ad}(C),\quad \rho_0=\operatorname{Ad}(\Pi_0).
\]

The base cubic has exact order three. Let E be the full free profinite group on X,Y and \(E'\) its closed derived subgroup. The actual two-strand half-twist induces

\[
 j(X)=X^{-1}K^{-1}=YX^{-1}Y^{-1},\quad j(Y)=KY^{-1},\quad
 j(K)=K,\quad j^2=\operatorname{Ad}(K).
\]

Define the involution

\[
 \theta=\operatorname{Ad}(Y^{-1})j.
\]

Thus theta sends X to X inverse and Y to X Y inverse X inverse. In the actual free basis (X,YX inverse), it inverts both generators. The literal identities are

\[
 \theta^2=1,\qquad j\theta=\operatorname{Ad}(Y),\qquad
 j^{-1}\operatorname{Ad}(Y)=\theta.
\tag{A.127.1}
\]

The three based deletion maps are the exact maps pi3, pi1=pi3 rho inverse and pi2=j inverse pi3 rho. The previous whole-generator proof gives

\[
 \pi_1\rho=\pi_3,\qquad \pi_2\rho=j\pi_1,\qquad
 \pi_3\rho=j\pi_2.
\tag{A.127.2}
\]

On the actual pure meridians
\[
 a=\sigma^2,\quad b=\tau^2,\quad
 m=\tau^{-1}\sigma^2\tau=\sigma\tau^2\sigma^{-1},
 \qquad \rho(a)=b,\quad\rho(b)=m,\quad\rho(m)=a.
\]
These are literal group identities. The based table used here is the
corrected complete table of A.126; the three deletion identities above
are unchanged by that literal-row correction.

It also proves the \emph{entire} continuous surjection

\[
 q=(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3):P'\longrightarrow Q=(E')^3.
\tag{A.127.3}
\]

For clarity, its surjectivity uses the three actual pair meridians: their images are (K,1,1), (1,K,1), and (1,1,K). Each meridian lies in \(P'\); conjugation by the whole pure group gives the full closed normal closure of K in its indicated E coordinate. That closure is \(E'\). Compactness makes these images closed, so all three independent coordinates occur.

Define the actual closed joint kernel

\[
 \mathcal B=\ker(q:P'\to Q).
\tag{A.127.4}
\]

This is the full kernel retained in the argument; it is not replaced by selected meridian relators or a finite family of words.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{A.127.2. Exact cyclic action and the complete cohomology space}

Because rho0=rho inverse followed by Ad(Y), equations (A.127.1)--(A.127.2) give the action induced on Q:

\[
 R(t_1,t_2,t_3)=(\theta(t_2),\theta(t_3),t_1).
\tag{A.127.5}
\]

Indeed pi1(Y)=1 and pi2(Y)=pi3(Y)=Y. The first two coordinates of rho inverse Ad(Y) are therefore j inverse Ad(Y), and the third is the first coordinate unchanged. In particular R cubed is the identity, and B is rho0-stable. Its fixed subgroup is exactly

\[
 Q^R=\{\iota(t)=(t,\theta(t),t):t\in E'\}\cong E'.
\tag{A.127.6}
\]

A.125 proves at this actual scope

\[
 (P')^{\rho_0}=1,\qquad H^1(C_3,P';\rho_0)=\{*\}.
\tag{A.127.7}
\]

Here a C3 cocycle is an element z with z rho0(z) rho0 squared(z)=1; equivalent cocycles differ by z becoming n inverse z rho0(n). For t in \(E'\), choose any g in \(P'\) with q(g)=iota(t). Then g inverse rho0(g) lies in B. Define

\[
 \partial(t)=[g^{-1}\rho_0(g)]\in H^1(C_3,\mathcal B;\rho_0).
\tag{A.127.8}
\]

The map is independent of the lift. Replacing g by gn, n in B, changes the cocycle by n inverse z rho0(n).

It is onto: a B-valued cocycle is a \(P'\)-coboundary by (A.127.7), say g inverse rho0(g); its quotient image forces q(g) to be R-fixed and hence of the form (A.127.6). It is one-to-one: if the two cocycles are B-equivalent, one may replace the first lift by its product with an element of B so that the cocycles become equal. Equality of g1 inverse rho0(g1) and g2 inverse rho0(g2) makes g2 g1 inverse rho0-fixed. By (A.127.7), g1=g2 and their quotient parameters agree.

The topology does not require a continuous choice of g. The compact space

\[
 \{(t,g)\in E'\times P':q(g)=\iota(t)\}
\]

maps continuously onto \(E'\) and to the cocycle-orbit space. The latter map is constant on the fibers of the former, so it descends continuously. Continuous cocycles form a closed compact set, and their orbit equivalence relation under the compact coefficient group is closed. Thus their orbit space is compact Hausdorff. The continuous bijection (A.127.8) is consequently a homeomorphism:

\[
 \boxed{H^1(C_3,\mathcal B;\rho_0)\cong E'}
\tag{A.127.9}
\]

as pointed compact spaces, with t=1 corresponding to the neutral class. This is not a claim that the pointed cohomology set has its usual group structure; the parameterization transports one if desired, but no such operation is used.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{A.127.3. Based half-braid actions and the actual involution}

Now fix the original negative full-GT involution, its original parameter phi and its canonical norm word u. Let Theta be its actual elliptic involution on G, and T its actual restriction to the two-strand pure group E. Put

\[
 \Phi=\phi(X,K),\quad F_b=\phi(\sigma^2,\tau^2).
\]

The complete actual section formulas are

\[
 \begin{aligned}

 T(Y)&=K^{-1}j(\Phi)Y\Phi^{-1},& T(K)&=K^{-1},\\
 \Theta(\sigma)&=F_b\sigma^{-1}F_b^{-1},&
 \Theta(\tau)&=\tau^{-1}.&&
 \end{aligned}
\tag{A.127.10}
\]

The two-strand formulas themselves imply, by substitution on X,Y,

\[
 Tj=j^{-1}T.
\tag{A.127.11}
\]

For example, Tj(X) and j inverse T(X) both equal
K inverse j(Phi) (YX Y inverse) j(Phi) inverse K. For Y both equal
K inverse Phi Y inverse j(Phi) inverse K. Thus no unbased naturality or extracted forgetting table is needed for (A.127.11).

Here is a complete convenient verification that all three based deletions commute with Theta. On joint tuples the two actual half-braid conjugations act as

\[
 \operatorname{Ad}(\sigma):(t_1,t_2,t_3)\mapsto(t_2,t_1,j(t_3)),
\qquad
 \operatorname{Ad}(\tau):(t_1,t_2,t_3)\mapsto(j(t_1),t_3,t_2).
\tag{A.127.12}

For the first formula use the generating set X,Y,X2,Y2,a= sigma squared,b= tau squared. Its first four conjugates are respectively X2 a inverse, a Y2, a X, Y a inverse; a is fixed and b maps to A13. The whole deletion table proves every coordinate. The second formula follows by composing the inverse first action with (A.127.2). In particular the full joint kernel of the pure group is stable under both half-braids; its intersection B with \(P'\) is normal in G.

The word Fb has all three deletion images one. Its conjugates therefore do also. For Theta(X2), use Theta(X2)=Ad(Theta sigma)(Theta X) times Theta(a). Formula (A.127.12) makes its tuple

\[
 (T(X),1,j^{-1}(T(X))K^{-1})=(T(X),1,T(X)^{-1}),

where the equality follows from (A.127.11) applied to j(X)=X inverse K inverse. Similarly Theta(Y2) has tuple

\[
 \pi_i\Theta=T\pi_i\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{A.127.13}
\]

Consequently B is Theta-stable as well. In this proof one may first use the full joint kernel on P to justify discarding the half-braid conjugates of Fb, and then restrict to its intersection B with \(P'\); no circular stability assumption is made.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{A.127.4. The corrected involution on the entire cyclic cohomology space}

Let h1 be the uniquely normalized actual derived transporter

\[
 \Theta(\Pi_0)=h_1\Pi_0h_1^{-1},\qquad

\tag{A.127.14}
\]

Its existence, uniqueness, derived membership and norm are A.124--A.125. Define

\[
 \Theta_0=\operatorname{Ad}(h_1^{-1})\Theta.
\tag{A.127.15}
\]

This is an involution, it preserves B, and it commutes with rho0. Indeed it fixes Pi0, and its square is the inner action of h1 inverse Theta(h1 inverse)=1. It therefore acts on both sides of (A.127.9).

Write e=pi1(h1). Below we verify pi3(h1)=e. Equivariance of the boundary map follows from applying Theta0 to an arbitrary lift in (A.127.8). Its action on the parameter t is exactly

\[
 t\longmapsto e^{-1}T(t)e.
\tag{A.127.16}
\]

This is an actual corrected reflection, not the simultaneous inversion theta. In particular T has abelianization diag(-1,1), whereas theta has abelianization minus the identity. They cannot be interchanged in a cohomology calculation.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{A.127.5. Base parameter and the original canonical common word}

Set beta2=Ad(Phi inverse)T. The original canonical norm gives

\[
 U=u(X,K),\qquad T(U)=U\Phi^{-1}.
\tag{A.127.17}

Applying T again gives T(Phi)=Phi inverse. Thus beta2 is an involution. The exact identity

\[
 \Phi^{-1}T(Y^{-1})K^{-1}=Y^{-1}j(\Phi^{-1})

\[
 \beta_2\theta=\theta\beta_2.
\tag{A.127.18}

To determine the three images of h1, left-multiply (A.127.14) by C. Modulo the full joint kernel, C Theta(L) equals K: this follows at once from (A.127.10), since every image of Fb is one and C tau sigma= sigma tau squared sigma=K. Consequently the three images of C Theta(Pi0) are

\[

Apply the same two based equations that gave the common-word description of \(p_w\) in the earlier note. There is a unique b0 in \(E'\) such that

\[
 q(h_1)=(\Phi b_0,\Phi\theta(b_0),\Phi b_0).

The parameter is derived because h1 is derived and Phi is a commutator-word evaluation. Equation (A.127.13) and the actual norm in (A.127.14) give

\[
 \beta_2(b_0)b_0=1.
\tag{A.127.20}

The norm in the second coordinate follows from the same equation by (A.127.18). Formula (A.127.16) can therefore also be written as Ad(b0 inverse) beta2. It is an involution because of (A.127.20).

For the actual canonical w=u, let \(p_u\) be the unique derived cubic transporter. The completely proved based-forgetting result is

 q(p_u)=(U a_u,U\theta(a_u),U a_u),\qquad a_u\in E'.

The actual transporter of Theta(\(\Pi_u\)) from \(\Pi_u\) is

 h_u=\Theta(p_u)h_1p_u^{-1}.

Substituting (A.127.17)--(A.127.21), in their displayed order, gives

The middle coordinate uses precisely (A.127.18). The norm beta2(tu)tu=1 is automatic from (A.127.20), for \emph{every} possible \(a_u\) in \(E'\). Explicitly,

\[
 \beta_2(t_u)t_u
 =a_u b_0^{-1}\beta_2(a_u)^{-1}
          \beta_2(a_u)b_0a_u^{-1}=1.

Thus the actual norm does not supply the missing equation \(a_u\) theta(\(a_u\))=1. Even if that different equation were proved, triviality of H1 for theta would supply a coboundary, not the equality \(a_u\)=1. The unique original \(p_u\) and its full joint-kernel coordinate remain indispensable.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{A.127.6. An actual original Brunnian cyclic cocycle, with its exact parameter}

\[
 B_u=u(\sigma^2,\tau^2),\qquad

\[

\tag{A.127.26}

For example Theta(j(U))=j inverse(U Phi inverse), and

The pure element D=C Theta(L) K inverse lies in B. Here it lies in \(P'\) as well: the abelian class of C Theta(L) equals that of K, since the Fb contributions vanish and the unreplaced braid factors have the same pure word K. More directly, D is a product of conjugates of Fb, each in B. Therefore

 c_u=\Theta(\Pi_u)\Pi_u^{-1}\in\mathcal B.

Indeed Theta(\(\Pi_u\))=L D W Bu Fb inverse; multiplying by \(\Pi_u\) inverse expresses the difference as a product of G-conjugates of D and Fb. The normality in (A.127.12) is used here. Since both \(\Pi_u\) and Theta(\(\Pi_u\)) have cube one, \(c_u\) is a C3-cocycle for \(\rho_u\)=Ad(\(\Pi_u\)).

\[
 [z_u]=*,\qquad t_u=1,\qquad h_u\in\mathcal B.

The last condition means that the unique pure transporter from \(\Pi_u\) to Theta(\(\Pi_u\)) belongs to the full joint kernel. It is not the assertion that its C2 class in H1(C2,\(P'\);Theta) is trivial, and no such implication is claimed.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{A.127.8. The additional corrected involution class.}
Set \(\kappa_0=\operatorname{Ad}(b_0^{-1})\beta_2\) and
\(\ell=b_0^{-1}\beta_2(a_u^{-1})a_u\). Its action is the
actual reflection in (A.127.16). Since

 \kappa_0(\ell)=a_u^{-1}\beta_2(a_u)b_0,
 \qquad \kappa_0(\ell)\ell=1,
 \qquad
 \ell=\kappa_0(a_u)^{-1}b_0^{-1}a_u.
 \tag{A.127.31}
\]
With the convention \(z\sim h^{-1}z\kappa_0(h)\), choose
\(h=\kappa_0(a_u)\). The last formula proves
\[
 [\ell]=[b_0^{-1}]
 \quad\hbox{in }H^1(C_2,E';\kappa_0).
 \tag{A.127.32}
\]
It is the class of the specified cyclic parameter under the corrected
action, not an equation \(a_u\theta(a_u)=1\). Neither this class nor
its specified representative is evaluated. Its relation to the boundary
word uses the different actions distinguished in A.129.

For every arbitrary \(a\in E'\), the actual surjection (A.127.3)
supplies a lift with tuple \((Ua,U\theta(a),Ua)\). Conjugating
\(\Pi_0\) by that lift and defining its involution transporter by

pentagon is reconstructed from \(a\). This observation concerns only
the reduced constraints; it is not a counterexample involving actual
Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller data.
