\subsection*{The actual annular bad-region centralizer}

\statement{B.112}{Proved}{The actual annular bad-region cone centralizer is its geometric free-five source, discretely and in full profinite completion}
\label{stmt:B112}
Let $R$ be the actual inner bad-region orbifold group of B.110--B.111
over $0<|q|<1$, with all nine inner critical values filled and the
zero loop retained. We use the common $q=0$-adapted marking specified
below. Its cone is denoted by $c$, and $\bar x=cxc$. This cone is a
specified even conjugate of B.110's cone, not an additional involution.
Let $R^0$ be the ordinary index-two subgroup, and let $S$ be the actual
fixed section, with its transported common basepoint. Then
\[
 S\cong\{0<|q|<1\}\setminus
       \{\beta_{11},\beta_{22},\beta_{33},\beta_{44}\},
 \qquad \pi_1(S)\cong F_5.
\]
Its map into $R^0$ is injective and has a retraction. Writing its
image as $L$, the exact centralizer equalities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-main}
 C_R(c)=L\times\langle c\rangle,
 \qquad C_{\widehat R}(c)=\widehat L\times\langle c\rangle,
 \qquad L\cong F_5.
\end{equation}
The completion in this formula is the full profinite completion, and
the source has its full induced topology. In particular
\[
 (R^0)^{\langle c\rangle}=L,
 \qquad (\widehat{R^0})^{\langle c\rangle}=\widehat L.
\]
The reflected outer bad region has the corresponding statement.
No other neck or whole-surface comparison is asserted here.

More explicitly, $R$ has the following marked HNN presentation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-finite-HNN}
 \begin{split}
 R=\langle B,h\mid{}&hch^{-1}=c,\quad hkh^{-1}=\bar k,\quad
       hdh^{-1}=p,\quad heh^{-1}=q\rangle,\\
 B={}&\Gamma_0\rtimes\langle c\rangle.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $\Gamma_0$ is the graph group on the fourteen vertices
$a,b,d,e,p,q,k$ and their barred mates. Its thirty-two edges are
the six pairs $(x,\bar x)$ for $x=a,b,d,e,p,q$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-finite-edges}
 \begin{gathered}
 (a,b),(\bar a,d),(\bar a,e),(d,\bar e),(b,p),(b,q),\quad
 (p,\bar q),\\
 (k,a),(k,b),(k,d),(k,e),(\bar k,p),(\bar k,q).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Include the barred images of these thirteen pairs as well.
The associated subgroups are the specified full-subgraph extensions
\[
 E_- =\langle c,k,d,e\rangle,
 \qquad E_+=\langle c,\bar k,p,q\rangle,
\]
with the isomorphism displayed in \eqref{eq:B112-finite-HNN}.
In particular these edge groups contain the cone. The completion
argument below uses exact finite-period quotients rather than a
profinite assertion about this HNN tree.

\begin{proof}
\emph{B.112.1. One actual adapted basis and the retained source lift.}
Keep the original six positive meridians $x_1,\ldots,x_6$ and
$c_0=x_2$ from B.110. For its actual zero whisker
\[
 W_0=(2,1,-2,-2,4,5,-4),\qquad
 J=\operatorname{back}(W_0),\qquad Y_i=J(x_i),

the inverse change is $J^{-1}=\operatorname{push}(W_0)$.

is imposed. Set
\[
 (a,b,c,f,d,e)=(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3,Y_4,Y_5,Y_6).
\]
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-cone-connector}
 K=x_3^{-1}x_1,\qquad c=Kc_0K^{-1},\qquad
 f=x_5x_6x_5^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The connector $K$ is even. In the orbifold free product the inverse
substitutions can be printed compactly as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-inverse-basis}
 \begin{gathered}
 x_1=acbc a^{-1},\qquad x_2=acb^{-1}cbc a^{-1},\qquad x_3=a,\\
 x_4=fde d^{-1}f^{-1},\qquad x_5=d,\qquad x_6=d^{-1}fd.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The relation $x_2^2=1$ becomes a conjugate of $c^2=1$.
The twelve inverse substitutions, both six-generator monodromy
comparisons, and all ten relation round trips were checked exactly.
The common record is
\path{work_CE/next_symmetric_nodes/inner_q0_adapted_basis22.json}.

In this basis the actual positive zero monodromy is the standard
positive full twist on $(c,f)$ and fixes $a,b,d,e$:
\[
 \psi_0(c)=cfcf^{-1}c,\qquad \psi_0(f)=cfc,
 \qquad \psi_0(U)=U\quad(U=a,b,d,e).
\]
The far-section generator satisfies $g_0^{-1}xg_0=\psi_0(x)$.
The unique even conjugator of the cone is $cfc$, so the actual
fixed-section lift in this marking is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-source-h}
 h=g_0cfc.
\end{equation}
Its geometric identification is made before filling: the fixed-section
Borel strip centralizes the cone, both lifts are even, and the
generic free-product cone centralizer is exactly $\langle c\rangle$.
The old-cone source lift is literally $K^{-1}hK$; equivalently its
fiber correction is $\psi_0(K)^{-1}cfcK$. Thus the common whisker
is retained, not replaced by an abstract conjugacy class.

The complete generic relations in the new stable direction are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-generic-action}
 [h,c]=1,\qquad h^{-1}fh=\bar f,\qquad
 h^{-1}Uh=\bar f^{-1}U\bar f\quad(U=a,b,d,e).
\end{equation}
Their inverse gives $hUh^{-1}=fUf^{-1}$ on the four outsiders.
Put $k=f^{-1}h$. Reversible substitution in
\eqref{eq:B112-generic-action} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-generic-k}
 [h,c]=1,\qquad hkh^{-1}=\bar k,\qquad
 [k,a]=[k,b]=[k,d]=[k,e]=1,
 \qquad f=hk^{-1}.
\end{equation}
In fact $k=g_0$: the full-twist relation says $fg_0=g_0\bar f$,
and \eqref{eq:B112-source-h} then gives this equality.
The ten filled relations are imposed simultaneously on this generic
presentation. This automatically imposes all their signed zero-loop
translates; no zero-loop automorphism of the slit quotient is assumed.

For the remaining words put

After using only the established $b$-diagonal, their normal closures

\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-remaining-five}

These are based whole-word equivalences. For an explicit check of
the only longer simplifications, the middle words in the actual
$\beta_{12}/\beta_{13}$ and $\beta_{22}$ arrays are respectively
\[
 cb^{-1}cbcb c=\bar b^{-1}b\bar b,
 \qquad
 cb^{-1}cb^{-1}cbcb c=\bar b^{-1}b^{-1}\bar b b c.

The displayed $\beta_{22}$ form also uses a whole-relator conjugation

The first two rows of \eqref{eq:B112-remaining-five} are equivalent

To treat the two middle rows put
$E=fde d^{-1}f^{-1}=h(de d^{-1})h^{-1}$.

gives $[d,\bar d\bar e\bar d^{-1}]=1$.
The established $d$-diagonal makes this equivalent to

Under the same changes the $\beta_{22}$ contact is
\[
 [de d^{-1},\bar d\bar e\bar d^{-1}]=1.
\]
Using just $[d,\bar d]$, $[d,\bar e]$ and $[\bar d,e]$, this

It is therefore equivalent to the missing $e$-diagonal. Finally,
the last row of \eqref{eq:B112-remaining-five} is equivalent to
$[a,\bar e]=1$, because $[\bar a,d]=1$ already gives

Thus, in the actual generic annular group, the simultaneous normal
closure of the ten relations is exactly that of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-ten-short}
 \begin{gathered}

Every reduction above is reversible. Independently, the complete

\emph{B.112.3. The finite HNN base and its exact unfolding.}
Adjoin $p=hdh^{-1}$ and $q=heh^{-1}$. We may add
\[
 [p,\bar p],\ [q,\bar q],\ [p,\bar q],\
 [\bar k,p],\ [\bar k,q]
\]
and their barred images: each is the $h$-conjugate of an already
established relation. Eliminating $p,q$ removes these redundant
relations and recovers \eqref{eq:B112-generic-k} and
\eqref{eq:B112-ten-short}. Reidemeister--Schreier rewriting over
$\langle c\rangle$ gives exactly
\eqref{eq:B112-finite-edges}. The full induced graphs on
\[
 \{k,\bar k,d,\bar d,e,\bar e\},\qquad
 \{k,\bar k,p,\bar p,q,\bar q\}
\]
are isomorphic under $k\mapsto\bar k,d\mapsto p,e\mapsto q$.
Their coordinate retractions prove their injections into the graph
group. Including $c$ preserves these retractions, so the associated
orbifold groups are the stated genuine subgroups. The ordinary HNN
normal-form theorem now proves \eqref{eq:B112-finite-HNN} with
injective base and edges.

Consider its presentation character $h\mapsto1$ and
$a,b,d,e,k,c\mapsto0$. This is a new character: since $k=g_0$,
it is not the geometric annular-base character, which sends both
$h$ and $k$ to one. Write $U_n=h^nUh^{-n}$ for
$U=a,b,d,e$, and let $k_0=k,k_1=\bar k$. The exact graph
$\mathcal G_\infty$ has the vertices $U_n,\bar U_n$ for all
$n\in\mathbb Z$, together with $k_0,k_1$, and the following edges:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-infinite-edges}
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{(i)}&(U_n,\bar U_n),\quad U=a,b,d,e;\\
 \text{(ii)}&(a_n,b_n),(\bar a_n,d_n),(\bar a_n,e_n),(d_n,\bar e_n);\\
 \text{(iii)}&(b_n,d_{n+1}),(b_n,e_{n+1});\\
 \text{(iv)}&(k_{n\bmod2},U_n),\quad U=a,b,d,e,
 \end{array}
\end{equation}
with the barred images of (ii)--(iv). There is no edge between
$k_0$ and $k_1$. Bar exchanges these two vertices and every
$U_n,\bar U_n$ pair. The shift advances $n$ and exchanges
$k_0,k_1$; it commutes with bar.

For completeness this is a presentation equality, not an inference
from a graph picture. The full Schreier rewriting gives
\[
 c_n=c,\quad p_n=d_{n+1},\quad q_n=e_{n+1},\quad
 k_n=k_{n\bmod2}.
\]
Eliminating these named generators leaves exactly
\eqref{eq:B112-infinite-edges}; the additional target edges are
duplicates at level $n+1$. Conversely introducing them recovers
every generator and relation of the finite HNN. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-unfold}
 R=A(\mathcal G_\infty)\rtimes
                (\mathbb Z_h\times C_2(c)).
\end{equation}
No geometric assertion about the cover defined by the original
annular-base character is needed.

\emph{B.112.4. The discrete cone centralizer.}
The dependence-word proof of B.110 applies to the countable graph:
every word and every reduction involves only finitely many vertices.
In a nonempty bar-fixed reduced word, a possible first signed letter
and its barred mate must both be possible first letters and therefore
must commute. The only such pairs in
\eqref{eq:B112-infinite-edges} are the four diagonal pairs at each
level. Removing them successively proves that the fixed graph
subgroup is generated by
\[
 \delta_{U,n}=U_n\bar U_n\qquad(U=a,b,d,e;\ n\in\mathbb Z).
\]
They freely generate. Keep exactly
$a_n,\bar b_n,d_n,e_n$ at every level and kill their four mates
and $k_0,k_1$. None of the four edge families joins two kept
vertices. The coordinate map therefore goes to a free group and
sends these diagonal products to its free basis; its diagonal section
proves injectivity of the free subgroup.

Since $h$ commutes with $c$, \eqref{eq:B112-unfold} now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-discrete-natural}
 C_R(c)=H\times\langle c\rangle,
 \quad H=F(\delta_{U,n})\rtimes\mathbb Z_h
       =F(\delta_a,\delta_b,\delta_d,\delta_e,h),
\end{equation}
where $\delta_U=U\bar U$ at level zero. The last equality follows
by eliminating each $\delta_{U,n}$ in favor of its specified
$h$-conjugate, with no residual relation. It is a free-basis
calculation, not merely a rank count.

\emph{B.112.6. Exact finite even-period quotients.}
Let $m\geq4$ be even. Identify the indices in
\eqref{eq:B112-infinite-edges} modulo $m$ and keep $k_0,k_1$
distinct. This gives a finite graph $\mathcal G_m$ on $8m+2$
vertices. The shift has order $m$ and commutes with bar. Exact
presentation elimination gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-period-head}
 R/\langle\!\langle h^m\rangle\!\rangle
  =R_m=A(\mathcal G_m)\rtimes(C_m(h)\times C_2(c)).
\end{equation}
Evenness is essential for the prescribed swap of $k_0,k_1$.
No $U_n$ is identified with $\bar U_n$, and no vertex becomes
fixed by bar. The only commuting barred pairs remain the $4m$
diagonal pairs; the other edge families do not join a vertex to
its own mate. In particular $k_0,k_1$ are still nonadjacent.
The same independent set $a_n,\bar b_n,d_n,e_n$ survives the
cyclic identification. The finite piling and coordinate arguments
therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B112-head-centralizer}
 C_{R_m}(c)=
       (F(\delta_{U,n}:n\bmod m)\rtimes C_m(h))
                       \times\langle c\rangle.
\end{equation}
This is precisely the image of $L\times\langle c\rangle$:
the four level-zero diagonal generators and $h$ generate every
level, and \eqref{eq:B112-source-basis} identifies them with
the actual source.

These heads are cofinal for all finite targets. Given any finite
quotient of $R$, choose even $m\geq4$ divisible by the order of
the image of $h$. It kills $h^m$ and therefore factors through
the exact quotient \eqref{eq:B112-period-head}. No requirement
that the quotient preserve a separately chosen graph marking is
being imposed.

\emph{B.112.7. Full hypotheses and passage to the completed centralizer.}
We verify the hypotheses at each head. A graph group on a finite
graph is residually finite by the vertex/full-link induction of
B.110: full-link subgroups are coordinate retracts, their finite
Britton nonmembership conditions survive in finite base quotients,
and the resulting finite-base HNN groups are virtually finite-rank
free. This proof does not require the graph to be bipartite.

It also has a finite classifying space of finite dimension.
Inductively attach the cylinder of a finite classifying space for
the full-link graph group to the model for the smaller graph group.
The coordinate retraction gives the required injection. Its universal
cover is a tree of contractible spaces and is contractible. Induction
gives the finite Salvetti model. The graphs here can contain triangles,
so no dimension-two bound is asserted. The finite cellular resolution
gives finite cohomology in every degree with finite coefficients.

By \citet[Theorem~3.15]{B_Lorensen2008}, these finite-graph groups
are good for every finite continuous module. The complete primary
proof and its full-subgraph/HNN dependencies were read at B.110.
For every discrete continuous $\mathbb F_p[[\widehat{A(\mathcal G_m)}]]$
module, finite compact orbits span finite invariant submodules.
Discrete cohomology commutes with their filtered union by the finite
resolution. Continuous cochains commute with it because their compact
domains have finite image, and filtered unions are exact. Thus the
comparison holds for all such discrete modules.

The graph group has finite index in $R_m$, with quotient
$C_m\times C_2$. Finite-index cores give its full topology and
residual finiteness of $R_m$. The two Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre
spectral sequences have the same $E_2$ pages and finitely many
nonzero kernel rows, so they give the all-module comparison for
$R_m$. With finite coefficients the same finite-row sequence gives
finite cohomology in every degree. Its finite-index torsion-free
graph subgroup has finite cohomological dimension. This verifies
finite virtual $p$-cohomological \emph{type}, not just a dimension
bound.

All hypotheses of the prime-order element case of
\citet[Theorem~4.3]{BoggiZalesskii2025} now hold for $c$ in $R_m$.
We use the same complete order-$p$ proof and Lemma~2.2 checked at
B.108--B.110. It yields
\[
 C_{\widehat{R_m}}(c)=\overline{C_{R_m}(c)}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:B112-head-centralizer}, this is the compact image of
$\widehat L\times C_2$ in that completed head.

Let $x\in C_{\widehat R}(c)$. Its image in every completed head
belongs to that source image. If $x$ lay outside the compact image
of $\widehat L\times C_2$ in $\widehat R$, some ordinary finite
quotient would separate it from that compact subgroup. That quotient
factors through one of the heads by the preceding cofinality, a
contradiction. Hence the completed centralizer is exactly the compact
source image. The actual retraction and index-two cores in Section~5
identify this image with $\widehat F_5\times C_2$, proving
\eqref{eq:B112-main}. No assertion about completion of the
infinite-height kernel or properness of a cone-bearing profinite
HNN is used.
