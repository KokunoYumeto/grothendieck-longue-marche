\statement{A.132}{Proved}{The original negative hexagon and the exact elliptic base transporter}
Retain the original negative full-GT involution and its actual elliptic
action from A.121 and A.127. In the complete based marking, put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 a=\sigma^2,\quad b=\tau^2,\quad \Phi=\phi(X,K),\\
 Z_\phi=\phi(X_2,b),\quad F_b=\phi(a,b),\quad
 \Pi_0=\tau^{-1}\sigma^{-1}Y.
\end{gathered}
\]
The unique pure transporter of the original base cubic is the literal
derived element
\[
 h_1=F_b p_\phi=\Phi Z_\phi,\qquad
 \Theta(\Pi_0)=h_1\Pi_0h_1^{-1}.
 \tag{A.132.1}
\]
Its three original based deletion images are
\((\Phi,\Phi,\Phi)\). Therefore the actual base parameter of
A.127 is
\[
                         b_0=1.                    \tag{A.132.2}
\]
Together with A.131, the specified canonical cyclic obstruction of
A.127 is neutral in the cohomology of the \emph{entire} Brunnian
kernel. The proof uses the original negative hexagon of \(\phi\),
and does not impose a hexagon on the canonical word \(u\).

\begin{proof}
\emph{1. Actual groups, words and the original cycle convention.}
Let \(G\) be the full profinite completion of the actual reduced
three-strand elliptic braid group, \(P\) its pure subgroup, and
\(P'\) its closed derived subgroup. All group words use
\([g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\). The original marking is
\[
\begin{gathered}
 C=\sigma\tau,\quad L=C^{-1},\quad
 K=[Y,X]=\sigma\tau^2\sigma,\quad K_2=\tau\sigma^2\tau,\\
 X_2=\sigma X\sigma,\quad X_3=\tau X_2\tau,\\
 z=YX^{-1}Y^{-1},\quad Q=\phi(z,K),\quad \Pi_0=LY.
\end{gathered}                                             \tag{A.132.3}
\]
The actual elliptic involution satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Theta(\sigma)&=F_b\sigma^{-1}F_b^{-1},&
 \Theta(\tau)&=\tau^{-1},\\
 \Theta(Y)&=K^{-1}QY\Phi^{-1},& \Theta^2&=1.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{A.132.4}
\]
These are the original full-GT section formulas, not an action
assigned to an unproved pair \((d,u)\).

The original parameter \(\phi\) is a profinite commutator word.
Its two-cycle and its multiplier-minus-one three-cycle have the
precise convention of C.30.1:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \phi(x,y)\phi(y,x)=1,\\
 \phi(x,y)x^{-1}\phi(c,x)c^{-1}
                 \phi(y,c)y^{-1}=1,\qquad c=(xy)^{-1}.
\end{gathered}                                             \tag{A.132.5}
\]
Only the original \(\phi\) is used in (A.132.5).

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{2. A full braid identity from the negative hexagon.}
Set
\[
 n=\tau a\tau^{-1},\qquad
 Z_c=(\sigma\tau)^3=a n b,\qquad c=(ab)^{-1}.
\]
The equality \(a n b=(\sigma\tau)^3\) follows directly from
the Artin relation: \(a n b=\sigma^2\tau\sigma^2\tau\).
The same relation makes \(Z_c\) central in
\(\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\). Consequently
\[
 n=(ba)^{-1}Z_c=(bcb^{-1})Z_c=(a^{-1}ca)Z_c.
 \tag{A.132.6}
\]
For a commutator word, multiplication of one argument by an element
commuting with both arguments leaves its value unchanged. Indeed
the inserted central factor occurs to the total exponent sum of
that argument, which is zero for ordinary commutator words; the
same assertion for their full profinite closure follows by
continuity. Thus (A.132.6) may be used in the original group
without replacing it by a quotient by its braid center.

Define the actual two conjugates
\[
 F_\tau=\operatorname{Ad}(\tau)(F_b)=\phi(n,b),\qquad
 F_B=\operatorname{Ad}(\tau\sigma)(F_b)=\phi(n,a).
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Ad}(\tau\sigma)(a)=n\) and
\(\operatorname{Ad}(\tau\sigma)(b)=a\) are literal braid
identities. Conjugation equivariance and the commuting-decoration
property give
\[
 F_\tau=b\phi(c,b)b^{-1},\qquad
 F_B=a^{-1}\phi(c,a)a.
 \tag{A.132.7}
\]
Apply (A.132.5) to the actual pair \((a,b)\). Duality and
(A.132.7) say
\[
 \phi(c,a)=aF_Ba^{-1},\qquad
 \phi(b,c)=b^{-1}F_\tau^{-1}b.
\]
Substitution in the original order now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 1
 &=F_ba^{-1}(aF_Ba^{-1})c^{-1}
                   (b^{-1}F_\tau^{-1}b)b^{-1}\\
 &=F_bF_B(a^{-1}c^{-1}b^{-1})F_\tau^{-1}
  =F_bF_BF_\tau^{-1}.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{A.132.8}
\]
The last equality uses \(c^{-1}=ab\). We have proved exactly
\[
 \operatorname{Ad}(\tau)(F_b)
       \operatorname{Ad}(\tau\sigma)(F_b)^{-1}=F_b.
 \tag{A.132.9}
\]
No deletion map or cyclic reordering of factors was used.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{3. The original base cubic, retaining every braid factor.}
Put
\[
 \Pi_\phi=LQY\Phi^{-1}F_b,\qquad
 M=\operatorname{Ad}(\tau)(F_b)
                  \operatorname{Ad}(\tau\sigma)(F_b)^{-1}.
\]
Direct application of (A.132.4) gives
\[
 \Theta(\Pi_0)
   =\tau F_b\sigma F_b^{-1}K^{-1}QY\Phi^{-1}.
\]
On the other hand, literal multiplication gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M\Pi_\phi F_b^{-1}
 &=\tau F_b\sigma F_b^{-1}
             \sigma^{-1}\tau^{-2}\sigma^{-1}QY\Phi^{-1}\\
 &=\Theta(\Pi_0),
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{A.132.10}
\]
since \(K^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-2}\sigma^{-1}\).
Using (A.132.9), this becomes
\[
                  \Theta(\Pi_0)=F_b\Pi_\phi F_b^{-1}.
 \tag{A.132.11}
\]

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{4. Identification of the actual unique pure transporter.}
The original pentagon of \(\phi\), with the complete type-B
marking of A.121.5, is
\[
 F_b E_\phi A_\phi=\Phi Z_\phi,\qquad
 E_\phi=\phi(X_3^{-1},K_2),\quad A_\phi=\phi(X,a).
\]
The complete explicit-transporter proof of A.131 gives
\[
 p_\phi=E_\phi A_\phi=F_b^{-1}\Phi Z_\phi,\qquad
 \Pi_\phi=p_\phi\Pi_0p_\phi^{-1}.
 \tag{A.132.12}
\]
Combining (A.132.11)--(A.132.12) proves (A.132.1).
Both factors \(\Phi,Z_\phi\) are commutator-word values on
pure arguments, so \(h_1\in P'\). A.124 gives
\(C_G(\Pi_0)=\langle\Pi_0\rangle\), and this subgroup has
trivial intersection with \(P\). Therefore (A.132.1) identifies
the \emph{unique original} pure transporter; it is not a chosen
lift of a convenient tuple. Applying \(\Theta\) twice to this
transporter equation makes \(\Theta(h_1)h_1\) centralize
\(\Pi_0\). That product belongs to \(P'\), hence
\[
                         \Theta(h_1)h_1=1.
\]

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{5. The whole tuple and the specified parameter.}
Let \(q=(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3)\) be the exact based map of A.126.
Its generator table gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
 q(X)=(1,X,X),\qquad q(K)=(1,K,K),\\
 q(X_2)=(X,1,X^{-1}),\qquad q(b)=(K,1,1).
\end{gathered}
\]
Since \(\phi\) is a commutator word, it follows that
\[
 q(\Phi)=(1,\Phi,\Phi),\qquad
 q(Z_\phi)=(\Phi,1,1),\qquad
 q(h_1)=(\Phi,\Phi,\Phi).
 \tag{A.132.13}
\]
The parameter in A.127 was defined by the actual equality
\[
 q(h_1)=(\Phi b_0,\Phi\theta(b_0),\Phi b_0).
\]
Its first coordinate in (A.132.13) proves \(b_0=1\) in the
full free profinite group, not merely in its abelianization.

For the actual canonical \(u\), A.37 supplies the original
pentagon and A.131 therefore gives \(a_u=1\). Thus the exact
A.127 formulas specialize to
\[
\begin{gathered}
 t_u=1,\qquad
 \ell=b_0^{-1}\beta_2(a_u^{-1})a_u=1,\\
 q(h_u)=1,\qquad h_u\in\mathcal B,\qquad
 [z_u]=\partial(1)=*,
\end{gathered}                                             \tag{A.132.14}
\]
where \(\mathcal B\) is the \emph{entire} closed joint kernel.
The corrected involution on the common-word target is exactly
\(\beta_2\). This evaluates its specified base parameter; it
does not make the whole space \(H^1(C_2,E';\beta_2)\) trivial.
The subsequent full-word comparison of \(h_u\), the specified
two-strand boundary, and the original CM frame are separate
questions. In particular no boundary or original CM vanishing
is inferred from (A.132.14).
\end{proof}
