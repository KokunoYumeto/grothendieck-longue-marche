\subsection*{The single fixed coset and the complete relative node kernel}
\statement{B.155}{Proved}{The remaining local fixed-kernel question has an exact paired-free presentation with one cone factor}
\label{stmt:B155}
Use the actual pre-node ordinary group and involution of B.123:
\[
 G_{\mathrm{gen}}=F_{11}\rtimes S,\qquad S=F(m,v),\qquad
 \Gamma_0=G_{\mathrm{gen}}/N.
\]
Here $N$ is the normal closure of the entire forty-node scheme, with
both regions, both ordinary covering sheets and every signed source
translate. Let $p_a:\Gamma_0\to\Gamma_a$ be the actual filled one-family
quotient and let $q_a$ be its composite from $G_{\mathrm{gen}}$. Put
\[
 J_a=\Gamma_a^\delta
 =\langle A,m,v\mid[A,m]=1\rangle,\qquad
 U=\ker(J_a\to S),\qquad W_a=\ker(\Gamma_a\to S),
\]
and define $P=q_a^{-1}(J_a)$ and $Q=P\cap F_{11}$. Then $P=Q\rtimes S$,
$N\le Q$, and there is a possibly infinite ordinary free basis $Z$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B155-paired}
 Q=F(z,\delta(z):z\in Z),\qquad Q^\delta=1.
\end{equation}
An exact presentation of $Q/N$ retains all the relators in
\eqref{eq:B155-node-scheme} below. In this presentation the ordinary
remaining fixed-source question is exactly
\[
 (Q/N)^\delta=H_f,
 \qquad H_f=I_{\mathrm{src}}\rtimes\sigma(U).
\]
There is also the actual-closure version
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B155-compact-question}
 (\mathcal Q/\mathcal N)^\delta=\overline{H_f},
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal Q,\mathcal N$ defined in the proof. Neither displayed
fixed-generation equality is asserted here.
\begin{proof}
\emph{The unique fixed coset.}
By B.149 and B.154,
$J_a\cong\mathbb Z^2*\mathbb Z$ and
$(\widehat{\Gamma_a})^\delta=\widehat J_a$, with the displayed actual
embedding. If $gJ_a$ is fixed in $\Gamma_a/J_a$, write
$\delta(g)=gh$ with $h\in J_a$. Applying $\delta$ again, and using that
$J_a$ is pointwise fixed, gives $h^2=1$. The group $J_a$ is torsion-free,
so $h=1$ and $g\in J_a$. Thus only the identity coset is fixed.
The same argument holds in $\widehat{\Gamma_a}/\widehat J_a$:
$\widehat J_a=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\,2}\amalg\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ is
torsion-free, because the full profinite free-product finite-order
theorem places torsion in a conjugate factor and both factors are
torsion-free. This statement does not supply a continuous transversal.

\emph{Exactly one cone in the ordinary lifted fiber.}
In the physical-cone marking the pre-node extended fiber is
\[
 E=F_{11}\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle
   =C_2(\tau)*C_2(c)*F_5,
 \qquad E_a=W_a\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle.
\]
Let $Q_+$ be the inverse image in $E$ of
$J_f=U\times\langle\tau\rangle\le E_a$. Then
$Q_+\cap F_{11}=Q$ and $Q_+=Q\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle$.
The already proved actual centralizer identifies
$C_{E_a}(\tau)=J_f$. Its only involution is $\tau$, since $U$ is free.

Apply the ordinary Kurosh theorem in the literal free product $E$.
If a conjugate of the $\tau$ factor meets $Q_+$ nontrivially, the image
of its involution in $J_f$ is $\tau$. Its conjugator therefore maps
into $C_{E_a}(\tau)=J_f$ and already belongs to $Q_+$. There is exactly
one such factor, represented by $\tau$. No conjugate of the other
cone contributes: the actual coarse map
$E_a\to C_2(C)*C_2(\tau)*\mathbb Z(f)$ kills $U$, maps $J_f$ to
$\langle\tau\rangle$, and maps $c$ to the other cone $C$.
These two nontrivial free-product factors are not conjugate.
Intersections with conjugates of $F_5$ are free and are absorbed into
the free factor. Consequently
\[
 Q_+=C_2(\tau)*F(Z).
\]
Multiply any odd free-basis element by $\tau$ to make every element of
$Z$ have ordinary orientation zero; this is an invertible basis change
fixing $\tau$. The two-coset Schreier rewriting then gives
\eqref{eq:B155-paired}, with $\delta$ exchanging the two halves.
No finiteness or canonical peripheral choice for $Z$ is claimed.

\emph{The whole node and operator scheme.}
Let $\mathscr R$ denote the forty literal ordinary node seeds.
Choose a left-coset transversal $\mathscr T$ for $G_{\mathrm{gen}}/P$,
identified through $q_a$ with $\Gamma_a/J_a$, as follows. Retain $1$;
on each other two-element $\delta$ orbit choose a representative and
its exact $\delta$ mate. Lift the first into $F_{11}$ and the second
by applying $\delta$ to that lift. Fiber lifts exist because $S\le J_a$.
This is an ordinary set-theoretic choice. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B155-node-scheme}
 N=\left\langle\!\left\langle
 t^{-1}\rho t,\ \delta(t^{-1}\rho t):
 t\in\mathscr T,\ \rho\in\mathscr R
 \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_P .
\end{equation}
Indeed every $g\in G_{\mathrm{gen}}$ has the form $tp$ and
$g^{-1}\rho g=p^{-1}(t^{-1}\rho t)p$. Conversely all displayed words
belong to $N$, and adjoining their $\delta$ images does not change the
actual $\delta$-stable normal closure. Since $P=Q\rtimes S$, this is
exactly the $Q$-normal closure of all signed $S$ translates of those
words. Both original node sheets and every retained-deck connector
remain; no peripheral conjugacy class or finite orbit cutoff replaces
them.

\emph{The ordinary source comparison.}
The four actual contact words lift to $Q$: $A_{\mathrm{src}}$ maps to
$A\in U$, and the three other contacts map to $1$, all with source
coordinate zero. B.150 and the actual section give
\[
 Q/N=K_{\mathrm{rem}}\rtimes\sigma(U),\qquad
 H_f=H_{\mathrm{src}}\cap\ker(\Gamma_0\to S)
     =I_{\mathrm{src}}\rtimes\sigma(U).
\]
Here $H_f$ is generated by the entire signed $S$ orbits of the four
contacts. The section is fixed, so the fixed subgroup of the first
semidirect product is
$K_{\mathrm{rem}}^\delta\rtimes\sigma(U)$.
This proves the stated equivalence. Unique fixed cosets do not by
themselves limit the fixed words created by the full filling.

\emph{Actual ambient compact groups.}
Work in $\widehat G_{\mathrm{gen}}=\widehat F_{11}\rtimes\widehat S$.
The generic and quotient fibers have their full induced topologies
by the characteristic-finite-quotient argument for finitely generated
fibers. Let $\overline U$ be its actual closure in $\widehat J_a$ and put
\[
 \mathcal Q=(\widehat F_{11}\to\widehat W_a)^{-1}(\overline U).
\]
This is exactly the ambient closure of $Q$. The kernel of the
completed quotient is the closure of its discrete kernel and is
contained in $\overline Q$; the compact image of $\overline Q$ is
exactly $\overline U$. This proves the equality without identifying
the abstract completion of the infinite group $Q$.

Let $\mathcal N$ be the actual closed normal node kernel from B.123.
The ambient free profinite product satisfies
$C_{\widehat E}(\tau)=\langle\tau\rangle$, so
$\mathcal Q^\delta=1$. No profinite Kurosh decomposition of
$\mathcal Q$ is used. The completed marked section and B.154 give
\[
 \mathcal Q/\mathcal N
   =\overline{K_{\mathrm{rem}}}\rtimes\widehat\sigma(\overline U),
 \qquad
 \overline{H_f}
   =\overline{I_{\mathrm{src}}}\rtimes\widehat\sigma(\overline U).
 \]
For the source equality one uses the literal source section and the
same net correction as in B.154, now over $\widehat S$. Taking fixed
subgroups proves that \eqref{eq:B155-compact-question} is precisely the
actual remaining compact comparison.
\end{proof}
