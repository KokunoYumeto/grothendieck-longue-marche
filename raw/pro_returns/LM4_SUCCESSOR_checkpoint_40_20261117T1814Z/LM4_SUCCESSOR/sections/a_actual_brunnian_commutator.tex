% CHECKPOINT37: actual marked two-retraction model and entire closed commutator.
\statement{A.128}{Proved}{The actual elliptic Brunnian kernel is a closed commutator}
Retain the actual pure completed elliptic group \(P\), its closed
derived subgroup \(P'\), and the based deletion maps of A.126.
Put \(E=\widehat F(X,Y)\), \(K=[Y,X]\), and
\[
 \mathcal B=\ker\bigl((\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3):P'\longrightarrow(E')^3\bigr).
\]
The actual third-deletion fiber has the marked free basis
\[
 N_3=\ker\pi_3=\widehat F(h,k,m),\quad
 h=X_3,\quad k=Y_3,\quad m=A_{13}=\tau^{-1}\sigma^2\tau.
                                                        \tag{A.128.1}
\]
Let \(H=\overline{\langle h,k\rangle}\), \(w=[k^{-1},h^{-1}]\).
The two continuous retractions \(r_0,r_1:N_3\to H\) fix
\(H\) and send \(m\) to \(1,w\), respectively. With
\(U=\ker r_0\), \(V=\ker r_1\), one has
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal B=U\cap V=\overline{[U,V]}.}               \tag{A.128.2}
\]
The last term means the closed subgroup generated by every
\([u,v]\) with \(u\in U,v\in V\). Moreover,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 N_3/\mathcal B\simeq H\times_{H_{\rm ab}}H,\qquad
 N_3/(UV)\simeq H_{\rm ab},\\
 U/\mathcal B\xrightarrow[\ r_1\ ]{\sim}H',\qquad
 V/\mathcal B\xrightarrow[\ r_0\ ]{\sim}H'.
 \end{gathered}                                           \tag{A.128.3}
\]
All these are actual topological isomorphisms. The kernel is
nontrivial: \([m,mw^{-1}]\ne1\). No freeness assertion about
\(\mathcal B\), and no value of A.127's specified cocycle,
is part of this statement.

\begin{proof}
\emph{1. The actual fiber and its two marked maps.}
The forget-third configuration fibration in A.124 has a
torus-minus-two-points fiber. Its original third-strand handle
loops \(X_3,Y_3\), together with the meridian \(A_{13}\),
freely generate its ordinary fundamental group: the surface
relation eliminates the second puncture meridian. A.124 proves
that this fiber has its full induced profinite topology, by
refining characteristic finite fiber targets through finite
source-action quotients. Thus its embedded full completion is
exactly (A.128.1), not an abstract completion assigned to an
infinite-index joint kernel.

The literal based table of A.126 gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
       &h&k&m\\\hline
 \pi_1&X^{-1}&Y^{-1}&1\\
 \pi_2&X^{-1}&Y^{-1}&K\\
 \pi_3&1&1&1
 \end{array}                                             \tag{A.128.4}
\]
For the handle rows use \(X_3=(XX_2)^{-1}\) and
\(Y_3=(YY_2)^{-1}\); the meridian row retains the original
whisker of \(A_{13}\). The actual isomorphism
\(\iota:H\to E\), \(h\mapsto X^{-1},k\mapsto Y^{-1}\),
sends \(w\) to \(K\). Thus
\(\pi_1|_{N_3}=\iota r_0\) and
\(\pi_2|_{N_3}=\iota r_1\).
Eliminating the third free generator proves
\[
 U=\langle\!\langle m\rangle\!\rangle_{N_3},\qquad
 V=\langle\!\langle mw^{-1}\rangle\!\rangle_{N_3},          \tag{A.128.5}
\]
where normal closures are closed. The second elimination is the
invertible Nielsen substitution \(m\mapsto mw^{-1}\).

The image of \((r_0,r_1)\) contains the diagonal \(H\) and
\((1,w)\). The closed normal closure of \(w\) in \(H\)
is \(H'\). Compactness therefore shows that the image contains
\(1\times H'\), and hence the entire fiber product in
(A.128.3). The reverse containment follows on the three
generators. Its kernel is \(U\cap V\).

There is no additional derived intersection to impose.
The actual meridian \(m\) belongs to \(P'\), by the integral
abelianization calculation in A.125. Since \(P'\) is closed
normal in \(P\), (A.128.5) gives \(U\subseteq P'\).
Thus \(U\cap V\) is killed by all three deletions in \(P'\),
and is exactly \(\mathcal B\). Quotienting by both relators
in (A.128.5) leaves \(H/\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle
=H_{\rm ab}\). The two other retraction images are \(H'\),
again by the normal closure of \(w\), and their kernels are
\(U\cap V\). This proves (A.128.3).

\emph{2. An entire ordinary intersection calculation.}
Let \(H_0=F(h,k)\), \(F_0=H_0*\langle m\rangle\), and
let \(U_0,V_0\) be the ordinary normal closures of
\(m,mw^{-1}\). We prove
\[
             U_0\cap V_0=[U_0,V_0].                       \tag{A.128.6}
\]
Put \(u=k^{-1},v=h^{-1}\), so \(w=[u,v]\), and choose the
abelianization transversal \(t_{ij}=u^iv^j\),
\(i,j\in\mathbb Z\). The spanning-tree Schreier basis of
\(H_0'\) is
\[
 e_{ij}=u^iv^j u v^{-j}u^{-i-1}\quad(j\ne0),\qquad e_{i0}=1.
                                                        \tag{A.128.7}
\]
Indeed the tree consists of the horizontal axis and all
vertical lines in the integer-grid covering graph. All
\(v\)-edge generators vanish; the remaining \(u\)-edge
generators are precisely (A.128.7).
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 w_{ij}:=t_{ij}wt_{ij}^{-1}=e_{ij}e_{i,j+1}^{-1}.
\]
The inverse substitutions are finite words:
\[
 e_{ij}=\begin{cases}
 w_{i,j-1}^{-1}w_{i,j-2}^{-1}\cdots w_{i0}^{-1},&j>0,\\
 w_{ij}w_{i,j+1}\cdots w_{i,-1},&j<0.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{A.128.8}
\]
They give inverse homomorphisms of ordinary free groups, so
\(\{w_{ij}:i,j\in\mathbb Z\}\) is a free basis of \(H_0'\).
No infinite product occurs.

The ordinary retraction kernel \(U_0\) has free basis
\(m_a=ama^{-1}\), \(a\in H_0\). Set \(W_0=[U_0,V_0]\).
Both subgroups are normal in \(F_0\), hence \(W_0\) is
normal and is contained in \(U_0\cap V_0\). In
\(F_0/W_0\), the two subgroup images commute. Because
\(m_aw_a^{-1}\in V_0\), where \(w_a=awa^{-1}\), their
conjugation actions on \(\overline U_0=U_0/W_0\) satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{Ad}(w_a)|_{\overline U_0}
       =\operatorname{Ad}(m_a)|_{\overline U_0}.            \tag{A.128.9}
\]
Write any \(a\in H_0\) as \(a=r t_{ij}\), with
\(r\in H_0'\). Express \(r\) as a finite word in the basis
\(w_{ij}\). By (A.128.9), its action on \(\overline U_0\)
is the inner action of the same word in the \(m_{t_{ij}}\).
Consequently \(m_a=r m_{t_{ij}}r^{-1}\) lies in the subgroup
generated by all \(m_{t_{ij}}W_0\). These elements generate
the entire quotient \(\overline U_0\).

Let \(Z\) be the ordinary free group on symbols \(z_{ij}\).
We have a surjection \(Z\to\overline U_0\) sending
\(z_{ij}\) to \(m_{t_{ij}}W_0\). The retraction \(m\mapsto w\)
factors through \(\overline U_0\); its composite from \(Z\)
sends \(z_{ij}\) to the free basis \(w_{ij}\) of \(H_0'\).
This composite is an isomorphism. Therefore both factors are
isomorphisms. Its second kernel is
\((U_0\cap V_0)/W_0\), proving (A.128.6).
The original marked retraction pair now identifies
\[
 F_0/[U_0,V_0]\simeq D_0,
 \qquad D_0=H_0\times_{\mathbb Z^2}H_0.                    \tag{A.128.10}
\]

\emph{3. Full induced topology for this particular fiber product.}
Put \(G_0=H_0\times H_0\). The group \(D_0\) is normal
with quotient \(\mathbb Z^2\), and is generated by diagonal
\(h,k\) and \((1,w)\). Let \(J\triangleleft D_0\) have
finite index. A finitely generated group has finitely many
subgroups of a fixed finite index, as follows by mapping to
the corresponding symmetric group. Thus the \(G_0\)-orbit
of \(J\) is finite, and
\(J_0=\bigcap_{g\in G_0}gJg^{-1}\) is normal in \(G_0\)
and of finite index in \(D_0\).

Write \(A=D_0/J_0\) and \(L_0=G_0/J_0\). The group
\(L_0\) is an extension of \(\mathbb Z^2\) by the finite
group \(A\). We verify residual finiteness directly.
The subgroup \(L_1=C_{L_0}(A)\) has finite index, kernel
\(Z(A)\) over its image in \(\mathbb Z^2\), and that
kernel is finite central. Choose lifts \(s,t\) of a basis
of that finite-index lattice. Their commutator is central
of finite order. If \(n\) is the exponent of \(Z(A)\),
then \(s^n,t^n\) commute with all of \(L_1\) and generate
a central free abelian subgroup of rank two and finite
index. Its finite-index core in \(L_0\) is a normal free
abelian subgroup \(B\) of finite index. The quotient
\(L_0/B\) separates elements outside \(B\). A nonzero
element of \(B\) is separated by \(L_0/qB\) for some
positive integer \(q\); \(qB\) is characteristic in \(B\)
and therefore normal of finite index in \(L_0\).
This proves residual finiteness of \(L_0\).

Intersecting finitely many separating kernels gives a finite
quotient of \(L_0\) injective on \(A\). Its pullback to
\(G_0\) has intersection with \(D_0\) contained in
\(J_0\subseteq J\). Every finite target of \(D_0\) is
therefore refined by an actual finite ambient target. This
proves its full induced profinite topology.

Completion of a direct product is the product of completions:
the restrictions of any finite target to the two commuting
factors supply a finite product refinement. The universal
property of completion of a normal quotient also gives
\(\widehat G_0/\overline{D_0}=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\,2}\).
Thus the proved embedding identifies
\[
 \widehat D_0=\overline{D_0}
           =H\times_{H_{\rm ab}}H.                        \tag{A.128.11}
\]
This argument proves the needed comparison for the specified
finitely generated fiber product; it assumes no unrestricted
completion theorem for infinite-index subgroups.

\emph{4. Transfer with the exact retraction marking.}
Completing (A.128.10), and using (A.128.11), gives
\[
 N_3/\overline{[U_0,V_0]}\simeq H\times_{H_{\rm ab}}H.
\]
The map sends \(h,k,m\) exactly as the actual retraction
pair, so its kernel is \(U\cap V\). The ordinary normal
closures \(U_0,V_0\) are dense in \(U,V\): the ambient
ordinary free group is dense in \(N_3\), and their defining
relators are those of (A.128.5). Continuity of commutators
now gives
\(\overline{[U_0,V_0]}=\overline{[U,V]}\), proving
(A.128.2).

Finally \([m,mw^{-1}]\) belongs to \([U,V]\). Its ordinary
free-product normal form is
\(m^2w^{-1}m^{-1}wm^{-1}\), a nonempty reduced word since
\(w\ne1\). The finite-rank ordinary free group embeds in
its full profinite completion, so the same word is nontrivial
in \(N_3\). This is an exact symbolic normal-form argument.
The cyclic action on \(\mathcal B\) permutes the deletion
fibers and need not preserve this chosen \(N_3\).
The commutator theorem is compatible with the entire
\(H^1(C_3,\mathcal B)\simeq E'\) of A.127; it does not
evaluate its specified canonical parameter or prove the
original boundary equation.
\end{proof}
