## AF.1: the profinite intersection bound

The proof applies to infinite maximal abelian extensions. Both fields contain the whole of \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\), so \(K,L\subset M\) and the subgroups \(A=\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)\), \(B=\operatorname{Gal}(M/L)\) are defined. Their product is closed (a product of compact subgroups), and its fixed field is \(K\cap L=\mathbb Q\); hence \(AB=G\). The intersection \(Z=A\cap B\) is central because each of its elements commutes with both abelian subgroups. The quotient is \(C_2\times C_4\). With the two specified lifts, every commutator belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by the central element \([a,b]\), and \([a,b]^2=1\). This subgroup is finite and therefore already closed. The maximal abelian subextension of \(M/\mathbb Q\) is exactly \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\). The claimed degree bound follows without an assumption of finiteness or finite generation of \(G\).

## AF.2: the Gaussian–quintic intersection

The dihedral field \(D_5=\mathbb Q(i,5^{1/4})\) has precisely the three listed quadratic subfields. The intersection with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)\) is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\): the only alternative is the whole cyclic quartic field, which is impossible since the dihedral group has no cyclic quartic quotient. The subgroup over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) is a Klein four group, so \(D_5K_5/K_5\) is abelian. Nonabelianity of \(D_5/\mathbb Q\), or nonnormality of its real quartic subfield, excludes \(5^{1/4}\) from \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\). AF.1 then proves equality.

## AF.3: the Eisenstein five-division quotient

The endomorphism argument proving \(K_3\subset\mathbb Q(E_3[5])\) is valid: the difference of the two conjugate order-three endomorphisms has norm three and therefore acts invertibly on five-torsion. Thus the Cartan image over \(K_3\) is faithfully a subgroup of \(\mathbb F_{25}^{\times}\). The point count at seven gives trace \(-4\), so the two possible Frobenius eigenvalues in the CM field are \(-2\pm\sqrt{-3}\). Each has order 24 modulo five; the exact certificate checks both. This proves the full Cartan image, independent of an unproved global surjectivity assertion. Complex conjugation is an actual order-two lift and conjugates the Cartan by the fifth power. Consequently the full group is \(C_{24}\rtimes_5 C_2\).

The odd subgroup of order three is characteristic in the Cartan and normal in the full group. The quotient is \(C_8\rtimes_5 C_2\), with derived group \(\langle b^4\rangle\). Its determinant kernel is exactly \(\{1,b^4,b^2c,b^6c\}=\langle b^2c\rangle\), because \((b^2c)^2=b^4\). It follows that the finite field \(M_{35}\) is abelian over both \(K_3\) and \(K_5\), but nonabelian over \(\mathbb Q\). AF.1 identifies its compositum with \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\) with the full intersection.

## AF.4: the CM field comparisons

Clark, Cook, and Stankewicz (2009, Theorem 8) state the Weber ray-class-field theorem in the form used here: for maximal-order CM, adjoining a Weber function on \(N\)-torsion, together with the CM field and \(j\)-invariant, gives the ray class field of modulus \(N\). The choices \(x^3\) and \(x^2\) are the quotients by the extra automorphisms for the two displayed elliptic curves. Integer moduli suffice: every finite ideal modulus divides an integer modulus, and the two CM fields have no infinite places. The reverse inclusion follows from the rank-one CM Tate modules. Thus \(L_3=K_3^{\mathrm{ab}}\) and \(L_4=K_4^{\mathrm{ab}}\).

The Jacobian upper bound uses only the explicit order-five curve automorphism, not a general assertion that the full torsion field equals the maximal abelian extension. Its four distinct eigenvalues on first homology show rational rank one over \(K_5\); a stable full lattice is free rank one over each discrete valuation factor of the maximal order. This remains true at the ramified prime five. The Galois centralizer is commutative, proving \(L_5\subseteq K_5^{\mathrm{ab}}\).

An optional explanatory sentence is useful at the later Rosati step: the order-five automorphism comes from an automorphism of the curve, so it preserves the canonical principal polarization; its Rosati adjoint is its inverse. This identifies Rosati on \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]\) with complex conjugation directly.

## AF.5: Yelton and the order-16 quotient from three-torsion

### The specialization theorem is sufficient

Yelton (2019, Corollary 1.2(a), arXiv version 2) applies to an arbitrary separable monic polynomial over a field of characteristic different from two, in genus at least two. It explicitly places the specified fourth roots in the eight-division field. It is not restricted to the generic polynomial. For odd degree the quantities \(\gamma_{i,j}\) are \(\alpha_j-\alpha_i\), and at \(\alpha_i=1\) for \(x^5-1\) their product is \(f'(1)=5\). Therefore the source gives the stronger statement

\[
5^{1/4}\in\mathbb Q(J_5[8]).
\]

The manuscript's additional reference to cyclotomic adjoining is unnecessary, but harmless for its weaker conclusion in \(L_5\).

### The order-16 quotient is valid without a splitting assertion

The proof that \(K_5\subset D_J=\mathbb Q(J_5[3])\) is sound. If an element trivial on three-torsion changed \(\zeta_5\) to \(\zeta_5^a\), then their difference would act trivially. For \(a\ne1\) this difference has norm five, so it is invertible modulo three, a contradiction. The rank-one representation identifies \(U=\operatorname{Gal}(D_J/K_5)\) with a cyclic subgroup of \(\mathbb F_{81}^{\times}\). Complex conjugation is the ninth-power map, and the polarization gives \(u^{10}=\chi_3(u)\), hence \(U\subset C_{20}\).

Frobenius at eleven proves that the two-primary subgroup is all of \(C_4\). There is also a shorter field-theoretic proof: \(K_5\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)=\mathbb Q\), so the cyclotomic multiplier on \(G_{K_5}\) is onto \(\mathbb F_3^\times\). In \(C_{20}\), the equation \(u^{10}=-1\) forces a two-part of order four.

The maximal odd subgroup \(O\) of \(U\) is characteristic in \(U\), hence normal in the full Galois group. The resulting quotient \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(D_J^O/\mathbb Q)\) is an extension of \(C_4\) by \(C_4\), of order sixteen. A quotient generator inducing \(\zeta_5\mapsto\zeta_5^2\) acts on the kernel by the twenty-seventh power and therefore by inversion. Write the kernel generator as \(u\), and a lift as \(v\). Then

\[
u^4=1,\qquad vuv^{-1}=u^{-1},\qquad v^4\in\{1,u^2\}.
\]

Both possible extension classes have \(G'=\langle u^2\rangle\), central of order two. No splitting is needed. The odd subgroup fixes \(\mu_3\), so the multiplier descends to \(G\), and \(\chi_3(u)=-1\). Replacing \(v\) by \(uv\) if necessary makes its multiplier one. Every element has a normal form \(u^a v^b\), and then the multiplier kernel consists exactly of the forms with even \(a\). Hence

\[
\ker\chi_3=\langle u^2,v\rangle,
\]

which is abelian and has order eight. This proves that \(M_J\) is abelian over \(K_3\), as well as over \(K_5\). Since \(G'\ne1\), its compositum with \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\) is the unique nontrivial quadratic subextension in AF.3. This gives exactly the required containment in the actual Jacobian torsion field.

## AF.6 and AF.7

The first elliptic intersection can also be checked independently with the analogous two-quadratic-field group lemma and the dihedral field \(\mathbb Q(i,3^{1/4})\). The remaining pairwise equalities follow from the verified upper bounds and witnesses. The same dihedral field is disjoint from \(K_5\), so adjoining \(K_5\) leaves its nonabelian Galois group intact; this excludes \(3^{1/4}\) from \(K_5^{\mathrm{ab}}\) and proves the triple intersection is \(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\).

The final linear-disjointness argument is stronger than mere pairwise distinctness and is correct: the first two quadratic extensions have their biquadratic compositum inside \(L_4\), whereas the third cannot lie in \(L_4\) by the triple-intersection statement. Their compositum therefore has degree eight. No order-five frame character of the full GT group follows from this field-theoretic calculation; AF.7 states that boundary accurately.
