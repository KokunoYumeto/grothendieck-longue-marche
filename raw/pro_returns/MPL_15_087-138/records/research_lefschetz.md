## Proof A — The precise correspondence/category maps

Use the cycle convention in Jannsen, pp. 447–448. For connected smooth projective X of dimension d, the pure-motive object is \((X,p,m)\), with p an idempotent correspondence and m an integer. The defining morphism formula is
\[
\operatorname{Hom}((X,p,m),(Y,q,n))
 =q\,A^{d-m+n}(X\times Y)\,p.
\]
The product of \(\alpha\in A^{d+r}(X\times Y)\) and \(\beta\in A^{\dim Y+s}(Y\times Z)\) is
\[
\beta\circ\alpha
 =(p_{13})_*\bigl(p_{12}^*\alpha\cdot p_{23}^*\beta\bigr)
 \in A^{d+r+s}(X\times Z).
\]
Thus the graded self-correspondence ring has
\[
\mathcal E_X^{2r}=A^{d+r}(X\times X)
 =\operatorname{Hom}(h(X),h(X)(r)),
\]
and the product of two such Hom elements is \(\beta(r)\circ\alpha\). At r=0 this is the endomorphism ring of h(X). Projectors give summands \((X,p,0)\), and the integer m records the invertible Tate object. These formulas supply an actual construction relating the rings to the category, rather than an assertion that no relation exists.

For a chosen cohomology theory, a correspondence acts by
\[
\alpha_*(x)=(p_2)_*\bigl(p_1^*x\smile\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)\bigr).
\]
On cohomological degree i its target is \(H^{i+2r}(Y)(r)\); omitting the twist gives the corresponding degree-2r linear operator. The cup/push–pull compatibility of cycle classes carries correspondence composition to composition of these operators.

For the homological-to-numerical quotient: a cycle z with \(\operatorname{cl}(z)=0\) has \(\deg(z\cdot w)=0\) against every complementary cycle w, because that degree is the trace of the cup product of their cycle classes. Consequently homological-zero cycles lie inside numerical-zero cycles, so the quotient \(q\) above exists and respects pullback, intersection, and pushforward. Applying q to an idempotent preserves its equation, and applying q to the displayed composition formula proves functoriality. Explicitly,
\[
\ker\!\left(q:\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm hom}((X,p,m),(Y,q_0,n))
\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm num}((X,q(p),m),(Y,q(q_0),n))\right)
\]
is the set of homological correspondence classes in that Hom space represented by numerical-zero cycles. No identification of that kernel with zero is made for the manuscript's unspecified M.

## Proof B — Projective-line correspondences, sl₂, and the Tate group

This is an unconditional calculation over \(\mathbf C\), with rational Chow correspondences and the Tate tensor subcategory. Let h be the class of a point on \(\mathbf P^1\); its degree is one. The projective-line Chow ring is
\[
CH^*(\mathbf P^1)_{\mathbf Q}=\mathbf Q[h]/(h^2),
\qquad
CH^*(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1)_{\mathbf Q}
 =\mathbf Q[h_1,h_2]/(h_1^2,h_2^2),
\]
where \(h_a=p_a^*h\). These presentations follow from the projective-bundle formula; equivalently here, rational equivalence on \(\mathbf P^1\) identifies all degree-one points, the two rulings generate divisor classes on the product, and their transverse intersection is a point. The diagonal has class \(h_1+h_2\).

Act on \(V=\mathbf Q\,1\oplus\mathbf Q\,h\) by \(z_*(x)=(p_2)_*(p_1^*x\cdot z)\). Integration along the first factor sends \(h_1\) to 1 and 1 to 0. It therefore gives the following exact table:

| Cycle z | \(z_*(1)\) | \(z_*(h)\) | Matrix in the ordered basis \((1,h)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(h_1\) | 1 | 0 | \(\pi_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) |
| \(h_2\) | 0 | h | \(\pi_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) |
| \(h_1h_2\) | h | 0 | \(L=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\) |
| 1 | 0 | 1 | \(F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) |

The four cycle classes are a basis and their four matrices are the matrix units; the action map is therefore an algebra isomorphism from the total correspondence ring, with correspondence composition, to \(\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbf Q)\). In particular \(LF=\pi_2\), \(FL=\pi_0\), and
\[
H=[L,F]=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
[H,L]=2L,\qquad [H,F]=-2F.
\]
This is the two-dimensional Lefschetz representation of sl₂, and its torus is \(\tau(s)=\operatorname{diag}(s^{-1},s)\).

The projector \(\pi_0\) identifies its motive summand with \(\mathbf1\): the two correspondence maps are the fundamental class and a degree-one point. Their composite on \(\mathbf1\) has degree one, and their opposite composite is \(h_1\). The analogous two maps with the Tate degree shifted have opposite composite \(h_2\) and identify the other summand with \(\mathbb L=\mathbf Q(-1)\) in the convention of Proof A. Thus
\[
h(\mathbf P^1)=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbb L.
\]
The Hom formula gives
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb L^a,\mathbb L^b)
 =CH^{a-b}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C)_{\mathbf Q}
 =\begin{cases}\mathbf Q&a=b,\\0&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\]
This makes its Tate-generated tensor category the category of finite-dimensional integer-graded rational vector spaces, with degree-preserving maps, tensor product adding degrees, and \(\mathbb L\) in generator degree one.

Fix a fibre functor taking \(\mathbb L\) to a one-dimensional rational vector space. For every rational algebra R, a tensor automorphism is determined by multiplication by \(t\in R^\times\) on that space: tensor products give \(t^a\) on \(\mathbb L^a\), duals give negative powers, and naturality for all inclusions, projections, and scalar matrices gives that same scalar on every copy of the weight. Conversely these scalars define a tensor automorphism. Hence the tensor-automorphism group is \(\mathbf G_m\), and its action on V is exactly
\[
\rho(t)=\operatorname{diag}(1,t).
\]
The Lie algebra image is \(\{\operatorname{diag}(0,a):a\in\mathbf Q\}\). The computed L is outside this image; more intrinsically, any tensor automorphism fixes the inclusion of \(\mathbf1\), and its infinitesimal action therefore kills 1, whereas \(L(1)=h\).

This also works when viewing the Tate subcategory inside a larger category: restriction sends a tensor automorphism to the just-computed Tate automorphism. Therefore the failure applies to a proposed subgroup realizing these particular Lefschetz operators on \(h(\mathbf P^1)\). It supplies no assertion about arbitrary abstract subgroups of other groups.

Now use cohomological weights, so \(\mathbf1\) has weight zero and \(\mathbb L\) weight two. The weight cocharacter and its relation to the sl₂ torus are
\[
j(s)=\rho(s^2)=\operatorname{diag}(1,s^2)
 =s\,\mathrm{id}_V\,\tau(s),\qquad dj=H+\mathrm{id}_V.
\]
For the character \(\chi(t)=t\) on \(\mathbb L\), \(\chi\circ j(s)=s^2\). The source writes a rank-one object of degree \(\alpha\) on cote 15 scan 090 and writes \(\alpha=2\) on scan 094; the computation matches that formal relation without changing its notation Q(1) to a modern sign convention.

Finally, the codimensions in the table give the motive maps
\[
L:h(\mathbf P^1)\longrightarrow h(\mathbf P^1)(1)
  =h(\mathbf P^1)\otimes\mathbb L^{-1},
\quad
F:h(\mathbf P^1)\longrightarrow h(\mathbf P^1)(-1)
  =h(\mathbf P^1)\otimes\mathbb L .
\]
Tensor naturality says
\[
\bigl(\rho(t)t^{-1}\bigr)L=L\rho(t),
\qquad
\bigl(\rho(t)t\bigr)F=F\rho(t),
\]
and therefore
\[
\rho(t)L\rho(t)^{-1}=tL,\qquad
\rho(t)F\rho(t)^{-1}=t^{-1}F.
\]
This proves the exact relationship between the tensor group, Tate character, and the operators in the total correspondence ring.

## Proof C — Explicit comparison of the two lowering operators

Define the concrete rational vector space \(S_r=\bigoplus_{a=0}^r\mathbf Qv_a\). Put \(v_{-1}=v_{r+1}=0\), and define
\[
Lv_a=v_{a+1},\quad Uv_a=v_{a-1},\quad
