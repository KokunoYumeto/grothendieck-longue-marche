## CM01 — The unique-prime NB supplies every finite valuation needed for the root-of-unity conclusion

**Location / kind:** cote 15, `pages/MPL_15_087-138_15_scan096.tex`, line 13; live proof completed by explicit valuations, confidence high. **Quote:** “donc \(\mu\) est une unité … donc une racine de l'unité”. **Context / register:** CT 16/021 gives the Tate roots-of-unity inverse limit; the current NB supplies the finite-prime calculation for the scalar factor in the eigenvalue; relevant register lead MPL_14_002-044 B04 and the ambient CM setting of LM6 A.22.

In the written Galois CM field \(L/\mathbb Q\), complex conjugation \(c\) commutes with every automorphism because each embedding carries the unique involution over the totally real subfield to ordinary complex conjugation; hence the unique prime above the chosen \(\mathfrak p_0\) is fixed by \(c\), and Galois transitivity makes every prime above the rational \(p\) fixed by \(c\). For \(\mu=\lambda^2/p^i\), the relation \(\mu c\mu=1\) now gives \(2v_{\mathfrak P}(\mu)=0\) at all \(\mathfrak P\mid p\), while integrality of \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda c\lambda=p^i\) force both nonnegative valuations of \(\lambda,c\lambda\) to vanish away from \(p\). Thus \(\mu\) is an algebraic unit with every conjugate of modulus one, and the monic polynomials of its powers have bounded integer coefficients, proving that only finitely many powers occur. Consequently \(\mu\) and \(u=\lambda/p^{i/2}\), with \(u^2=\mu\), are roots of unity, exactly as the NB asserts.

## CM02 — Divisors prove the valuation functional's minimal field of rationality

**Location / kind:** cote 15, `pages/MPL_15_087-138_15_scan110.tex`, lines 17–29, with scan 096 lines 3–10 and cancelled scan 100 lines 24–28; live unproved claim and normalization check, confidence high from explicit evaluations. **Quote:** “\(L_0\) est le plus petit corps de rationalité de \(\nu\)”. **Context / register:** no decomposition-cocharacter construction occurs in CT under the recorded searches; CT 16/021 supplies the nearest Tate-module object, while these pages add valuation descent; relevant MPL_14_002-044 B04 and the CM quotient in LM6 A.22.

For the integer-valued divisor valuation \(\nu_{\mathfrak P}(m)=v_{\mathfrak P}(m)\), the action is \((g\nu_{\mathfrak P})(m)=v_{\mathfrak P}(g^{-1}m)=v_{g\mathfrak P}(m)\). The finite-index divisor image proved on 096 supplies a norm-one \(p\)-unit \(u_{\mathfrak P}\) with divisor \(h(\mathfrak P-c\mathfrak P)\), \(h>0\), whose evaluations at \(\mathfrak P,c\mathfrak P\), and all other primes are respectively \(h,-h,0\), so the valuation functionals distinguish every prime under 110's splitting assumption. Therefore \(\operatorname{Stab}(\nu_{\mathfrak P})=D_{\mathfrak P}\), and the fixed field \(L^{D_{\mathfrak P}}\) is precisely the minimal field of definition of that functional and of its integrally normalized cocharacter. The scale requires attention: the cancelled convention \(v_{\mathfrak P}(p)=1\) on 100 gives \(v_{\mathfrak P}(\sqrt5)=1/2\) in \(L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,i)\), \(p=5\), while \(\sqrt5\,c(\sqrt5)=5\) places this element in \(M(5)\); the integral valuation there is \(2v_{\mathfrak P}\).

## CM02b — The conjugate valuations have explicit cokernels on the whole eigenvalue lattice

**Location / kind:** cote 15, `pages/MPL_15_087-138_15_scan110.tex`, line 21, with scan 114 line 3; live “mod torsion” qualification made explicit, own calculation with high confidence. **Quote:** “engendre … \(\check M\) mod. torsion”. **Context / register:** the norm-one class-group example is proved in research_frobenius.md, FR14; CT has no matching integral eigenvalue-lattice computation, and this is additional arithmetic information for the weight lead MPL_14_002-044 B04 and the CM setting named in LM6 A.22.

In \(L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})\), put \(\alpha=(3+\sqrt{-23})/2\), \(\beta=\alpha/2\), and \(u=\alpha/\bar\alpha\); their norms are \(8,2,1\), and \(2=\beta^2/u\). The order-three ideal class exhibited in FR14 makes the principal 2-divisor lattice exactly \(I=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:a-b\equiv0\pmod3\}\), and the vectors \(\operatorname{div}\beta=(2,-1)\), \(\operatorname{div}u=(3,-3)\) form its basis, giving \(M(2)/\{\pm1\}=\langle\beta,u\rangle_{\mathbb Z}\). On this basis the conjugate valuations have matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\-1&-3\end{pmatrix}\), determinant \(-3\), so their integral span in \(\operatorname{Hom}(M(2)/\{\pm1\},\mathbb Z)\) has cokernel \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\), whereas rationalization gives the whole dual; their restrictions to the weight-zero generator \(u\) are \(3,-3\). For \(q=8\), the further norm condition \(a+b\equiv0\pmod3\) gives \(I_8=3\mathbb Z^2\), with basis \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\), so the conjugate valuation matrix is \(3I_2\) and the integral cokernel is \((\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^2\).

## CM03 — The completed local ring is \(\mathbb Z_p\) through the decomposition-field map

**Location / kind:** cote 15, `pages/MPL_15_087-138_15_scan110.tex`, lines 26–31; live unproved claim completed by a local-degree calculation, confidence high. **Quote:** “l'anneau local complété … isom. à celui de `\mathbb Z_{\unsure{\ell|p}}`”. **Context / register:** CT has no decomposition-field passage under the recorded searches; current 110 contributes the local realization of the CM valuation; relevant MPL_14_002-044 B04 and the ambient group in LM6 A.22.

Put \(D=D_{\mathfrak P}\), \(L_0=L^D\), and \(\mathfrak P_0=\mathfrak P\cap L_0\); extension to completions is the isomorphism \(D\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\mathfrak P}/\mathbb Q_p)\), and \(\mathfrak P\) is the unique prime of \(L\) above \(\mathfrak P_0\) [ANT, Propositions 8.10–8.11(a), pp.139–140]. Applying this same map to \(L/L_0\) gives both \([L_{\mathfrak P}:\mathbb Q_p]=|D|\) and \([L_{\mathfrak P}:(L_0)_{\mathfrak P_0}]=|D|\), hence \((L_0)_{\mathfrak P_0}=\mathbb Q_p\) and \(\widehat{(\mathcal O_{L_0})_{\mathfrak P_0}}=\mathbb Z_p\). The assumption \(c\mathfrak P\ne\mathfrak P\) gives \(c\notin D\); centrality of \(c\) then makes every conjugate of \(D\) omit \(c\), which excludes a real embedding of \(L_0\). Conjugating \(\mathfrak P\) conjugates the pointed field \((L_0,\mathfrak P_0)\), and two such conjugations differ by \(D\), whose elements act trivially on \(L_0\), proving the written uniqueness of that pointed-field isomorphism.

## CM04 — The nonzero coinvariant character is explicitly restriction to \(i\)

**Location / kind:** cote 15, `pages/MPL_15_087-138_15_scan128.tex`, lines 14–21, and `pages/MPL_15_087-138_15_scan129.tex`, lines 2–18; live quotient calculation completed, confidence high. **Quote:** “nécessairement égal à \(H_G\)”. **Context / register:** CT 16/021's Tate inverse limit is the nearby roots-of-unity construction; CT and CN contain no matching coinvariant calculation; relevant MPL_14_002-044 B04 and LM6 A.22, whose subgroup-existence problem remains open in the register.

Write \(\Pi=G_{\mathbb Q}\), \(N=\overline{\langle gcg^{-1}:g\in\Pi\rangle}\), \(\mathbb K=\overline{\mathbb Q}^{N}\), \(H=N/\overline{[N,N]}\), and \(G=\Pi/N\). The conjugates of \(c\) have order two and topologically generate \(N\), so \(2H=0\), while their images coincide in the continuous coinvariants \(H_G=H/\overline{\langle gh-h\rangle}\), making \(H_G\) a quotient of \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). Every conjugate of \(c\) sends \(i\) to \(-i\), so restriction to \(\mathbb K(i)/\mathbb K\) supplies a nonzero \(G\)-invariant map \(H\to\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), proving \(H_G\simeq\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) and identifying its corresponding field as \(\mathbb L=\mathbb K(i)\).

## CM05 — The central-conjugation quotient and the subfield inclusion are explicit

**Location / kind:** cote 15, `pages/MPL_15_087-138_15_scan130.tex`, lines 2–14, following scans 128–129; live maximality claim completed by quotient and field maps, confidence high. **Quote:** “l'image de \(\tau\) … soit centrale”. **Context / register:** CT and CN have no corresponding complex-conjugation quotient under the searches below; these pages supply its exact construction, relevant to MPL_14_002-044 B04 and the full CM ambient quotient named in LM6 A.22.

Let \(C\) be the closed normal subgroup of \(\Pi\) generated by \([g,c]\) for \(g\in\Pi\); the image of \(N\) in \(\Pi/C\) is generated by the single central involution \(c\), so \([\overline{\mathbb Q}^{C}:\mathbb K]\le2\), while every automorphism of \(\mathbb K(i)\) commutes with the involution fixing \(\mathbb K\) and sending \(i\) to \(-i\), giving \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{C}=\mathbb K(i)\). For a subfield \(F\subset\mathbb K(i)\), centrality makes its fixing subgroup stable under \(c\), hence \(cF=F\), \(F^c=F\cap\mathbb K\), and \([F:F^c]\le2\). Each embedding \(\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) satisfies \(\sigma(cx)=\overline{\sigma(x)}\), so \(F^c\) is totally real and a nontrivial \(c\)-action excludes every real embedding of \(F\). Conversely, a quadratic totally imaginary extension \(E/F^+\) of a totally real algebraic field can be written \(E=F^+(z)\), \(z^2=-d\), with \(d\) totally positive; every conjugate of \(\sqrt d\) is real, so \(\sqrt d\in\mathbb K\) and the actual inclusion is given by \(z=\pm i\sqrt d\in\mathbb K(i)\).
