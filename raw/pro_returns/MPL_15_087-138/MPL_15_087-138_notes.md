## Proof A— The precise correspondence/category maps

Use the cycle convention in Jannsen, pp. 447–448. For connected smooth projective X of dimension d, the pure-motive object is \((X,p,m)\), with p an idempotent correspondence and m an integer. The defining morphism formula is
\[
\operatorname{Hom}((X,p,m),(Y,q,n))
 =q\,A^{d-m+n}(X\times Y)\,p.
\]
The product of \(\alpha\in A^{d+r}(X\times Y)\) and \(\beta\in A^{\dim Y+s}(Y\times Z)\) is
\[
\beta\circ\alpha
 =(p_{13})_*\bigl(p_{12}^*\alpha\cdot p_{23}^*\beta\bigr)

\]
Thus the graded self-correspondence ring has
\[
\mathcal E_X^{2r}=A^{d+r}(X\times X)
 =\operatorname{Hom}(h(X),h(X)(r)),
\]
and the product of two such Hom elements is \(\beta(r)\circ\alpha\). At r=0 this is the endomorphism ring of h(X). Projectors give summands \((X,p,0)\), and the integer m records the invertible Tate object. These formulas supply an actual construction relating the rings to the category, rather than an assertion that no relation exists.

For a chosen cohomology theory, a correspondence acts by
\[
\alpha_*(x)=(p_2)_*\bigl(p_1^*x\smile\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)\bigr).
\]
On cohomological degree i its target is \(H^{i+2r}(Y)(r)\); omitting the twist gives the corresponding degree-2r linear operator. The cup/push–pull compatibility of cycle classes carries correspondence composition to composition of these operators.

For the homological-to-numerical quotient: a cycle z with \(\operatorname{cl}(z)=0\) has \(\deg(z\cdot w)=0\) against every complementary cycle w, because that degree is the trace of the cup product of their cycle classes. Consequently homological-zero cycles lie inside numerical-zero cycles, so the quotient \(q\) above exists and respects pullback, intersection, and pushforward. ## Proof B — Projective-line correspondences, sl₂, and the Tate group

This is an unconditional calculation over \(\mathbf C\), with rational Chow correspondences and the Tate tensor subcategory. Let h be the class of a point on \(\mathbf P^1\); its degree is one. The projective-line Chow ring is
\[
CH^*(\mathbf P^1)_{\mathbf Q}=\mathbf Q[h]/(h^2),
\qquad
CH^*(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1)_{\mathbf Q}
 =\mathbf Q[h_1,h_2]/(h_1^2,h_2^2),
\]
where \(h_a=p_a^*h\). These presentations follow from the projective-bundle formula; equivalently here, rational equivalence on \(\mathbf P^1\) identifies all degree-one points, the two rulings generate divisor classes on the product, and their transverse intersection is a point. The diagonal has class \(h_1+h_2\).

Act on \(V=\mathbf Q\,1\oplus\mathbf Q\,h\) by \(z_*(x)=(p_2)_*(p_1^*x\cdot z)\). Integration along the first factor sends \(h_1\) to 1 and 1 to 0. It therefore gives the following exact table:

| Cycle z | \(z_*(1)\) | \(z_*(h)\) | Matrix in the ordered basis \((1,h)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(h_1\) | 1 | 0 | \(\pi_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) |
| \(h_2\) | 0 | h | \(\pi_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) |
| \(h_1h_2\) | h | 0 | \(L=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\) |
| 1 | 0 | 1 | \(F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) |

The four cycle classes are a basis and their four matrices are the matrix units; the action map is therefore an algebra isomorphism from the total correspondence ring, with correspondence composition, to \(\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbf Q)\). In particular \(LF=\pi_2\), \(FL=\pi_0\), and
\[
H=[L,F]=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
[H,L]=2L,\qquad [H,F]=-2F.
\]
This is the two-dimensional Lefschetz representation of sl₂, and its torus is \(\tau(s)=\operatorname{diag}(s^{-1},s)\).

The projector \(\pi_0\) identifies its motive summand with \(\mathbf1\): the two correspondence maps are the fundamental class and a degree-one point. Their composite on \(\mathbf1\) has degree one, and their opposite composite is \(h_1\). The analogous two maps with the Tate degree shifted have opposite composite \(h_2\) and identify the other summand with \(\mathbb L=\mathbf Q(-1)\) in the convention of Proof A. Thus
\[
h(\mathbf P^1)=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbb L.
\]
The Hom formula gives
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb L^a,\mathbb L^b)
 =CH^{a-b}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C)_{\mathbf Q}
 =\begin{cases}\mathbf Q&a=b,\\0&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\]
This makes its Tate-generated tensor category the category of finite-dimensional integer-graded rational vector spaces, with degree-preserving maps, tensor product adding degrees, and \(\mathbb L\) in generator degree one.

Fix a fibre functor taking \(\mathbb L\) to a one-dimensional rational vector space. For every rational algebra R, a tensor automorphism is determined by multiplication by \(t\in R^\times\) on that space: tensor products give \(t^a\) on \(\mathbb L^a\), duals give negative powers, and naturality for all inclusions, projections, and scalar matrices gives that same scalar on every copy of the weight. Conversely these scalars define a tensor automorphism. Hence the tensor-automorphism group is \(\mathbf G_m\), and its action on V is exactly
\[
\rho(t)=\operatorname{diag}(1,t).
\]
The Lie algebra image is \(\{\operatorname{diag}(0,a):a\in\mathbf Q\}\). The computed L is outside this image; more intrinsically, any tensor automorphism fixes the inclusion of \(\mathbf1\), and its infinitesimal action therefore kills 1, whereas \(L(1)=h\).

This also works when viewing the Tate subcategory inside a larger category: restriction sends a tensor automorphism to the just-computed Tate automorphism. Therefore the failure applies to a proposed subgroup realizing these particular Lefschetz operators on \(h(\mathbf P^1)\). It supplies no assertion about arbitrary abstract subgroups of other groups.

Now use cohomological weights, so \(\mathbf1\) has weight zero and \(\mathbb L\) weight two. The weight cocharacter and its relation to the sl₂ torus are
\[
j(s)=\rho(s^2)=\operatorname{diag}(1,s^2)
 =s\,\mathrm{id}_V\,\tau(s),\qquad dj=H+\mathrm{id}_V.
\]
For the character \(\chi(t)=t\) on \(\mathbb L\), \(\chi\circ j(s)=s^2\). The source writes a rank-one object of degree \(\alpha\) on cote 15 scan 090 and writes \(\alpha=2\) on scan 094; the computation matches that formal relation without changing its notation Q(1) to a modern sign convention.

Finally, the codimensions in the table give the motive maps
\[
L:h(\mathbf P^1)\longrightarrow h(\mathbf P^1)(1)
  =h(\mathbf P^1)\otimes\mathbb L^{-1},
\quad
F:h(\mathbf P^1)\longrightarrow h(\mathbf P^1)(-1)
  =h(\mathbf P^1)\otimes\mathbb L .
\]
Tensor naturality says
\[
\bigl(\rho(t)t^{-1}\bigr)L=L\rho(t),
\qquad
\bigl(\rho(t)t\bigr)F=F\rho(t),
\]
and therefore

\rho(t)L\rho(t)^{-1}=tL,\qquad
\rho(t)F\rho(t)^{-1}=t^{-1}F.
\]
This proves the exact relationship between the tensor group, Tate character, and the operators in the total correspondence ring.

## Proof C — Explicit comparison of the two lowering operators

Define the concrete rational vector space \(S_r=\bigoplus_{a=0}^r\mathbf Qv_a\). Put \(v_{-1}=v_{r+1}=0\), and define
\[
Lv_a=v_{a+1},\quad Uv_a=v_{a-1},\quad
Fv_a=a(r-a+1)v_{a-1},\quad Hv_a=(2a-r)v_a.
\]
The unit-step operators satisfy \(LU=1-e_0\) and \(UL=1-e_r\), exactly the endpoint shape of the written identities (6.15). Direct evaluation gives
\[
[L,F]v_a=
\{a(r-a+1)-(a+1)(r-a)\}v_a
=(2a-r)v_a,
\]
including the endpoints because the missing terms have zero coefficient. Evaluating H before and after the raising or lowering step similarly gives \([H,L]=2L\) and \([H,F]=-2F\).

Define diagonal operators D and \(D^\#\) by
\[
Dv_a=a(r-a+1)v_a,\qquad
D^\#v_a=
\begin{cases}
0&a=0,\\
\dfrac1{a(r-a+1)}v_a&1\le a\le r.
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(F=UD\) and \(U=FD^\#\), as evaluation on every \(v_a\) proves. The diagonal projectors are the explicit polynomials
\[
e_a=\prod_{0\le b\le r,\ b\ne a}
 \frac{H-(2b-r)}{2(a-b)},
\]
so both D and \(D^\#\) are rational polynomials in H. This is the exact rational operator conversion. For the cohomological degrees \(i+2a\) and \(r=n-i\), H acts by \(i+2a-n\), proving the centred grading formula \(H=W-n\,\mathrm{id}\) in this concrete model.

For completeness, take \(S=\bigoplus_{r=0}^n S_r\) with the blockwise operators just defined. The element
\[
C=H^2+2H+4FL
\]
acts on \(S_r\) by \(r(r+2)\), as expansion on \(v_a\) shows. The polynomials
\[
p_r=\prod_{\substack{0\le s\le n\\s\ne r}}
 \frac{C-s(s+2)}{r(r+2)-s(s+2)}
\]
therefore project onto its different blocks. Within a block, the \(e_a\) above and the operators \(L^{b-a}e_a\) for \(b\ge a\), or
\[
\left(\prod_{k=b+1}^{a} k(r-k+1)\right)^{-1}F^{a-b}e_a
\quad(b<a),
\]
are all its matrix units. Thus the image of \(U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\) on this explicitly defined S is
\[
\prod_{r=0}^n\operatorname{Mat}_{r+1}(\mathbf Q).
\]
This computes the product of matrix algebras appearing in RESULTS_STATE **WG2 §2.3 #2** for the explicitly defined finite-string representation S. The source's particular exterior-algebra representation and its written integral order require their own comparison maps; no identification of either with S is asserted here.

The integral test can also be made explicit. In the ordinary exterior algebra on \(e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2\), put \(a=e_1\wedge f_1\), \(b=e_2\wedge f_2\), and \(\xi=a+b\). Since a and b have even exterior degree, \(ab=ba\), while \(a^2=b^2=0\), so

## Proof D — Semisimplicity, centres, conjugation, and the determinant

Let \(\rho:V\to V\otimes\mathbf Q[t,t^{-1}]\) be a rational representation of \(\mathbf G_m\). Write the finite Laurent expansion \(\rho(v)=\sum_n v_n\otimes t^n\). Coassociativity gives \(\rho(v_n)=v_n\otimes t^n\), and the counit gives \(v=\sum_n v_n\). Uniqueness of Laurent coefficients gives \(V=\bigoplus_n V_n\). Every subrepresentation W has \(W=\bigoplus_n(W\cap V_n)\); choose a vector-space complement in each Vn and sum those complements to split W equivariantly. This proves semisimplicity directly.

Every pair of points of \(\mathbf G_m\) over every coefficient algebra commutes. Its centre is consequently all of \(\mathbf G_m\), agreeing with the group computed by the tensor-automorphism functor in Proof B.

For the group-theoretic comparison, define \(c(g)(x)=gxg^{-1}\). The equation \(c(g)=\mathrm{id}\) is exactly the equation \(gx=xg\) for every x, so \(\ker c=Z(P)\). For the quotient homomorphism \(q:P\to P^{\mathrm{ab}}\),
\[
q(c(g)(x))=q(g)q(x)q(g)^{-1}=q(x).
\]
The same computation applies to the topological abelianization of a profinite group, with the commutator subgroup replaced by its closure.

For RESULTS_STATE **LM1 A.8**, compute only the requested additional consequence. Scalar centrality makes \(\theta(g)=\det(g)^{-1}g\) multiplicative, \(\theta^2=\mathrm{id}\), and \(\theta\) fixes determinant-one matrices. In rank two,

\]
Thus the specified character \(\varepsilon=\det\) pulls back to \(\varepsilon^{-1}\). On \(g=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\) their values are 2 and \(1/2\), proving that preserving this character excludes this particular automorphism.

## Proof E — Rational Artin correspondences and the arithmetic quotient

### 1. Rational zero-dimensional correspondences and their matrices

Fix the separable closure \(k^{\mathrm{sep}}\), the profinite group \(\Gamma_k\), and finite étale \(k\)-schemes \(X,Y\). The symbols \(\mathbb Q^{S_X}\) and \(\mathbb Q[S_X]\) are identified by the delta-function basis \(e_x\), with \(\gamma e_x=e_{\gamma x}\). This is a rational permutation representation, independently of the characteristic or coefficient field of \(k\).

The closed points of \(X\times_kY\) correspond to the \(\Gamma_k\)-orbits in \(S_X\times S_Y\). This zero-dimensional scheme has no positive-dimensional subvariety supporting a rational-equivalence relation between zero-cycles, so
\[
CH_0(X\times_kY)_{\mathbb Q}
=
\bigoplus_{O\in\Gamma_k\backslash(S_X\times S_Y)}\mathbb Q[O].
\]
The base change of a closed point is the sum of its distinct geometric points, each with multiplicity one, because the scheme is étale.

Use the convention that a correspondence \(X\to Y\) has matrix rows indexed by \(y\in S_Y\) and columns by \(x\in S_X\). Define
\[
R_{XY}\left(\sum_O a_O[O]\right)(e_x)
=
\sum_{y\in S_Y}a_{O(x,y)}e_y.
\]
A rational matrix \(B=(b_{y,x})\) intertwines the permutation actions precisely when
\[
b_{\gamma y,\gamma x}=b_{y,x}
\quad(\gamma\in\Gamma_k),
\]
so the characteristic matrices of the orbits give a basis of the target. Therefore \(R_{XY}\) is an isomorphism, with inverse obtained by reading the common entry on each orbit.

For correspondences \(B:X\to Y\) and \(C:Y\to Z\), the pullback–intersection–pushforward definition of composition gives, at the geometric pair \((x,z)\),
\[
(C\circ B)_{z,x}=\sum_{y\in S_Y}C_{z,y}B_{y,x}.
\]
Over a separable closure this is the direct count of the intermediate points; descent gives the same formula over \(k\), with residue-field pushforward degrees counting those geometric points. The diagonal of \(X\) gives the identity matrix. The basis map
\[
e_{(x,y)}\longmapsto e_x\otimes e_y
\]
identifies the correspondence tensor product for \(X\times_kY\) with the representation tensor product; the unit is \(\operatorname{Spec}k\), and transpose matrices implement duals through the delta-basis pairing. Graphs and transposed graphs give the corresponding pushforward and pullback conventions explicitly.

Define \(\operatorname{Art}(k)\) to be the additive idempotent completion of this rational correspondence category. Its extended realization \(R\) remains fully faithful: on objects \((X,p)\), \((Y,q)\), both sides restrict \(R_{XY}\) to the subspace \(q\,\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)\,p\).

### 2. Every finite-image rational representation is obtained

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional continuous rational representation of \(\Gamma_k\) with finite image; it factors through \(H=\Gamma_k/N\) for an open normal subgroup \(N\). The corresponding finite Galois extension \(l=(k^{\mathrm{sep}})^N\) gives \(X=\operatorname{Spec}l\), whose geometric points form the regular \(H\)-set, so \(R(hX)\simeq\mathbb Q[H]\).

Choose a rational basis \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\) of \(V\). The map
\[
\theta:\mathbb Q[H]^{\oplus d}\longrightarrow V,\qquad
\theta\bigl((a_{h,j})_{h,j}\bigr)=
\sum_{h\in H,\;1\le j\le d}a_{h,j}\,h v_j
\]
is an \(H\)-equivariant surjection. Choose a rational-linear section \(s\) and form the explicit average
\[
s_H(v)=\frac1{|H|}\sum_{h\in H}h\,s(h^{-1}v).
\]
Then \(s_H\) is \(H\)-equivariant and \(\theta s_H=1_V\), so \(p=s_H\theta\) satisfies \(p^2=p\) and has image isomorphic to \(V\). The orbit-matrix isomorphism represents \(p\) by a rational correspondence on the disjoint union of \(d\) copies of \(X\). The object defined by that idempotent realizes \(V\), proving essential surjectivity without assuming that every representation is itself a permutation representation.

The displayed construction proves the tensor equivalence
\[
R:\operatorname{Art}(k)\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{Rep}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{\mathbb Q}(\Gamma_k),
\]
where the superscript means finite-image continuous representations. Continuity uses the discrete topology on the finite-dimensional rational vector space, equivalently on its representation matrices.

### 3. Tensor automorphisms over a general rational coefficient algebra

Let \(A\) be a commutative \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra and first restrict to \(\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb Q}(H)\) for a finite quotient \(H\). Naturality with respect to the maps
\[
\mathbb Q[H]\longrightarrow V,\qquad h\longmapsto hv
\]
shows that every natural endomorphism of the underlying-vector-space functor after extension to \(A\) acts on all representations by a single element \(a\in A[H]\). Write
\[
a=\sum_{h\in H}e_hh.
\]
Tensor compatibility and the unit condition say
\[
\Delta(a)=a\otimes a,\qquad \epsilon(a)=1.
\]
Comparing the coefficients of \(h\otimes g\) gives exactly
\[
e_h^2=e_h,\qquad e_he_g=0\ (h\ne g),\qquad
\sum_{h\in H}e_h=1.
\]
These equations also give the inverse \(\sum_he_hh^{-1}\). Such an orthogonal idempotent partition describes a locally constant map \(\operatorname{Spec}A\to H\), which is precisely an \(A\)-point of the constant finite group scheme \(\underline H_{\mathbb Q}\).

All objects of \(\operatorname{Rep}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{\mathbb Q}(\Gamma_k)\) factor through finite quotients, and the constructions agree under refinement of those quotients. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega)
=
\varprojlim_{N\triangleleft_{\mathrm{open}}\Gamma_k}
\underline{\Gamma_k/N}_{\mathbb Q}
=
\underline{\Gamma_k}_{\mathbb Q}.
\]
In particular its \(\mathbb Q\)-points recover \(\Gamma_k\); the general \(A\)-point description keeps track of the locally constant choices on components.

### 5. Base field and coefficient field

Here \(k\) is the field over which the finite étale schemes are defined; \(\mathbb Q\) is the coefficient field of correspondences and representations.
