# Independent review of two proposed counterexamples

Date: 2026-11-18. Reviewer: `/root/jacobians_earlier_forms` (ChatGPT, GPT-6 Astra Pro). This is a mathematical check of the parent's proposed examples against frozen TeX passages; no manuscript scan was consulted and no transcription, inventory, doubts, notes, or CHECK_EVENTS file was changed.

**Verdict:** both examples are valid for the unqualified readings stated below. The first distinguishes two adjacent assertions: weakening normality to regularity in codimension one fails for the preceding rational-function/divisor criterion, but does suffice for the following proper-target rational-map theorem. The second disproves “Cohen–Macaulay means free” for arbitrary finite modules over a regular local ring when “codim coh” means depth; the correct condition is maximal Cohen–Macaulay.

## 1. The first N.B. on scan 170: codimension-one regularity does not recover all functions

**Frozen locators:** `pages/MPL_57_133-179_57_scan169.tex`, lines 4, 10–26; `pages/MPL_57_133-179_57_scan170.tex`, lines 3–7. The initial hypothesis at 57:169 is normality; its theorem says that `div f ≥ 0` is equivalent to global regularity. The next page says the undefined locus is the union of the supports where the valuations are negative and is consequently pure of dimension `n−1`; its first N.B. proposes that the preceding results remain essentially valid if the singular locus has dimension at most `n−2`.

### Construction and checks

Let k be any field, put

\[
B=k[x,y],\qquad \mathfrak n=(x,y)B,\qquad
A=k+\mathfrak n^2\subset B,
\]

and write \(\mathfrak m=\mathfrak n^2\), now regarded as an ideal of A. Thus A consists precisely of polynomials whose homogeneous part of degree one vanishes; \(A/\mathfrak m=k\).

1. **A is a finite-type domain.** It is the subalgebra
   \[
   k[x^2,xy,y^2,x^3,x^2y,xy^2,y^3].
   \]
   Every monomial of degree at least four is a product of monomials of degrees at least two, so the displayed generators give all of A. Being a subring of B, A is a domain.

2. **Its fraction field is k(x,y).** In the fraction field of A,
   \[
   x=x^3/x^2,\qquad y=y^3/y^2.
   \]
   Both numerators and denominators belong to A, and A is already contained in k[x,y].

3. **B is finite over A and is its normalization.** As an A-module, B is generated by \(1,x,y\): the only terms of a polynomial not already in A are its degree-one terms. Consequently B is integral over A; alternatively x and y satisfy the monic equations \(T^2-x^2\) and \(T^2-y^2\). Since B is integrally closed in the common fraction field, any element integral over A is also integral over B and lies in B, proving that B is exactly the normalization.

4. **X=Spec A has dimension two and the origin has codimension two.** The finite integral extension \(A\subset B\) gives \(\dim A=\dim B=2\). Explicitly, \((0)\subsetneq ((x)B\cap A)\subsetneq\mathfrak m\) is a chain of prime ideals, so \(\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak m=2\).

5. **The complement of the origin is covered by D(x²) and D(y²).** A prime containing x² and y² contains all seven displayed positive-degree generators, since a power of each belongs to \((x^2,y^2)A\). Hence \(\sqrt{(x^2,y^2)A}=\mathfrak m\). On these two opens,
   \[
   A_{x^2}=B_{x^2},\qquad A_{y^2}=B_{y^2},
   \]
   because the first contains \(x=x^3/x^2\), \(y=x^2y/x^2\), and the second contains \(x=xy^2/y^2\), \(y=y^3/y^2\). Thus X is regular, indeed smooth over k, away from the origin.

6. **The origin is genuinely singular.** As k-vector spaces, \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) consists of degree-two and degree-three polynomials, so it has dimension \(3+4=7\). Localization does not change this tangent-space quotient, whereas \(\dim A_{\mathfrak m}=2\); hence this local ring is not regular. The singular locus is exactly this one closed point, of codimension two.

7. **The rational function f=x is regular at every height-one point.** All height-one points lie in the regular open from step 5, on which x is a regular function. Their local rings are DVRs, so \(v_{\mathfrak p}(x)\geq0\) for every height-one prime; the divisorial expression used by the manuscript therefore exists and has no negative coefficient. In fact it is the effective prime divisor corresponding to \((x)B\cap A\), with coefficient one, since near that generic point the ring is the ordinary polynomial ring localized at (x).

8. **The function is not regular at the origin.** If \(x=a/s\in A_{\mathfrak m}\) with \(a\in A\) and \(s\in A\setminus\mathfrak m\), write \(s=c+h\) with \(c\in k^\times\) and \(h\in\mathfrak n^2\). Then \(a=sx=cx+hx\) has the nonzero linear term cx, contradicting \(a\in A\). Thus \(x\notin A_{\mathfrak m}\).

These checks prove that the undefined locus of \(x:X\dashrightarrow\mathbb A^1\) is exactly \(\{\mathfrak m\}\), although its negative-valuation locus is empty. Therefore the literal unqualified extension proposed in the first N.B. fails both for the implication `div f ≥ 0 ⇒ f regular` and for the pure-codimension-one description of the undefined locus. **Confidence: high**, by the explicit localization and degree-one-term calculations; this does not rule out an intended version that allows an additional codimension-two exceptional set under “sans changement essentiel.”

The structural distinction is \((R_1)\) versus normality: the example has regular local rings in codimension one, but is not normal. Serre's criterion requires both \((R_1)\) and \((S_2)\), as stated in the primary [Stacks Project, Lemma 28.12.5, Tag 033P](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/033P). The example itself, rather than that criterion alone, supplies the counterexample to the manuscript's proposed weakening.

### Why the following proper-target theorem survives

**Frozen locators:** `pages/MPL_57_133-179_57_scan170.tex`, lines 9–18, and `pages/MPL_57_133-179_57_scan171.tex`, lines 5–7.

For an integral finite-type k-scheme X satisfying \((R_1)\), every codimension-one local ring is a DVR. If Y is proper over k, a rational map \(X\dashrightarrow Y\) extends to the spectrum of each such local ring by the valuative criterion, and then to a neighbourhood of that point. The exact neighbourhood-extension statement is [Stacks Project, Lemma 29.43.5, Tag 0BX7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BX7); its hypotheses apply to an integral variety and a proper target. It follows that the maximal domain of the rational map contains every codimension-one point, so its complement has codimension at least two, or dimension at most \(n-2\) for an n-dimensional variety.

This is also the type of argument written on 57:171: it needs a valuation ring at each prime divisor, not an intersection-of-all-height-one-local-rings criterion for every rational function. The counterexample concerns the first N.B. and its preceding affine-target claims, **not** a counterexample to the later proper-target statement or its second N.B. One should not transfer the normality-dependent “proper birational quasi-finite implies isomorphism” step in proof (a) wholesale to a nonnormal X; proof (b), using the DVRs, is the valid route under \((R_1)\).

## 2. Scan 136: Cohen–Macaulay modules need not be free

**Frozen locator:** `pages/MPL_57_133-179_57_scan136.tex`, line 3. The paragraph first defines a finite module to be “Coh Mac” when its dimension equals its “codim coh,” then says “si l'anneau local est régulier, Cohen Mac sur lui signifie libre.”

### Interpretation and historical check

The counterexample uses “codim coh” in its historical sense of **depth**, not projective dimension or codimension of the support. There is direct primary corroboration in SGA 1, Exposé I, §9 “Propriétés de permanence,” printed p. 13 of the recomposed edition (old pagination p. 16; PDF page 29, zero-based index 28): the text explicitly identifies depth with Serre's “codimension cohomologique.” See [Grothendieck et al., *Revêtements étales et groupe fondamental*, arXiv:math/0206203v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0206203), passage beginning “A et B ont même dimension de Krull.”

This supports the terminology but does not prove the omitted preceding hypotheses or the author's intended repair of this isolated manuscript paragraph. The scope of the objection is therefore: **with the visible finite-module definition, interpreted as `dim Supp M = depth M`, the unqualified regular-ring/free equivalence is false**. A separate standing assumption that the module has full dimension or full support would materially change the statement and must be documented before being supplied editorially.

### Explicit calculation

Take

\[
R=k[[t]],\qquad M=R/(t)\cong k.
\]

The ring R is a regular local ring of dimension one: it is a DVR with maximal ideal (t). The finite nonzero module M has support consisting only of the closed point, so \(\dim_R M=0\). Every element of the maximal ideal annihilates M, so no M-regular sequence has positive length, and \(\operatorname{depth}_R M=0\). Hence M is Cohen–Macaulay under the displayed depth/dimension definition; this matches [Stacks Project, Definition 10.103.1, Tag 00N2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00N2).

Nevertheless M is not free: multiplication by the nonzero element t kills M, while multiplication by t is injective on every free R-module, and M is nonzero. This is a complete counterexample to the unqualified sentence. It does not contradict the later statement on this leaf about **flat** modules, since M is not R-flat.

The valid replacement is: **a finite maximal Cohen–Macaulay module over a regular local ring is free**, where maximal means \(\operatorname{depth}_R M=\dim R\), rather than merely \(\operatorname{depth}_R M=\dim_R M\). The distinction is explicit in [Stacks Project, Definition 10.103.8, Tag 00N2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00N2), and the freeness theorem is [Lemma 10.106.6, Tag 00NT](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00NT). **Confidence: high** for the mathematical counterexample and corrected statement; the historical scope qualification above is retained.

### CE136 — Full depth is the missing condition

**File/scan:** 57:136 L3.  
**Kind:** own idea (independent proof check of the parent's proposed counterexample).  
**No direct RESULTS_STATE ID found — local scope correction to a reused commutative-algebra leaf.**  
**Quote:** “Cohen Mac sur lui signifie libre.”

If “codim coh” means depth, as explicitly in SGA 1, Exposé I §9, then \(k\) over \(k[[t]]\) is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension and depth zero but is not free because t annihilates it. The valid assertion requires maximal Cohen–Macaulay, meaning depth equal to the dimension of the regular local ring (Stacks Tags 00N2 and 00NT). **Confidence: high** for the counterexample; an omitted full-support assumption would alter the target claim and is not supplied by this isolated paragraph.
