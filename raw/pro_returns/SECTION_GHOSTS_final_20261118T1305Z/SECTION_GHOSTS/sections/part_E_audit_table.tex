Two elementary scope checks in this register are worth making explicit.
The stronger span clause of GT C.1.2 follows from A.3 and E.101:
ordinary nonzero valuation vectors lie on the three lines represented
by \((1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\). If their span outside a finite set had
dimension at most one, one of \(A,B,A/B\) would have finite support,
contrary to E.101 for a ghost. Two independent integer vectors generate
an open \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-submodule. A cuspidal abelian pair has one
coordinate or ratio with zero valuations and is excluded in the same way.

For SQ A.51, the source's phrase ``of the form A.44'' explicitly permits
\(n_j=(j+k)!\), \(k\ge4\). The E.102 recursion remains valid: every
exponent is divisible by 24, every prime dividing \(n_{j+1}\) is below
the preceding local-solubility bound, and the same Newton precision
inequality holds. Choose \(k\) with \(\exp(Q^{\rm ab})\mid k!\),
so that the abelian perturbation has zero image in \(Q^{\rm ab}\),
and use the actual fibre product proved in E.20. If \(Q\) is perfect,
that common arithmetic quotient is simply \(G_{\mathbb Q}\), so any
abelian ghost section pairs with the point-three section. This proves
the extra perfect-quotient clause without claiming local matching for
every such ghost.

For the optional congruence in GT C.3.9, take the old 3-adic root
\(\xi_3=1\) in E.102. Initially the companion root is also one.
Thereafter the maintained error bound and Newton's estimate give a
companion congruent to one modulo 27. The CRT and the independent
cubic condition of E.114 therefore retain the two mod-27 congruences.
E.117 proves the required affine abelian lifting, in fact without
needing those extra congruences.
