## Arithmetic and integral checks

For (K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)), with odd \(\ell\) and \(\ell\nmid h_K\), I checked the literal five-term inflation argument. An invariant character of the normal subgroup generated by inertia outside the unique coefficient place has finite-support residue data: an open normal subgroup of the absolute group cuts out a finite extension and contains its inertia at every other prime. Invariance and normal generation make those residues determine the character. Raising its associated fractional ideal to the class number and choosing an inverse of that class number modulo \(\ell\) produces a global Kummer class with exactly those residues. Therefore the required first arrow is surjective and degree-two inflation is injective. The resulting Brauer class has only one possible finite invariant, and reciprocity forces that invariant to vanish.

The normal-closure argument gives the exact cyclotomic descent sequence. Averaging over its quotient of order \(\ell-1\) applies to every mod-\(\ell\) Tate twist, including weight zero and negative weights. The displayed coefficient injections are equivariant, and the induction uses only the degree-two exact segment. Fixed-ramification finiteness applies to the bounded-degree semidirect-product extensions associated to finite cocycles. Consequently the first derived limit vanishes. I checked the distinction between this assertion and the generally nonzero absolute strict-localization group, and re-read A.22 and B.25–B.26 as the stated in-run inputs to the tower conclusions.

For weights \(w\ge2\), I independently checked the global Euler-characteristic count, the compact Nakayama argument, and the invariant-coset description of torsion. If \(\ell-1\mid w\), the ideal generated by \(u^w-1\), for \(u\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times\), is exactly \(\ell^{1+v_\ell(w)}\mathbb Z_\ell\). Thus the displayed finite generator and its order are exact. In odd weight the resulting module is free of rank one.

Local duality gives the stated finite local orders; the inverse-limit degree-two group is dual to the invariant subgroup of \(\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(1-w)\). The coefficient sequence then gives the exact local integral reduction orders. The Poitou–Tate injectivity argument uses the dual weight \(1-w\), covered by the all-weight vanishing in E.209. In particular it does not assume that finite local first cohomology always has dimension one: when \(w\equiv1\pmod{\ell-1}\), the local integral reduction is a one-dimensional subspace of a two-dimensional finite group. Nonzero reduction still proves that the map between the two integral free rank-one groups is a unit.

As an independent cross-check of that finite injectivity, after restriction to (K), a locally trivial Kummer class at its unique coefficient place would define a cyclic degree-\(\ell\) extension unramified everywhere. The class-number hypothesis excludes such an extension; restriction from \(\mathbb Q\) to (K) is injective by averaging. This agrees with the duality proof in the manuscript.

I also checked the off-diagonal Frobenius evaluation and exact order of the finite global torsion class at each prime, and the absence of geometric stabilizers needed for unique aligning frames. These conclusions retain the fixed predecessor. The correction-torsor description is understood for a nonempty lift fibre; the automatic existence assertion of E.209 starts at class two. Weight one is deliberately outside E.210, since its coefficient-place integral group has rank two.
