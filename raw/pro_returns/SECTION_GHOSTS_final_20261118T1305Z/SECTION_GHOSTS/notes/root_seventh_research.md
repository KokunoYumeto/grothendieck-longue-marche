# Seventh-period research and exact scope

## Ordinary support density

The elliptic detectors E.192–E.194 led to considering density restrictions
for linear Galois representations. A direct Kummer argument is stronger for
that purpose: A.33 proves that every ordinary completed pair already has
support of upper density zero relative to the rational primes. No section,
semisimplicity, Fontaine–Mazur hypothesis, or uniform Chebotarev estimate is
used. Finite squarefree Kummer affine images are onto because one ordinary
integer valuation is prime to the selected modulus. The exact proportion
of affine permutations having a fixed point is phi(N)/N, which tends to
zero along primorials avoiding finitely many primes.

This does not distinguish surviving ghosts and is not claimed as a strict
reduction of the unresolved all-depth gate. The original ordinary ghosts
are consistent with density zero.

Literature searches on 18 November 2026 used the phrases “completed unit
equation density primes Kummer”, “Kummer affine Galois group primes radical
roots fixed point density zero”, and “infinitely ramified p adic
representations semistable”. The first two searches did not identify this
precise completed-pair assertion in the retrieved material; that is not a
priority claim. The proof uses the already opened primary Chebotarev
statement in Milne, Algebraic Number Theory v3.08, Theorem8.31, printed147.
Finite affine combinatorics are proved directly and enumerated independently.

The linear-representation search returned Ramakrishna's infinitely ramified
representations and Khare–Rajan's semisimple density theorem. Their existence
or density conclusions must not be used as a finiteness theorem for the
detector pullbacks. No such inference is made in the paper.

## Further Massey-lifting route

Primary searches for integral Massey products and simultaneous nilpotent section lifting led to Harpaz-Wittenberg (2023) and the published July2026 strong theorem of Maire-Minac-Ramakrishna-Tan. P.14 gives the exact coefficient specialization and failed transfer. Harpaz-Wittenberg Theorem7.4 already permits rank-one twists, but still chooses a defining system for each individual finite product; it does not retain the shared tripod predecessor. The 2026 finite p-group theorem cannot absorb the nontrivial prime-to-p cyclotomic quotient over Q, and restriction to Q(zeta_p) violates its explicit hypothesis. Its finite prime-power lifting construction chooses auxiliary primes depending on the requested modulus. No new all-depth gate is solved or narrowed by this literature comparison.
