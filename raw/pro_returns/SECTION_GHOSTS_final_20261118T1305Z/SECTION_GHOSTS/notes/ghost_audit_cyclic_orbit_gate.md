# The cyclic-cover and orbit reductions

These are structural reductions, not a proof of separation at every rational prime or a resolution of the main all-depth question.

Let k=F_q, q≡−1mod3, and let ψ_3:Fbar_q^×→μ_(3∞) be the intrinsic projection to the three-primary part of a finite-order element. This projection is independent of the finite field containing that element. In particular a nonzero integral Z3-Kummer class of a fixed finite-field unit does not disappear on extending constants. A fixed **finite-modulus** Kummer class can disappear; that different statement must not be substituted for the integral one.

## A genus-zero cover already gives the unit condition

For the cusp zero and a∈k\{0,1}, use

    V_n = P1 − ({0,∞}∪μ_(3^n)),       t=z^(3^n).

This is an actual geometric three-power cover of the tripod. Its unique k-point over a is r_n, where r_n^(3^n)=a, and its cusp above zero is zero. After extending constants to contain μ_(3^n), each unit 1−ζz has cusp value1. Thus equality of the full point and cusp sections forces

    ψ_3(1−r_n ζ)=1       for every ζ^(3^n)=1 and every n.

The genus growth of the full Fermat cover is therefore unnecessary for this necessary condition.

This condition is also sufficient for equality in the particular polylogarithmic quotient F/[K,K], where F is the free pro-three group on x,y and K=ker(F→Z3) for x↦1,y↦0. Its normal abelian subgroup is the completed group ring M=Z3[[Z3]], with the Tate twist on its generator. Tangential Frobenius sends x to x^q and sends the conjugate x^i y x^(−i), after abelianizing K, to q times the conjugate with index qi. The actual conjugating word in the Galois action lies in K and hence disappears on this abelianization.

For M_n=Z3[Z/3^n], finite-field Shapiro identifies H1(k,M_n(1)) with the product of integral Kummer groups of the constant fields of the μ_(3^n)-orbits. The point class is given by the units 1−r_n ζ above. The x-coordinate is already zero, since q−1 is a three-adic unit. Each H0(k,M_n(1)) is zero: around an orbit of length d the fixed-point equation is multiplication by q^d−1, a nonzero divisor on Z3. Consequently, whenever the point cocycle has a primitive in every M_n, that primitive is unique and the primitives are compatible. Their inverse limit is a primitive in M. This proves the stated equality criterion for this quotient, without identifying it with equality in the full free pro-three group.

## One fixed multiplicative parameter

The prime-to-three root r_n is periodic in n. For any one value r in this finite orbit, r_n=r occurs for arbitrarily large n. The preceding necessary condition therefore implies

    C(r):  ψ_3(1−rζ)=1 for every ζ∈μ_(3∞).

The product identity

    ∏_(ξ³=1)(1−r ξζ) = 1−r³ζ³

shows C(r)⇒C(r³). Since cubing permutes the finite prime-to-three order group generated by a, this implication cycles through the entire root orbit. Hence the all-n condition is equivalent to the single-parameter condition C(a).

It is also stable under a↦a^p, where p is the characteristic: apply Frobenius to the identity and use that raising to p is an automorphism of μ_(3∞). If −1 belongs to the subgroup generated by3 and p in (Z/ord(a))^×, then C(a) would imply C(a^(−1)). For a nontrivial ζ∈μ_(3∞), however,

    1−a^(−1)ζ^(−1) = −a^(−1)ζ^(−1)(1−aζ)

has three-primary part ζ^(−1) under C(a), contradicting C(a^(−1)). This proves separation in that exact inverse-orbit case. It does not cover all primes: for a=2 in F23, ord(a)=11, the element3 has order5 modulo11 and p=23 acts trivially there.

## The full collision set has additional dynamics

For fixed k=F_q, q≡−1mod3, let Z(k) be the points whose **full** geometrically pro-three section equals a cusp-zero section. All rational cusp-zero tangents give the same class here because k^× has no three-primary torsion.

The set Z(k) is invariant under a↦a^(1/3). To check the lift carefully, pass through z³=t. Normalize the quotient cocycles to zero. The image in the geometric C3 deck quotient of a conjugator between the two sections is G_k-invariant. Since q−1 is invertible modulo3, that image is zero. Thus the conjugator lies in the actual cover subgroup, and the two lifted classes coincide. The ordinary point is the unique k-rational cube root and the cusp is zero with its unique k-rational tangent-root class. Forgetting the two extra primitive cube-root punctures then maps the two classes to the point a^(1/3) and the cusp zero on the tripod. Because cubing permutes a finite set, Z(k) is invariant under cubing as well.

It is also invariant under the actual cusp-zero-fixing automorphism

    M(a)=a/(a−1).

Under u=1/a these two permutations become u↦u³ and u↦1−u. A collision therefore occupies a union of their orbits. Any orbit meeting−1 in the original a-coordinate is excluded by the inverse-orbit unit argument.

The executed exploratory certificate `code/ghost_audit_cubic_orbits.py` and its saved `.out` show one orbit on F_q\{0,1} for each of the616 rational primes q≡2mod3 with5≤q≤10000. This is not a proof for all primes. The corresponding assertion for arbitrary nonprime fields is false: both maps preserve every subfield and its complement, so an orbit outside the prime field cannot meet−1. No applicable general prime-field permutation theorem has yet been located.

## Why the proposed affine-translation shortcut is not yet justified

One may enlarge the collision set to points over Fbar_p that collide with **some** cusp-zero tangent after a finite constant extension. Root closure remains valid, allowing compatible choices of tangent roots. All nontrivial μ_(3∞)-points are excluded directly when p≡−1mod3: choose m with p^m≡−1 modulo the order of a. For y=ψ_3(1−a),

    y^(p^m−1)=a^(−1).

Since p^m−1 is a three-adic unit, y cannot be1. This avoids an invalid downward cubing step.

For a point a of order prime to3, repeated distinguished roots cycle and therefore root closure gives collisions at every aζ, ζ∈μ_(3∞). This scaling conclusion has **not** been established for an arbitrary collision point whose order has a nontrivial three-primary factor: taking cube roots then increases that factor and does not return to the original point. Also the map z↦z³ has domain V_1; equality of sections on the tripod does not automatically lift through the open immersion V_1→U. Thus downward cubing cannot be used indiscriminately on this enlarged set.

The proposed affine commutator R_ζ S R_ζ^(−1) S would require μ-scaling at its intermediate points, which can have nontrivial three-primary components. Initial scaling at prime-to-three points does not suffice. Accordingly the claimed translation-invariance and the ensuing contradiction have not been proved.

The earlier finite-field separation question is now resolved by E.192--194 in `sections/part_E_finite_field_detector.tex`.
