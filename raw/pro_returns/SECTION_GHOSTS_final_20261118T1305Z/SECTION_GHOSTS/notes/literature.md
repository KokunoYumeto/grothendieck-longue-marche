## Outcome relevant to the main question

No opened theorem proves or refutes the existence of a single infinite-support ordinary completed pair over Q that survives the full pronilpotent geometric quotient. There are, however, three substantive corrections or stronger comparisons:

1. Full profinite local point sections with a rational abelian pair are the global point section, by local Kummer injectivity and Stix's finite-support theorem. Thus an affirmative main limit theorem would settle the full section conjecture for the locally ordinary sections under consideration; there is no residual twenty-four-class ambiguity under those full local hypotheses.
2. Hoshi–Tsujimura's 2021 nongeometricity criterion omits the infinity cusp. Its lifting theorem remains usable, but the printed nongeometricity conclusion is false for the explicit pair (3,3) over Q(mu_3). A corrected four-case criterion is proved in P.3.
3. For rational unipotent tripod torsors, local ordinary abelianization gives more than a bare abelian comparison. Away from the coefficient prime, it determines the entire rational unipotent local class. At the coefficient prime every such torsor is semistable/de Rham, and unit abelianization forces crystallinity at every depth. What remains missing for a Chabauty–Kim argument is the equality with the specified local point's full path class at that prime, together with the independent fixed-finite-S condition.

## Nilpotent obstructions: exact scope

## Nongeometric pro-p sections, the infinity cusp, and descent to Q

Hoshi (2010), Theorem A (pp.3–4), concerns odd regular p, number fields in the maximal pro-p extension of Q(mu_p) unramified outside p, geometric p-power covers of the tripod, and proper genus-at-least-two compactifications with the stated point and reduction hypotheses. It produces nongeometric sections after finite extension. Theorem B (p.5) gives a Fermat-family consequence for p>7. Neither statement says every coefficient prime, every Fermat curve over Q, or an ordinary completed pair.

Hoshi–Tsujimura (2021), RIMS1950, Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.5(i) (article pp.9–11), applies to p-unit pairs in F intersect Omega_p-unr, for odd regular p and F containing mu_p. The field Omega_p-unr is the maximal pro-p extension of Q(mu_p) unramified outside p; it is Galois over Q. It is not the unrestricted maximal extension unramified outside p. Lemma 2.2 (article pp.8–9) supplies the needed geometric quotient over that Galois group.

The printed Proposition 1.4(ii) (article p.6) lists ordinary points and finite cusps but omits infinity. Its notion of geometric is unambiguous: Hoshi (2010), Definition 1.1(iii), p.7, includes decomposition subgroups of every rational point of the smooth compactification. Theorem A(iii) of the 2021 preprint itself repeats that definition on article p.3. No choice of ordering or affine coordinate removes infinity from it. Section 1 uses coordinates a_i-t whereas the introduction uses (t,1-t); the sign difference is harmless for the odd-prime counterexample.

In the convention (t,1-t), write z=1/t. A nonzero tangent of z-coordinate v at infinity gives leading-coefficient pair (v^{-1},-v^{-1}). For p=3, F=Q(mu_3), and v=1/3, this becomes (3,-3), which equals (3,3) in pro-3 Kummer cohomology. The prime-to-3 roots of unity in F are ±1, and 3*±1+3*±1 is never 1. Neither coordinate is a root of unity. Thus all printed nongeometricity hypotheses hold, but the tangent is geometric by the stated definition. The finite root calculation was executed; the identity that -1 is its own odd-power root proves the inverse-limit assertion at every coefficient depth.

The corrected criterion for actual x1,x2 in a number field or finite extension of Q_p is as follows. Let D_p=mu_{prime-to-p}(F), the pro-p Kummer kernel. A geometric section above the pair exists exactly when one of these holds: zeta1*x1+zeta2*x2=1 for zeta_i in D_p; x2 in D_p; x1 in D_p; or -x2/x1 in D_p. These are respectively an ordinary point or the cusps 0,1,infinity. Necessity follows from the three abelian cusp patterns (u,1),(1,u),(u,-u); sufficiency is given by the indicated actual point or tangent. This repairs the nongeometricity assertion without changing the lifting result.

P.4 proves a useful Q-version rather than asserting that an arbitrary Hoshi section descends. With the rational unit tangent 0+1, the pointed action on the free geometric pro-p group factors through Gamma_p=Gal(Omega_p-unr/Q). For odd regular p the subgroup over Q(mu_p) is free pro-p (Hoshi Lemma 3.2, p.14), and its index p-1 implies cd_p(Gamma_p)<=1. The graph of any cocycle (a*kappa_p(p), b*kappa_p(p)) therefore lifts through P semidirect Gamma_p -> P^ab semidirect Gamma_p, whose kernel is pro-p. The general lifting criterion is Pacheco–Stevenson (2000), Proposition 4.3, printed p.149, citing Serre I.§3.4, Proposition 16. This is a direct solution over Gamma_p, with no equivariant-descent assumption.

At p=2, Gras–Jaulent (2009), Theorem and Definition 0 (p.2) and Theorem 2 (p.3), give Gamma_2=Z_2 * C_2 because Q is 2-rational. The C_2 factor is the real decomposition group. The abelian cocycle (kappa_2(2),2*kappa_2(2)) is zero there when compatible positive roots are chosen. Lift its graph on C_2 by the tangent and on Z_2 by lifting one generator, then use the pro-2 free-product universal property. At every p under discussion, the pair (p,p^2) has valuations (1,2) at p, excluding the ordinary pattern and each cusp pattern. Thus these are full pro-p nongeometric sections over Q, but they deliberately fail the local ordinarity required by the main question. They are finite-ramification constructions and cannot produce an ordinary ghost with infinitely many nonzero integral valuations.

Saidi (2023), published pp.573–575, condition (*) and Theorem A, is a local-field result about certain pronilpotent-by-abelian quotients of proper curves after finite extension. Condition (*)(iv) requires Gamma^(ell)->Delta^(ell) nonisomorphic for some ell different from the residue characteristic. If Delta is the full pronilpotent quotient of the geometric group Pi, its pro-ell quotient already equals Pi^(ell), and every intermediate Gamma has the same quotient. That hypothesis then fails. This is an explicit quotient comparison, in addition to the base-field and properness differences.

## Kim and the strongest verified local comparison

Kim (2005), conclusion of §3 (author pp.17–18), proves Siegel finiteness for the tripod over Z[S^{-1}] for fixed finite S by rational unipotent Selmer methods. In that older author's notation H_f is defined using a de Rham period condition; it should not silently be identified with every later author's crystalline notation. BDCKW (2018), §§1.3–1.4 and Conjecture 3.1 (pp.2–3,12), with the finite-S discussion in §8, formulates eventual equality of the appropriate local Chabauty–Kim locus with integral points. Fixed ramification and the full local path conditions are part of its setup.

BKL `2305.09462v2`, 11 April 2026, Theorems A–C and Remark 1.8 (pp.4–5), proves a finite-S comparison and the refined tripod conjecture for S={2} and every odd auxiliary prime. It explicitly credits Stix's Corollary 6 with the corresponding full Selmer section assertion for the tripod and arbitrary finite S. Definition 2.1 (p.9) requires an adelic tuple. Section 2.2.1 and Lemma 2.19 (pp.17–18) verify that the Malcev completion comparison sends path torsors to rational unipotent path torsors.

The key additional result is BKL Lemma 4.2 (p.31): at a place q unequal to the coefficient p, H^1(G_q,U_{p,n}) -> H^1(G_q,U_p^ab) is an isomorphism. The reason is H^1(G_q,Q_p(j))=H^2(G_q,Q_p(j))=0 for j>=2. Therefore ordinary abelianization really does give full rational unipotent local point equality away from p. Corollary 4.3 (pp.31–32) expresses refined local conditions as pullbacks from abelian ones **inside the already defined fixed-S crystalline Selmer scheme**.

At p itself there is also a precise positive Hodge comparison, proved in P.9. Chatzistamatiou–Unver (2013), Definition 1.3.7 and Proposition 1.3.8 (arXiv author pp.12–13), proves every mixed Tate p-adic Galois representation semistable. On a tripod unipotent torsor, cocycle twisting preserves the finite weight pieces of its coordinate algebra and acts trivially on the graded Tate pieces. The proposition applies to all these pieces; applying D_st gives a torsor under a unipotent Q_p-group, hence a rational point and a B_st trivialization. Thus every finite-depth local rational unipotent torsor is semistable/de Rham, including at p=2 and with nonintegral abelianization.

For crystalline torsors, BKL §3.3, equation (3.8) (p.27), gives the natural Bloch–Kato identification H_f^1(G_p,U_n)=U_n^dR(Q_p). This makes crystalline lifts surject onto the preceding crystalline depth. Appendix A (p.47), citing Bloch–Kato Example 3.9, gives H_f^1(G_p,Q_p(j))=H^1 for j>=2. All lifts of a given previous class differ by such central classes; multiplication of their B_cris trivializations proves every lift crystalline. By induction, a finite-depth class is crystalline exactly when its abelianization is. The argument only uses Tate weights and works at 2 also. For an ordinary local point this means crystallinity when x_p and 1-x_p are both units.

This is not a proof of local point equality at p: already the depth-two fibre over a fixed crystalline abelianization is a nontrivial affine torsor under H^1(G_p,Q_p(2)), which has dimension one. Nor does it remove the finite-S obstruction. Every nonzero ordinary valuation is an actual integer and remains nonzero in every Q_p, so an ordinary ghost is infinitely ramified even after rational unipotent completion. A union over finite S contains only finite-support classes. No reverse map from rational unipotent classes to integral pro-p sections is claimed.

## Full sections, real place, Brauer–Manin, and the cover gap

Mochizuki (2003), Corollary 3.13, printed pp.142–143, proves the full real section statement for hyperbolic curves, including open curves. The remark after Corollary 3.7, p.139, describes the relationship of tangential sections and adjacent real components. Wickelgren (2014), Theorem 1.1 (p.2), proves that class two already detects the real components under its normalization hypothesis. For the tripod, the three abelian sign pairs distinguish its three components.

Independently checked the parent's correction using Stix (2015), Corollary 5 (author p.5) and Corollary 6 (p.6): if a full profinite global section has ordinary local point sections and rational abelian pair (t,1-t), local full Kummer injectivity forces every finite local point to equal t. Its geometric support is finite and its tuple is adelic. Stix then gives s=s_t; alternatively the real component is fixed by the sign pair and Corollary 6 applies. The twenty-four class-two lifts are irrelevant to that final full-section conclusion. Betts–Stix (2025), Definition 1.1 and Theorem A (arXiv author p.1), proves finite local images for full Selmer sections of proper genus>=2 curves over number fields with no CM subfield. Its full-section and abelian-by-finite-family hypotheses do not give a pronilpotent quotient theorem for the tripod.

Harari–Szamuely (2009), Theorem 1.2 (corrected author pp.2–3), characterizes splitting of the Albanese quotient by membership of the Albanese torsor in the maximal divisible subgroup. Remark 2.4 (p.7) extends to the generalized Albanese for smooth quasi-projective varieties. Their author's current page explicitly says this PDF corrects the published version. The criterion adds nothing to existence of an abelian splitting for the tripod, whose generalized Albanese is the already pointed torus G_m^2. It does not force a specified local tuple on the immersed unit equation to be one rational point.

Harari–Szamuely (2008), Theorem 1.1 (author p.2; published p.532), concerns torsors under semiabelian varieties, with finite Tate–Shafarevich group of the abelian quotient. Corollary 4.8 and Lemma 4.9 (author p.16) allow a product-of-completions version for the specified unramified Brauer subgroup, replacing almost all local points to obtain an adelic tuple. That replacement does not preserve the particular ordinary completed pair. This is an existence theorem for the semiabelian torsor, not an assertion of diagonal equality on the tripod.

Harari–Stix (2012), checked in `1005.1302v2`, Theorem 2.1 and Remark (3) (pp.11–12), explicitly includes nilpotent geometric monodromy and the full pronilpotent quotient. It does not require adelicity for its interpolation formulation, but it requires equality with the local point sections **in the quotient being used**. Abelian ordinarity does not establish that hypothesis at the full integral pronilpotent level. Corollary 3.1 (p.13 of this specific arXiv version) gives Brauer–Manin orthogonality on proper curves from the full geometric abelianization and local point conditions. The later 2011 author PDF has different pagination and numbering; the citation makes its version explicit.

Applying that criterion to a finite cover introduces another map that must be proved: the full abelianization of the covering subgroup need not be retained in the base group's pronilpotent completion. A pro-p finite-index subgroup retains its pro-p completion, but its other-prime Tate modules can be lost. Consequently passing to a nilpotent cover does not by itself produce the all-prime abelian section of its Jacobian needed for this route. The parent independently treats this group-theoretic gap.

At completions, or over a fixed finite ramification set, finite first-cohomology groups eliminate the relevant derived limit. Globally without a ramification bound they need not be finite. Thus a derived-limit class can be invisible both locally and at every finite coefficient level. The stronger comparison, now recorded in P.11, is this. The global completed pair does define an element of Pridham's *cohomological* adelic space: each of its finite reductions factors through a finite Galois extension, ramified at finitely many primes. There need not be one finite set working for all reductions. Consequently it would be incorrect to dismiss this formalism merely because an ordinary pair has infinite support. In its defining cocone, however, the first boundary of those localizations is zero simply because they restrict from global classes. This gives no new exclusion. A higher obstruction using the local ordinary points needs their higher path classes to agree with the chosen global higher lift; that agreement is not supplied by abelian ordinaryness. Moreover the actual point tower in the paper begins with U(A_f), whose points are integral on the standard tripod model at almost all primes. An infinite-support ordinary tuple is outside that point domain. Taking an infinite set of allowed primes does not change the restricted-product definition.

There is a useful but insufficient further observation. Over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, the tripod has H² with torsion coefficients equal to zero (its geometric fundamental group has cohomological dimension one). Thus the geometric H²-surjectivity condition in Proposition 3.26 is automatic for it. This still concerns actual adelic tuples with matching quotient section data. It supplies no inference that the completed infinite-support tuple is adelic, nor that an arbitrary global lift matches its point paths. The universal relative-cover case on p.744 explicitly requires a full fundamental-group section at the outset, so it cannot be used to construct one from the pronilpotent quotient.

### Real-place qualification: exact test pending independent review

This is recorded for independent checking and is **not** labelled an external refutation in the paper. The published notation on p.704 takes arbitrary F and only finite places; Definition 3.2 uses ordinary continuous cohomology, and Definition 3.10 uses its unmodified cocone. I found no totally imaginary assumption or odd-order restriction in Lemma 3.14, and no truncation convention eliminating high cohomology. The only explicit Archimedean qualification located is Remark 3.28, p.744, in the comparison to Harpaz–Schlank. The final published PDF has the same relevant definitions as the author version. A text search for `Archimedean`, `real place`, `imaginary`, `modified`, and `Tate cohom` was performed.

Under these literal definitions, test F=Q, Σ=all finite places, and M=Z/2 with trivial action. This coefficient satisfies the stated unit-outside-Σ condition. Let L be the finite-place restricted cohomology complex from Definition 3.2. For every finite q, H^i(G_Qq,M)=0 for i>2, and H^i(G_Qq/I_q,M)=0 for i>1. Products and filtered colimits are exact in abelian groups, so H^i(L)=0 for i>2. If C=cocone(RΓ(G_Q,M)→L), the long exact sequence yields

    H^4(C) = H^4(G_Q,Z/2) ≠ 0.

Indeed, the fourth cup power of the Kummer class of −1 restricts to the nonzero fourth power of the generator in H*(G_R,Z/2)=F_2[u]. In contrast, the degree-four cohomology of RΓ(G_Q,M^∨(1))^∨[−3] is dual to H^(−1), hence zero. Thus the printed duality formula appears to need the usual modified real local terms, or an appropriate odd-order/totally imaginary restriction. This calculation should be independently checked against any implicit convention before assigning a verdict to the external statement. P.11 relies only on the formal cone and exactness; it expressly avoids this unqualified 2-primary duality inference. The first-boundary vanishing remains true after adding real terms if the local data are the actual restrictions of the global pair.
