### P26 — Density of the image has an explicit test against density of its generated subgroup

**Kind:** own idea, explicit calculation. **Witness:** `18/059`, “image Zariski dense”; `18/061`, corrected coboundary. **Context search:** `groupe.*engendr|P19|image.*dense|transcend` → PN P19, reported `18/012:L9–11`, and RS's corresponding P19 row; executed and recorded in `context_searches.jsonl`. **Relation:** adds a concrete test of the density interpretation already flagged by PN P19. **Register:** `MPL_15_195-228+18_002-017 P19` [first-pass lead], `WG2 §2.4 #3` [unchecked plan]. **Confidence:** high; reason: the two conjugates and the roots of a nonzero Laurent polynomial give the complete calculation.

For $G=\mathbf G_m$, $k_0=\mathbf Q$, $k=\mathbf C$ and $g=1+\sqrt2$, the written map $\varphi(\sigma)=\sigma(g)^{-1}g$ has image $\{1,-3-2\sqrt2\}$. This finite set has proper Zariski closure, while the subgroup generated by $r=-3-2\sqrt2$ is infinite because $|r|>1$, and is Zariski dense because a nonzero Laurent polynomial has finitely many roots. Thus the exact inclusion $\operatorname{im}(\varphi)\hookrightarrow\langle\operatorname{im}(\varphi)\rangle$ changes the density answer in this algebraic example, and the author's word “image” on scan 059 must remain explicit.
