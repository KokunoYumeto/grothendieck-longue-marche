### MB03 — The partial-inverse lowering operator has an explicit rational conversion to the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) lowering operator

**Kind:** own calculation attached to a prior context question. **Witness:** 18/027, \(H^2(\mathbf P^1)=\mathbf Z(-1)\), provides a new elementary test object; the relevant operator formulas are CT16/013–014 and 033. **Context search:** `Lambda | sl_2 | Lefschetz | projecteurs` → CT16/013–018,033; PN M02, R01; RS WG2 §2.3 #2. **RS:** WG2 §2.3 #2, §2.4 #3 [unchecked]; PN M02 [lead]. **Relation:** supplies the conversion map needed before transporting the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) terminology.

On the explicit primitive string \(v_j=L^jv\), \(0\leq j\leq m\), put \(E(v_j)=v_{j+1}\), \(\Lambda(v_j)=v_{j-1}\), \(F(v_j)=j(m-j+1)v_{j-1}\), and \(H(v_j)=(2j-m)v_j\), with zero endpoint values. Direct subtraction gives \([E,F]=H\), \([H,E]=2E\) and \([H,F]=-2F\), while \(F=\sum_{j\geq1}j(m-j+1)\Lambda\pi_j\) and \(\Lambda=\sum_{j\geq1}[j(m-j+1)]^{-1}F\pi_j\) over \(\mathbf Q\). CT's displayed endpoint-projector identities are therefore related to the conventional \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) matrices by these coefficients; the transcription's separate contraction-model formulas and its integral assertions still require their own normalization check.

**Explicit rational image calculation:** On the model \(W=\bigoplus_{m=0}^d V_m\), where \(V_m\) has the displayed string basis, write \(B=1-E\Lambda\) and \(T=1-\Lambda E\); then \(B_m=B\Lambda^mTE^mB\) projects precisely onto the bottom vector of the length-\(m\) string. The operators \(E^bB_m\Lambda^a\), \(0\leq a,b\leq m\), are its matrix units, proving \(\mathbf Q[E,\Lambda]\cong\prod_{m=0}^d\operatorname{Mat}_{m+1}(\mathbf Q)\) by direct action on the basis. For the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) image, \(C=H^2+2H+4FE\) acts on \(V_m\) as \(m(m+2)\), so polynomial interpolation in \(C\) projects onto each \(V_m\) and interpolation in \(H\) projects onto each \(v_j\); the formulas above recover \(\Lambda\) and the same matrix units from \(E,F,H\). These are the explicit morphisms identifying the two rational operator images, with multiplicity spaces carried by identity operators in the exterior-algebra model; no integral equality is inferred from divisions by \(j(m-j+1)\).

### MB04 — \(\mathbf P^1\) gives both the failed factorization and the surviving relation

**Kind:** explicit calculation, sharpening an already registered example. **Witness:** 18/027, “\(\xi(\mathbf Z(-1))\)”. **Context search:** `P^1 | Lefschetz | SL_2 | Tate` → CT16/017; PN M02; RS MPL_14_087-132 M04. **RS:** MPL_14_087-132 M04 [first-pass own-idea lead]; WG2 §2.4 #3 [unchecked]. **Relation:** restates the \(\mathbf P^1\) test with matrices and a normalization action, rather than claiming categorical separation.

In \(V=\mathbf Qe_0\oplus\mathbf Qe_2=H^*(\mathbf P^1)\), \(E=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), \(F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\), \(H=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\), whereas the pure Tate category generated by \(\mathbf Q(-1)\) has group \(\mathbf G_m\) acting as \(\rho(z)=\operatorname{diag}(1,z)\). Every \(\rho(z)\) fixes \(e_0\), and the displayed Lefschetz \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) action moves \(e_0\), proving that this particular representation does not factor through \(\rho(\mathbf G_m)\). The relation is explicit nonetheless: \(\rho(z)E\rho(z)^{-1}=zE\), \(\rho(z)F\rho(z)^{-1}=z^{-1}F\), and the correspondence \(E:h^0\to h^2(1)\) is equivariant after its Tate twist.

### MB05 — Cohomological grading and Lefschetz Cartan grading differ by a computed scalar

### MB13 — The Hom comparison has a common number-field base and an abelianization morphism

**Kind:** literature comparison and explicit functorial bridge. **Witness:** 18/027, \(\ell\)-adic comparison; 18/055, generated category; earlier map-reconstruction wording is PN A39 (15/227). **Context search:** `Tate | morphisme | anab | Hom | foncteur` → CT44/008; PN B01, A39. **RS:** PN B01, A39 [leads]. **Relation:** adds a common-base bridge to the previous finite-field-Tate/sub-\(p\)-adic comparison.

Faltings's Satz 4 identifies \(\operatorname{End}_K(A)\otimes\mathbf Z_\ell\) with \(\operatorname{End}_{G_K}(T_\ell A)\) for a number field \(K\); the off-diagonal block of \(A=J_X\times J_Y\) gives the corresponding Hom map [F83], while Tate's original main theorem treats finite fields [T66]. For proper smooth curves a morphism \(f:X\to Y\) induces \(\pi_1(f)\), whose geometric pro-\(\ell\) abelianization is \(T_\ell(f_*):T_\ell J_X\to T_\ell J_Y\); this is the exact route from the fundamental-group Hom data to the abelian-variety Hom data. Mochizuki's Theorem A reconstructs dominant morphisms from the prescribed open homomorphisms of arithmetic fundamental groups modulo geometric inner automorphisms over sub-\(p\)-adic fields, including number fields [M99].

### MB14 — Semisimplicity and centres can be compared through conjugation

**Kind:** explicit example plus literature comparison. **Witness:** 18/027, Tate object; 18/037, \(G=\operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}(T_B)\). **Context search:** `centre | center | semi-simple | slim | anab` → CT16/049; PN B02; RS LM1 A.8. **RS:** PN B02 [lead]; LM1 A.8 [Refuted, return's claim; unchanged]. **Relation:** supplies the actual kernel map behind the comparison.

For \(\mathbf G_m\), every rational representation is a direct sum of characters, while conjugation \(\mathbf G_m\to\operatorname{Inn}(\mathbf G_m)\) is the trivial map with kernel \(Z(\mathbf G_m)=\mathbf G_m\). For the hyperbolic geometric fundamental group \(\Delta\) in Mochizuki's setting, Lemma 1.3.1 and Corollary 1.3.2 identify \(\Delta\) with \(\operatorname{Inn}(\Delta)\) and recover \(\Pi\) as the pullback \(G_K\times_{\operatorname{Out}(\Delta)}\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta)\) [M04]. Thus the useful relationship is the sequence \(1\to Z(G)\to G\to\operatorname{Inn}(G)\to1\): the Tate example computes its kernel explicitly, and the anabelian reconstruction uses the zero-kernel case.
