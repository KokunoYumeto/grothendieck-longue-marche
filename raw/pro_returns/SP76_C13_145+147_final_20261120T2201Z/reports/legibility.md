Most main-text lines are readable at the supplied resolution. Their long horizontal joins can make different words look alike at page scale; enlarged crops often settle a descender, terminal s, omitted point, or insertion attachment. Enlarging the scans does not recover strokes already merged in the source. The least secure places are heavily retraced symbols, small ornate alphabets, text under cancellation strokes, and oblique marginal notes that cross main-text baselines. Those places retain narrow alternatives or explicit doubt in the transcription. A physically visible but unidentified canceled shape is reproduced without assigning it a convenient letter.

Witness C usually supplies useful page structure and preserves many short forms and revision states. It also contains supplied words, lost insertions, overconfident glyph choices and terminal letters that the enlarged ink does not support. H sometimes identifies a word or revision that C misses, but also expands abbreviations, regularizes spelling or agreement, changes quote forms, relocates additions, and replaces difficult wording with a plausible expression. Agreement of the witnesses was not used as evidence: their shared points after ens, some punctuation, and some short-form readings required correction or retained doubt. H's editorial acknowledgment of a strike or an added sign is counted as recognition of that feature, even where its typeset main text uses different wrappers.

Confidence labels concern the local reading, not a calibrated probability or a guarantee of mathematical correctness. The notes distinguish a visually preserved surprise from an independently checked assertion. The small light footer numerals are included, but their hand is left unidentified. Remaining doubts and the independent second-reading decisions should be consulted together when using a difficult passage.
