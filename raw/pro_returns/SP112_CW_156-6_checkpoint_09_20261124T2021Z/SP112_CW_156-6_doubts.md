## Legibility and the witnesses

The main writing is generally readable, but its small connective letters often flatten into long strokes. The least secure readings are words under dense cancellation, crowded interlinear corrections, very small points or indices, and writing at the photographed lower edge. Enlarged crops clarify the surviving strokes; enlargement cannot recover ink that is covered by a later stroke or falls outside the image.

Witness C is substantially closer to the visible writing than H in these sheets, but agreement between them was not treated as proof. C still omits or mis-scopes some deleted and inserted material, leaves some readable words qualified, and occasionally presents an alphabet or attachment too confidently. H has many additional omissions, expansions, misread operators and changed deletion states. Several apparent differences in the supplied comparison are editorial or macro-only; those are excluded from the textual correction counts.

The mathematical notation is especially sensitive to the case of x/X, the number of strokes in an order relation, the distinction between a family letter and an operand, the spelling of the shadow operators, and the attachment of an insertion. Clear contrary forms and delimiter mismatches are preserved even where a more regular formula would be expected. The displayed double points on scans030 and033 remain provisional because their function is unclear. Canceled words retain uncertainty unless their letters can actually be followed.

The records distinguish physical reading from mathematical checking. A transcribed assertion is not thereby verified. The limited checks or uses actually undertaken are listed in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv at their stated strength.
