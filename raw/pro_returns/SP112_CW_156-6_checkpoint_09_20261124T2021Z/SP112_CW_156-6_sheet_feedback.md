## Recurring observations beyond a simple character substitution

The localized categories above overlap in their causes, but each changed place is counted once. In these pages, four patterns deserve particular attention in future reading instructions:

- **Shared letters across a correction.** On scan032 the live d is shared by the old dans and replacement deux. A deletion wrapper that mechanically covers an entire printed word can cancel a letter the manuscript kept. Scans031–033 supply further examples where an adjacent live word must stay outside a cancellation.
- **Leaders mistaken for punctuation, parentheses or strikes.** The inserted groupoïde on scan033, the interlinear explanation on scan029, and several corrections on scans021 and031 require checking both the words and the leader’s endpoint. Reproducing the correct words at the wrong attachment is still an error.
- **Descender returns mistaken for emphasis.** The g in figure can return far to the left under a word. Scans032–033 show why a separate underline must be distinguished from that return. Similarly, the underline scope of an axiom label must be checked through the numeral and star rather than inherited from a nearby label.
- **A small mark with uncertain purpose.** The paired points associated with M on scans030 and033 are visible but do not have a secure mathematical interpretation. Their presence and their uncertain function must be recorded separately. A plausible theory must not manufacture an extra sign from faint or incidental ink.

Alphabet uncertainty is a typographic problem as well as a reading problem. A consistent primary approximation can be used for a recurring family or ambient letter after its first qualification on each page, but it must not erase a clear local operand or a contrary index. Repeated local operator spellings—Omb, Om, Multomb, Multom, Mulomb, Mulom—also need individual checking; neither dictionary form nor notation consistency licenses adding a missing letter.

The supplied witnesses can agree on a missed layer. The most useful completeness checks were therefore the separate inventory of insertions/deletions and the review of C’s deletion/insertion scope against the image, not merely the C/H difference list.
