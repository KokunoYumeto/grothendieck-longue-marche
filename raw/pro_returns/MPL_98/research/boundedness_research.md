## BR01. Fixed quotient and finitely many Hilbert polynomials

Let S be noetherian, X projective over S with a specified relatively ample polarization, and E coherent on X. Sheaves on geometric or extended residue-field fibres which are quotients of the corresponding E and have Hilbert polynomial in a fixed finite set form a bounded family. A modern construction is the finite disjoint union of the schemes Quot^P(E/X/S), carrying their universal quotients. The quotient parameter must be flat over its parameter base, as required by the Quot functor; a point of the Quot scheme supplies precisely such a quotient over its residue field. The construction bounds sheaves as objects and does not assert that an arbitrary pre-existing family of quotients is already flat.

For projective space, the primary modern reference is the Stacks Project, Lemma 108.6.2, [Tag 0DPB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DPB). The relatively ample proper case is Lemma 108.6.3, [Tag 0DPC](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DPC). The finite coefficient construction behind the uniform boundedness is in [Tag 0DP9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DP9). These are source checks of the modern construction; this memorandum does not claim an independent proof of the complete uniform regularity theorem.

Two hypotheses must not be silently discarded. First, a fixed Hilbert polynomial alone does not imply boundedness: on P^1_k the bundles O(n) ⊕ O(−n), n≥1, all have polynomial 2m+2, while h^0=n+1 is unbounded. In any bounded family on a projective scheme, generic flatness produces finitely many flat strata, on each of which upper semicontinuity and quasi-compactness bound h^0. Second, bounded top-dimensional cycle degree alone does not control lower-dimensional torsion. Fix a k-rational point P on P^1 and take E=O⊕O and F_n=O⊕O_{nP}. Every F_n is a quotient of E; its one-dimensional cycle has degree 1, but its Hilbert polynomial is m+1+n. This disproves the unrestricted top-cycle interpretation of the tentative variant discussed in Hua IV, pp. 26–27. It does not decide uncertain handwriting, and a reduced pure-dimensional support assertion has different hypotheses.

The repair in Hua IV concerning kernels K_i of a fixed sheaf mapping onto a bounded family is compatible with the projective setting: one must first parameterize the relevant maps and stratify for formation of their kernels. Mere assertion that arbitrary subsheaves of a fixed coherent sheaf are bounded would be false.

**BR01_PURE — stronger counterexample, constructed by the root and independently checked here.** On X=P^1_k, put E=O_X^2 and F_n=O_X(n), n≥1. The sections X^n and Y^n have no common zero, so they define a surjection E→F_n: on each standard affine chart one coefficient is a unit in a local trivialization. Every F_n is invertible, hence pure of dimension one, and its associated one-dimensional cycle is exactly [P^1], with multiplicity one at the generic point. Nevertheless its Hilbert polynomial is m+n+1. Thus fixed quotient plus bounded associated-cycle degree does not imply boundedness even under purity. This is stronger than the lower-dimensional-torsion example above. It tests that mathematical formulation, not uncertain ink. A statement about structure sheaves of reduced pure-dimensional supports is different; the varying line bundles here are not their supports' structure sheaves.

## BR02. Field equivalence, common overfields, and relative embeddings

For X of finite type over k, two coherent sheaves over X_{K_1} and X_{K_2} are k-equivalent when some embeddings K_i→L over k make their scalar extensions isomorphic. Requiring L algebraically closed gives the same relation, since any witnessing L can be replaced by an algebraic closure.

Transitivity needs an amalgam over the middle field, not only over k. If L witnesses F_1≈F_2 and L' witnesses F_2≈F_3, regard both as extensions of the same K_2 via the specified embeddings. The tensor product L⊗_{K_2}L' is nonzero. A quotient by a maximal ideal is a field into which both factors embed, because their maps from fields are unital; this is a compatible common extension. Pulling the two isomorphisms there proves transitivity. This independently verifies the repair already identified in Hua IV, pp. 28–29.

Finite presentation gives descent to a finitely generated k-subfield: finitely many coefficients suffice for a coherent sheaf on finite type X, and finitely many more suffice for an isomorphism, its inverse, and their identities. The general statement used here is the Stacks Project, Lemma 32.10.2, [Tag 01ZR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01ZR), for finitely presented modules, morphisms, and equalities on inverse limits of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes with affine transition maps. Finite type X over a field is noetherian and quasi-compact quasi-separated, so those conditions apply.

## BR03. “Some compositum” cannot be replaced by “any compositum”

This is an independently constructed counterexample relevant if the question on archive scan 030 reads “extension composée” or “extension commune,” as the root reports, rather than Hua's proposed “extension universelle.” Take X=A^1_k, K_1=K_2=k(t) as abstract copies, and F_i the sheaf corresponding to K_i[x]/(x−t). Align the two embeddings into L=k(t). The resulting sheaves are identical.

Instead embed the two copies independently in L=k(t_1,t_2), where t_1,t_2 are algebraically independent. This is a compositum generated by the two images. The two L[x]-modules are L[x]/(x−t_1) and L[x]/(x−t_2). Their annihilators and supports differ, so they are not isomorphic. They remain nonisomorphic after every further field extension of L, including algebraic closure. Thus existence of one witnessing compositum says nothing about an arbitrary specified relative position of the two fields.

The same example explains why a set-valued operation on k-equivalence classes must range over all compatible relative embeddings/composita. For a fixed extension k'=k(t), the class of a transcendental point over k can become the k'-rational point x=t or an independent transcendental point x=u, depending on the amalgamation. These are distinct k'-classes. One cannot define a single-valued change-of-ground-field operation on these coarse classes merely by choosing an unspecified compositum.

## BR04. One algebraically closed field and automorphism orbits

Let Ω/k be algebraically closed of infinite transcendence degree. Every coherent sheaf in the preceding equivalence relation has a representative on X_Ω. Indeed it descends to a finitely generated k-field, which embeds into Ω. Two sheaves on X_Ω are equivalent precisely when their isomorphism classes lie in one Aut(Ω/k)-orbit.

For completeness, the orbit assertion requires two separate finite-data steps. Given an equivalence in a larger field, descend the two sheaves and the witnessing isomorphism to a field finitely generated over k containing the finite coefficient fields of both sheaves (with their specified embeddings). Embed this field into Ω. An isomorphism between finitely generated k-subfields of Ω extends to an automorphism of Ω: first extend to their algebraic closures in Ω, choose complementary transcendence bases of equal cardinality, then extend across algebraic closures. The complementary bases have the same cardinality because removing finitely many transcendence generators from an infinite basis leaves its cardinality unchanged. Two resulting automorphisms carry the original sheaves to isomorphic sheaves, and their composite proves the orbit statement. The converse is immediate from a common overfield with the appropriate embeddings.

The size qualification matters. Ω is universal for these finite-presentation objects, not a field containing every field extension of k. No set-sized field contains all extensions. Infinite transcendence degree is enough here because every individual object and comparison uses finite data. Finite transcendence degree is insufficient uniformly: a degree-n effective divisor on A^1 whose monic defining polynomial has n algebraically independent coefficients has invariant coefficient field of transcendence degree n; it cannot be represented over an Ω of smaller transcendence degree. For categories containing arbitrary non-finitely-presented or non-quasi-compact objects the argument must be reconsidered.

## BR05. Detecting boundedness after changing the base

Within the noetherian setting, a map T→S whose image meets every base point occurring in the family detects boundedness. No finite type hypothesis on T→S is needed. Here is the finite-data proof omitted from the terse published formulation. A bound after pullback is supplied by a coherent G on X×_S U with U finite type over T. Since T is noetherian, U is quasi-compact. Cover U by finitely many affines Spec R_j mapping into affines Spec A_j of S. The restriction of G descends, by Tag 01ZR, to X×_S Spec R_{j,0} for some finitely generated A_j-subalgebra R_{j,0}⊂R_j. These finitely many parameter schemes are of finite type over S. Their disjoint union with the descended sheaves bounds the underlying k(s)-classes. The image condition supplies a lift of each relevant s; equivalence over residue-field extensions returns the original class.

Quasi-compactness is a substantive condition in generalizations. For S=A^1_k take T to be the disjoint union of its closed points, and let X=S. Choose unbounded positive ranks n(s) on closed points. The family k(s)^{n(s)} is unbounded relative to S, since a finite type parameter scheme is quasi-compact and its coherent sheaf has a uniform bound on fibre dimensions. On T it is represented by the coherent sheaf which has rank n(s) on its component, so it is bounded if the definition is extended to this non-quasi-compact base. T is not noetherian as a scheme. This example, proposed by the root and checked here, therefore warns against expanding the scope; it does not refute the natural noetherian reading of the published statement.

## BR06. Generic flatness and finite ranges of a complex

Let Y→T be finite type, T noetherian, and K a complex of coherent O_Y-modules. For a prescribed finite set of cohomological degrees, only finitely many terms, kernels, images, and cokernels surrounding those degrees enter. On a reduced integral stratum of T, generic flatness makes this finite list flat on a dense open. The two short exact sequences defining cycles/boundaries/cohomology then remain exact after arbitrary base change. Noetherian induction on the complement produces a finite partition by reduced locally closed strata. The result concerns the complex pulled back to each stratum; it does not identify its cohomology with pullback of the original cohomology across a nonflat stratum inclusion without a further argument.

The source theorem used is the Stacks Project, Proposition 29.28.1, [Tag 052A](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/052A). The classical statement is EGA IV_2, Théorème 6.9.1, with algebraic generic freeness in Lemme 6.9.2: [original publication record](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1965__24__5_0/) and [primary PDF mirror](https://wstein.org/sga/circle/EGAIV.2.pdf). The exact theorem and lemma were retrieved as indexed text from that primary PDF; the large PDF could not be opened in full by the retrieval tool. The proof used here is a finite list of applications of generic flatness plus exact-sequence arguments, rather than one application to infinitely many cohomology sheaves.

A uniform finite range is essential for arbitrary unbounded complexes. Over A=k[t], with k infinite, choose infinitely many distinct a_n. Form the complex with disjoint two-term blocks A --(t−a_n)→ A in degrees 2n and 2n+1. Its even cohomology is zero, but at t=a_n the pulled-back complex has nonzero cohomology in degree 2n. Every dense open in A^1 contains some a_n. Hence no finite locally closed stratification makes ordinary cohomology commute with every base change in every degree simultaneously. For each fixed finite range, only finitely many blocks matter, and stratification works.

Likewise, a bounded-input statement for Tor_i must be read degree by degree. On B=k[x,y]/(x,y)^2, Tor_i^B(k,k) has k-dimension 2^i. A minimal free resolution has ranks 2^i, since the maximal ideal is k^2 and its square is zero; tensoring with k makes all differentials zero. The two singleton input families are bounded, but the union of all their Tor groups is unbounded. Each fixed degree still gives a bounded family.

## BR07. Tor base change: necessity of the input-flatness hypothesis present in the lemma

**Corrected source disposition.** The root's subsequent rereading of frozen scan 039 confirms that the lemma explicitly assumes the F_α flat over S. The root's earlier report that it omitted this assumption was mistaken. This researcher has not inspected that scan or its transcription. The counterexample below therefore proves necessity of an assumption present in the frozen lemma, according to the corrected root report. It refutes only the weakened formulation obtained by deleting input flatness. Hua's footnote claims that its modernization supplied this hypothesis; that claim is a reading disagreement with the root's frozen witness, not evidence that the manuscript omitted it.

The following counterexample was constructed by the root and independently checked here. Let A=k[t], B=A[x], M_1=B/(tx), M_2=B/(x)=A. All ambient affine schemes Spec B are flat over A. The B-free resolution 0→B --tx→ B→M_1→0 is exact because tx is a nonzerodivisor. After tensoring with M_2 its differential is zero. Therefore Tor_0^B(M_1,M_2)=A, Tor_1^B(M_1,M_2)=A, and all higher groups vanish. Every one of these Tor groups is A-flat.

Now set A'=A/(t)=k. Then B'=k[x], M_1'=B', and M_2'=k. Consequently Tor_1^{B'}(M_1',M_2')=0, whereas Tor_1^B(M_1,M_2)⊗_A A'=k. The comparison fails. M_1 is not A-flat: multiplication by t kills the nonzero class of x. Thus flatness of all output Tor groups, even together with flatness of the ambient schemes, cannot replace flatness of the input sheaves.

A correct two-module sufficient statement assumes A→B flat and M_1,M_2 flat over A. The spectral sequence

Tor_p^A(Tor_q^B(M_1,M_2),A') ⇒ Tor_{p+q}^{B'}(M_1⊗_A A',M_2⊗_A A')

is then supplied by a B-free resolution; see the Stacks Project, Example 15.63.3, [Tag 0620](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0620). If the Tor_q groups in the requested range are A-flat, its first-quadrant edge maps give base change in that range. The precise ring of Tor in archive scan 039 is an independent reading issue: the root reports an O_S/O_Y doubt. This counterexample tests the intended relative Tor over B, not a silently substituted coefficient ring.

## BR08. Tor proof: freeness over B_i is not freeness over B

There is a second, independent problem. The root reports that scan 040 literally uses resolutions L^(i) free over B_i and tensors them over B. Hua IV repeats the assertion that this computes Tor over B. That computation is false without extra flatness over B.

Take A=k, B=k[x], B_1=B_2=k=B/(x), M_1=M_2=k. All modules and algebras are A-flat. Each M_i has the B_i-free resolution concentrated in degree zero. Tensoring these resolutions over B gives k concentrated in degree zero. But the B-free resolution 0→B --x→ B→k→0 gives Tor_1^B(k,k)=k. This refutes the claimed model for the derived tensor product. It does not refute field-extension base change, which remains true here.

Repair BR08R: a polynomial-algebra resolution supplies both the correct derived tensor product and the needed finiteness. Assume A noetherian, B a finite type A-algebra, B_i finite type B-algebras, and M_i finite B_i-modules, for a finite number of indices i. Choose polynomial B-algebras C_i=B[T_{i,1},…,T_{i,r_i}] with surjections C_i→B_i. Since C_i is noetherian, M_i admits a resolution L^(i) by finite-rank free C_i-modules. Since C_i is B-flat, this is a B-flat resolution. The total complex K of the tensor product of these resolutions over B computes the intended derived multi-tensor product, hence its multi-Tor groups. All resolutions are in nonnegative homological degrees, so only finitely many summands occur in each total degree.

Put C=C_1⊗_B⋯⊗_B C_n. This is a polynomial B-algebra, and each K_d is a finite-rank free C-module. Its homology is consequently finite over C. To identify its support and its module structure on the intended fibre product, write I_i=ker(C_i→B_i). Multiplication by an element of I_i on L^(i) lifts the zero endomorphism of M_i. The comparison theorem for projective resolutions makes this chain map C_i-linearly null-homotopic. Tensoring the homotopy with the other complexes makes the corresponding C-linear action on K null-homotopic. Thus each I_i, and hence J=Σ_i I_i C, annihilates H_d(K). Since

C/J ≅ B_1⊗_B⋯⊗_B B_n,

the finite C-module H_d(K) is a finite module over the coordinate ring of the intended fibre product. The construction has the usual functorial Tor action, so these local coherent modules give the relative Tor sheaves. This explicitly supplies the coherence step which the B_i-free resolution failed to prove.

For arbitrary A-base change, assume additionally that B and every M_i are A-flat. Every L^(i)_d is then A-flat; all its syzygies are A-flat by induction through the short exact sequences ending in M_i. Tensoring with any A-algebra A' therefore preserves each resolution. Its terms become free over C_i'=B'[T_i], which is B'-flat, so K⊗_A A' computes the required multi-Tor over B'. The hyper-Tor spectral sequence applies because K is bounded below with A-flat terms. If H_q(K) is A-flat for 0≤q≤N, its first-quadrant edge maps are isomorphisms in total degrees 0≤q≤N. Generic freeness can impose this finite list of flatness conditions, since all these H_q are finite C-modules and C is finite type over A; after reduction to an integral base it can also impose the required flatness of B and the M_i. No single localization is asserted to make all infinitely many H_q flat simultaneously. This is an independently checked finite-type affine repair, not a reading proposed for the manuscript.

Tag 0620 is a correct comparator for the two-module case, while [Tag 061Z](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/061Z) gives the underlying hyper-Tor spectral sequence. The polynomial-resolution repair above supplies the additional finite generation and quotient-action arguments explicitly.

## BR09. Ext: the terminal cokernel is a real hypothesis

Let A=k[t], B=A[x], M=B/(x), Q=B/(x−t). Both M and Q are A-flat, as is B. A B-free resolution of M is B --x→ B. Its Hom complex with values in Q is A --t→ A. Thus Hom_B(M,Q)=0 is A-flat, but the terminal cokernel Ext^1_B(M,Q)=A/(t) is not. On the fibre t=0, M'=Q'=k over k[x], so Hom_{k[x]}(M',Q')=k. Degree-zero base change is the nonisomorphism 0→k. This independently constructed example proves that merely requiring the lower Ext groups to be flat does not suffice; a condition controlling the next obstruction is necessary. The terminal-cokernel condition in the manuscript's partial-resolution argument supplies exactly such control.

## BR10. All degrees for the truncated Hom complex; only a range for Ext

Let K be a complex in degrees 0,…,N+1, with every K^i A-flat, every H^i(K) A-flat for i≤N, and terminal cokernel C=coker(K^N→K^{N+1}) A-flat. Then C=H^{N+1}(K), so all the cohomology groups of this bounded complex are A-flat. Descending from the right, the exact sequences

0→B^{N+1}→K^{N+1}→C→0,

0→Z^i→K^i→B^{i+1}→0,

0→B^i→Z^i→H^i(K)→0

show that every relevant cycle and boundary is flat. Each sequence remains exact after tensoring with any A-algebra A'. Consequently H^i(K)⊗_A A'→H^i(K⊗_A A') is an isomorphism for every i.

If K is Hom of a partial free resolution of length N+1, its identification with Ext is justified only in degrees ≤N. These are different claims. The root confirms that scan 043 attaches “en tt degré” to the H(K) comparison and then separately specializes to p≤N for Ext; scan 042 gives the truncated length. Under that reading, the all-degree sentence is mathematically correct. Hua IV's footnote restricting it to the Ext range overcorrects the source. This is a substantive disagreement with a supplied modernized reading, supported by an independent elementary proof, not a proposed alteration of the frozen wording.

## BR11. Proper direct images and families of arbitrary global maps

Properness, or an appropriate proper-support replacement, is needed for coherent direct-image boundedness. The basic counterexample is A^1_k→Spec k: f_*O=k[x] is not a coherent sheaf on Spec k. Even a singleton bounded family can leave the category of coherent sheaves. The primary coherence theorem is the Stacks Project, Proposition 30.19.1, [Tag 02O5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02O5), which explicitly cites EGA III, Théorème 3.2.1.

Proper coherent cohomology plus flatness yields a finite projective complex computing arbitrary derived base change: the Stacks Project, Lemma 30.22.1 and its following remark, [Tags 07VK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07VK) and [07VJ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07VJ). To obtain ordinary higher-direct-image base change, stratify further so that the cohomology of this finite complex is flat. Do not confuse derived base change with the individual underived comparison maps.

A related counterexample distinguishes internal Hom from the family of all global morphisms. On A^1_k, both input families {O} are bounded, but the maps O --x^n→ O have cokernels k[x]/(x^n) of arbitrarily large length. These objects cannot lie in a finite type parameter family: on each of finitely many affine parameter charts a presentation has a fixed number r of generators and polynomial matrix entries of bounded degree D; on any torsion fibre a nonzero r-by-r minor bounds its length by rD, by the structure theorem over the principal ideal domain κ(t)[x]. Thus the family of cokernels of all maps between bounded families need not be bounded on a nonproper scheme. This does not contradict boundedness of the internal Hom sheaf, which is O in this example. Properness controls the global parameter spaces of morphisms and extension classes. In the projective argument at the start of Hua IV this condition is available; it cannot be dropped when abstracting the method.

For relative Ext, a clean modern sufficient result is the Stacks Project, Lemma 36.30.7, [Tag 0A1J](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A1J): a perfect first argument and a bounded complex of finitely presented base-flat sheaves with proper support give a perfect relative Hom direct image and arbitrary derived base change. This is a sufficient theorem with explicit hypotheses, not a claim that every coherent first argument is perfect. The manuscript's qualification “quand ça marche,” as reported by context/root, should not be expanded to unconditional relative Ext boundedness.
