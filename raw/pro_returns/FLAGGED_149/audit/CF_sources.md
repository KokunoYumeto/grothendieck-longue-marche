# C/F primary-source audit

All entries below were opened. The manuscripts and the supplied prior return are treated as claims to verify, not as unquestioned authority. Bibliography keys match `CF_refs.bib`. A reference to a numbered theorem below records its actual hypotheses used in our text; the derivation is in the indicated result.

## Scheme reconstruction

- **CF_CHW2024** — Carlson–Haine–Wolf, *Reconstruction of schemes from their étale topoi*. [Author PDF](https://peterjhaine.github.io/files/reconstruction_of_schemes_from_etale_topoi.pdf). Opened Theorems0.1 and0.3 (intro pp2–3), Theorem2.7 (localization), Examples2.30–2.31 (pp15–16), Theorem3.9 (p18). The main field is **infinite and finitely generated over its prime field**, the schemes are finite type, the localization is at universal homeomorphisms, and morphisms of topoi are pinned. The reduced-fibre hypothesis in their Theorem3.9 must not be confused with Carlson–Stix's stronger Lemma5.4. Used in F9.1–F9.4.
- **CF_CarlsonStix2026** — *The étale topos reconstructs varieties over sub-p-adic fields*, arXiv2410.22474v2, **20 September2026**. [Full v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22474v2). Opened TheoremA/4.1 (sub-p-adic field, finite-type schemes, pinned morphisms, universal-homeomorphism localization); TheoremB (actual Hom from seminormal source); Lemma5.4 (qcqs base, finite-presentation source/target, **source reduced**, faithfulness); TheoremD/6.12 with Proposition6.11 (noetherian Nagata geometrically unibranch base, sub-p-adic generic fields, componentwise universally open finite-type schemes, chivalrous topos morphisms); CorollaryE (normal source); Remark6.13 (pinned is the wrong unrestricted relative condition). Used in F9.1–F9.4. The literal title and date have been checked.
- **CF_Tsujimura2023** — [RIMS1957 primary PDF](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/preprint/file/RIMS1957.pdf), dated24February2022; published Advances in Mathematics435(2023),109380. Opened Corollary4.10, printed p35: one-variable function fields over a subfield of a finitely generated extension of a number field enlarged by all roots of a fixed prime. A number field itself satisfies this hypothesis by inclusion. The comparison is over the base absolute Galois group modulo geometric inner automorphisms. Used in F9.5. No properness/genus restriction is inserted into the birational theorem; properness is used separately to extend the field automorphism to the curve.
- **CF_Mochizuki1999** — [Author PDF](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~motizuki/The%20Local%20Pro-p%20Anabelian%20Geometry%20of%20Curves.pdf). Theorem16.5, printed p86/PDF85, and following Remark: smooth pro-variety and hyperbolic pro-curve over a sub-p-adic field; birational transition maps; dominant Hom compared with continuous open Hom modulo geometric inner automorphisms, first pro-p and then full profinite. Theorem17.1, printed p87/PDF86: regular finitely generated function fields of arbitrary transcendence degree over the same sub-p-adic field. The ground field is explicitly assumed algebraically closed in both function fields just before the theorem. The following Remark, printed p88/PDF87, explicitly supplies the higher-dimensional ample-hyperplane failure. Used in ZCF1,ZCF4; source itself opened, not inherited only from another agent.

## Reduction and the explicit counterexamples

- **CF_Tamagawa1997** — supplied primary PDF `background/literature/tamagawa-1997-affine/tamagawa_affine_curves.pdf`, [publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1992BA14A2D63FA076DB39A34EC45E83/S0010437X97000614a.pdf/div-class-title-the-grothendieck-conjecture-for-affine-curves-div.pdf). Opened the entire hypotheses at the start of §5, Definition5.1, Remark5.2 and Theorem5.3, printed pp176–177; also introductory Theorem0.8. R is a DVR, X a smooth proper geometrically connected generic curve, D a relatively étale effective divisor, and 2−2g−degD<0. Smooth proper marked reduction is equivalent to trivial outer inertia on the geometric prime-to-p group and on one/every pro-l group, l≠p. The theorem includes proper curves. Used in F17.1 and to certify bad reduction in the explicit examples.
- **CF_Nakamura1994** — [Correct primary PDF](https://www.ms.u-tokyo.ac.jp/journal/pdf/jms010103.pdf), J.Math.Sci.Univ.Tokyo1(1994)71–136. Opened the hypotheses immediately preceding and the statement/proof of Theorem2.1.1, printed pp93–97: affine hyperbolic curve over a number field, full class of finite groups closed under subgroups, quotients, extensions; cyclotomic normalizer projects onto an open arithmetic subgroup and its action factors through that quotient. Used with the class of all finite groups in F17.4. The local node computation gives exactly such a closed normalizing subgroup, not an assumed point section. A second agent independently checked this use and the compatibility x^(1/n)z^(1/n)=t^(1/n). Also opened §1.5.2 and Corollary1.5.7 concerning centre-free extensions. A different guessed URL ending010105 was an unrelated article and was not used.
- **CF_SGA2** — [Primary recomposed PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0511279). Opened and visually inspected ExposéXII, Corollary3.5, recomposed p120/PDF127. Ample effective Cartier divisor in a projective scheme, depth at least2 for surjectivity, depth at least3 along the divisor plus purity off it for isomorphism. Smooth projective threefold Y=C³ satisfies the isomorphism hypotheses. Used in ZCF4 after the entirely explicit smoothness check, not as a substitute for that check. The OCR turns ≥ into >, so the image was inspected.
- **CF_StacksLimits** — [Tag01ZB, Proposition32.6.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01ZB). Opened: maps from an inverse system of qcqs schemes with affine transitions to a locally finitely presented target are the colimit of maps from finite levels; equality also descends. Used with finite covering transitions in ZCF8,ZCF12. The proof then shows the descended maps are finite étale, rather than assuming this from descent alone.
- **CF_PSV2009** — Panin–Stavrova–Vavilov, [primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.5465). Opened Theorem1 (Bertini with a finite set of prescribed closed points), Definition1, Proposition1 (elementary-fibration Zariski neighborhood over an infinite field for a smooth geometrically irreducible variety), Proposition2 (finite map to the relative projective line over a regular semilocal base). Used in ZCF9. The additional simple-branch/distinct-value refinement retaining the point is proved there by jet-surjectivity and dimension counts; it is not falsely attributed to Proposition2.

## Attached prior return checked

`PriorCyclotomic` is root's bibliography key for the supplied `new_results/CYCLOTOMIC_KERNEL_return/CYCLOTOMIC_KERNEL_paper.tex`. The proofs checked for this contribution are A6–A7 (centralizer, germs and larger subgroups), AE1 (finitely generated characteristic-zero extension), B7 (birational section-by-section centralizer criterion), C6 (polycurve point sections), D0b (fixed pro-point uniqueness), D4 (explicit Fermat cover), D5 (genus-four cyclotomic-base counterexample and projectivity argument), D10–D11 (common covering and finite-generator continuity descent), D13 (the exact unrestricted-action objective), AE4 (H1 correction), E4 (precise open H2 comparison). Our ZCF5–ZCF7 and ZCF12 state which portions are reused and which unproved existence statements remain.

The final ZCF12 correction checks the finite-generator proof of D11 in full, with the topology stated as pointwise convergence on the discrete union Ω of finite-level function fields. It then strengthens the conclusion using the finite orbit of every generator under the whole compact Galois group: the compositum is a finite covering field stable under all of G_k; its open deck subgroup is normalized; finite Galois cover descent acts on the actual open curve and its boundary; and the explicit logarithmic-canonical projective descent gives a quotient over k itself. The cofinal cover-system map is an equivariant bijection on compatible geometric pro-points. Thus continuous B′ is equivalent at fixed k to B with the G_k^circ clause of scan61. D0b also identifies plain B in the finitely generated characteristic-zero arithmetic range. The earlier draft's claimed invariant-level gap is withdrawn. D13's extra abstract-action scope without open function stabilizers remains unproved; no theorem about arbitrary abstract actions is inferred from continuity.

## Completed log-specialization and stable-degeneration audit

- **CF_Illusie2002** — [Primary Numdam PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/AST_2002__279__271_0.pdf). - **CF_Hoshi2009** — [RIMS1558 primary PDF, July2006 version](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/preprint/file/RIMS1558.pdf). - **CF_MochizukiComb2007** — [April2007 author PDF](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~motizuki/Combinatorial%20Grothendieck%20Conjecture.pdf). - **CF_HoshiMochizukiNodes** — [December2011 author PDF](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~motizuki/Nodally%20Nondegenerate%20Outer%20Representations.pdf), also supplied locally. - **CF_DupuyRabinoff2017** — [Primary arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.03034). Opened §1, p1: the relative Picard scheme of a compact-type model is smooth and separated, its special fibre is the product of component Jacobians, and the properness argument makes it an abelian scheme; also Lemma1.4 for faithful-flat comparison. The exposition assumes algebraically closed residue field. F17.2 explicitly passes to the strict-henselian extension and descends the relative Picard scheme's smoothness/properness. This supports the mixed-characteristic-5 Barsotti–Tate counterexample added to ZCF11 as well as the original characteristic-zero example.

The initial route also opened [Mochizuki, *On the Combinatorial Cuspidalization of Hyperbolic Curves*](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~motizuki/Combinatorial%20Cuspidalization.pdf), §0, printed pp12–13, where the generic/log-special comparison is displayed. The final proof replaces its cross-references by the numbered Hoshi, Illusie, and combinatorial results above. No unverified log comparison remains an input to F17.6. The source audit establishes the full-group theorem only in the stated characteristic-zero residue range; it makes no full-group claim in positive residue characteristic.

F17.7 constructs its effective descent datum explicitly: a finite Galois good-reduction extension acts on the unique stable model, the chosen residue splitting pulls that action back to a finite Galois residue extension, and the canonically linearized cube of the logarithmic canonical bundle descends the projective pair. The full arithmetic comparison uses Q = Gal(K1/K) ×_{Gal(k1/k)} G_k and the surjection q:G_K→Q with kernel I_{K1}. Good reduction kills that kernel in Out(Π), and the special-pair action of (sigma,g) uses both the finite semilinear isomorphism lambda_sigma and the full scalar action of g. Pullback by q∘s:g↦(alpha(g),g) is exactly the descended arithmetic action, retaining the ordinary infinite G_{k2} action. Thus the finite-extension and no-extension formulations of e agree over the stated finitely generated characteristic-zero residue fields. This conclusion is not inferred from an unproved polarized-Tate-module Torelli shortcut.
