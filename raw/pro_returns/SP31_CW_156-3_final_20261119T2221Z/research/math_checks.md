## MC01 — The ordinary product order is not an admissible test model

**Loci:** frozen scan 009, axiom (a); scan 017(c); sweep `readings/156-3.md` §1.2 and the later SA21-R3-11 example. **Type:** targeted proof check; a sweep assertion is refuted under the ordinary meaning of product order.

Take the ordinary componentwise order on \(\mathbb R^2\), with \(u<v\) meaning \(u\leq v\) and \(u\neq v\). For \(a=(0,0)\), both \(x=(1,0)\) and \(y=(0,1)\) lie in \(L_{>a}\), but any common lower bound of x and y which is at least a is a itself. Thus \(L_{>a}\) is not downward directed, and this poset fails the tronçon condition.

Consequently the sweep's assertion that ordinary product-ordered \(\mathbb R^2\) is a tronçon cannot support a counterexample *within* that class. This does not establish that its intended conclusion is true: it identifies an invalid example/hypothesis match. A different order on the same underlying set is introduced explicitly in MC02; it is not silently substituted into the sweep's claim.

## MC02 — A non-total tronçon with Hausdorff canonical topology

**Loci:** frozen scan 023, “réciproque ?”; sweep §1.5, “Only for these are points closed.” **Type:** own illustrative construction / targeted counterexample; confidence high because the order axioms and topology can be calculated directly. No novelty claim.

Define \(u\leq_s v\) on \(\mathbb R^2\) by
\[
u\leq_s v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u=v\ \text{or}\ (u_1<v_1\ \text{and}\ u_2<v_2).
\]
This is a partial order, distinct from the ordinary product order. It is upward and downward directed by choosing both coordinates beyond the corresponding maxima or minima; every strict interval is divisible by its coordinatewise midpoint. The strict upper cone of a is \((a_1,\infty)\times(a_2,\infty)\): given two points in it, a point coordinatewise between a and their coordinatewise minimum is a common lower bound still in the cone; the dual argument handles strict lower cones. Hence the explicit cone axioms hold, and nonempty cones again satisfy them.

The canonical intervals \(]a,b[\) are exactly ordinary open rectangles \((a_1,b_1)\times(a_2,b_2)\) for \(a<_s b\); the strict rays are open quadrants. They generate precisely the Euclidean topology. The order is non-total, for example \((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\) are incomparable, but its canonical topology is Hausdorff and its points are closed. Thus total order is sufficient, not necessary, for these separation properties, and the natural converse posed on scan 023 is false.

The parent independently proposed the same construction. A second valid model proposed by the parent is a rational line split into two incomparable branches for heights \(0<q<1\), with the junction heights 0 and 1 omitted and single common exterior tails; its small branch intervals give Hausdorff neighborhoods. The missing junctions are essential to the local strict-cone condition. MC02 is the simpler example used for the principal conclusion.

## MC03 — The doubled rational line does satisfy the axioms

**Loci:** frozen scans 009, 017, 022–023; sweep SA21-R3-08. **Type:** targeted proof check of a cited counterexample.

Let \(D=\mathbb Q\times\{0,1\}\), with \((q,i)<(r,j)\) exactly when \(q<r\), and equality added for the non-strict order. Both global directions have bounds by taking a rational height below or above the two given heights. Density gives an intermediate height whenever two elements are strictly comparable. For a strict upper cone over height q, a rational height strictly between q and the minimum of two higher heights supplies a lower bound in the cone; the lower-cone argument is dual. All nonempty strict cones contain more than one element.

Every canonical basic open is the inverse image of a rational open interval or ray under the height projection \(D\to\mathbb Q\). The twins \((q,0)\) and \((q,1)\) therefore have identical neighborhoods. Their common closure is exactly the two-point fibre: intervals exclude every other height, and no open separates the twins. This verifies the negative T0 answer in SA21-R3-08 without relying on the invalid ordinary-product example.

## MC04 — Unrestricted sobriety is already answered negatively; the refined question remains

## MC05 — The strict-cone profile gives an exact T0 criterion

**Loci:** scans 022–023; **RESULTS_STATE link:** WG2 B15 (§1.2), STRATA 0.1, as a distinction of topologies rather than a proof of their register claims. **Type:** own explanatory deduction; confidence high, by the generating-open test below. No novelty claim.

For a poset with the topology generated by strict lower and upper rays, define \(D(x)=L_{<x}\) and \(U(x)=L_{>x}\). Then
\[
b\in\overline{\{a\}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
D(b)\subseteq D(a)\ \text{and}\ U(b)\subseteq U(a).
\]
Indeed, membership in every ray containing b is exactly these two inclusion conditions, and the same property passes to finite intersections and arbitrary unions. Consequently the topology is T0 exactly when \(x\mapsto(D(x),U(x))\) is injective; it is T1 exactly when no two distinct profiles are comparable by simultaneous inclusion. This organizes MC02 and MC03 and gives a precise order-theoretic form of the question without identifying the specialization order with the original partial order.

## MC13 — The closure warning on scan 020 survives a direct check

## MC16 — Order reconstruction holds, with a correction to the degenerate-triple shortcut

**Loci:** frozen scan 012 lines 21–45 (weak relation, comparability, choice of pair); scan 013 lines 13–18 and 28–46 (anchor sets and reconstruction); scan 014 lines 5–26 (four-variable lemma); scan 015 lines 3–10 (comparability supplied separately). **Context IDs:** SA21-R3-01, SA21-R3-02; RESULTS_STATE item 156-3-order-from-betweenness. **Type:** narrow targeted proof check. No literature or novelty claim; no image consultation or TeX change.

Assume L has at least two elements, is directed upward and downward, and is dense: every x<z has some x<y<z. Define R(x,y,z) by x<y<z or z<y<x. The local strict-cone axioms are not used in this reconstruction.

First, distinct comparability D(x,z) is recovered as \(\exists y\,R(x,y,z)\); density is precisely what supplies the forward implication. Write \(C(x,z)\) for \(x=z\) or D(x,z). The correct weak-betweenness relation recovered from these data is
\[
B(x,y,z)=R(x,y,z)\ \lor\
\bigl((y=x\ \lor\ y=z)\ \land\ C(x,z)\bigr).
\]
It is equivalent to \(x\leq y\leq z\) or \(z\leq y\leq x\).

**Literal gap on scan 012.** The marginal alternative “R, or \(\{x,y,z\}\) is a flag of cardinal at most two” is insufficient: x=z=0, y=1 in Q satisfies its second clause, but y is not weakly between x and z. The middle entry must coincide with an endpoint in the degenerate case. If the shortcut were used literally in scan 013, the anchors a=0, b=1 and the witnesses x=0, y=1, u=1, v=0 would pass every displayed “between” test, wrongly asserting \(1\leq0\). Thus this is a consequential formula gap, although the assertion that knowledge of R determines the weak relation is true by the corrected formula. The frozen uncertainty between opposed arrows and an equivalence sign is not resolved here.

Choose a comparable pair and orient it a<b. Such a pair exists because two different points have common lower and upper bounds, which must be different. The R-defined sets
\[
A_-=\{a\}\cup\{x:R(b,a,x)\}=L_{\leq a},\qquad
A_+=\{b\}\cup\{y:R(a,b,y)\}=L_{\geq b}
\]
are exactly the scan 013 anchor sets. Then the order is given entirely by the known data:
\[
u\leq v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists x\in A_-,\,y\in A_+:
B(x,u,y)\land B(x,v,y)\land B(x,u,v).
\]
For the forward direction choose x below a and u, and y above b and v, using the two global directedness hypotheses. For the converse, x≤a<b≤y gives x<y. The first two betweenness conditions give x≤u,v≤y. The third gives either x≤u≤v or v≤u≤x; in the latter case the preceding inequalities force u=v=x, again yielding u≤v. The additional condition B(u,v,y) in scan 013 is redundant. This proves the four-variable lemma with non-strict “between,” including all endpoint coincidences.

Any other order with the same R can be reversed, if necessary, to orient the chosen pair a<b. The displayed formula then forces it to coincide with the first order; hence R determines the order up to opposite. If distinct comparability is given separately, the same proof needs no density, verifying the opening observation of scan 015. The scan 014 count “trois” for four named entries is a literal counting slip, not a mathematical obstruction. Its final permutation of the three variable names can be treated as a reference to the known ternary relation under renaming; it is not needed as a new recovery formula.

## MC17 — Pair cut partitions recover strict betweenness under the prescribed cut interpretation

**Loci:** frozen scan 011 lines 5 and 13–15 (complement and component display); scan 015 lines 13–24; scan 016 lines 3–32. **Context IDs:** SA21-R3-03; RESULTS_STATE item 156-3-betweenness-from-cuts; sweep reading §1.3. **Type:** narrow targeted proof check with explicit interpretation and hypotheses.

\[
C(\varepsilon)=\{t\notin\varepsilon:\varepsilon\cup\{t\}\text{ is a chain}\}
\]
into the nonempty members of \(L_{<p},\,]p,q[,\,L_{>q}\). “Unlabelled” means that the order labels below/between/above are forgotten, but each class is still known as a subset of L. These are the prescribed combinatorial order-cut classes. The theorem does not follow from arbitrary equivalence relations on the complements.

For three distinct pairwise comparable points, write a<b<c temporarily to verify the test. Let \(X_\varepsilon\) be the class of C(ε) containing the third point. Direct inequalities give
\[
X_{\{a,b\}}=L_{>b},\quad X_{\{b,c\}}=L_{<b},\quad
X_{\{a,c\}}=]a,c[,
\]
and therefore
\[
X_{\{a,b\}}\cap X_{\{b,c\}}=\varnothing,\quad
X_{\{a,b\}}\cap X_{\{a,c\}}=]b,c[,\quad
X_{\{b,c\}}\cap X_{\{a,c\}}=]a,b[.
\]
Density makes the last two intersections nonempty. Thus the middle point is exactly the point common to the unique pair of two-element subsets whose associated classes are disjoint. This is independent of the temporary orientation, proves the criterion in scan 016, and recovers strict R by first requiring a three-element chain. The partition step uses density but neither global directedness nor the local cone axioms. Combining it with MC16 proves the coherent two-step reconstruction for the intended tronçons.

Density is substantive here: on a three-element total order each pair complement is a singleton, so all three intersections are empty and the underlying pair-partition data are the same for every permutation of the order. The test cannot identify a middle point in that weaker setting.

The coherent cut interpretation above uses open intervals, as the explicit scan 016 equalities do. The typed set expression on scan 015 must be understood mathematically as \(\varepsilon\cup\{t\}\), and the cardinal-three condition excludes t∈ε. The duplicated primed pair variables at the end of scan 016 must refer to the two distinct pairs containing the candidate point; taking them literally equal would give the nonempty class intersected with itself. The uncertain subscript in the third intersection is not adjudicated as ink; the verified identity is the one with \(\{b,c\}\).

Finally, if “components” meant actual connected components of the canonical topology, this proof would not apply: for Q every such component of a pair complement is a singleton, and all three intersections above would be empty. This is the same distinction checked in MC12, now consequential for reconstruction. No topological connectedness assumption is smuggled into the verified combinatorial result.
