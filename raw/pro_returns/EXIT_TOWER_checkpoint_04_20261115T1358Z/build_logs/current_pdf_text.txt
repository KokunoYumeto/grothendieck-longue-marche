A.1. Boolean intervals: the complete list of stable types           Proved.
      cote 150; folio 9 and continuation; scans 28–30.
    Let Gg,n be the poset of isomorphism classes of connected stable weighted graphs with
n labelled legs, ordered by H ≤ G when G is a contraction of H. Every interval is Boolean
precisely for the stable types

                           (g, n) = (0, n),     n ≥ 3,       and   (g, n) = (1, 1).

For g = 0 the order-reversing map from a graph to its set of internal-edge splits identifies this
poset with the face poset of the complex of compatible stable splits, including the empty face.
This decides Part A(a)(i),(ii) and the first determination requested there; it addresses cote 150,
scans 28–30.

Proof. A genus-zero stable graph is a tree. Deleting an edge gives a bipartition A | Ac of the
labelled legs with both parts of size at least two. Different edges give different splits: the
path between two edges would otherwise have no leg or branching edge, leaving a valence-
two rational vertex. A family of splits is realized by a tree precisely when every two splits are
compatible, meaning that one of the four intersections of their two parts is empty. To reconstruct
the tree, fix a leg as root, orient each split away from it, and take the inclusion hierarchy of the
resulting subsets. Attach each leg to the smallest containing subset and add an edge for each
subset in the family. Compatibility makes the hierarchy nested or disjoint. Suppressing the
root convention recovers the original tree. Thus contraction deletes a specified subset of splits
and every interval is the Boolean interval of subsets between two fixed families.
   For (1, 1) there are exactly two graphs: a smooth genus-one vertex and a rational vertex
with one loop and the leg. Their poset has two elements.
    For (1, n) with n ≥ 2, take two rational vertices joined by two parallel edges, distributing the
legs so that each vertex carries at least one leg. Contracting either edge gives the same one-loop
rational graph with all legs; contracting both gives the smooth genus-one graph. This rank-two
interval has three elements. For g ≥ 2, take one vertex of weight g − 2, two loops, and all n
legs. Stability is 2g − 2 + n > 0. The two one-edge contractions are isomorphic, so this interval
also has three elements. A Boolean interval of rank two has four elements. These constructions
cover every remaining stable type.

finite lattice cokernel is invertible in characteristic zero. This is the explicit chart proof of log
étaleness and toroidality.

D.9. Theorem R for projective threefolds, with the factorization reference corrected         Proved.
    Two good smooth projective normal-crossings completions (X, D) and (Y, E) of the same

Proof. First blow up the closures of the self-intersection strata in increasing dimension, obtaining
simple normal-crossings boundaries. Locally these are barycentric subdivisions of the normal
orthants. Every new open stratum is a torus bundle over an old open stratum, hence is aspherical
by the homotopy exact sequence. Its normal-meridian lattice maps by a linearly independent set
of primitive fan vectors into the old lattice, hence still injects into π1 (U ). This proves goodness
at every step, and also proves the same preservation for the further stratal blow-ups below.
    By D.8 every component Ej of E has log discrepancy zero over (X, D). For a smooth SNC
pair, the divisorial valuations of log discrepancy zero are exactly those produced by successive
blow-ups of strata. One way to see the equality criterion is to pull back the logarithmic volume
form: a center lying properly inside an open stratum supplies at least one ordinary coordinate
and gives strictly positive discrepancy. A sequence following the center of the valuation
therefore has discrepancy zero only at corner centers. In a normal orthant this is precisely
a primitive monomial valuation. This criterion is also stated after Definition 2.7 in Svaldi’s
opened text, by reduction to the SNC discrepancy calculation. A primitive integral ray can be
extracted by smooth star subdivisions: in a chart containing the ray, subtract the least positive
coordinate from the others, reducing their sum until the ray is a coordinate ray. Finitely many
such extractions give a good smooth projective X 0 → X on which every prime divisor of Y
occurs.

the one or two marked branches. The chosen Rees component is not itself in this support, since

the Cartier pullback on Z. It follows that every Rees vertex has at least two incident directions

component of the intersection of two boundary divisors. Moreover T0 is the path between the

smooth curve germs, the vertices on that path are exactly those obtained by successive blow-ups

of corner intersection points. Induction verifies this: a corner blow-up subdivides the path,

v(f ) ≥ mv(I) over these finitely many Rees valuations. We use the existence and normalized-

center curves meet. In a triple-crossing chart x, y, z, a Rees valuation over x = y = 0 has weights
(a, b, 0), and its valuation ideal is generated by the monomials xi y j z k with ai + bj ≥ mv(I).

special point the local integral closure is the intersection of all these valuation ideals. A finite
intersection of monomial ideals is monomial: its generators are the least common multiples of
choices of generators. The same argument applies to every subcollection of the stratum curves.
An additional isolated integral-closure condition would require a Rees valuation centered at
that point, already excluded by finiteness on p-fibres. Thus the normalized blow-up is toroidal
everywhere, including the special triple points.

actions and étale charts. Applied to a monomial ideal its smooth centers are unions of torus
orbits closed in the chart, hence closures of strata; the local procedures agree on overlaps. We
obtain a good Xs → X 0 and a morphism Xs → Y . D.8 makes the latter toroidal. The corrected
toroidal weak factorization theorem, AKMW Theorem 2.7.1 and Matsuki’s erratum §2 Matsuki
(2000), factors it into smooth stratal blow-ups and blow-downs.
     We use its relative morphism case. For the projective toroidal morphism Xs → Y , the
cobordism has a fan map to the fan of Y ; its subdivisions preserve this map, and every
elementary quotient fan still maps to that endpoint fan. These are the explicit fan maps in the
proof of AKMW Theorem 2.7.1. Thus every intermediate variety is projective over Y and its fan
subdivides the fan of Y . This relative assertion proves goodness through the blow-down steps:
every intermediate open stratum is a torus bundle over an open stratum of Y , and its meridian
lattice injects, by the independent primitive generators of its cone, into the normal lattice of
that endpoint stratum. Asphericity and meridian injectivity follow directly from goodness of
Y , independently of the direction of the adjacent blow-up arrow. This gives the entire stated
zigzag.
   The proof uses only weak factorization. The algorithm for strong toroidal factorization in
the earlier Morelli/AMR citation was withdrawn in the opened erratum and is not used.

D.16. Canonical reconstruction from the supplied face and collar diagram                            Proved.
    Let V be the real oriented blow-up of a smooth complex normal-crossings Deligne–Mumford
pair (X , D), with its full deployed face diagram, collars, and maps to the strata. Collapsing
precisely the normal angular circles over the zero radial coordinates recovers X as a topological

For the supplied data, the final isomorphism is canonical and independent of the order of a
compatible induction.
    For Mg,n , the fundamental groupoid is already that of the compactification after all open
divisors have been attached to the open stratum. In the deepest-strata-first reconstruction
starting from V, it is already that groupoid after the first layer, consisting of all maximal-corner

explicitly related filtrations of the same final quotient.

Proof. On a uniformizing chart, write the boundary equation as z1 · · · zq = 0. The blow-up and

Its fibres identify exactly the θi for which ρi = 0. The resulting quotient chart is the original
complex chart: polar coordinates give a continuous bijection from the compact local quotient
to its Hausdorff image, and the same assertion on relatively compact subcharts gives a
homeomorphism. The construction is equivariant for the finite chart group and for coordinate
changes. Taking the quotient groupoids therefore gives the original topological stack.
