## Legibility and witnesses

The main lines on these supplied scans are usually readable, and many disagreements can be settled by a visible letter body, detached dot, final return, or separate stroke. Confidence is lower in dense cancellation layers, retraced indices, compressed final syllables, very pale foliation, and the large slanted note and drawing on scan059. An enlarged crop makes existing strokes easier to inspect; it does not recover detail absent from the supplied image.

The least secure readings remain marked in the transcription and listed above. In particular, alphabet distinctions are not forced where the same retraced contour permits both; an unreadable old word under a strike is not made certain because both witnesses propose it. Local punctuation and the scope of underlining are more fragile than the ordinary prose. A mark that joins a descender or insertion leader is not automatically a stop, underline, or cancellation.

Witness C generally preserves more of the page’s revision layers, marginal matter and picture structure. It still supplies some punctuation, misses local index details, and sometimes uses a plain alphabet where the ink is scripted. Witness H often supplies a useful competing word or symbol, but also omits marginal material, expands short forms, changes agreement, or mistakes an insertion leader for a cancellation. Neither witness’s agreement with the other is independent evidence. Counts in the changes log describe recorded physical places, including qualified tentative disagreements, and are not error rates per word or a measure of certainty about the entire page.
