# Targeted cross-page checks for scan 060

All checks below distinguish the written ink from a mathematical interpretation of its displayed definitions. The first partition label has an extra written star not repeated in (12), and the S/Sigma retracing in (10) remains doubtful. No repair of either is inserted.

## The two index subsets and the union assertion

Read the two displayed index subsets by their predicates X_ell not containing X_j and X_ell containing X_j. These predicates partition all ell<k. The first union description of R_{j,k} follows from scan057 (3bis), for j≤k and the common-lower-piece intersection condition (21d). The second description of S_{j,k} is not automatic: its definition (11) uses j≤ell<k, whereas the second subset uses X_j contained in X_ell. The setup explicitly allows lack of order reflection. If the family reflects order, the predicates agree, and R_{j,k} union S_{j,k}=dotted X_k follows. The optional nonempty-star reduction on scan055 supplies order reflection as proved in scan057.N13.

Strict lower inclusion alone is not enough for this new step. A finite counterexample has indices o,a,b,j,l,k ordered by o<a,b<j,l<k, with a,b incomparable and j,l incomparable. Give the discrete ambient set {p,q,r,s} the pieces X_o=empty, X_a={p}, X_b={q}, X_j={p,q}, X_l={p,q,r}, X_k={p,q,r,s}. Every strict comparison gives proper inclusion. All pairwise intersections equal the union of common lower pieces: the only nontrivial incomparable pair j,l has intersection {p,q}=X_a union X_b; a,b intersect in X_o. The family is a finite closed cover. Yet S_{j,k}=X_j={p,q}, while the union over ell<k with X_ell containing X_j is X_j union X_l={p,q,r}. Also R_{j,k}={p,q}, so its union with S_{j,k} misses r in dotted X_k. Thus both the second union identification and the displayed Donc equality fail without an additional condition such as order reflection.

## The parenthetical intersection

The literal intersection equality R_{j,k} cap S_{j,k}=dotted X_i is false even for a strictly increasing, order-reflecting finite chain with nonempty starred pieces. Let I={0<1<2}, X_0={0}, X_1={0,1}, X_2={0,1,2}, and take i=0,j=1,k=2. Then R_{1,2}=X_0 and S_{1,2}=X_1, so the left side is {0}, whereas dotted X_0 is empty. When k is a successor of j, S_{j,k}=X_j follows directly from (11); with the condition needed for scan057 (4), the intersection is dotted X_j, not generally dotted X_i. For general k even replacement of i by j is insufficient. For example take a finite poset of all subsets of {a,b,c}, set X_A to be its principal downset viewed as a discrete space, and take j={a}, k={a,b,c}. This family has nonempty stars and satisfies (21d). The point {b} belongs to X_{ {a,b} } and hence S_{j,k}; it also belongs to R_{j,k}, but not to dotted X_j. A valid general description is simply the union over j≤ell<k of R_j cap X_ell, using the definition of S and R_{j,k}=R_j cap X_k. The successor case is the useful sharper refinement.

## The extreme-index equalities (13)

Under the preferred S reading and live R_{i,k} in (10), S_{k,k}=empty immediately gives V_{i,k}^k=V_{i,k}, by (5). The other equality V_{i,k}^i=V_{i,k}^* follows if R_{i,k} union S_{i,k}=dotted X_k, e.g. under order reflection; then subtracting either set from the same barred neighbourhood gives the same result. It is not independently certified for the unreduced general family. The diagonal equalities inherit scan058.N9's qualification. These are set-algebra checks under the ordinary set interpretation, not proofs of any tubular-neighbourhood or fibration existence claim.
