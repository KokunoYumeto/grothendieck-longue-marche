The equality R_i intersect X_i = dotted X_i needs a qualification under the earlier setup. Scan049 expressly allows the increasing family of closed pieces to have repeated values and does not require it to reflect order. Take the two-element ordered set 0<1 and let both X_0 and X_1 be the same one-point space. This is a finite closed cover and satisfies the common-lower-piece intersection condition (21). Yet dotted X_1=X_0 is the whole point, whereas R_1, the union of pieces not containing X_1, is empty. Thus the reverse inclusion called trivial on scan057 fails for that allowed unreduced family.

One refinement is to assume every strict comparison l<i gives a proper inclusion X_l strictly contained in X_i. Then dotted X_i is contained in R_i immediately. Conversely, if X_k does not contain X_i, the common-lower-piece decomposition of X_k intersect X_i uses indices l at most k and i. Such an l cannot equal i, for that would imply i at most k and X_i contained in X_k. Hence each l<i, proving the other inclusion using (21). For i at most j, X_i is contained in X_j, so R_ij intersect X_i equals R_i intersect X_i.

The NB reduction on scan055 supplies a useful sufficient condition: retain only indices with nonempty X_i^*. If i<j had X_i=X_j, the lower union in X_j would already be all of X_j, contradicting its nonempty starred part. Thus the reduction enforces the strict-inclusion condition. More strongly, if two retained pieces satisfy X_i contained in X_j, choose x in X_i^*; its least containing index is i, so i is at most j. The retained family reflects order. Whether the author intended to adopt that optional reduction is not asserted by these mathematical checks, and no condition is added to the transcription.
