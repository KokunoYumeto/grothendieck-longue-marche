## Legibility and confidence

The handwritten mathematics ranges from clear prose to compressed scratch calculations, with many overwritten indices, cancellations, interlinear insertions and drawings in several orientations. Scans 048–059 and 073–075 need particular care where prose and diagrams overlap; the small superscripts throughout 061–086, the lower Hom sketch on 074, and the partly cancelled calculations on 076–079 are the least secure mathematical readings. The reused printer forms and typed Kimchi sheets are generally clearer, but cropped paper edges, exact counts of border characters, typewriter overstrikes and fading leave local doubts on 043, 047, 050, 056, 059, 062, 072–074 and 080–087. The right edge of 085 does not contain the missing ends of several words; those ends have not been supplied from another copy.

Every scan was read personally as a whole page and in its supplied halves before its first page file was saved. Each completed file then received a second visual audit. No OCR or other image-to-text reading software was used. Computer extraction below reads only the already written LaTeX doubt macros; its raw spans and source hashes are retained in `records/doubts_extracted.json`.

The register lists each surviving doubt occurrence, including repetitions and nested occurrences. Its location is the line and column of the corresponding page fragment, an unambiguous locator for the approximate manuscript position. **Medium** means a single preferred reading remains plausible but a stroke or ending is insecure; **low** means competing readings remain, the relevant writing is strongly overwritten, or clipping/complex overlap limits the reading. No doubt occurrence is rated high: the clearer resolved readings were removed from doubt markup during audit. Confidence is an editorial assessment, not a measured error rate. The explicit mathematical surprises recorded in the audit are retained even when no doubt macro is required.
