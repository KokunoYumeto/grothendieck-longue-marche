REGULAR PANTS: A PRIMITIVE-CUSP WREATH QUOTIENT
Root's construction, independently verified in CE.  Research note only.

Let S=A5, choose a of order3 and b of order5, and set
E=(S x S) semidirect C2, with the last factor interchanging the two
simple factors.  Map the free source basis(x,z) to the swap and(a,b).
This is an epimorphism of the actual full Fhat2 onto E, of order7200.

Uniform generation proof.
Any subgroup L containing z=(a,b) contains(a,1) and(1,b), by the
coprime orders3 and5.  If it contains any element h=(u,v)swap,
conjugating those two elements supplies(1,vav^-1) and(ubu^-1,1).
Each factor is therefore generated by an element of order3 and one
of order5, so both full factors belong to L.  Thus L=E.

The generation assertion for A5 is elementary.  A subgroup containing
orders3 and5 has order15,30 or60.  Order15 would have a normal Sylow5
and trivial order3 action on it, giving an element of order15, which
A5 has no room to contain.  Order30 would have index2, but A5 is
generated by3-cycles and therefore has no nontrivial C2 quotient.
Only order60 remains.  No subgroup enumeration is needed for this
argument; the exact checker nevertheless verifies all60 conjugations.

C44 consequence.
The image in E/(S x S)=C2 of a word g is its x-exponent
modulo2, since z maps into the base.  C44's full unit exponent is
odd, so its actual section conjugator lies in the nontrivial coset.
Consequently <g,z> maps onto this actual non-solvable wreath quotient.
The argument uses no unknown evaluation of g within the base factors.

It also proves a sharp obstruction to a misleading prosolvable
retract construction.  A prosolvable subgroup of F containing the
literal primitive z cannot surject onto this particular C2 quotient:
its finite image would contain z and an opposite-coset element and
therefore equal the non-solvable E.  Thus a section of the free
prosolvable quotient cannot fix the actual z while preserving the
x-exponent modulo2.  The existence of an abstract prosolvable
retract with full abelianization does not bypass the primitive-cusp
condition in the regular-pants problem.
