# Root review: every discrete mixed conjugate intersection

Date:2026-11-16. The complete B.72 source was read and accepted.

The statement concerns H_T intersect g H_5 g^-1 for every discrete ambient g. It does not assert the corresponding profinite result or the individual malnormality of either factor.

The cyclic source comparison uses the actual unramified punctured covering z to z^5 and the parameter covering u to u^5. Its degree and the absence of a nonidentity pure deck element justify the embedding into the pure four-point mapping class group. The hyperbolic/parabolic classification there gives every nontrivial source element either pseudo-Anosov type or a cusp power. Pullback of the two invariant measured foliations proves preservation of the pseudo-Anosov property, since the ramification poles remain punctures. The seven cusps lift to a six-point cluster twist (one curve) or five pair twists (five curves), each with nonzero exponent.

In the reducible case, A4 acting on a set of one or five curves has a fixed curve: nontrivial orbit sizes at most five can only be3or4. A4 has no C2 quotient, so this curve's sides would each be invariant. That contradicts the actual transitive A4-action on the twelve punctures.

For the pseudo-Anosov case, we opened the primary McCarthy paper at https://users.math.msu.edu/users/mccarthy/publications/normcent.pdf and read the whole four-page text, especially Lemmas1–4 and the proof of Theorem1. Its introductory surface hypothesis is compact and of negative Euler characteristic, so the manuscript includes the marked-sphere adaptation explicitly. Marked one-prong separatrices remain in the finite set; a measure-preserving symmetry fixing a separatrix fixes its dense leaf and hence the surface. Representatives inject relative to the punctures, since an identity-class periodic sphere map fixes all punctures and is the identity. This gives the needed finite kernel of the dilation homomorphism on the punctured-sphere centralizer.

The finite groups A4 and the conjugate C5 therefore belong to the same finite symmetry kernel, so their generated subgroup is finite. Finite rotation-group classification makes it A5. Its action on twelve punctures is the rigid exceptional orbit, whose discrete centralizer is trivial by the already proved quotient triangle argument. This contradicts a nonidentity intersection element.

The source proof keeps both comparison directions and all group categories explicit. No discrete Nielsen–Thurston type is assigned to a profinite element, and no density or full finite-index refinement conclusion follows. The full icosahedral centralizer remains Open.
