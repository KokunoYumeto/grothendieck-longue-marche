## Legibility and limits of this reading

The main lines and most large formulae are reasonably recoverable, but the fast cursive, retracing, tiny superscripts, and densely revised margins make this a fallible diplomatic reading. All 41 whole pages and their 82 supplied half-page images were inspected personally in one transcription stream, and all 41 pages received a separate second visual audit. No OCR was used. Technical compilation and inventory checks establish syntax and recorded coverage; they cannot establish that an uncertain word has been read correctly.

The least secure passages are the interwoven margins on 44–46 and 49; the cancelled and inserted layers on 55 and 58; the small marginal word on 63; the minute parenthesis above the margin on 65; the central comparison of two theories on 72; the extra signs after `Si` and the rewritten exponent in the title on 77; the wholly cancelled draft on 80; the squeezed right margin on 81; the overlapping revision before (67) on 82; and especially the slanting left margin on 84. The long uncertain phrases on 72 and 84 are tentative readings of surviving strokes, not reconstructed prose. They should not be quoted as established wording.

Only two places use `\illegible`: three cancelled words in the margin on 49, and two cancelled words before condition e) on 51. Those counts are estimates. Elsewhere, even where confidence is low, a proposed reading and any plausible graphic alternative occupy the relevant place in the text. No mathematical symbol is replaced by an illegible-word count.

Graphic variation has intentionally survived the consistency check: a plain dash is sometimes all that can be seen; strict-flag signs sometimes resemble stars; some limits have only an underline; an upper or lower index can be surprising; and grammatical repetitions may come from unreconciled additions. The doubts table identifies these cases without converting them into a corrected mathematical edition. Later contextual suggestions belong only in the separate research notes, after the transcription freeze.
