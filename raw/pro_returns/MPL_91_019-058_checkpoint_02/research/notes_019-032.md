### A03 — The coefficient needed is regularity of the parameter on the ideal

**Location:** cote 91, scan 019, the sequence labelled (*). **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the algebraic distinction, because the example below checks the exact module condition directly; limited as a reconstruction of omitted hypotheses. **Exact quote:** `0\longrightarrow J\xrightarrow{\pi^{\unsure{n+1|-n}}}J\longrightarrow J_n\longrightarrow0`. **Search:** `torsion|intègre|associated|paramètre|N91-20`. **Context and relation:** **differs** from the integral-base explanation in **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, §II, “La démonstration : l'obstruction”, pp.18–19 = scans 019–020; **adds** a test to `same_cote_91/MPL_90+91_002-018_notes.md`, N91-20/NCTX-05 (91/018). **Register:** H-H, external candidate status retained.

The displayed injection requires that the indicated power of the parameter act injectively on (J), and the localization argument also needs (J\to J_\eta) injective. If (R=k[t]_{(t)}), (A=R[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)), and (J=(\epsilon)), then (A) is not integral but (J\cong R) has both properties for (\pi=t); conversely an integral (A) has no nonzero square-zero ideal. Thus the sufficient hypothesis should be stated on the coefficient module or reduced base, with the original one-dimensional/associated-prime conditions checked, rather than importing “(A) integral” as a harmless explanation of a nontrivial square-zero extension.

### A12 — The universal quotient is controlled by the kernel of the joint restriction map

**Location:** cote 91, scan 025, margin diagram and first main paragraph. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the universal-property calculation; the identification with the geometric functor remains conditional on A09–A10. **Exact quote:** `$(\sigma,\sigma\alpha\pi')=\rho$`; `Le foncteur en $G$ est repr. par $\operatorname{Coker}\rho$`. **Search:** `rho|conoyau|Coker|quotient universel|R_1`. **Context and relation:** **differs** from the identification (J/R=\operatorname{coker}\rho) in **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, §II, “Une variante sous hypothèse de rigidité”, pp.23–24 = scans 024–025. **Register:** H-H, external candidate status retained.

Put (q=(i,\beta):L\oplus M'\twoheadrightarrow M) and retain the written (\rho=(\sigma,\sigma\alpha\pi'):L\oplus M'\to J): pairs ((\mu,\lambda)) with (\mu q=\lambda\rho) are represented by (C=\operatorname{coker}(q,-\rho)\cong J/\rho(\ker q)), since surjectivity of (q) permits elimination of the (M)-coordinate. This gives the missing family of compatibility relations directly and requires a proof that (\rho(\ker q)=R_1+R_2) before using the source's quotient. It is not in general (\operatorname{coker}\rho): with (L=M=J=k), (i=\sigma=\mathrm{id}), and all primed modules zero, (C=k) while (\operatorname{coker}\rho=0). The margin could require a different intended domain for its symbol (\rho), but that interpretive repair belongs only in notes.

### A13 — Flat base change and arbitrary base change are distinct assertions here

**Location:** cote 91, scan 025, margin and final paragraph before the corollaries. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Exact quote:** `commute à tout chgt de base`; `commute à l'extension plate de la base $S$`. **Search:** `changement de base|chgt de base|extensions plates|localisation|N91-17`. **Context and relation:** **adds** the distinction to **Hua, modernised reading of cote 91**, §II, “Une variante sous hypothèse de rigidité”, p.24 = scan 025, which reports flat extension; compare `same_cote_91/MPL_90+91_002-018_notes.md`, N91-17 (91/016). **Register:** H-H, external candidate status retained.

The main text states flat base-change compatibility for (J/(R_1+R_2)), while the margin makes an arbitrary-base-change assertion for its cokernel. Cokernels commute with scalar extension for a fixed module presentation, but kernels entering the relations require flatness unless a further argument is supplied, and the representing modules themselves must be compatible with the same base change. The distinction matters for the claimed criteria 7°/8°: localization follows from the flat case, whereas a full universal obstruction construction demands a separately specified functorial presentation.
