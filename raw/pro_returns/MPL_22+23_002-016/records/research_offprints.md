The source is the frozen transcription. The two offprints identify their printed author as Jean-Pierre Jouanolou; the handwritten responses are treated as annotations in the Grothendieck archive, with their reading doubts retained. ## O02. The exact comparison between D and Omega

**Kind: reading question. Location: 22/006, opening notation paragraph, “\(D_A(B)\) le \(B\)-module des différentielles.”** For comparison with the context, the map \(D_A(B)\to\Omega^1_{B/A}\) sends the universal differential of \(b\) to \(d_{B/A}b\); the inverse sends \(d_{B/A}b\) to that same universal differential in \(D_A(B)\), and both composites fix the differential generators. This specifies the notation comparison used below while keeping every printed D in the frozen transcription. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, 20.4.3, 20.4.6–20.4.8, especially the representing map \(h\mapsto h\circ d_{B/A}\); no matching settled register entry.

## O03. The corrected Tor argument and its missing second target

**Kind: reading question / unproved claim. Location: 22/002, Proposition 1, conditions (b) and (c).** In the first Tor expression the annotation replaces the argument A by \(A/\underline a\), while in the higher-Tor expression the A is deleted without a visible replacement. The relevant explicit quotient map is \((A/\mathfrak a)\otimes_A D(A)\to D(A/\mathfrak a)\), \(\bar b\otimes da\mapsto\bar b\,d\bar a\), whose kernel in the conormal sequence is the image of \(\mathfrak a/\mathfrak a^2\) under \(x\mapsto1\otimes dx\); the printed equivalence requires a precise choice of quotient module in every Tor group and a proof relating those groups to regularity. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, 20.5.11–20.5.12, and `EGA_chap0_ega0-22.tex`, 22.2.2 and 22.6.7; these offer concrete comparison maps, with no matching settled register item validating the annotated statement.

## O04. The “Séries formelles ??” qualification

**Kind: self-flagged. Location: 22/002, margins alongside Corollary 3 and Proposition 2.** The annotations “assez rare !” and “Séries formelles ??” focus attention on the hypotheses that the separated differential module be of finite type and that the absolute differential module be free. The comparison to make is through the actual canonical map \(\Omega^1_{B/k}\to\widehat\Omega^1_{B/k}=\varprojlim_n\Omega^1_{B/k}/\mathfrak n^{n+1}\Omega^1_{B/k}\), with the topology and target recorded, rather than by dropping completion or finiteness from the printed criterion. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, 20.7.14–20.7.16, and `EGA_chap0_ega0-21.tex`, 21.9 on completed differentials of power-series rings; the later discrete-topology pages bear on the Hua **22-power-series-discrete-obstruction** lead [conjecture / candidate], but that entry does not settle this marginal question about the offprint's hypotheses.

## O05. Completion, the kernel lemma, and the absent D

**Kind: unproved claim / reading question. Location: 22/003, upper third, lemma and following sequence.** The lemma states a containment for the kernel of \(\widehat A\otimes_A D(A)\to D(\widehat A)\), then claims exactness under “semi-fini”; the displayed sequence itself visibly has \((\widehat A)\) in the middle. The context's transitivity morphisms supply the specific candidate \(D(\widehat A)\), with \(b\otimes da\mapsto b\,da\) from the first term and \(dx\mapsto d_{\widehat A/A}x\) to the last, so this is a recorded reading question rather than a change to the frozen formula. The proof obligation left by the offprint is the kernel/intersection containment and the separation argument for the stated direct sum of a free and a finite module. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, 20.3.3, 20.5.7 and 20.7.14; no matching settled register entry.

## O06. The printed claim to repair Lang's argument

**Kind: unproved claim. ## O07. Injectivity added to the analytic-reduction argument

**Kind: self-flagged / withdrawn in the same text. Location: 22/003, lower lemma and margin; 22/004, opening theorem continuation and following margin.** The margin asks, tentatively, “faux si on ne suppose pas \(A\to B\) injectif ??”; the continuation adds “\(C\supset A\) !!!,” and the next margin says “faux” beside the localisation explanation. The supplied context gives a directly comparable use of the missing inclusion: 0.22.3.2 assumes an integral finite extension containing A and uses injectivity in the square \(A\to B\), \(\widehat A\to\widehat B\) to obtain \(\mathfrak p\cap A=0\), enabling passage to \(\widehat A_{\mathfrak p}\otimes_{K_A}K_B\). This documents a precise proof obligation and a handwritten correction, while the exact scope of the isolated “faux” must remain tied to its visible placement rather than treated as a disproof of every subsequent corollary. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-22.tex`, 22.3.2, printed pp. 191–192; no matching settled register entry.

## O08. “Connu (Nagata)” and the question about the application

**Kind: unproved claim / self-flagged. Location: 22/004, Proposition 7 and the following application sentence.** The margin labels the finite-normalisation assertion “connu (Nagata),” while a separate “?” accompanies its application to localities, analytic localities and regular Hopf algebras over a perfect field. The supplied 0.23.1.7 treats the same finiteness mechanism by placing the base change of the integral closure inside \(R\otimes_{K_A}L\), where R is the total fraction ring of \(\widehat A\), and descending finite generation along \(A\to\widehat A\); this identifies the concrete map and the completion/separability assumptions to check for each proposed application. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-23.tex`, 23.1.5 and 23.1.7, printed pp. 215–217; no matching settled register entry, and the marginal question remains a source question.

## O09. “Quasi-séparable,” the proposed “non ramifiée,” and the exclamation/question

**Kind: reading question / self-flagged. Location: 22/006, definition and final two lines.** The printed definition is “quasi-séparable lorsque \(D_A(B)=0\),” beside a tentative handwritten “non ramifiée,” and the last implication from \(\mathfrak n=\mathfrak mB\) has the annotation “!?”. The exact infinitesimal bridge is the difference of two lifts \(u,v:B\to E\) through a square-zero ideal L: \((u-v)(bb')=\bar u(b)(u-v)(b')+\bar u(b')(u-v)(b)\), so the difference is an A-derivation represented by \(\operatorname{Hom}_B(D_A(B),L)\). This establishes the lift-uniqueness comparison attached to the differential condition, while assessing the final local assertion also requires retaining its residue-field and finiteness conditions. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-19.tex`, 19.10.2 and 19.10.4; `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, 20.4.8 and 20.7.4; `EGA_chap0_ega0-21.tex`, 21.7.4; no matching settled register entry.

## O10. The two cotangent sequences behind the rank criterion

**Kind: unproved claim. Location: 22/006, two displayed sequences; 22/007, regularity theorem.** The two source sequences map the class of \(x\in\mathfrak n\) to \(dx\otimes1\in D_A(B)\otimes_B L_b\), using respectively \(\mathfrak n^2+\mathfrak mB\) and \(\mathfrak n^2+(\mathfrak n\cap A[B^p])B\) in the denominator. The offprint attributes these sequences to Berger and Kunz and then gives the freeness/rank criterion; the proof tasks are the asserted left-hand injectivity in each residue-field case and the passage from fibre dimension to freeness of the finite module. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, 20.5.11–20.5.12; `EGA_chap0_ega0-22.tex`, 22.2.9 and its exact sequence (22.2.9.1); `EGA_chap0_ega0-21.tex`, 21.1–21.4 for p-bases and differentials; no matching settled register entry.

## O11. The printed reversal of the residue-field letters

**Kind: reading question. Location: 22/008, Proposition 1, first sentence.** The proposition prints “\(K_b\) (resp. \(L_a\)) est séparable sur \(K_a\) (resp. \(L_b\)),” while the source's local map \(u:A\to B\) supplies the residue-field homomorphism \(L_a=A/\mathfrak m\to L_b=B/\mathfrak n\), \(a+\mathfrak m\mapsto u(a)+\mathfrak n\), whose kernel is zero because \(u^{-1}(\mathfrak n)=\mathfrak m\). This direction also matches the source's \(D_{L_a}(L_b)\) and \(\{L_b:L_a\}\) elsewhere, so \(L_b\) over \(L_a\) is a precise comparison candidate; the original reversed printed order remains unchanged in the transcription. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, 20.5.7, and `EGA_chap0_ega0-22.tex`, 22.2.9; no matching settled register item.

## O12. The separated quotient as an explicit morphism

**Kind: unpursued implication / unproved claim. Location: 22/008, concluding paragraph, “il vaut mieux séparer.”** The printed construction is the quotient map \(q:D_A(B)\to D'_A(B)=D_A(B)/\bigcap_n\mathfrak n^nD_A(B)\); the map \(x\mapsto(x\bmod\mathfrak n^nD_A(B))_n\) into \(\varprojlim_nD_A(B)/\mathfrak n^nD_A(B)\) has precisely that intersection as kernel, hence induces an injection of \(D'_A(B)\) into the inverse limit. This explicit factorisation is the comparison with the completed differential module in the context, and the offprint's final assertion that Proposition 2 survives substitution of \(D'_K(A)\) still needs its own rank/regularity argument. **Context / state:** `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, 20.7.14–20.7.16; `EGA_chap0_ega0-21.tex`, 21.9; no matching settled register entry.

## O13. Local completion maps defining trace and norm

**Kind: unproved claim. Location: 22/028, formula (40) and paragraph following it.** The reused typescript defines \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/K'}\) and \(N_{K/K'}\) on répartitions through the component maps \(\widehat K_P\to\widehat K'_{P'}\), summing local traces and multiplying local norms over \(P\mid P'\), then claims agreement with field trace and norm on principal répartitions. ## O14. The divisor/idèle compatibility squares

**Kind: unpursued implication. Location: 22/030, formulas (31)–(38); 22/028, formulas (39), (44).** The two concrete commutative-square claims are \(D\circ\operatorname{Ext}_{K',K}=\operatorname{Ext}_{K',K}\circ D'\) and \(D'\circ N_{K/K'}=N_{K/K'}\circ D\), with the upper maps acting on idèles and the lower maps on divisors. On the divisor generators the written maps are \(P'\mapsto\sum_{P\mid P'}e(P)P\) and \(P\mapsto f(P)P'\), so their composite sends \(P'\) to \((\sum e(P)f(P))P'\); the further equality with \(nP'\) in formula (35) uses the source's explicit “Comme \(\sum e(P)f(P)=n\),” whose ambient field/valuation hypotheses and proof are not supplied on the retained leaf. **Context / state:** `ega4-backmatter-contents-fr.tex`, IV 21.4–21.5 and 21.9–21.10, printed p. 343; EGA I introduction p. 6, “Résidus et dualité”; these are comparison locators, with no matching settled register item or supplied proof of all idèle-level compatibilities.

## O15. The elided verification of projection and transitivity identities

## Context-text caution used in these notes

The supplied edition itself is a fallible transcription. In `EGA_chap0_ega0-20.tex`, the Hom display within the proof of 20.7.2 points in the opposite direction from precomposition by the stated map, and the following proof paragraph at 20.7.3 contains incompatible surjectivity/injectivity language; these notes therefore use the explicit maps and the separately stated locators, without treating those particular supplied proof lines as an independently checked argument. The original printed pp. 147–148 were subsequently inspected: the precomposition arrow has the correct direction and the proof of 20.7.3 uses induced topologies and references, as recorded in S02 of the final notes; nothing in the frozen archive transcription has been changed.
