# SR03_71 — hand log

Only observations made on supplied images in this run belong below. Scope is local unless explicitly extended; writers are not identified from conjecture.

## 71_scan011

Heavy t exponents can form an inverted-U cap: compare b02, b04 and b08 with b01's thin 2. All four heavy instances on this leaf are 2, not 3. The q in b01 has a closed oval and straight descending right stem; it is not the looped-descender g. Its operator and indices remain uncertain, so this is not a blanket rule for later pages. The comma between lambda factors has a low bulb and descending left tail (b07), unlike a centered multiplication dot. Arrowheads may occur midway along the shaft: the tiny upward head above e in b10 was missed in the supplied text. Other headless strokes are retained individually, not inferred from the diagram's meaning.

## 71_scan012

This hand's W has two connected low lobes, and its final rising stroke may meet a closure bar (b22). It must not be split into U plus a prime. Compare the clearly separate prime after adjacent U'_K and the live W in b04. The b22 shape supports the earlier W reading locally; it is not a general licence to replace U-prime elsewhere. A standalone equals in prose is two parallel horizontal strokes (b19), used for même. Joined-letter writing has irregular pen lifts, so gaps within pseudopartition do not by themselves justify a new orthography. Boundary dot/bar corrections and heavily crossed small signs stay uncertain when stroke order cannot be recovered.

## 71_scan015

## 71_scan013

Sigma may have a detached overbar as well as its own curved upper stroke (top diagram and lower-left identity). This was missed by both supplied text and C; it is not present over every Sigma on the leaf. The three compact defining abbreviations join the f return to a very short terminal wave, making df and dfn hard to separate. K/Delta overwrites leave both a vertical branched form and a triangular base; darkness alone does not establish chronological order. The top R has a clear lower right leg and is not H's P. ## 71_scan017

Scan 017: the heavy I has serifs but no independent doubled vertical; A is distinctly triangular. In the first union, the j/beta-like indices have two closed loops and a descending stem; compare them with later clear j and do not regularize them. The defined delta endpoint-set sign acquires a long overshooting return that resembles beta in the isolated list. The clear membership epsilon in b) has a middle tooth that distinguishes it from a simple subset cup. Compressed d with a flat return leaves du/de and dans/ds open; the same-sign can be written in one stroke joining its two bars at the left. The defining df/dfn label again ends in a short wavy return, as on 013. The long descenders of g in joignant and j in surjectif are not underlines. These observations are local to the cited instances; they do not license a global letter substitution.

## 71_scan019

The heavy hand on scan 019 reduces on a to a point, a short low wave and a point. A similarly reduced a-accent can resemble a colon, so that instance remains uncertain rather than being normalised from grammar. The serifed capital I has detached top and bottom bars and is distinguished from the genuine closure bars above Gamma-I. Distinct short primes can meet a relocation leader; b07 establishes one such prime. This is a local observation, not a rule that every mark near a leader is a prime.

## 71_scan021

This memoir hand has ornate capital C with an inclined upper loop and closed lower curl: compare Carlos and both surname components, scan 021. That supports C rather than the blind B in the surname; the isolated initial is less secure. Its r can be a small rising return, visible in interieurs. The heavier annotating hand has a square r in or. ?. Partial underlines deliberately span only letters in geometr(iq), aussi and couple; the blind reading sometimes marked their target words more broadly. These observations distinguish the main and annotation layers and are not generalized to the whole cote.

## 71_scan022

The heavy hand reduces sommet to low connected returns followed by a tall t and detached upper bar; no final s is present in either instance on scan 022. Its barred m can resemble equality, but the lower stroke has a small initial cup and uprights (b09). This is not a universal substitution for every equality elsewhere. The o of polygone carries a small detached circumflex here.

## 71_scan023

On scan 023 the heavy marginal initial in fin/bien/lin has a tall hollow loop with a low cross, making f plausible where prior readers chose b. This remains local and uncertain. The thin hand places i dots to the left of their stems; these can be mistaken for e accents in interieur/exterieur (blind b24/b28 and unboxed text). Both i positions were checked; no universal rule about missing accents is inferred. A flattened last return can be e or s (non vides/vide), so the plural is no longer declared certain.
