# Research notes: octahedron and four-point moduli, scans 84–89

These notes concern the frozen transcription only; none changes it. “Hua §XI” means Michel Hua's project's **modernised reading of cote 67**, `context/hua_project/67.modern.tex`, section **XI. Modules M₀,₄ et l’octaèdre (pages 83 à 88)**; its page numbers are one below the scan numbers here. Its assertions are credited to that project and are checked only to the extent stated below. The register has no settled row specifically covering this octahedral construction; the relevant open comparison and frame questions are delimited in O16. Every note has its own actual context searches in `search_ledger.json`; hits are search leads, not assertions that every hit was proof-checked.

## O01 — Four labels already carry a canonical affine plane

**Location:** 67/84, opening displays. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “formé de l’identité et des ‘bitranspositions’”.

The three partitions of a four-element set into two pairs give the three nonzero elements of its canonical Klein four group, and each double transposition extends uniquely to the projective line containing those four points. This **restates** the affine-plane and partition construction of `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans 551–555, particularly (32)–(36), and **adds** the six fixed points of the resulting action on the ambient line, as also described in Hua §XI, their p.83/our scan84. The elementary extension argument is checked in `proof_checks.md` §1; it does not establish any profinite frame or Galois-image comparison from the register.

## O02 — The normalizer claim survives the small-characteristic correction

**Location:** 67/84–86, standard matrices and normalizer extension. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “Le normalisateur … est isomorphe … S₄”.

Over a quadratically closed field of characteristic different from 2, the standard group generated by −z and 1/z has centralizer exactly itself, and conjugation by iz and (z+1)/(z−1) realizes the two transpositions generating the permutation group of its three fixed-point pairs, so its normalizer has order 24 and acts faithfully on four antipodal face-pairs as S₄. This **supports** Hua §XI, their pp.83–85/our scans84–86, and **adds a direct local verification** to `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan569, whose level-four/octahedral comparison is phrased expectantly. This normalizer calculation remains valid in characteristic 5 even though the bare-six-point assertion in O03 fails; the finite computation and general argument are in `finite_octahedron_check.py` and `proof_checks.md` §2.

## O03 — Characteristic 5 refutes recovery from the six-point set alone

**Location:** 67/84 and 86, the asserted “corr 1-1” between V and S. **Kind:** own idea; counterexample check. **Quote:** “de car. ≠2” and “les parties … qui sont ‘octaédrales’”.

Over k = algebraic closure of F₅, take i=2, so the displayed six-set is all of P¹(F₅); translation z↦z+1 preserves that set but carries V={z,−z,1/z,−1/z} to a different Klein four group, with the same union of fixed points. This **contradicts** the bare-six-set bijection repeated in Hua §XI, their pp.83 and85/our scans84 and86, and is a refinement absent from the retrieved context; `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan571, already records the order-120 projective group over F₅, but does not make this connection. **Own check, high confidence:** explicit matrices and exhaustive projective-matrix enumeration give five conjugate V's with this same S, Aut(P¹,S) of order120, and each individual normalizer of order24 (`finite_octahedron_certificate.json`); this is not a claim that the four-point moduli theorem itself fails in characteristic5.

## O04 — Retaining the antipodal pairing repairs the loss of data

**Location:** 67/86, marginal categorical corollary and final antipodism paragraph. **Kind:** own idea; refined statement. **Quote:** “la catégorie … (X,V) (ou (X,Γ) (X,S))”.

The proposed equivalence using a bare pair (X,S) cannot hold in characteristic5, because its automorphism group has order120 whereas an oriented combinatorial octahedron has automorphism group of order24. A sufficient repair is to retain the antipodal pairing on S: its even pair-flips recover V canonically, and its projective stabilizer is then the normalizer, as verified in `proof_checks.md` §§2–3. This **differs from** Hua §XI, their p.85/our scan86, which keeps the unqualified three-way bijection, while **using the extra datum already present** in the same manuscript paragraph and Hua's following description of the antipodism; **own refinement, high confidence**, because recovery of V is an explicit finite permutation construction, with no assertion of novelty.

## O05 — A face and its antipodal pair have different stabilizers

**Location:** 67/85, first prose paragraph. **Kind:** unproved claim with a checked correction in context. **Quote:** “Pour un tel triplet, le sous-groupe de Γ qui l’invarie est un scindage”.

Hua §XI, their p.84/our scan85, explicitly corrects this to the stabilizer of an **antipodal pair of faces**, crediting the distinction to that project's reading. The manuscript **differs from** that correction as transcribed: a single face has stabilizer C₃, whereas its antipodal pair has stabilizer S₃, of order6, which is the complement to V required for a splitting; both counts are rechecked by the faithful six-vertex action and exact enumeration. `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans551–552, gives the corresponding four S₃ complements in the permutation extension, and thus **supports the corrected comparison**, not the literal triplet-stabilizer assertion.

## O06 — The four splittings form a V-torsor

**Location:** 67/84–86, extension isomorphisms and I(V). **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “défini modulo autom. intérieurs par un élément de V”.

Hua §XI, their pp.83–84/our scans84–85, supplies its own cohomological explanation H¹(S₃,V)=0, which is explicitly credited to that project here. The present check **supports** this addition: exhaustive enumeration of all 4⁶ cochains for the natural GL₂(F₂)-module finds four cocycles, exactly the four coboundaries, so the four complements are conjugate simply transitively under V (`complements_certificate.json`). This also **restates geometrically** the four point-stabilizer complements in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans551–554; it is a finite complement calculation and does not settle **LM4 C.12**'s infinite-operator-group comparison.

## O07 — The orientation datum points to a precise descent question

**Location:** 67/85–86, choice of orientation and marginal corollary. **Kind:** unpursued implication / own idea. **Quote:** “revient au choix d’une racine carrée de −1”.

The orientation torsor is identified with the two square roots of −1, so an arithmetic version should retain the equivariant map {±i}→or(Oct), rather than silently choose one orientation. This **adds a concrete combinatorial reading** to the base change S′=μ₄* and the proposed model over Z[1/2][i] in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans568–570, and **restates** Hua §XI's orientation discussion, their pp.84–85/our scans85–86. **Own suggestion, medium confidence:** the displayed two-element torsor makes the descent datum plausible, but equivariance over arbitrary bases and behavior at small primes have not been proved here; it does not determine **EXIT_TOWER E.24**'s arithmetic image.

## O08 — The usual 6+12+8 ramification picture is not uniform in characteristic3

**Location:** 67/86, NB on the larger ramification set of Γ. **Kind:** own idea; checked qualification. **Quote:** “‘milieux d’arêtes’ et ‘centres des faces’”.

The complex octahedron's three distinct exceptional orbits cannot be imported unchanged into the stated characteristic≠2 generality: in characteristic3, the same normalizer S₄ has geometric fixed-point orbits of sizes6 and4, with stabilizers4 and6, and order-three elements are unipotent. This **qualifies** the manuscript's geometric shorthand and **contradicts the unqualified distinct counts** “twelve edge midpoints and eight face centres” in Hua §XI, their p.85/our scan86; `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans569–571, links the octahedral cover with modular levels, but does not supply this bad-characteristic analysis. **Own check, high confidence:** enumeration over F₉ finds all geometric fixed points and verifies the two orbit sizes, while `proof_checks.md` §5 explains why no missing extension-field points are being excluded.

## O09 — The six-vertex condition reconstructs the even-flip Klein group

**Location:** 67/87, opening NB. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “équivaut à celle de S, avec une involution sans pts fixes”.

Given three antipodal pairs, the three permutations that fix one pair and swap the other two pairs form, with the identity, a canonical Klein four subgroup; conversely the displayed fixed-point condition recovers exactly those pairs. This **supports** the finite combinatorial assertion in Hua §XI, their p.86/our scan87, and **adds the explicit inverse construction** to the partition-based affine plane of `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans551–554. It also identifies precisely what must be retained in O04: an unpaired six-element subset of a projective line does not by itself supply this antipodism in characteristic5.

## O10 — An orbit torsor is not the canonical face-pair torsor

**Location:** 67/87–89, I versus I(V), and the parenthetical choice of a point. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “et non X≃X(I)”.

The live correction distinguishes the arbitrary free orbit I from the four antipodal face-pairs I(V), and the difference torsor records the absence of a preferred identification. This **restates and sharpens** Hua §XI, their pp.86–88/our scans87–89, which warns against confusing these torsors, and **differs in purpose from** `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan554: that earlier page makes I itself an affine F₂-plane without constructing the second face-pair torsor. Choosing i₀∈I only trivializes one torsor; the last paragraph of scan89 still requires choosing a point over ζ, so no canonical global trivialization is established.

## O11 — The twisting identity is explicitly left to be written out

**Location:** 67/88, upper display and margin. **Kind:** self-flagged: unverified step. **Quote:** “à expliciter”.

The equation X(T∧T′)=X(T)∧ᵥT′ follows formally from associativity of contracted products **if** X(T) has first been defined functorially by twisting a fixed V-line, but that does not by itself finish the almost-cancelled canonical construction of scan87. Hua §XI, their pp.86–87/our scans87–88, **restates** the identity and explicitly replaces the uncertain construction by a chosen reference line, whereas `LM_140-3_text.tex`, scan204, offers a separate example of a group twisted by a torsor with its left/right actions relevant. Thus the context supports the twisting method while leaving a real distinction between an arbitrary reference model and the manuscript's asserted canonical one; only the conditional contracted-product identity is checked here (`proof_checks.md` §6).

## O12 — The quotient has a concrete degree-four coordinate

**Location:** 67/88, canonical quotient and punctured base. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “X/V≃X(I)/V”.

In the standard model, q(z)=z²+z⁻² is V-invariant of degree4, and its three branch fibres are {0,∞}, {±1}, {±i}, over ∞,2,−2; removing them gives the free V-torsor used in scans88–89. This **restates** the coordinate calculation already proposed by `MPL_142_094-122_notes.md`, **B01**, cote142, scan108, and **makes explicit** Hua §XI's quotient description, their p.87/our scan88; the degree and branching have been checked directly here, without treating that earlier note as an established result. The coordinate is a useful comparison with `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans363 and568, but the equality of all arithmetic level-four models remains outside this small calculation.

## O13 — The groupoid formulation retains automorphisms that a coarse line loses

**Location:** 67/89, theorem. **Kind:** unproved claim / unpursued implication. **Quote:** “Ce groupoïde est équivalent”.

Allowing bijections of the four-element label set leaves generic automorphism group V on both sides, since the S₄-action on the cross-ratio line factors through S₃; fixing all labels instead gives the usual rigid labelled moduli problem. This **supports** the automorphism warning supplied by Hua §XI, their p.88/our scan89, and **restates the distinction** already explicit in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scans549–552, between M₀,₄^! and the symmetric-group action, with scan548 warning that coarse quotients need not be equivalences. The generic isotropy check is made here, but the full functorial equivalence over schemes is not proved by checking generic automorphism groups and should not be conflated with the register's **EXIT_TOWER E.23** completion claim.

## O14 — Context suggests reading questions, not transcript replacements

**Location:** 67/86, 87–89. **Kind:** reading question. **Quote:** “droite projective [réelle?]” and X(J), then Z(J).

The frozen text retains an uncertain “réelle” on scan86 and the progression X(J), overwritten Z|X(J), then Z(J) on scans88–89; Hua §XI, their pp.85–87/our scans86–88, omits the adjective on mathematical grounds and uses Σ(J), explaining that its own transcription reads a barred X as Sigma. This **differs from** the image-based readings here, so the precise questions are whether scan86's adjective is really “réelle” and whether the quotient glyph on scan88 is X, Z or Σ, with the context suggesting alternatives but not deciding the ink. Hua also flags the scan87 reference-octahedron construction as almost entirely cancelled; this **supports retaining its cancellations and uncertainty**, rather than filling them from the later theorem.

## O15 — The octahedral cover is an explicit link to the Longue Marche

**Location:** 67/84–89 as a block. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “parties octaédriques d’une droite projective”.

The three fixed-point pairs, their degree-four quotient, and their S₄ normalizer **add geometric detail to** the “revêtement octaédral” of the Fermat conic in `LM_140-2_text.tex`, scan363, and the proposed level-four model in scans568–570. This is also the bridge proposed, as a prior-run lead, in `LM_140-2_notes.md`, **C02 at scan363**, and `MPL_142_094-122_notes.md`, **B01 at scan108**; Hua §XI, their pp.83–88/our scans84–89, develops the projective-line side but does not by that alone identify the modular family. The new contribution here is the finite configuration and its torsor bookkeeping, together with the characteristic3/5 qualifications O03 and O08, rather than a new proof of the level-four modular comparison.
