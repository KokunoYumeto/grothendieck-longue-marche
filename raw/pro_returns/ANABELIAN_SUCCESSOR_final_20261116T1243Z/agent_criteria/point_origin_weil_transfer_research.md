# Point-origin constraints after the configuration obstruction

Status: precise research-route assessment. No new collision exclusion, universal relative witness, or exponent bound is asserted.

## 1. What survives a cuspidally pro-r truncation

Fix a prime r different from the characteristic p. Put F=pi_1(C−P)^(p′), let K be the kernel toward the proper group, and let N=ker(K→K^(r)). This holds for EVERY open subgroup I containing N, not merely one with r-group Galois closure: N is killed by every finite r-quotient I/J. Indeed, let I_0 be the F-core of I and M the F-core of J intersect I_0. Then I_0/M is an r-group. Since N is F-normal and contained in I, it is contained in I_0; hence K's image in F/I_0 is an r-group. Its full image in F/M is an extension of r-groups and kills N. Thus N is contained in J. Consequently I and I/N have the same full pro-r completion. Killing the puncture inertia gives the same full pro-r completion as the proper compactification D.

The geometric-routes agent owns the full statement and proof of this positive complement to its cross-prime dimension obstruction. If the cover and the relevant comparison carry their arithmetic Frobenius actions, this retained pro-r completion determines H^1(D_bar,Q_r), and hence the entire Weil polynomial. Thus the loss of H^1 at primes different from r does not itself prevent Weil-polynomial transfer.

## 2. What that Weil polynomial does not supply

A comparison of cuspidal groups under a hypothetical collision relates a curve ramified over P to another curve ramified over Q. It does not produce two rational lifts with p-primary difference on either one of those curves. R1 requires an etale cover of the original proper curve; these ramified covers do not satisfy that premise. Thus an odd relative class number for one of them cannot simply be fed into L1 or the small-annihilator criterion.

The Weil polynomial gives the order of the rational p-primary Jacobian group, not its integral group structure or exponent. Equality of Weil polynomials gives an isogeny statement, and does not by itself identify integral p-divisible lattices. Deck endomorphisms can sometimes give additional annihilators, as in the proved cyclotomic criteria, but the ramification problem in the preceding paragraph remains.

Finally, unlabeled collections of Weil polynomials cannot locate a point. Any automorphism of a curve carrying P to Q transports all covers branched at P to covers branched at Q with the same Weil polynomials. An argument must retain the comparison over the identity of the proper arithmetic group and its labeled Green splittings; an equality of spectral sets discards the crucial information.

## 3. The global H^1 ambiguity is small, but the common-open obstruction is not

For a smooth proper geometrically connected curve C over a finite field k, the Kummer sequence gives, for every n,

    0 → k×/(k×)^(r^n) → H^1(C,mu_(r^n)) → Pic(C)[r^n] → 0.

Under the transition maps defining Z_r(1), the last terms have inverse limit zero: Pic^0(C)(k) is finite, and multiplication by r eventually kills its r-primary part. The left terms are a finite Mittag–Leffler system. Hence H^1(C,Z_r(1)) is just the r-primary component of k×. The corresponding assertion for the arithmetic fundamental group follows from the classification of finite etale Kummer torsors. Thus automorphisms of a fixed cuspidally central extension over the identity proper group do not have arbitrary nonconstant global Kummer classes available.

This observation is insufficient to identify the two actual Green splittings. If P−Q is killed by p^e, choose f with div(f)=p^e(P−Q), and put U=C−{P,Q}. On U, the function f is a unit and its r-adic Kummer class exists. Since p^e is invertible in Z_r,

    (1/p^e) kappa_f ∈ H^1(U,Z_r(1))

has residue +1 at P and −1 at Q. In particular it does not extend to H^1(C,Z_r(1)). Its restrictions at rational points R of U record the generally nonconstant values of f(R), modulo r-power Kummer equivalence. This common-open class can compare splittings coming from two different punctured curves even though the automorphisms of either fixed proper-base extension have only the constant ambiguity above.

Consequently, a claim that the exceptional-coordinate Green trivializations are identified needs an additional premise. It cannot be deduced merely from equality of the proper point sections, the existence of an isomorphism of the two central extensions, and the computation of global H^1. The geometric-routes agent is checking the exact Green-compatibility scope in the primary cuspidalization sources. No preservation at exceptional coordinates is assumed here.

## 5. Focused source audit at the 11:39 review

The Green route was investigated further rather than dismissed just because
the published global corollary assumes separatedness. Two conditional steps
are sound. First, a Frobenius-compatible isomorphism of the cuspidally pro-r
quotients preserving cusp data and all visible cover zeta functions preserves
noncuspidal rational sections: for every open neighbourhood of a source
section the corresponding target cover has a noncuspidal rational point;
compactness of the finite point sets supplies a target point with the same
section. Second, the constant-comparison test in the geometric agent's
`cuspidal_green_research.md` forces a function to be constant. Neither step
supplies the required normalized comparison at a colliding cusp.

The exact source limitation was independently checked in Saidi–Tamagawa,
arXiv:0801.1452v2. Corollary 2.3 constructs alpha^(c-ab)_(S,T) only for
S,T outside the exceptional sets. Theorem 2.7 begins with arbitrary rational
points but its compatibility clause invokes precisely that Corollary 2.3
object; its proof consists of the reference to Mochizuki's Theorem 3.10.
It therefore cannot, on its own, be used to assert the exceptional-coordinate
normalization needed to identify the actual Green sections in one fixed
diagonal pullback.

A separate source conflict must also be resolved before relying on the
strongest reading of that reference. Mochizuki's June 2007 PDF, printed
pp. 14–15, Proposition 1.8(ii), identifies the once-punctured fibre group with
the inverse image of its point decomposition group in the configuration
group. The LaTeX-format version gives this as Proposition 1.6(ii), printed
pp. 14–15. Its definition of the configuration quotient, printed p. 11 in
both formats, appears to be maximal pro-Sigma followed by cuspidally
pro-Sigma-dagger. For Sigma equal to all primes other than p this appears to
assert the full configuration injectivity obstructed in AG.39. This is an
unresolved comparison of statements and conventions, not a declaration here
that the source contains an error. The February 2014 author comments were
opened in full: they repair the quasi-point recognition argument and three
other issues, but do not discuss this configuration assertion.
