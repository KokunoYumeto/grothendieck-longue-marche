# Hyperelliptic fixed paths: exact obstruction to the shortcut

This is a research note for A1, not a proof or refutation of A1 and not an assertion that the finite module examples occur as collisions.

Let C/k be hyperelliptic in characteristic2, let P,Q be distinct k-rational Weierstrass points, and let Π=π1(C_kbar,P)^(2′). The hyperelliptic involution ι gives an automorphism τ of Π of order2, through the point P. The point-section Frobenius F commutes with τ. The geometric prime-to-two path set from P to Q is a Π-torsor with commuting actions of F and ι.

Schur–Zassenhaus, first in finite odd quotients and then by compactness, shows that this path torsor has an ι-fixed point: the two order-two complements arising from P and Q are geometrically conjugate in Π semidirect C2. The set of such paths is a torsor under Z=Π^τ, not a singleton. Choosing one ι-fixed path gives a Frobenius cocycle in Z; changing the path changes it by a coboundary. The needed arithmetic conclusion is that this particular torsor has no Frobenius-fixed point when P≠Q. Complement conjugacy alone says nothing about that conclusion.

The fixed group Z is already visibly nontrivial in odd nilpotent quotients. For an odd prime ell, use the class-two exponent-ell quotient of the geometric surface group. Its abelian quotient is A=(F_ell)^(2g), and its central commutator quotient is Λ²A modulo the symplectic surface relation. For g≥2 this central quotient is nonzero. Hyperelliptic inversion acts as −1 on A and hence as +1 on this central quotient. Coprime fixed-point lifting is surjective for invariant finite odd quotients: the obstruction to adjusting a lift into a fixed lift is a nonabelian H¹(C2,N), which vanishes by conjugacy of complements in N semidirect C2. Compactness therefore lifts the nonzero fixed central classes to Π^τ. It is incorrect to infer uniqueness of an ι-fixed path from the fact that τ has no fixed vectors on the abelianization of Π.

The relevant universal odd nilpotent quotient and its single symplectic relation are described in Raynaud, *Revêtements des courbes en caractéristique p>0 et ordinarité*, Compositio123(2000), §2, pp78–80, especially the construction of N and the quotient by ε before Corollary9. At the finite central quotient, arithmetic Frobenius acts through the exterior-square action on A, with the symplectic relation removed. This supplies arithmetic structure but does not calculate the cocycle of a specified pair P,Q. No vanishing or nonvanishing theorem for that cocycle has been established here. In particular this note neither constructs a collision nor excludes one.

# Raynaud's hereditary-cover theorem and the remaining arithmetic gap

The same primary paper was read through Theorem2 (p76), Theorem17 (p85), and the construction/proof on pp83–86. Theorem2 supplies a solvable prime-to-p geometric Galois cover and a finite representation having no theta divisor. In the genus-two, characteristic-two case, the stated general construction first takes a cyclic degree5 cover of genus6 and then a Heisenberg construction with n=3; see the last case in the proof of Theorem2, p86. The no-theta assertion means every rank-one twist has a nonzero kernel of coherent Frobenius. It is substantially stronger than nonordinariness at one isolated character.

Nevertheless this conclusion does not yield a relative rational class-number unit. The relevant multiplicity spaces have dimension greater than1. A nonzero Frobenius kernel does not make their entire geometric stable part vanish, and does not prevent arithmetic eigenvalue1 on the remaining stable part. The distinction is the same one that separates a nonordinary Prym from a primitive-rank-zero Prym, now with an additional arithmetic eigenvalue condition. No argument has been found that turns this representation with no theta divisor into a successful relative cyclic-prime test over a normal intermediate base as required by AC.40/AR.18.

The prospective routes considered were repeated tensoring, passage to successive intermediate covers, and a small integral group-ring annihilator. Tensoring does not preserve the required nonordinariness: Proposition1(6), p75, explicitly constructs a further finite representation whose tensor product is ordinary. Passing to a cover can increase the dimension of the remaining stable part and has no proved monotone descent to zero. For an integral annihilator, a missing kernel vector or one missing character factor does not supply a small integer group-ring element annihilating the full rational p-primary point-difference. These are precise unresolved steps, not consequences attributed to Raynaud.
