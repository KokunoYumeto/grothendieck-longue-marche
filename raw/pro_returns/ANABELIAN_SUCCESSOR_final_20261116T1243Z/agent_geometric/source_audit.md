### Why several possible universal arguments still stop

1. Generic ordinariness: Ozman–Pries gives a nonempty open on which every cyclic cover at each one fixed prime is new-ordinary. Finite intersection yields the finite-prime obstruction AC.13, verified independently. It does not produce a finite-field curve lying in the intersection for all infinitely many primes. Fbar_p is countable, so a countable union of proper closed subsets can exhaust its points. We do not use an uncountability argument to claim a finite-field counterexample.
2. CM or superspecial base: this constrains the invariant Jacobian factor only. It provides no control of all nontrivial character factors of every cover. The explicit y²+y=x⁷ example has base two-rank0 but its natural odd cyclic cover has positive primitive rank. Thus zero p-rank of the base does not solve the nontrivial-cover version of A6 automatically; the identity cover does solve its literal degree-one-permitted version.
3. Raynaud's Heisenberg representation: reopened Theorem17 and Corollary18, with the proof of Theorem2. The global section obtained from E tensor B1 is nonzero; this proves a nonzero Frobenius kernel. It does not give kernel dimension equal to the whole H¹ space, or zero stable rank. A group-ring projector acts on a representation factor, so positive stable multiplicity in that representation cannot be eliminated merely by selecting a matrix row.
4. Dynamics on theta: a torsion point of theta kills one Frobenius determinant. To kill every primitive orbit requires orbit coverage; in higher genus even that does not force nilpotence. A Frobenius-stable cyclic subgroup with arithmetic Frobenius and p generating the unit group would help with orbit coverage in genus2, but the available torsion-density theorem does not guarantee a theta point in such a subgroup. ### Limits of the simultaneous counting route

The useful gain is to take H=ell J(k), simultaneously treating all cyclic quotients. A single character has a substantially larger kernel and generally gives a weak pair bound. The primitive-root hypothesis is essential in the present argument: failure of one cyclic quotient otherwise need only force one multiplication-p orbit of Fourier values, and does not make every nontrivial Fourier coefficient equal−1. Even for scalar supersingular Frobenius, minimal field sizes can grow exponentially with the prime while the primitive-root condition bounds its multiplicative order linearly; the displayed inequality therefore does not supply an unbounded-prime argument. In higher genus the nontrivial Artin L-polynomials have degree2g−2, and L(1) modulo p is not determined by the single point-character sum; in addition theta-difference intersections become positive-dimensional. Neither obstruction has been removed here.
