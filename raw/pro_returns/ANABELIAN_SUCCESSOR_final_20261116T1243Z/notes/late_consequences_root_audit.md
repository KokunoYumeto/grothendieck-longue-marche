## AC.45: section origin versus point origin

The root read the full proposed proof. Let Omega be the full geometric
fundamental group, Delta its prime-to-p quotient, N the kernel and
V the maximal abelian pro-p quotient of N. The norm maps, rather than
degree-multiplied transfer maps, identify V with the inverse limit of
the p-adic Tate modules of the pointed prime-to-p covers. The elementary
extension of a character from N to NO proves the necessary continuity
statement and makes its direction explicit.

Milne's corrected author text, Jacobian Varieties, was opened directly
at https://www.jmilne.org/math/xnotes/JVs.pdf. Proposition9.1 identifies
the abelianized curve group with the Jacobian group over a separably
closed field. Lemmas9.4–9.5 and the following Witt-vector paragraph
cover the characteristic prime; Corollary9.6 states the cohomological
comparison for all primes. Root retrievals turn626view0 and
turn627view0–2 include the actual hypotheses and proofs at PDF pages30–32.

At each stable level the cokernel of F−1 on the Tate module is rational
p-primary Jacobian torsion. Compactness gives compatible preimages in
the inverse limit; unique preimages under F−1 prove exactness on the
left. Thus M_P^F is V/(F−1)V, not the zero group V^F.

For any lift n in N of a representative of a coinvariant, the element
nF defines a continuous section by the profinite power homomorphism.
Its prime-to-p projection is exactly the P section. The specified
Kummer sign matches its cocycle. If its coinvariant is nonzero, A1 and
Saidi–Tamagawa Lemma1.7 show it cannot be geometrically conjugate to a
point section: a possible conjugator must already lie in N, where it
would make the coinvariant zero. This does not produce the deck-orbit
freeness required in A10(c).

Moreover, norm followed by pullback is multiplication by the
prime-to-p covering degree. The norm on each rational p-primary group
is therefore surjective. The fixed norm module consequently surjects
onto the base Jacobian's rational p-primary group. This gives actual
nonzero section-origin classes whenever that base group is nonzero,
without asserting the missing freeness.

## FF.50: integral norms and a genus-two slice

The root read the full proposed proof and independently checked the
Tsen dimension count for norm surjectivity, including the argument
that a nonzero norm-form solution has nonzero homogenizing coordinate.
Under integral Poincare duality the cohomological trace corresponds
to pushforward on homology, in the direction used in the proof.
This removes the earlier need to lift a principal divisor while
controlling a Prym.

For y^2=(u^2−1)(u^2−2)(u^2−3), T=u^2, the three V4 quotients
jointly separate completed geometric points: for every cyclic inertia
D in V4, the intersection of the three groups H_i D equals D.
The two elliptic norm-pullback operators sum to[2] on the Jacobian.
Their multiplication-cover pullbacks are etale over the smooth curve,
so their points really are tuples and no normalized-branch ambiguity
is hidden. The induced homology images are divisible by arbitrarily
large prime powers, losing only one power at2. Trace of the unramified
Kummer errors then vanishes integrally. The degree-four norm to the
rational base kills the residual constant character in a torsion-free
character group, also at2.

The claim is Kummer rigidity on the specified slice, not full
reconstruction over B_p. For a curve with a higher-dimensional
absolutely simple Jacobian factor, further elliptic quotients cannot
supply the required homology injection, even after passing to a finite
cover: pullback and norm retain the original Jacobian as an isogeny
factor. Actual completed-point compatibility on general Abel covers
remains the missing input.
