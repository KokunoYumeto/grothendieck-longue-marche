## Corrections, enlargements and distinctions

- No material mathematical error was found in the stated AP reconstruction core. The new proper-normal-extension result is an enlargement, not a refutation of AP-F.49.
- A draft mistake, caught before integration, identified the whole geometric fundamental group of G_m in characteristic p with its tame group. It was corrected to the maximal prime-to-p quotient, which is exactly what the unit-exponent argument needs; wild covers are not discarded from the theorem.
- Absolute Frobenius and the identity can be isomorphic as absolute topos maps while their specified relative structural 2-cells differ. Consequently Frobenius does not refute relative faithfulness; a nonintegral profinite twist does refute fullness.
- Finite normal extensions of a Hilbertian base do not need to be invoked through an inaccessible original Weissauer source: Jarden–Razon Corollary 2.3 provides the precise form used here. Their Theorem 2.2 also prevents the cyclotomic compositum from becoming the full algebraic closure.
- Candidate F3(b) is false. Qbar(t) reconstructs, but its constant four-punctured lines have the same relative fundamental groups while cross ratios distinguish them.
- Q^tr now belongs to the positive class. Qbar, real closed fields, Laurent fields, AP-F.42 and the Abhyankar henselian fields remain negative. Fields with divisible multiplicative group occur on both lists, so multiplicative divisibility does not decide the question.
- A map over a finitely generated model is not automatically a scalar descent. The explicit coherent model condition is stated; no small-etale-topos transcendental fibre-product formula is assumed.
- No canonical “smallest field on neither list” exists without an ordering convention. FF.18 gives an explicit low-transcendence-degree test K_* and makes no false minimality assertion. FF.17 proves that transcendence degree two is minimal for positive valuation transcendence defect, a different and well-defined numerical statement.

## Searches and attempted routes beyond the settled cases

Before any priority language, searches were made for reconstruction from etale topoi, Hilbertian normal algebraic extensions, composita of Galois extensions, small extensions, field centralizers, rigid henselian fields, and automorphisms of henselian fields. No priority is claimed. The verified Hilbertian enlargement follows from the mathematical arguments in FF.9–FF.11 and opened primary sources, not absence of a search hit.

For centralizer sufficiency: attempts via field rigidity, PAC/free absolute Galois groups, immediate henselian extensions and pro-system descent have not yielded a theorem. A rigid base field would not by itself prove the required condition for every finite extension. Conversely a trivial centralizer controls automorphisms, whereas fullness includes arbitrary noninvertible maps. One must produce an actual topos map for a counterexample, not merely a failure of the present proof.

For equicharacteristic-zero transcendence defect: the explicit lacunary formal branch was chosen so that its transcendence has an elementary proof. Algebraically closed residue gives a decisive procyclic-centre obstruction. Replacing the residue field by Q removes that obstruction, and the old t+t^2 substitution fixing u demonstrably fails to preserve valuation. The next concrete step is to determine the automorphisms of the henselized formal branch or find a different pinned map; neither is settled by the finite coefficient check.

For ordinary pro-morphisms: model transitions are not pinned, so the naive pro-category of pinned topoi does not contain the diagrams. Coherent same-base level maps reconstruct uniquely and glue. It remains a separate statement to identify them with any broader proposed pro-Hom notion. The original question is not marked completed by silently selecting the stronger condition.
