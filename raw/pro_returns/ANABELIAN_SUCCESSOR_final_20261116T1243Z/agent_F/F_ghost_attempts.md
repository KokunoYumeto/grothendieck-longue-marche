# Concrete metabelian ghost attempts and the remaining existence problem

Updated 16 November 2026, 12:25 UTC. This is an attempt record. FF.40–FF.50 in the main LaTeX source contain the independently reviewed theorems. No pinned ghost over B_p has been constructed, and R(B_p) remains open here.

## 1. The analytic Tate parameter

For the global elliptic curve A:y²+xy=x³−p, FF.41 proves that the visible quotient of its H=I''-equivariant p-adic functional space has no inertia-invariant lift at any finite local stage. A natural attempted point function assigns a multiplicative Tate uniformization parameter to an algebraic point.

This has not supplied a global B_p-valued function. The uniformization produces a parameter in a finite p-adic completion, and the period q is local. More precisely, q is transcendental over Q: its j-value is algebraic, and the p-adic Mahler–Manin theorem says that an algebraic nonzero q with |q|<1 cannot have algebraic J(q). The directly opened author-primary account is François Gramain, *Transcendance et fonctions modulaires*, Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux 11 (1999), 73–90, printed page 74, named unnumbered “Théorème stéphanois” and the immediately following paragraph asserting its p-adic analogue. The abstract also states the two-field version explicitly. Source: https://numdam.org/item/JTNB_1999__11_1_73_0.pdf. Gramain is one of the four original theorem authors. The original Inventiones paper's publisher record was located by geometric_routes, but its full proof was subscription-only; it is not marked directly opened.

The period's transcendence does not settle the attempted point function. On H the period Kummer class already vanishes, and the local version of FF.42 has the much larger local fixed field as constants. A different globally algebraic value could potentially have the same restricted Kummer class as a local parameter. No theorem excluding that possibility for all actual points has been proved here.

Even a successful point-value assignment would require compatible full étale-topos maps after deleting arbitrary target points. A Tate-module homomorphism and an analytic fundamental-domain choice do not supply those maps. FF.43 makes this requirement useful for uniqueness but does not establish existence of the cohomology comparisons.

## 2. A residue-field-preserving trace bijection that fails the punctured-target test

There is an explicit Galois-equivariant candidate far better behaved topologically than a trace projection. Put k=B_p and define

\[
\tau(a)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}_{E/k}(a)}{[E:k]}
\quad(a\in E,\ E/k\text{ finite}),
\qquad h(a)=\frac{a+\tau(a)}2.
\]

The normalized trace is independent of the finite field E chosen, by trace transitivity. It is k-linear, is the identity on k, and is G_k-invariant. Consequently h is a G_k-equivariant bijection of Qbar, with inverse a↦2a−τ(a). It preserves each field k(a): both h(a) and a differ by a nonzero rational scalar and an element of k. It fixes every k-rational point and induces a permutation of the closed points of A¹_k, hence a Zariski homeomorphism when the generic point is fixed. The finite étale category of A¹ in characteristic zero only sees the constant covers, so that category alone does not rule it out.

Nevertheless h cannot be the actual geometric point function of a pinned self-map of the full étale topos of A¹_k.

Suppose such a map exists. Since h is bijective and fixes 0 and 1, its inverse image of U=A¹\{0,1} is U. The pullback of κ(T) on U has the form

\[
\eta+\alpha\kappa(T)+\beta\kappa(1-T),
\quad \eta\in H^1(G_k,\mathbf Q_p(1)),\quad
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf Q_p.
\]

Indeed the two displayed unit classes form a basis of the geometric H¹ and give global lifts; a rational point splits off the base summand. Evaluating at 2 gives η=0, because h(2)=2 and all rational nonzero constants lie in the Kummer kernel W.

There exists x∈W\K_p^ab. To see this, I' is a nontrivial closed subgroup of free wild inertia and hence a nontrivial free pro-p group; it has a nontrivial F_p quotient. Thus K_p^ab has a nontrivial cyclic p-extension in B_p, generated by a radical x. FF.42 gives κ(x)=0 and κ(1−x)≠0. Since h fixes every k-point, evaluating the pullback at x forces β=0. Evaluating at 1−x then forces α=1. The identical calculation for κ(1−T) shows that h pulls back both unit classes identically.

For any a outside k, both a and h(a) avoid 0 and 1. Evaluate the two cohomology identities over the finite field k(a)=k(h(a)). FF.42's pair detector forces h(a)=a. But every nontrivial finite extension E/k contains a nonzero element a of trace zero, and for such an a one has h(a)=a/2≠a. This contradiction rules out the candidate.

This is an actual failed ghost attempt, not merely a failure to find a proof. It isolates exactly where the full topology is stronger than its unpunctured finite étale category: the two Kummer classes after deleting 0 and 1 detect the defect. The argument does not assert that all bijections of closed points behave this way, nor that all pinned endomorphisms of A¹ are algebraic.

## 3. The current existence problem

The positive arguments through FF.39 manufacture an arithmetic invariant functional, a rational unit with the correct Kummer class, and finally the actual point function. FF.41 supplies a specific reason the first manufacture step cannot be reused over B_p. FF.42 and FF.43 recover exact point-value uniqueness from coherent translated data, but do not manufacture a rational unit or the comparison data.

A concrete next statement sufficient to advance the Tate case would be: for an actual pinned map from an open of A to A¹, its compatible pullbacks of the unit pairs on every translated two-puncture open force the nonsplit visible Tate component to vanish, or force an algebraic function realizing those pairs. No such statement has been proved. The ordinary Carlson–Stix openness argument cannot simply be imported: its semiabelian point-group homomorphism uses Kummer-faithfulness, which fails here.

Thus the remaining obstruction is existence and descent for coherent target-open data. The work does not substitute point-value uniqueness, nonexistence of the trace candidate, or absence of a literature search hit for that theorem.


## 4. The residual pointwise automorphism problem after FF.44–FF.47

The automorphism problem has advanced beyond the previous existence record. FF.44 proves that any pinned automorphism of the affine line over every finite E/B_p has an affine actual point function. After composing with that unique affine scheme automorphism, its point function is the identity.

FF.45 proves that its remaining generic outer action cannot be geometrically inner unless the entire topos automorphism is the identity. The proof uses all qcs separated étale objects, not only finite covers: each is an open in the maximal étale locus of a finite normalization, and the local comparison on inertia-fixed sheets is forced by pro-Yoneda and the inertia normalizer. FF.46 supplies the converse site construction when geometric inertia classes are transported.

The cyclic rational covers U^m=T−a then force every inertia exponent to be one. This controls all primes, even though finite-point Kummer classes at primes different from p alone do not do so. Every rational Kummer class is fixed after every finite constant extension. Descent from a split divisor is explicit: the error is an inflated constant class, killed at one unchanged rational point.

FF.47 goes beyond this abelian condition. The inverse-image functor is naturally the identity on all genus-zero qcs étale objects. It consequently excludes the explicit full-profinite partial-conjugation word: a connected degree-three S3 cover with four transposition branch permutations has genus zero and is moved by that word. The earlier pro-p class-two example and this full-profinite S3 example are deliberately distinguished. The program checks their finite data; neither is an arithmetic-compatible ghost.

The remaining exact problem is a relative birational automorphism over G_E which fixes geometric inertia classes, every abelian quotient after finite constant extension, and the genus-zero part of the site, but might act on positive-genus covers. No such noninner automorphism has been constructed, and its absence has not been proved. Genus-zero objects do not form a covering basis: a positive-genus curve cannot receive a dominant map from a genus-zero curve in characteristic zero. Nor is there a proof that Galois closures of genus-zero covers are cofinal. Thus neither ordinary étale descent nor an unproved cofinality statement closes the gap.

A tempting further use of a second rational function on a positive-genus cover also fails at a precise place. The generic automorphism is initially defined on G_{E(T)}; the absolute Galois group attached to a different rational subfield E(U) of that cover is generally not a subgroup of G_{E(T)}. Extending the automorphism to that different overgroup is a new compatibility problem. The étale correspondences controlling such an extension can themselves have positive genus, returning to the missing case rather than resolving it.

## 5. A proper-elliptic lifting attempt does not yet produce a ghost

The Tate curve in FF.41 has trivial H=I'' action on its p-adic Tate module. Its compatible point Kummer cocycles lie in the toric Tate line. Thus a linear automorphism of the Tate module that fixes this line can preserve every such point class while acting on the invisible quotient. This is genuine flexibility in the proper abelian fundamental-group data.

It does not automatically extend to the full étale topos. One must lift it compatibly through the fundamental groups obtained by deleting arbitrary algebraic points, retain all decomposition groups and every finite cover, and preserve the relative structural cells. Projectivity of the free pro-p group H eliminates certain H2 obstructions but does not eliminate the H1 obstruction to an equivariant lift in a prescribed automorphism fibre. No such lift is supplied here.

The comparison with FF.32 is decisive against treating this as a completed construction: an invisible p-adic functional also occurs over U_p, while FF.35 proves full reconstruction there. Thus preserving proper abelian point classes cannot, by itself, imply the existence of a pinned ghost. The exact extension through positive-genus punctured covers remains the active question.


## 6. The former cyclic-cover obstruction, now resolved by FF.48

The next test after FF.47 was an étale restriction of y^m=f(T) whose smooth completion has positive genus; already a quadratic cover with four branch points gives genus one. FF.46 fixes its Kummer class, so its inverse-image object is isomorphic to the original mu_m-torsor over the base. This fixes the torsor object, not the actual point function of the induced equivalence of slice topoi.

After choosing a torsor isomorphism, that actual point function can a priori rotate the fibre over x by an element c_x of mu_m. Deck equivariance and Galois equivariance do not by themselves make c_x independent of x. In the genus-zero case FF.44 makes the lifted point function affine and therefore makes the rotation constant; that step is unavailable for a positive-genus completion.

A graph argument has a precise obstruction. The lifted equivalence lies over the original base automorphism, not over the identity geometric morphism of the base topos. Its graph is therefore in a twisted fibre product. Identifying that product through the torsor-object isomorphism does not identify the unique relative closed-point comparisons with that chosen global torsor isomorphism. Nor does continuity on the underlying curve force an arbitrary fibrewise permutation to vary locally constantly: the generic scheme point is a single point even when the geometric generic fibre has several sheets. Thus equality of torsor classes, a twisted-diagonal construction, or ordinary point-space continuity does not prove the desired constancy.

FF.48 resolves the cyclic-cover point-function question by a different argument. Actual scheme realizations are stable under finite fibre products, open-and-closed components and finite group quotients. Over finite constants, every cyclic cover y^m=f(T) is a quotient of the product of genus-zero linear root covers u_i^m=T-a_i. Thus every cyclic and abelian cover, of arbitrary genus, has an algebraic actual slice point function, and c_x is constant. Lattès maps additionally put every Galois genus-one cover in the same generated subcategory. The equivalence on all further covers of those curves remains separate; no full generic-kernel conclusion follows from this result.

## 7. Remaining nonabelian covering data after FF.48

The generated subcategory is not cofinal: Guralnick2003 Theorem1.1, recording the proved Frohardt--Magaard theorem, bounds the nonalternating nonabelian simple composition factors of genus-zero monodromy to a finite set. Finite composita of Galois closures introduce only subdirect products and therefore no new simple factors. A geometric simple-group cover outside that set is omitted. The manuscript gives the exact argument and source; no explicit unverified group is selected and no nontrivial automorphism is inferred.

A further Abel cover of a hyperelliptic curve of genus g≥2 gives a concrete test where the elliptic construction stops. For odd n, the full [n]-pullback in its Jacobian has genus 1+n^(2g)(g−1); quotient by the lifted hyperelliptic involution has genus (g−1)(n^(2g)−1)/2, because precisely 2g+2 two-torsion points are fixed. Thus that quotient is not a rational source. This calculation alone neither excludes a different genus-zero construction nor controls the remaining topos action. The generic kernel and arbitrary noninvertible pinned maps remain unresolved.


The non-Galois cubic genus-one slice has now also been settled within FF48. Every S3 branch tuple of genus1 has an index-preserving S4 lift with genus-zero quartic source, so the cubic is a subcover of a realized genus-zero Galois closure. The exhaustive certificate and independent checker cover all582 possible product-one generating tuples, not merely simple branching. This extends the realized subcategory without addressing its proved noncofinality or the action on arbitrary higher-cover data. No further small-monodromy census is being substituted for that remaining structural question.


## 8. The elliptic unramified Kummer error is now eliminated

FF49 kills a genuine residual ambiguity on Galois genus-one slices. Even after all point inertia exponents are fixed, a Kummer character might have differed by unramified elliptic H1. The realized multiplication covers and their deck translations impose an integral averaging identity n b1(f)=n² bn(h), which rules out that difference. A norm then eliminates its constant-field part. Completed-point agreement at the omitted cusps is proved using separating rational quotients of a larger Lattes cover.

This controls all rational functions of an elliptic slice, not just its original rational base coordinate. It still gives invariance of cyclic covering objects, rather than the actual point function of each new cyclic cover. For a higher-genus curve, translations in an Abel-cover tower act nontrivially on the new Prym cohomology; the elliptic equality of every translate with the same integral class has no established substitute. No generic nonabelian-kernel vanishing or pinned ghost has been proved.

## 9. Norms remove one obstruction, but not the point compatibility requirement

FF50 supplies a different integral argument. Over the algebraically closed constant field every finite function-field norm is surjective, by the explicit Tsen dimension proof. If a coherently realized finite etale cover D→C has completed-point compatibility, the Kummer error of a norm preimage is unramified on D. Its trace is the error on C. Integral Poincare duality identifies that trace with homology pushforward. Thus covers whose homology images are arbitrarily prime-power divisible force every base Kummer error to vanish. The full Abel mod-n cover has homology image exactly nH1(C); no principal-divisor lift through it and no trivial Prym translation action are needed.

This gives a genuine genus-two result for y²=(u²−1)(u²−2)(u²−3), T=u². Its three quadratic V4 quotients are rational or elliptic and jointly separate every completed point. The two elliptic quotient maps induce a Jacobian isogeny with a dual composite[2]. Pulling back their multiplication covers produces finite etale, hence smooth, covers of C inside already coherently realized diagrams. Their tuple coordinates separate all completed points. The homology images become arbitrarily divisible, with a uniform loss of only one2-adic power. FF50 consequently fixes all Kummer classes on this higher-genus slice after every finite constant extension.

The decisive condition has not become automatic. A characteristic Abel cover can be preserved as an isomorphism class while its actual point map still permutes each finite torsor fiber. The étale-site action and arithmetic equivariance alone have not made those permutations constant or algebraic. Applying the norm argument without completed-point compatibility would leave residue errors on D; transfer can turn those errors into an unramified class on C, so the compact-homology divisibility would no longer apply. This is precisely why a class-level identification of the Abel cover is insufficient.

Elliptic quotient methods also have a proved structural limit. A curve whose Jacobian has an absolutely simple factor of dimension greater than one cannot acquire an entirely elliptic-factor Jacobian after taking a finite cover: pullback followed by norm is multiplication by the degree, so the original Jacobian remains an isogeny factor. More elliptic division constructions cannot detect that factor. A new source of coherent completed-point realizations, or a direct control of their residue permutation error, is still required.

The independently proved fixed-prime configuration lemma in Part A does not supply that source. It proves full induced topology for particular finite fiber quotients and thereby a relative cuspidal group embedding. It does not identify actual point maps on arbitrary Abel covers over B_p. Likewise the finite-field point-recognition proof relies on Frobenius and finite rational-point counts; the residue field of the henselian B_p situation is algebraically closed and provides no such arithmetic Frobenius inside G_(B_p). Neither transfer is asserted.

One additional tempting reduction is also incomplete. FF19 proves reconstruction for B_p(t), so an extension of the pointwise topos automorphism to a transcendental constant field could settle it. The small etale topos of a base change by a transcendental field is not merely the topos fiber product with its absolute Galois group: new moving punctures and new closed points occur. No canonical extension of an arbitrary pointwise automorphism to that larger site has been constructed. Treating algebraic-extension descent as if it covered this regular extension would be a false step.

As of this review, there is no concrete new mechanism in hand for the absolutely simple higher-dimensional factor case, the whole nonabelian pointwise kernel, or arbitrary noninvertible pinned maps. The genus-two result is substantive progress within a proved class, not evidence that one more round of the same elliptic constructions will establish R(B_p).
