## Conventions

`k` a finite field of characteristic `p`; `C/k` smooth, proper, geometrically connected of genus `g ≥ 2`; `E_C^(p′) → G_k` the geometrically prime-to-`p` fundamental group (the quotient of `π_1(C)` by the kernel of `π_1(C_k̄) → π_1(C_k̄)^(p′)`); `s_P` the section of `P ∈ C(k)`, up to conjugation by the geometric group. A *collision* is a pair `P ≠ Q` in `C(k)` with `s_P` and `s_Q` conjugate under the geometric group. **A1** is the statement that no curve as above has a collision. A cover is *`P`-pointed over `L`* when it is defined over the finite field `L ⊇ k` with a chosen `L`-rational point over `P`. For a character `χ : J_C(L) → Z/n`, `D_χ → C_L` is the pull-back, by the Abel map based at `P`, of the quotient of the Lang isogeny of `J_C` over `L` cut out by `χ`: a `Z/n`-torsor with rational deck group in which `P` splits completely (class field theory: these are the `P`-split unramified abelian covers over `L`); `L_P(χ, T)` is its twisted `L`-function. `h(D) = #J_D(L)` is the class number. `P_n(D) = im ∏_{ℓ∣n}(1 − σ^{n/ℓ}) ⊂ J_D` is the primitive part of a cyclic cover with generator `σ`. `f_p` is the `p`-rank.

*Reconstruction over a field `k₀`* means: for all finite-type `k₀`-schemes `X, Y`, the map `Hom_{k₀}(X^awn, Y) → π₀ Hom^cp_{BG_{k₀}}(X_ét, Y_ét)` is bijective, where `X^awn` is the absolute weak normalization and `Hom^cp` the morphisms of topoi over `BG_{k₀}` that send closed points to closed points. This is the "pinned" of Carlson–Haine–Wolf (their Definition 2.19). The reading "a morphism given with its commutation isomorphism", used in the first prompt, was wrong.

A *ghost* is an object that passes every test at one prime, at one level or in one quotient and fails only where they mix: here the class `Q′ − P′`, `p`-primary on every cover; an exponent in `Ẑ` that should be in `Z`; the substitution `t ↦ t + t²`.

## Leads for Part A: one cluster

- **L0** (the principle; found by the intake reader and by the manuscript reader independently; each derivation checked by its author only). *Let `P ≠ Q` collide on `C/k`. Let `L/k` be finite and `D → C_L` a connected étale Galois cover with group `G` of order prime to `p`, defined over `L`, with an `L`-point `P′` over `P`. Then: (a) there are an `L`-point `Q′` over `Q` and `g ∈ G` such that `T = [Q′ − gP′]` is a non-zero `p`-primary class in `J_D(L)` (R1); (b) if `#G` is odd, `δT ≠ T` for every `δ ≠ 1` in `G` (R2); (c) `h(T) = 0` for every homomorphism `h : J_D → B` of abelian varieties over `L` with `B(L)[p] = 0`.* Only the least field over which the cover and the lift of `P` are defined matters: larger fields only add rational torsion. The levers below are choices of `B`. (Item A2.)
- **L1** (lever 1, criterion (R): the relative class number; intake reader and manuscript reader). *If `#G` is odd, `P` splits completely in `D`, and `p ∤ h(D)/h(C_L)`, then `P` collides with no point of `C(k)`.* Proof: `T = T₀ + T₁` with `T₀` the `G`-invariant part and `T₁` in the new part, both rational; the hypothesis kills `T₁`; so `δT = T` for all `δ`, against L0 (b). For `G = Z/ℓ` and `D = D_χ`: `h(D)/h(C) = N_{Q(ζ_ℓ)/Q} L_P(χ, 1)`. This is what his margin of scan 35 points to: go down to a finite field, where only the rational `p`-torsion can carry `T`. The return used the geometric form only (R3: no `p`-torsion over any field) and set the rational form aside (R8). (Items A2, A3.)
- **L2** (lever 1 as a count; intake reader and manuscript reader). Modulo a prime over `p`, `L_P(χ, 1) ≡ det(1 − Φ ∣ H¹(C, L_χ))`, `Φ` the `L`-linear Frobenius. In genus two, `L_P(χ, 1) ≡ 1 + Σ_{x ∈ C(L)} χ([x − P])`. The group-ring form: for a quotient `A` of `J_C(L)` of odd order prime to `p` put `θ = [0] + Σ_{R ∈ C(L)} [R − P]` in `F_p[A]`; in genus two, L1 holds for the cover with group `A` exactly when `ψ(θ) ≠ 0` for every non-trivial character `ψ` of `A` with values in `F̄_p`. When `A = Z/ℓ` and `p` is a primitive root modulo `ℓ`, a collision forces, for the cosets `b` of `ℓJ_C(L)` and `N_b = #{x ∈ C(L) : [x − P] ∈ b}`: `N_b ≡ N_0 + 1 (mod p)` for every `b ≠ 0`. A random `χ` fails the test with probability about `p^{−f}` per prime above `p` (`f` the order of `p` modulo `ℓ`), so every `(C, P)` should have witnesses with small `ℓ`. (Items A2, A3, A4.)
- **L3** (towards a proof by counting; intake reader, with the manuscript reader's singleton lemma). With more cosets than points and `p` a primitive root modulo `ℓ`, every non-zero coset holds a multiple of `p` points of `C(L) − P`. The pairs `x ≠ y` with `x − y ∈ ℓJ_C(L)` number no more than `N²/ℓ^r + ((2g − 2)² − 1)#L` by the Weil bound and no fewer than `(p − 1)(N − N_0)`; for `p > (2g − 2)²` this is a contradiction once such an `ℓ` exists for one `L`. In genus two: if `χ : J_C(L) → Z/ℓ` is onto with kernel `H`, then `2(#H − 1)` points or fewer share a coset with another point (the count of AP-A.C2.21), so L1 holds for `χ` as soon as `#C(L) ≥ 2 #H` and `ℓ > #C(L)`. (Item A3.)
- **L4** (lever 1 for the primitive part; intake reader). If `C` is not a separable double cover of a curve of genus one, a collision forces `p ∣ #P_n(D_χ)(L)` for every `χ` of odd order `n` prime to `p`: the endgame of R3 uses only `A·T = 0`. (Item A2.)
- **L5** (lever 2: correspondences; Poincaré expert, with the rational form by the intake and manuscript readers). For a correspondence `C ← D → X₀` over `L` with `D → C` étale, `P`-pointed, prime to `p`, and `X₀` a curve of genus ≥ 1 with `J_{X₀}(L)[p] = 0`, a collision forces `φ(Q′) = φ(gP′)`, and, applied to all deck translates, `φ_*π^*(P) = φ_*π^*(Q)` as divisors. The target may be supersingular (the Hermitian curve `x^{p+1} + y^{p+1} = 1`) or an ordinary elliptic curve `B` over `L` with `p ∤ #B(L)`. `P ↦ φ_*π^*(P)` is a morphism `C → Sym^N X₀`; if it is not constant it fails to separate only on a proper closed set of pairs. - **L6** (the closure principle; manuscript reader, Poincaré expert). Collisions go up along prime-to-`p` étale covers and forward along morphisms of proper curves. So the class of curves with no collision over any finite field contains `C` whenever it contains a prime-to-`p` étale cover of `C`, and contains `D` whenever `D` has a point-separating family of morphisms to curves in the class. Seeds: R4, R6. L1 and R3 may be applied anywhere in the prime-to-`p` tower. (Item A5.)
- **L7** (lever 3: Frobenius at closed points; Longue Marche expert, with the caution of the intake and manuscript readers). Class field theory for the full étale `π_1` separates points at the abelian level (`C(k) → Pic¹` is injective): that is his statement of scan 35 for the pro-étale part, and it is not a route to A1, which is what remains when the `p`-part is removed. - **L8** (lever 4: the geometric form; the return, with the Longue Marche expert's weakening). `f_p(P_n) = 0` exactly when, for every orbit `O` of multiplication by `p` on the primitive characters, the composite of the `#O` Frobenius maps around `O` is nilpotent on the `(g − 1)`-dimensional space `H¹(C, L_χ)`. The expert's target: not rank zero, only "not ordinary in the eigenspace that carries `T`", from the torsion points on Raynaud's theta divisor (Raynaud, Tamagawa, Pop–Saïdi: from memory). (Item A6.)
- **L9** (Poincaré expert). A1 is "the exceptional set of Saïdi–Tamagawa is empty" (their paper of 2009 and the refined version). (Item A8.)
- **L10** (the author of the dictionary). The coverings of Conway, McKay and Trojan are explicit coverings whose deck group acts faithfully on an `F_p`-space; they are wild, so they do not enter R2 as stated, but they test a non-abelian form of the criterion. (Item A7.)
- **L11** (the author of the ghost-symmetries translation, item GS6; a hunch).
