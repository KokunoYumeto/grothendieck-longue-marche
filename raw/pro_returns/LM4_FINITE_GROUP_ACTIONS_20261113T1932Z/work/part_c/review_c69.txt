1. The original Mobius action and partial compactification.

Formula (C.69.5) proves the convergence statement for every sequence
of distinct elements of the discrete Mobius group. If the inverse
images of zero remained in a compact subdisk, compactness of the
rotation parameter would give a convergent subsequence of distinct
group elements, contradicting discreteness. After passing to the
stated subsequence the exact numerator identity gives uniform
convergence on compact subsets away from the single repelling point.
The two endpoints belong to the actual limit set by the original
orbit sequences for the transformations and their inverses.

D_Gamma is an actual second-countable surface with boundary Omega,
and radial contraction remains in this space. Freeness follows
because the nonidentity torsion-free discrete Mobius elements are
hyperbolic or parabolic and their fixed points are excluded.
Properness follows from the convergence formula and the positive
distance between a compact subset of D_Gamma and the closed limit
set. The quotient is thus the stated regular universal covering
of surfaces with boundary, with the original labelled deck group.
The closed-disk compactification is not substituted for this
partial compactification.

The density argument for controlled accesses is complete. A group
containing a hyperbolic element has two chosen hyperbolic endpoints;
at least one differs from any repelling limit point. Its images
under the converging sequence are attracting fixed points of actual
conjugate elements and approach the prescribed limit point. If there
is no hyperbolic element, two parabolics with different fixed points
give a hyperbolic product by the explicit unbounded trace formula.
Hence all parabolics have one common endpoint, and discreteness of
the resulting real translation subgroup makes the nontrivial group
cyclic. Its ordinary boundary set is dense. The trivial group is
handled separately.

2. The exact cap-side extension.

The access calculation controls both the given map and its inverse.
A terminal hyperbolic axis ray is the union of translates of one
compact segment. Commutation makes its image the translates of a
compact image segment, which converge uniformly to the same actual
attracting endpoint. At ordinary boundary points continuity already
gives that endpoint. These assertions supply genuine continuous
embedded closures for the inverse images of the chosen crosscuts.

For arbitrary zeta, dense controlled endpoints p,q can be selected
on either side in a sufficiently small circular arc. The tails may
be truncated inside disjoint endpoint neighborhoods, and an embedded
joining arc in a small convex cap yields the specified crosscut.
The Jordan cap lies in the convex hull of its boundary, proving its
diameter bound.

The third controlled point is essential and is used correctly.
For r strictly between p,q, the proof constructs z_n tending to r
with h^{-1}(z_n) also tending to r. Both sequences eventually lie
in the designated Jordan components, because r is distinct from
the two crosscut endpoints. Thus h carries the inverse crosscut's
component adjacent to the named arc to the small target cap.
This proves the exact side equation (C.69.10), rather than choosing
a side from the unordered endpoint set. A neighborhood of zeta in
the closed disk lies in the corresponding source component together
with its boundary arc. The cap estimate gives identity extension
at every limit point; the same proof for the inverse gives a
closed-disk homeomorphism. The actual commutation equations extend

Restriction is therefore a continuous bijective homomorphism between

3. Proper collars and the relative inclusion-kernel result.

The proper-collar construction is valid for every second-countable
surface with boundary, including countably many boundary components.
A locally finite triangulation gives an initial product collar.
A positive continuous minorant for the local allowed width gives
the stated delta(b), uniformly keeping the shortened collar within
distance one of b in a compatible proper metric. The preimage of a
compact target is a closed subset of the compact unit-neighborhood
of that target intersected with the boundary, times [0,1].
Thus the shortened closed collar is proper.

A connected based parameter-disk family preserves every boundary
component and its orientation. Components of the boundary
one-manifold are open in that boundary, so a path cannot move a
boundary point to a different component. On a circle, the joint
map from D^2 times R has the normalized real lift used in the
proof. Degree one gives integer-translation equivariance. On the
parameter boundary the original maps are identity, so the integer
lift discrepancy is constant on that connected boundary and zero
at its chosen basepoint. Lifting the inverse family with the same
normalization gives the actual inverse by uniqueness.

The convex interpolation on each real coordinate is increasing and
proper, and its inverse varies continuously. Compact parameter sets
provide common properness bounds using the original inverse family;
these bounds persist under the convex interpolation. The formula is
identity near the collar's outer edge and therefore pastes with the
identity outside. A compact source or target meets only finitely
many collar boundary components, by properness and compactness in
the boundary one-manifold. This supplies both compact-open and
inverse continuity even with infinitely many boundary components.

The exact correction E_z^{-1} B_z fixes the whole boundary of N
and agrees with the original loop on the entire parameter boundary.
It is based at the same identity point. This proves the claimed
pi_1 inclusion injection without assuming a restriction fibration

  https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0010223

Theorem 1.1 on its first page lists the noncompact exceptions.
