1. Access data and nonconstant landing on every circle arc.
A genuine crosscut has distinct actual endpoints. Its prime-end
closure has the corresponding distinct endpoints with singleton
principal sets. Choose an open neighborhood in the prime-end disk
whose closure meets its circle only in the prescribed interval.
A sufficiently small crosscut neighborhood from the actual basis
lies inside it. The closure of that side, including its two
crosscut endpoints, stays in the chosen closed neighborhood.
Both accessible endpoints therefore lie in the interval and have
distinct landing points. This proves the required density and
nonconstancy without assuming a continuous landing map on the arc.
Ambient homeomorphisms of the compact sphere preserve null chains
and their equivalence, including vanishing spherical diameters by
uniform continuity. The inverse gives the actual inverse action.
The orientation of the disk interior determines the same orientation
on its prime-end circle. A complement-fixed ambient map consequently
preserves accessible ends and their individual actual landing points.

2. Unlinked-access argument and pointwise prime-end rigidity.
The two radial tails at a,c, joined inside the disk, give a disk
crosscut. When their actual landing points are the same p, its image
closes to a Jordan curve: the quotient circle map is continuous
and injective, since its interior is embedded in O and p is not
an interior point. The image meets the frontier only at p.
The two disk sides map to opposite Jordan sides, as follows by
examining a local crosscut point inside O and then using connectedness.
The radial tails at b,d in the alternating order lie on those
opposite sides. A common landing point q would lie in both closures,
hence on that Jordan curve, forcing q=p. This proves the unlinked
assertion for the exact access fibres.
A moved accessible a for a positive circle homeomorphism has a
small interval of b with the cyclic order a,b,u(a),u(b). The proved
nonconstancy supplies b with a different landing point. The
same-landing equations for u contradict unlinkedness. Thus every
prime end is fixed, and the stated relative disk extension exists.
Interior conjugation and identity on the ambient complement give
the exact homomorphism and injectivity.

3. Topology and density.
K_O is a closed Polish subgroup of the positive sphere homeomorphism
group; the boundary-fixed disk group is Polish. The displayed graph
is closed because every interior point equation passes to a limit;
the disk boundary is already fixed and the interior is dense.
Projection of this closed Polish group onto K_O is a continuous
bijective homomorphism. The Polish open-mapping/orbit theorem applies
to its transitive action, so its inverse is continuous.
Composing with the other projection gives the claimed forward
continuity, with maps and inverses in their actual topologies.
No reverse continuity of iota is inferred.
Every compactly supported disk map extends after the actual
conjugation by identity outside O. The radial compression g^[r]
is a boundary-fixed homeomorphism, with the displayed inverse.
Uniform continuity, including the outer thin annulus on which it
is the identity, proves uniform convergence of maps and inverses
as r tends to one. Hence the image is dense.

4. Nontrivial impressions and both exact witnesses.
The lens formula for I(zeta) is the actual cluster set because
disk neighborhoods and prime-end crosscut neighborhoods are
cofinal. Its members are compact nested connected sets, and the
intersection is nonempty and lies in the spherical frontier.
If all impressions were singletons, compactness gives the limit
of every interior sequence approaching any zeta. Approximating
the assigned value at each member of a boundary sequence by an
interior value proves continuity on the entire closed disk.
Every frontier point occurs, using a compact subsequence of the
preimages of an interior approximation. Lemma 3.2 then gives
local connectedness. This verifies the contrapositive used.

For distinct a,b in a nontrivial impression, the chosen points
u_n,v_n can be taken in arbitrarily small open convex lenses,
with their image limits a,b. A compact convex neighborhood of
their segment lies inside each lens. Shrinking the next lens
below the positive distance from all earlier disks gives the
claimed disjoint, locally finite supports, accumulating only
at zeta. The ray map centered first at u_n and then at v_n
is a homeomorphism of the convex disk fixing its boundary;
its inverse is the same formula with the two points exchanged.
Both displacement bounds follow from the support diameter.
The disk maps therefore tend to the identity, while their
ambient conjugates move a sequence tending to a to one tending
to the distinct point b. This proves failure of inverse continuity.
The disjoint piecewise union is a single boundary-fixed disk
homeomorphism: local finiteness handles the interior, and shrinking
supports handle zeta and its inverse. An ambient relative extension
would be continuous and fix a, contradicting its required images
u_n -> v_n after conjugation. Thus this particular disk map is
outside the image, proving properness independently of the
sequence argument.
