\paragraph{Independent audit of the inverse-pair pants construction.}
Write $P_1=\widehat F(l,a)$, $P_2=\widehat F(l,b)$ and
$H=\widehat F(l,a,b)$.  The two embedded factors intersect in
$\overline{\langle l\rangle}$; alternatively every compatible pair
of continuous homomorphisms from them extends uniquely to $H$ by
its three free generators.  Use the ordered pants bases
$(l^{-1},a)$ and $(l,b)$.
For the chosen normalized pants automorphism of multiplier
$\lambda=-\epsilon$, its induced isomorphism $\tau:P_1\to P_2$
has
\[
 \tau(l)=l^\epsilon,\qquad
 \tau(a)\sim b^\lambda,\qquad
 \tau(a^{-1}l)\sim(b^{-1}l^{-1})^\lambda.
\]
Here the latter two conjugacies occur inside $P_2$.
The inverse isomorphism has multiplier $\lambda^{-1}=\lambda$,
so the reverse two conjugacies also hold inside $P_1$.
Consequently the assignments
$\alpha|_{P_1}=\tau$ and
$\alpha|_{P_2}=\operatorname{Inn}(c)\tau^{-1}$ agree on the shared
procyclic group, because $c=l^\kappa$ and $\epsilon^2=1$.
They define a continuous endomorphism of $H$.
The equality $\epsilon\kappa=\kappa$ gives $\alpha(c)=c$;
substitution on each factor gives
$\alpha^2=\operatorname{Inn}(c)$.  Hence this endomorphism is an
automorphism, with inverse
$\operatorname{Inn}(c^{-1})\alpha$.

The tuple
\[
 (a,\ a^{-1}l,\ b,\ b^{-1}l^{-1})
\]
has product one, and its first three entries are a free basis:
the inverse substitution is $l=a(a^{-1}l)$.  It therefore is an
actual type-$(0,4)$ peripheral system.  The four conjugacy classes
are interchanged by $(1\ 3)(2\ 4)$, with the same multiplier
$\lambda$ on every entry.  The square identity is an equality of
automorphisms and does not require the chosen pants automorphism
to have finite order.

The retraction $H\to\overline{\langle l\rangle}$ killing $a,b$
is compatible with $\alpha$: the normal closures of $a$ and $b$
are interchanged by $\tau$ and its inverse, while $l$ maps to
$l^\epsilon$.  It extends over the twisted extension to the
specified torsion-free virtually procyclic group $N$ and preserves
the quotient $C_2$.  Thus every finite-order element of the full
extension first maps trivially to $N$, hence into the torsion-free
kernel $H$, and is the identity.

Modulo three, on $H^{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb F_3$ the matrix
is $\operatorname{diag}(-1,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right))$.
The quotient sending $[l]\mapsto e_1$, $[a]\mapsto e_2$ and
$[b]\mapsto-e_2$ therefore gives the inversion action on
$\mathbb F_3^2$.  The square $c$ maps to zero because
$\kappa_3=0$.  This proves the exact surjection onto
$\mathbb F_3^2\rtimes C_2$ used by the three-generator obstruction.

Finally there is an exact comparison with the preceding Haran
construction.  Set $b'=\tau(a)$.  The pair $(l^\epsilon,b')$
is a free basis of $P_2$, so $(l,a,b')$ is a free basis of $H$.
In this basis
\[
 \alpha(l)=l^\epsilon,\qquad
 \alpha(a)=b',\qquad
 \alpha(b')=c a c^{-1}.
\]
These are precisely the previously defined Haran formulas.  Thus
the newly preserved peripheral tuple supplies a loop structure on
that same group through the displayed change of free basis.
