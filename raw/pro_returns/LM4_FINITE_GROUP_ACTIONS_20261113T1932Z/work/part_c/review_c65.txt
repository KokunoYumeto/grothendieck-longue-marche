1. Exact source groups and normality.
Every original word commutes with the local centralizer identity
component, so the principal-base action is the stated actual
homomorphism. Products and inverses of two finite-word paths remain
finite-word paths, with the complete base action equal to identity.
Conjugating by a fixed original word merely adds constant letters and
keeps that base equality. Thus the endpoint set N is a normal subgroup.
Reparametrizing to any intermediate time puts every intermediate
endpoint in the same N. No abstract kernel identification is assumed.

2. Intrinsic topology for exactly the required paths.
On an intrinsic line orbit, any compact interval is contained in one
of C.28's finite-word compact arcs: its two endpoints belong to some
common compact word set, and the unique arc between them lies there.
A fixed-length word applied to that interval remains in one larger
compact word set, which is a compact embedded interval. Ambient joint
continuity then gives intrinsic joint continuity, including inverse
words, on every compact input interval. On a circular orbit the
embedded and intrinsic topologies already agree. This proves actual
homeomorphism-group continuity of the specified finite-word paths.
It makes no claim for a sequence of words of unbounded length.

The restricting path stays in rho_B(N), so this image is path connected.
The restrictions of all original generators are orientation preserving
because their intrinsic paths begin at the identity; the same holds
for their finite products and inverses.

3. The boundary image is abelian through an exact map comparison.
In the principal product, an element of ker(beta) has the displayed
fiber translation form, with the base fixed. Such translations commute
for both a real and a circular fiber. Their actual global commutator
is therefore identity on O, and ambient continuity makes it identity
on the closure of O. In particular it is identity on the specified
frontier orbit B. Hence rho_B(N) is abelian, and its normality in
rho_B(G) follows from the actual homomorphism and the source normality.

4. Nontrivial image forces the stated metabelian restriction.
If T=rho_B(N) is nontrivial, its common fixed set is invariant under
the transitive full restriction image K. It must therefore be empty.
Every T orbit is a nontrivial path-connected subset of a line or circle,
so contains a nonempty open arc. Translating an interior point back to
any chosen orbit point gives a neighborhood there. Thus all T orbits
are open, and connectedness of B makes T transitive.

An abelian transitive action is free. Its actual intrinsic-topology
closure remains abelian, connected and transitive and hence free.
Its full centralizer equals this closure by evaluation at one point
and commutation with the transitive action. The C.30 evaluation proof
in dimension one, with its Polish orbit-open-map input, makes evaluation
a homeomorphism onto B. This supplies local compactness in the actual
topology. The same verified Hilbert--Smith reduction as C.33, applied
faithfully on B times R^2, yields a one-dimensional connected Lie group.
Evaluation is the exact conjugacy to translations or rotations.

Every member of K normalizes this closure. On a line, the induced
continuous additive automorphism is multiplication by a positive real
number, and evaluation gives the actual positive affine formula.
On a circle its degree is +1, so it induces the identity circle-group
automorphism and is a rotation. These groups and all their subgroups
are metabelian. This contradicts the stipulated nonmetabelian K.
Thus the restriction of N is trivial. The two-path consequence follows
by applying that result to p_s q_s^(-1), whose whole base map is
identity at every time. Equality at a single base point is never used.

5. The planar inclusion map is injective on fundamental groups.
A loop in a component U of V intersected with the given open disk
has a based polygonal approximation and based homotopy inside U.
Its finite planar graph admits the specified compact connected planar
regular neighborhood M there. The outer Jordan boundary is compact
inside the Euclidean disk. Its filled Jordan disk is contained in
the convex hull of its boundary and hence in that same open disk.
Therefore every inner complementary Jordan disk is also in it.

Filling precisely those inner disks entirely contained in V gives the
actual compact planar surface M' contained in V intersected with the
disk. It is connected and meets U, so lies in that exact component.
For every remaining hole, the chosen excluded point lies outside V.
The inclusion of M' into the plane punctured at these finitely many
points has the explicit strong deformation retraction in C65.9.
The inner radial homotopies and outer radial homotopy fix all their
boundary circles; with the identity on M' they paste on a finite
closed cover of the punctured plane. Each selected basepoint in M'
is fixed throughout. This also covers the case of no remaining holes.

The null-homotopy in V maps into that punctured plane since its
punctures were chosen outside V. Applying the retraction gives a
based filling in M', then appending the original based approximation
homotopy gives the original loop a filling in U. This proves the
injection for the actual inclusion U->V.

Neither argument chooses a continuous family of fillings, lifts an
