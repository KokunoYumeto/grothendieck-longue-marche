# Exact limitation of a prefix-track lift of the median

This note records a proved limitation of the proposed lifting method. It does not assert a failure of C12 or of median lifting itself.

## The actual plane action and its one global quotient partition

Let the additive group R² act on the plane by

\[
A_{a,b}(x,y)=(x+b,e^a y).
\]

The composition law is \(A_{a,b}A_{c,d}=A_{a+c,b+d}\). Its open orbits are the upper and lower half-planes. Its only nonopen orbit is the horizontal axis B. On the upper orbit O, the exact coordinates

\[
(u,v)=(\log y,x)
\]

give \(A_{a,b}(u,v)=(u+a,v+b)\). The action on O is simply transitive. The group is closed in the ambient homeomorphism group: a homeomorphic limit has finite horizontal translation parameter by evaluation, and its vertical scale cannot tend to zero or infinity, since evaluating two points on one vertical line would respectively contradict injectivity or convergence. The scale and translation parameters are then recovered continuously.

For a source basepoint \((0,1)\), its stabilizer is trivial. Every nontrivial global quotient with nonopen target is therefore

\[
\Phi_z(x,y)=(x+z,0),\qquad z\in\mathbb R.
\]

All these maps have the same fiber partition. This is an actual one-partition case of the earlier setup. Take the original operator group H to be this same abelian plane group.

## The based loop and its prescribed base median

Define the continuous piecewise linear functions \(\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\) by their values at successive times:

| Time | \(\alpha\) | \(\beta\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(0\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(1/4\) | \(1/3\) | \(2/3\) |
| \(1/2\) | \(2/3\) | \(1/3\) |
| \(3/4\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| \(1\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |

Set \(f_t=A_{\alpha(t),\beta(t)}\). This is a based ambient-null loop in the H-centralizer; scaling both parameters supplies its actual null-homotopy. Its generated group is the full R² action. The first two parameter segments provide intervals in two independent directions, whose determinant is \(-1/3\); the subgroups generated by those intervals contain both full one-parameter groups.

Fix \(T=3/4\). On the prefix \([0,T]\), \(\alpha(t)=4t/3\) is strictly increasing. The minimum of every \(\beta\)-prefix is zero. Its running maximum is

\[
M_\rho=
\begin{cases}
\frac83\rho,&0\leq\rho\leq\frac14,\\
\frac23,&\frac14\leq\rho\leq\frac58,\\
\frac83\rho-1,&\frac58\leq\rho\leq\frac34,\\
1,&\frac34\leq\rho\leq1.
\end{cases}
\]

The endpoint base translation at T is one. Consequently C27's median, with prefix parameter \(\rho=1-s\), acts on the base by \(v\mapsto v+M_\rho\).

## No continuous lift can stay in the original point prefix

There is no continuous family \(K_\rho\), even merely evaluated at the one point \((0,1)\), satisfying all three requirements:

1. \(K_0(0,1)=(0,1)\) and \(K_T(0,1)=f_T(0,1)\).
2. The base coordinate of \(K_\rho(0,1)\) is \(M_\rho\).
3. \(K_\rho(0,1)\) belongs to the original prefix set \(\{f_t(0,1):0\leq t\leq T\}\).

Indeed, strict increase of \(\alpha\) makes the prefix track an embedded arc and recovers a continuous parameter \(t=t(\rho)\) from the logarithm of its second coordinate. The endpoint conditions give \(t(0)=0\) and \(t(T)=T\), while the base condition gives

\[
\beta(t(\rho))=M_\rho.
\]

The right-hand side is nondecreasing. By continuity, the parameter must first reach \(1/4\) and then reach \(1/2\). At those two successively ordered parameter values, the displayed equation has values \(2/3\) and \(1/3\), respectively. This contradicts nondecrease.

The contradiction applies even when all original prefix times up to T are allowed. Restricting the selectable times further to \([0,\rho]\) cannot repair it.

## Fiber-interval filling repairs this example exactly

The two prefix crossings of the level \(2/3\) occur at \(t=1/4\) and \(t=5/8\). Their fiber coordinates are \(1/3\) and \(5/6\). During the running-maximum plateau, use the interval between those coordinates instead of the two isolated prefix points.

Explicitly, put

\[
\widehat K_\rho=A_{\min(4\rho/3,1),\,M_\rho}.
\]

This is a continuous family of actual global H-centralizing plane homeomorphisms, starts at the identity, reaches \(f_T\) at \(\rho=T\), and has precisely the prescribed median base coordinate. During the plateau it fills the fiber interval just described. Thus the example establishes a limitation of an exact point-prefix inclusion, while exhibiting a valid interval-filled lift.

The algebra, breakpoints, crossing times and interval bounds are checked by exact rational arithmetic in code/prefix_selection_certificate.py and its JSON output. The no-selection argument is the continuity and order proof above.

## Current frontier issue

The general next step remains to control such same-fiber intervals when a compact family of original prefix tracks approaches a nonopen frontier orbit. C60 controls an interval when the whole original prefix remains in that fiber. The example above shows why that direct argument does not cover a moving base. No assertion that arbitrary foliation intervals satisfy the necessary ambient bound is used here.
