\subsection{The escaping-support obstruction on the actual tripod}
\begin{statement}{A.136}{Proved}
{Finite controlled initialization without a completed tripod coordinate}
There is an ordinary completed unit-equation pair
\((A,B)\in\widehat{\Q^\times}^{\,2}\), with both valuation
vectors infinitely supported, whose local centres
\(x_p\in\Q_p\setminus\{0,1,-8\}\) satisfy
\[
             A_p=\kappa_p(x_p),\qquad
             B_p=\kappa_p(1-x_p).
\tag{A.136.1}
\]
Its classes modulo four are \((3/4,1/4)\), and its real
ordinary centre is \(3/4\).
For the new puncture \(-8\), every finite common-character
obstruction is zero, and at every finite exponent every
finite collection of the actual old local points lifts
to an everywhere locally soluble augmented Kummer cover.
Nevertheless no rational squareclass, and hence no
completed global class, has local values \(x_p+8\) at
all finite places.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
Use the strengthened A.133 tuple, which avoids both
\(T=2\) and \(T=-1\), and the actual degree-two etale
map of A.107:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 V=\mathbf P^1\setminus\{0,1,\tfrac12,\infty\}
       \longrightarrow U=\mathbf P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\},\\
 f(T)=4T(1-T),\qquad
       1-f(T)=4(T-\tfrac12)^2,\qquad
       f(T)+8=-4(T-2)(T+1).
 \end{gathered}
\tag{A.136.2}
\]
The displayed identities give every relevant fibre:
\(f^{-1}(0)=\{0,1\}\),
\(f^{-1}(1)=\{1/2\}\),
\(f^{-1}(\infty)=\{\infty\}\), and
\(f^{-1}(-8)=\{2,-1\}\).
On the stated opens the derivative vanishes only
at the removed point \(1/2\), so the map is etale.

Define the global classes by the actual pullbacks
of the two coordinate functions:
\[
                A=-4A_0A_1,\qquad B=4A_h^2,
                       \qquad h=\tfrac12,
\tag{A.136.3}
\]
and put \(x_p=f(t_p)\).
Equations (A.133.2) and (A.136.2) prove (A.136.1).
Avoidance of the four old boundary points and of
\(2,-1\) proves \(x_p\notin\{0,1,-8\}\).
At the real point \(R=1/4\), the image is \(3/4\);
the same formulas give the classes \((3/4,1/4)\)
modulo four.

The corresponding finite-cover map is also literal.
Given old harmonic representatives \(a_0,a_1,a_h\)
at exponent \(N\), set
\[
               a=-4a_0a_1,\qquad b=4a_h^2 .
\]
The morphism to the tripod Kummer cover is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 T-i=a_iX_i^N
      \longmapsto
        t=f(T),\qquad X=X_0X_1,\qquad Y=X_h^2,\\
                       t=aX^N,\qquad1-t=bY^N.
 \end{gathered}
\tag{A.136.4}
\]
Direct substitution verifies both equations.
These maps commute with the compatible coefficient
power maps used in A.133: the multipliers of \(X\)
and \(Y\) are respectively \(u_0u_1\) and \(u_h^2\).
Thus (A.136.3) is the full completed map induced by
these finite algebraic morphisms.

The fresh coefficient primes belonging to coordinates
zero and one in A.133 have positive valuations in
exactly that harmonic coordinate and zero in the
other two.  They therefore give infinitely many
nonzero valuations of \(A\).
The fresh primes belonging to the coordinate \(h\)
give twice its positive valuations in \(B\).
These sets are disjoint from the fixed prime support
of the constants in (A.136.3), proving both infinite
supports.

At every forced prime \(p_j>3\), A.133 gives
\(t_{p_j}=2+p_j\).
The last identity in (A.136.2) gives
\[
             x_{p_j}+8=-4p_j(p_j+3),\qquad
             v_{p_j}(x_{p_j}+8)=1 .
\tag{A.136.5}
\]
No rational number can have odd valuation at all
these distinct primes.  Hence a global squareclass
matching \(x_p+8\) at every finite place does not
exist.  This is the exact restricted-product
failure of A.132, and excludes a completed class
by its projection modulo two.

It remains to verify all the finite controlled-point
claims on the tripod itself.
For \(4\mid N\), take any rational representatives
\(a,b\) of \(A,B\bmod N\).
They differ from \(3/4,1/4\), respectively, by
fourth powers, because (A.136.3) and A.133 preserve
these classes modulo four.
Use the old coordinate equations \(t=aX^N\),
\(t-1=-bY^N\).
At the new boundary \(t=-8\), their two radicands
have the fixed squareclasses
\[
       \frac{-8}{a}\ \equiv\ -6,\qquad
       \frac{-9}{-b}=\frac9b\ \equiv\ 1
                    \quad\text{in }\Q^\times/(\Q^\times)^2.
\tag{A.136.6}
\]
Their span \(\{1,-6\}\) excludes \(-1\).
The actual common-field theorem A.80 therefore gives
\[
                B_{N,-8}=\Q(\sqrt{-6}).
\tag{A.136.7}
\]
There is no omitted higher-order common character.
Its character is the individual quadratic one
of \(-8/a\), and the literal norm identity gives
\[
        (t+8,-8/a)=(a,-8/a)
                           \quad\text{in }\operatorname{Br}(C_N).
\tag{A.136.8}
\]
Its global invariant sum is zero by reciprocity
for the constant right side.
The old cover has ordinary points at every finite
place by (A.136.1), and a real point by its signs.
A.83 or the equivalent A.85 criterion now supplies
an augmented everywhere locally soluble cover
\(t+8=dZ^N\) lifting any finite prescribed collection
of those old points.
For an arbitrary positive exponent, apply this
argument at four times that exponent and use
the coordinate-power map, which retains \(t\).

All these assertions concern the displayed Kummer
coordinates and finite Kummer covers.
The argument does not construct a full fundamental-group
section, and it does not infer failure of such a section
from the missing new coordinate alone.
\end{proof}
