\subsection{Actual unit transfer and one coherent prime branch in the constant tower}

\begin{statement}{A.147}{Proved}
{Onto pure-tower unit norms and the exact first prime generator}
Keep the compatible positive roots
\(\alpha_n^{3^n}=3\), \(\alpha_{n+1}^3=\alpha_n\), and
\(K_n=\Q(\alpha_n)\) of A.138, for \(n\geq1\).
Every actual unit norm
\[
 N_{K_{n+1}/K_n}:\mathcal O_{K_{n+1}}^\times
                         \longrightarrow\mathcal O_{K_n}^\times
\tag{A.147.1}
\]
is onto.

For the particular \(q=511177\) and
\(\gamma=-68+64\alpha_1+\alpha_1^2\) of A.144,
there is a compatible sequence
\[
 \gamma_n\in\widehat{K_n^\times}_{(3)},\qquad
 \gamma_1=\gamma,\qquad
 N_{K_{n+1}/K_n}\gamma_{n+1}=\gamma_n,\qquad
 N_{K_n/\Q}\gamma_n=q.
\tag{A.147.2}
\]
Its divisors are the single degree-one primes on the
infinite \(q\)-adic branch through
\(\alpha_1\equiv13382\pmod q\).
The completion in (A.147.2) is explicitly
\(\widehat{K_n^\times}_{(3)}=\varprojlim_r
K_n^\times/(K_n^\times)^{3^r}\).

The scalar \(q\in K_1^\times\) is not a norm from
\(K_2\) to \(K_1\), even after these completions.
The same is true of the A.144 norm-one cofactor
\(Uq/\gamma^3\).
Thus (A.147.2) has precisely the specified starting
element \(\gamma\), while its norm down to \(\Q\)
is the scalar \(q\).
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The class-group input on the actual intermediate fields.}
Write \(L_m=K_m(\mu_{3^m})\).
A.138 proves that its three-primary class group is zero,
and that \(L_m/\Q\) is totally ramified at its unique
prime above three.
The three-primary class group of every intermediate
field \(F\subset L_m\) is also zero.
Indeed, let \(H/F\) be its three-primary Hilbert class
field.  Base change makes \(HL_m/L_m\) an unramified
abelian three-extension, so A.138 gives \(H\subset L_m\).
Every intermediate extension inside \(L_m/F\) is
totally ramified at three: the unique local degree
there equals the global degree.
Since \(H/F\) is unramified, it is trivial.
This proves the claim for the literal fields used below.

Set
\[
 F'=K_n(\mu_3),\qquad E'=K_{n+1}(\mu_3).
\tag{A.147.3}
\]
Both are subfields of \(L_{n+1}\), and
\(E'/F'\) is cyclic cubic.
The fields \(K_n,K_{n+1}\) have odd degree over
\(\Q\), so adjoining \(\mu_3\) has degree two
in both cases; the degrees in (A.147.3) are therefore
exactly as asserted.
The only ramified finite prime of \(E'/F'\) is its
unique three-prime.

\emph{Cyclic unit correction and quadratic transfer.}
Let \(\epsilon\in\mathcal O_{F'}^\times\).
It is a local norm at every place away from three:
unit norms in unramified local extensions are onto,
and there is no real place of \(F'\).
Global cyclic reciprocity forces the remaining local
norm obstruction to be zero.
The Hasse norm theorem then gives
\(b\in E'^\times\) with \(N_{E'/F'}b=\epsilon\)
\cite[Chapter V, Theorem 5.3; Chapter VIII, Theorem 3.1]{MilneCFT}.
For a generator \(\sigma\) of the cyclic group,
the exact ideal-norm kernel is
\[
 \ker(\operatorname{Div}(E')\xrightarrow{N}\operatorname{Div}(F'))
                         =(\sigma-1)\operatorname{Div}(E').
\tag{A.147.4}
\]
On each prime orbit, the norm sends all its basis
elements to the same residue-degree multiple of the
base prime; the zero-sum vectors are exactly the
differences generated by \(\sigma-1\).
Thus \(\operatorname{div}(b)=(\sigma-1)J\).
The order \(d\) of the ideal class of \(J\) is prime
to three by the preceding class-group argument.
Choose \(h\in E'^\times\) with
\(\operatorname{div}(h)=dJ\).
Then
\[
 u=b^d\frac{h}{\sigma h}\in\mathcal O_{E'}^\times,
                         \qquad Nu=\epsilon^d.
\tag{A.147.5}
\]
If \(ad+3b_0=1\), the actual unit
\(u^a\epsilon^{b_0}\) has norm \(\epsilon\).
This is the unit-norm proof on these particular cyclic
intermediate fields, including its ideal correction.

Now start with \(\epsilon\in\mathcal O_{K_n}^\times\)
and choose an actual unit \(\eta\in\mathcal O_{E'}^\times\)
whose norm to \(F'\) is \(\epsilon\).
Put \(v=N_{E'/K_{n+1}}\eta\).
The two transitive norm maps give
\[
 N_{K_{n+1}/K_n}v
   =N_{F'/K_n}N_{E'/F'}\eta
   =\epsilon^2,
 \qquad
 N_{K_{n+1}/K_n}\left(\frac{v^2}{\epsilon}\right)
   =\epsilon^{4-3}=\epsilon.
\tag{A.147.6}
\]
Every element in this last expression is an actual
global unit.  This proves (A.147.1).
Completing the finitely generated unit groups at three
also gives onto completed unit norms.

\emph{The chosen degree-one branch at every depth.}
The A.144 certificate gives
\[
 v_3(q-1)=1,
 \qquad (\alpha_{1,0},\alpha_{1,1},\alpha_{1,2})
             \equiv(13382,29914,467881)\pmod q,
\tag{A.147.7}
\]
and their cubic residue characters, relative to the
specified generator of \(\mu_3\), are \((0,1,2)\).
On the first \(\Q_q\)-embedding, \(\alpha_{1,0}\)
has trivial three-primary residue-unit component.
In
\(\mathcal O_{\Q_q}^\times=\mu_{q-1}\times(1+q\Z_q)\),
the subgroup of units with trivial three-primary
component has an invertible cube map.
Indeed, its finite root-of-unity factor has order
\((q-1)/3\), prime to three, and the cube map is
invertible on the principal units.
There are therefore unique compatible roots
\(\alpha_{n+1,0}^3=\alpha_{n,0}\) in this subgroup.
They give compatible embeddings \(K_n\hookrightarrow\Q_q\)
and degree-one primes \(P_n\) such that
\[
                    N_{K_{n+1}/K_n}P_{n+1}=P_n.
\tag{A.147.8}
\]
The extensions are unramified at \(q\), since their
defining radicand is three and \(q\ne3\).
By the prime norm and residue computation of A.144,
\(\operatorname{div}(\gamma)=P_1\).

\emph{Literal completed principal generators and their unit corrections.}
The divisor sequence gives the exact completed sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal O_{K_n}^\times}_{(3)}
 \longrightarrow\widehat{K_n^\times}_{(3)}
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{div}}
 \widehat{\operatorname{Div}(K_n)}_{(3)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{A.147.9}
\]
For clarity, the principal divisor group is free abelian,
so its sequence with units splits as a sequence of
abelian groups.  Its inclusion in the full divisor
group has finite cokernel of order prime to three.
Completing these two facts proves (A.147.9), including
the displayed unit kernel.
The completed divisor group consists of coefficient
families in \(\Z_3\) tending to zero outside finite
sets; the single primes in (A.147.8) are elements of it.

If the class of \(P_n\) has order \(d_n\), choose
\(\pi_n\) with divisor \(d_nP_n\).
Since \(3\nmid d_n\), the completed element
\(\pi_n^{d_n^{-1}}\) has divisor exactly \(P_n\).
Suppose \(\gamma_n\) has already been chosen.
Take any such completed generator \(w_{n+1}\) of
\(P_{n+1}\).
By (A.147.8)--(A.147.9),
\[
       \frac{Nw_{n+1}}{\gamma_n}
                     \in\widehat{\mathcal O_{K_n}^\times}_{(3)}.
\tag{A.147.10}
\]
The onto completed unit norm supplies
\(e_{n+1}\) with norm the inverse of this ratio.
Set \(\gamma_{n+1}=w_{n+1}e_{n+1}\), beginning with
the prescribed \(\gamma_1=\gamma\).
This constructs (A.147.2) recursively, with no
unspecified inverse-limit selection.
Transitivity and \(N_{K_1/\Q}\gamma=q\) give its
last norm identity.

\emph{The scalar comparison through the actual divisor maps.}
At either of the other two places of \(K_1\) above
\(q\), the residue of \(\alpha_1\) is not a cube.
The polynomial \(T^3-\alpha_1\) therefore defines
the unramified cubic extension of \(\Q_q\).
The norm valuation of that field is a multiple of
three.  This remains the map
\(\Z_3\xrightarrow{\,3\,}\Z_3\) on completed
valuation groups.
The scalar \(q\) has valuation one at each of these
two places, so it cannot be a norm from \(K_2\).
The cofactor \(Uq/\gamma^3\) has the same valuation
one there, because \(U\) is a unit and \(\gamma\)
has divisor \(P_1\).
The different first-level divisors are exactly
\[
 \operatorname{div}(\gamma)=P_1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{div}(q)=P_1+P_1'+P_1'',
 \qquad
 \operatorname{div}(Uq/\gamma^3)
                   =-2P_1+P_1'+P_1''.
\tag{A.147.11}
\]
The map \(N_{K_1/\Q}\) sends all three degree-one
primes to the rational prime \(q\), whereas
\(N_{K_2/K_1}\) has residue-degree three at
\(P_1',P_1''\).
These are the explicit maps giving both the coherent
family and the stated failure for the scalar.
They do not transfer that family to the changed
radicand tower of A.144.
\end{proof}
