### R121-14 — The field and vector-space assertion may use a missing hypothesis

**Source:** 121:056, opening sentence. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “Moyennant 1°,2°”; “corps ... des pts modérés”. **Context comparison:** `MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex`, 181–182: no corresponding scalar assertion; this is an additional algebraic issue in the present axiomatization. **Register:** no direct entry; it qualifies the proposed tame-theory dictionary rather than changing a register status. **Confidence:** high, because rational linear elimination verifies the closure properties and the scalar set fails multiplicative closure.

Before the multiplication clause, consider compact semilinear sets defined using rational coefficients on variables and constant terms in D=Q+Qα, where α is the real cube root of 2, and maps with such graphs. Thus the stated field/vector-space conclusion needs an additional hypothesis or a different intended scope; this test concerns the literal dependence on clauses 1° and 2° and does not challenge the later system including multiplication.

## Comparison of tame theories — the Esquisse and Shiota

### TC15 — The published Hauptvermutung: hypotheses and date

**Cote/scan:** 121:065. **Kind:** unpursued implication (theorem comparison). **Brief manuscript quotation:** “triangulation.” **Context/relation:** differs from the reconstruction of stratified homotopy types in context/MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scans 179–180, and context/MPL_149_091-136_transcription.tex, scan 133. **Register:** MPL_150_001-043 Q4b, first-pass research lead, now supplied with a precise published theorem.

### TC18 — The Esquisse asks for higher isomorphism spaces and more

**Cote/scan:** 121:065, compared with Grothendieck's Esquisse, §5, printed pp. 29–30 (public PDF pages 25–26). **Kind:** conjecture (external statement of the proposed comparison). **Brief quotation:** “catégories isotopiques (ou même \(\infty\)-isotopiques).” **Context/relation:** differs from ordinary groupoid reconstruction in context/MPL_147_138-190_transcription.tex, scans 179–180, and the incidence model of context/MPL_149_091-136_transcription.tex, scan 133. **Register:** MPL_150_001-043 Q4b, first-pass lead; INTAKE 20261114 §13.2, intake reading.

### A26 — Freeness does not give uniqueness after the hypotheses are broadened

**Source:** 153:004, marginal freeness condition and tensor uniqueness. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “\(k_0\)-module libre”; “un élément bien déterminé de \(\Omega\otimes_{k_0}\Omega\).” **Context:** A; searched free modules, scalar extension, and tensor uniqueness. **Register:** no direct entry. **Confidence:** high, because linear independence proves the tensor nonzero while its polynomial image can be computed directly.

Take \(k_0=\mathbb F_q\), a proper field extension \(K_0/k_0\), \(a\in K_0\setminus k_0\), and \(P(T)=T^q-T\); then \(P,aP\in\Omega\) are linearly independent over \(k_0\), although \(\Omega\), being a vector space, is free. Consequently \((aP)\otimes P-P\otimes(aP)\) is nonzero in \(\Omega\otimes_{k_0}\Omega\), but its image in \(K_0[X,Y]\) is \(aP(X)P(Y)-P(X)aP(Y)=0\). Any tensor lift, if it exists, can therefore be changed by this kernel element without changing its polynomial image, so freeness does not imply the marginal uniqueness claim for the broadened class of ring inclusions. ## Cote 155 — Tensor category and a fibre functor over \(k'/k\)

### T01 — The initial equivalence is a reconstruction claim

**Source:** 155:002, opening. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “équivaut à la donnée.” **Context:** T; differs from the set-valued étale-covering fibre functor of MPL_149_091-136_transcription.tex, scan 107. **Register:** no direct entry.

It does not give the reconstruction functors or verify that all morphisms, tensor constraints, and the exact structure are recovered. The opening deletions around “exact” make it necessary to state explicitly what “Abel” is assumed to require before applying a modern theorem.

### T02 — The pro-object qualification cannot be dropped

**Source:** 155:002, \(A=U^\vee\) and completed tensor products. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “Sur le pro-objet \(A\).” **Context:** T; searched pro-objects and duals. **Register:** no direct entry.

For infinite-dimensional \(U\), its linear dual is naturally handled through finite-dimensional subspaces and a corresponding inverse system; the completed tensor notation signals this issue. Later statements involving \(D(A)\), the regular module, and \(M=A\) therefore need continuity or extension to a pro-category. A finite-dimensional module proof cannot simply be applied to \(A\) as if it were already an object of \(C\).

### T03 — The copy of \(k'\) is not asserted central

**Source:** 155:002, condition (i); 155:004, example. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “la structure de pro-anneau de \(A\) prolonge celle de \(k'\).” **Context:** T; searched scalar extension. **Register:** no direct entry.

The explicit example \(A=\operatorname{End}_k(k')\) includes \(k'\) by multiplication operators, which need not lie in its center. Consequently \(A\otimes_{k'}A\) is not automatically a tensor product of ordinary central \(k'\)-algebras. This is the structural reason to take the normalizer/idealizer quotient in condition (ii) seriously.

### T04 — The idealizer quotient repairs a genuine algebra problem

**Source:** 155:002, condition (ii). **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “\(\widetilde J/J\)”; “\(\lambda J\subset J\).” **Context:** T; searched normalizer, quotient, tensor ring. **Register:** no direct entry.

For a right ideal \(J\) of a ring \(B\), the set \(\widetilde J=\{b\in B:bJ\subset J\}\) is a subring containing \(J\), and \(J\) is two-sided inside that subring. Thus \(\widetilde J/J\) is a ring even when \(B/J\) is only a module. The manuscript's multiplicativity requirement into this smaller ring should not be replaced by an ordinary bialgebra assertion over \(k'\).

### T05 — The example is descent data, not merely an automorphism group

**Source:** 155:004, \(C=\mathrm{Modf}(k)\). **Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Quote:** “\(U=k'\otimes_k k'\).” **Context:** T; differs from the pointed arithmetic group in MPL_149_091-136_transcription.tex, scan 107. **Register:** no direct entry. **Confidence:** high for the example; the pair-groupoid interpretation follows from its two scalar factors.

For \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(k')\), \(\operatorname{Spec}(k'\otimes_k k')=S\times_kS\) is the arrow scheme of the pair groupoid presenting descent to \(k\). By contrast, the group of tensor automorphisms over \(k'\) of \(M\mapsto M\otimes_k k'\) is trivial. Hence the manuscript's \(G'\), viewed over one \(k'\)-factor, cannot simply be identified with \(\operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}_{k'}(F)\).

### T06 — The endomorphism-ring example has the expected Morita test

**Source:** 155:004, \(A\simeq\operatorname{End}_k(k')\). **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “on y prend la structure d'anneau habituelle.” **Context:** T; searched endomorphism algebras. **Register:** no direct entry. **Confidence:** high for finite extensions, by the matrix-algebra calculation.

If \(d=[k':k]\), choosing a \(k\)-basis identifies this ring with \(M_d(k)\), and its finite-dimensional modules are Morita-equivalent to finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector spaces. This provides a concrete consistency test for the opening reconstruction claim and for the distinguished unit object. It works for finite inseparable extensions as well and therefore does not require the optional étaleness assumption in the following margin.

### T07 — The marginal warning blocks a naive Hopf-algebra replacement

**Source:** 155:004, upper marginal NB. **Kind:** self-flagged / reading question. **Quote:** “\(\Delta_U(1)\ne1\) en général”; “pas multiplicatif.” **Context:** T; searched coproduct and dual multiplication. **Register:** no direct entry.

### T08 — The central question is exactness and faithfulness after diagonal tensoring

**Source:** 155:004, lower half. **Kind:** conjecture / self-flagged. **Quote:** “Suffit il que \(C\) soit rigide”; “\(k\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{End}_C(1_C)\quad???\).” **Context:** T; searched rigidity and scalar descent. **Register:** no direct entry.

The object of the question is the restricted functor \(M'\mapsto M'\otimes_{k'\otimes_k k'}k'\) on the displayed \(A_{k'}\)-module category. ### T09 — A precise modern affirmative comparison

**Source:** 155:004, diagonal tensor functor. **Kind:** unpursued implication / literature comparison. **Quote:** “\(M'\longrightarrow M'\otimes_{k'\otimes_k k'}k'\).” **Context:** T; adds a tensor-category question not present in the supplied 147/149 material. **Register:** no direct entry.

Under the usual exact symmetric fibre-functor hypotheses, Deligne and Milne (1982, corrected 2012, formula 3.10.1) give exactly the same diagonal tensor construction on \(k'\)-module objects in \(C\), and Proposition 3.11 identifies its representation lift with the neutralized category. Deligne (1990, §§1.2, 1.9, 1.12) supplies the needed recognition/reconstruction theorem for a rigid symmetric abelian \(k\)-linear category with \(\operatorname{End}(1)=k\) and an exact field-valued tensor functor. This yields the desired exact faithful fibre functor in that setting, while leaving the manuscript's specific pro-ring presentation and its word “Abel” to be matched carefully.

### T10 — The chronology of that comparison matters

**Source:** 155:004, intrinsic-conditions question. **Kind:** reading question / historical comparison. **Quote:** “conditions intrinsèques.” **Context:** T. **Register:** no direct entry.

The corrected 2012 Deligne–Milne text retains a stronger gerbe/reconstruction definition in §3.7 and adds a footnote invoking Deligne's 1990 theorem to obtain it from a field-valued fibre functor. Deligne's original 1990 introduction explicitly identifies a gap in Saavedra's proof and the necessity of the condition on \(\operatorname{End}(1)\). Thus the matching 1982 diagonal formula should not be cited as if the full intrinsic recognition asked about here had already been proved there without qualification.

### T11 — Étale exactness does not give faithfulness on every module

**Source:** 155:004, parenthetical “OK.” **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “exact (OK si \(k'/k\) est étale) et fidèle.” **Context:** T; searched faithful descent. **Register:** no direct entry. **Confidence:** high; a split product gives an explicit test.

For a nontrivial finite Galois extension \(L/k\), \(L\otimes_kL\simeq\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)}L\), and multiplication selects the identity component. Tensoring along this map is exact, but kills a nonzero module supported in another component. The sought faithfulness must therefore use the special \(A_{k'}\)-module structure, not merely étaleness of the scalar extension.

### T12 — Inseparability isolates the exactness obstruction

**Source:** 155:004, same question. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “OK si \(k'/k\) est étale.” **Context:** T; searched finite extensions. **Register:** no direct entry. **Confidence:** high; the module calculation is explicit.

For \(k=\mathbb F_p(t)\) and \(L=k(t^{1/p})\), the ring \(R=L\otimes_kL\) is \(L[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^p)\), and the diagonal quotient is \(R/(\epsilon)=L\). Tensoring the injection \((\epsilon)\hookrightarrow R\) with \(L\) sends the nonzero source \((\epsilon)/(\epsilon^2)\) to zero, so the unrestricted functor is not exact. This does not refute T09, whose objects carry the additional compatible action.

### T13 — Rigidity gives contravariant exactness

**Source:** 155:006, opening. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quote:** “foncteur équivalence de catégories, donc exact.” **Context:** T; searched duality. **Register:** no direct entry.

### T14 — The regular object may live outside C

**Source:** 155:006, \(I=D(A)\); 155:010, reduction to \(M=A\). **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “\(I=D(A)\)”; “Il suffit de le prouver pour \(M=A\).” **Context:** T; searched pro-objects and generators. **Register:** no direct entry.

### T15 — The two commuting actions are a necessary intermediate construction

**Source:** 155:006, middle paragraph. **Kind:** unproved claim. **Quote:** “mettre sur \(U\) une structure … de module sur \(A\otimes A\).” **Context:** T; searched module duality. **Register:** no direct entry.

### T16 — k-duals and k′-duals differ by a choice

**Source:** 155:006, warning and possible diagonal marginal question. **Kind:** own idea responding to an authorial warning and a possible question. **Quote:** “pas néc. \(k'\)-linéaires”; “identifier \(U\) à \(D_k(A)\).” **Context:** T; searched scalar duality. **Register:** no direct entry. **Confidence:** high for finite-dimensional modules; extension to the pro setting requires continuity.
