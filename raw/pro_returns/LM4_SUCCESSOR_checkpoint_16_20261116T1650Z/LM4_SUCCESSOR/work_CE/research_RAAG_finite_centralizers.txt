Bestvina--Brady / Leary--Nucinkis finite-subgroup route: exact boundary
2026-11-16. Research audit for the proposed B.6 counterexample route.

Conclusion.
The standard finite-complex, admissible-action Leary--Nucinkis examples
do not give nonconjugate old finite complements that become profinitely
conjugate. Their relevant full profinite centralizers can be computed
exactly using only the VALID prime-power comparison and commuting RAAG
retractions. The result is a positive comparison in this family, not a
counterexample to the nonsolvable centralizer statement under audit.

1. Actual construction and hypotheses.

Let L be a finite contractible flag complex and let a finite group Q act
admissibly on L. Admissible means that a simplex stabilized setwise is
fixed pointwise; subdivision supplies this hypothesis in the standard
construction. Let A=A_L be the right-angled Artin group and let
phi:A->Z send every vertex generator to 1. Set H=ker(phi),
Gamma=A semidirect Q, and E=H semidirect Q. The height map extends to
Gamma by killing Q. Leary--Nucinkis (2003), Theorem 1, gives H of type F
for contractible L. Their Example 3 uses the Oliver group A5 and a finite
contractible admissible complex with L^A5 empty. The resulting groups
are linear over Z and residually finite. Gamma is a finite extension of
a RAAG, hence is good and has finite virtual cohomological type, as
required by the valid prime-power comparison. No acyclic group without
finite quotients, and no infinite-complex variant, is being substituted.

2. Discrete centralizers and why admissibility matters.

Theorem 2 of Leary--Nucinkis, a stated special case of Crisp's theorem,
identifies C_A(P)=A_{L^P} for any finite subgroup P of Q. Under
admissibility, L^P is the full subcomplex on the P-fixed vertices. Thus
A_{L^P} is a STANDARD RAAG parabolic, with the canonical retraction that
fixes its vertex generators and kills all other generators. Without the
admissibility/full-subcomplex identification, the following argument
cannot simply be reused: a setwise stabilized commuting simplex can
produce diagonal fixed elements instead of a vertex parabolic.

3. Full profinite comparison, without using the disputed all-finite step.

Choose generators q_1,...,q_t of Q of prime-power orders. Such generators
exist for every finite Q, for example by taking generators of all Sylow
subgroups. Apply the valid prime-power centralizer theorem of
Boggi--Zalesskii (2025) separately to <q_i> in Gamma. Its hypotheses hold
because Gamma is residually finite, good, and of finite virtual
cohomological type. Thus
  C_{Gammahat}(q_i)=closure(C_Gamma(q_i)).
Here C_Gamma(q_i)=A_{L^{q_i}} semidirect C_Q(q_i). Intersecting with the
open normal Ahat (finite index in Gammahat) gives
  C_{Ahat}(q_i)=closure(A_{L^{q_i}}).
The finite-index intersection is legitimate: intersection of a closure
with an open subgroup is the closure of the discrete intersection.

For vertex sets S,T, the standard RAAG retractions r_S,r_T commute and
satisfy r_S r_T=r_{S intersection T}. Their continuous extensions to
Ahat have images exactly the closures of the corresponding parabolics,
because a compact image containing the dense discrete parabolic is that
closure. If retractions commute, their image intersection is the image
of their product: a point fixed by both is fixed by their product, and
the converse follows by idempotence/commutation. Consequently
  intersection_i closure(A_{L^{q_i}})
       = closure(A_{intersection_i L^{q_i}})
       = closure(A_{L^Q}).
This proves
  C_{Ahat}(Q)=closure(C_A(Q))=Ahat_{L^Q}
for this entire admissible RAAG family. It does not assume that closure
commutes with arbitrary subgroup intersections. It proves that exact
intersection by explicit commuting retractions.

In the A5 examples with L^Q empty, C_{Ahat}(Q)=1. An element of Ahat
normalizing the standard Q must centralize it: conjugation has identity
effect after projecting Gammahat to Q, so its induced automorphism of Q
is the identity. Hence N_{Gammahat}(Q)=Q in these examples.

4. Consequence for the height-indexed complements.

Choose a vertex v with phi(v)=1. The discrete complements
Q_n=v^n Q v^{-n} in E are indexed by integers. If an element h of Ehat
conjugated Q_n to Q_m, then v^{-m} h v^n would normalize Q in Gammahat,
and hence belong to Q. Taking height gives n=m. Thus none of the
nonconjugate integer-height complements collapses under completion.
The proposed HNN construction based on such a collapse has no input
from these standard examples.

The embedding Ehat->Gammahat used here is justified separately. E is
finitely generated, normal, and Gamma=E semidirect <v>. Characteristic
finite-index subgroups of E are cofinal; v has finite-order action on
each finite E-quotient, so that quotient extends to a finite quotient
of Gamma. This proves the full induced topology and the exact completed
semidirect product Ehat semidirect Zhat. Thus no induced-topology claim
is hidden in the height argument.

One can also define Q_t=v^t Q v^{-t} for t in Zhat. The same argument
shows that these are pairwise nonconjugate under Ehat. Leary--Nucinkis's
discrete conjugacy theorem says that all discrete complements are
represented by the integer heights. Hence there are new profinite
finite-subgroup classes, rather than mergers of old ones. This fact is
not a centralizer counterexample and does not contradict the valid
prime-power theorem. The issue under B.6 is a centralizer comparison,
not an unrestricted assertion of surjectivity on all finite-subgroup
conjugacy classes.

5. Possible reuse of the argument, and its exact missing hypothesis in
the mapping-class problem.

The useful sufficient criterion is: the individual closed centralizers
are images of continuous extensions of pairwise commuting DISCRETE
retractions, whose product has discrete image the desired common
centralizer. Then closure/intersection equality follows formally.
There is no established such family for the actual tetrahedral and
icosahedral mapping-class source images. A forgetful map with a section
provides one retraction onto a selected section image, but the cyclic
source here is an infinite-index geometric image with a nontrivial
Birman cocycle. Neither its being a retract nor commutation with an
independent tetrahedral retraction has been proved. The RAAG criterion
therefore supplies an exact boundary, not a new solution of B.75 or K=1.
