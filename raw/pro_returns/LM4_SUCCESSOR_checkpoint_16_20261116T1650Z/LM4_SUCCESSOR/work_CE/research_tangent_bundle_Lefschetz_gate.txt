Feasibility audit for a relative weak-Lefschetz/Nori route
2026-11-16, checkpoint 13

This note checks geometry only.  It does not assert that a specific Nori
theorem applies, and no broad literature search was made on an unverified
finite-index principle.

In homogeneous coordinates [Q:T:W], the retained boundary consists of
the vertical lines Q=0 and Q=uW (u in mu_5), the conic QW=T^2, the retained
tangent line Q-2T+W=0, and W=0.  The source is the line C:T=W.  The other
four tangent lines Q-2uT+u^2W=0 are the deleted boundary components.

At the finite retained point (q,T)=(1,1), use eps=q-1 and t=T-1.  The
retained local branches are
  eps=0,  eps=2t,  eps=2t+t^2,
whereas the source is t=0.  Its tangent is different from the vertical
branch and the common tangent of the other two branches.  A first blowup
there separates the strict source from every subsequent resolution center.

At the source point at infinity [1:0:0], use t=T/Q and w=W/Q.  The source
is t=w, while the retained conic and infinity are w=t^2 and w=0.  Again the
source tangent differs from their common tangent.  The first blowup at
this point separates the strict source from subsequent resolution centers.

No other retained multiple point lies on C: all other vertical intersections
are simple, and the four deleted-line intersections are precisely the
distinct transverse source punctures q=2u-u^2, u != 1.  In particular the
source is not a generic section of the resolved boundary pair.

The projective source line has self-intersection 1.  The two required
blowups on it reduce the strict-transform self-intersection to -1; further
boundary resolution is away from that strict transform.  Consequently a
weak-Lefschetz or Nori result requiring a positive-self-intersection source
on the resolved boundary pair cannot be invoked directly.  A generic-line
surjectivity theorem is also insufficient, as the previous exact relative
counterexamples demonstrate.

There may be another theorem with weaker hypotheses, but no such theorem
has been identified or applied here.  No bound on [closure R:closure I],
finite solvability of that quotient, or internal normality follows from
this geometric audit.  The inherited internal normal generation remains
available if a valid independent internal-normality or quotient bound is
eventually proved.

A separate group-theoretic caveat: finite index, even prime-power index or
a solvable finite permutation quotient, is insufficient when I is not
known normal.  A transposition subgroup C2 of S3 has index 3 and normally
generates S3.  A nilpotent finite quotient containing the image, with the
inherited normal-generation property, is different: then the Frattini
argument forces equality.  An actual normality theorem would likewise
settle equality immediately, independently of a numerical index bound.
