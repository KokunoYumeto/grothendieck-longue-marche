## Legibility and witness comparison

Provisional assessment through scan 086; to be completed after all 25 pages. Clean typescript is generally easier to read than fast handwriting, pale pencil, overprinted letters, or small detached marks. The most fragile readings so far are the compressed handwritten words in scans 052–054, short exponents and deletions in the Hartshorne letter, and the function of detached graphic marks. Some letters are readable while their punctuation or correction state remains uncertain. Enlarging a crop improves inspection but does not create lost strokes.

Witness C has provided a close draft, including useful explicit doubts; the second pass has both resolved some doubts and retained others, and has found omitted graphical details and overprinted letters. Witness H has full text for only eight selected pages; its descriptions of the other pages do not count as transcriptions or omissions in the collation. On the four fully transcribed H pages reviewed so far, disagreements include wording, abbreviations, certainty, placement and graphic strokes. Counts in the changes log measure places where the final record differs, including changed uncertainty; they are not measured error rates for either model.

The later typescripts show that free, curved numeral designs can still be typewriter impressions. In scan 071, independent close reading adds an alphabetic Z candidate beneath a corrected page-reference digit and resolves an m underprint, while other overlapping bowls and cancelled joins remain uncertain. Faint detached marks are preserved only with an explicit statement that authorship or punctuation function may be unestablished.

The clipped edge of scan 074 distinguishes a genuinely missing continuation from a partly surviving but recognizable s. H marks those restorations explicitly; this is reported separately from an unmarked misreading. The visible initial stem after diso cannot decide among several lowercase letters.

The two-leaf Deligne letter has mostly readable flowing blue handwriting, but doubled or retraced stems, a crowded target index and a possible overwritten d/t remain less secure. Mathematical context was useful as a diagnostic, yet it did not decide those shapes. The source records also preserve d_2 eta beside an earlier class e and the grammatical donné exactly as read. Its retraced first Roman group and faint accent trace also remain uncertain. The record separates the writer’s question marks from editorial doubts.
