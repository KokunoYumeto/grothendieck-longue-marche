## I. Positional games: definitions, proof revisions, and unresolved scope

### G01 — The two drafts use different terminal conventions

**File/scan:** 68/002–003 and 024, initiative and complement definitions. **Kind:** reading question. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H0, H-I.1, H-II.1; **differs from** Hua's normalization.

The first draft writes `i` and `\bar C(j)=C\setminus C(j)`, while the restart writes `\alpha` and makes `\bar C(j)` a subset of the nonterminal positions; the insertion on 002 restricting initiative to `C\setminus C_0` is part of this development. Hua explicitly imposes the second convention throughout, so formulae in his first-draft discussion cannot be compared literally without restoring the original terminal exclusions. The marginal `G_0(j)\subset C_0(j)` question in 002 and the barred `C_0(j)` in 013 should be studied as remnants of these changing conventions, not silently repaired notation.

### G02 — Finite height and absence of infinite play are different conditions

**File/scan:** 68/002, lower half, and 003, upper half. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.2; **restates and supports** Hua's distinction.

The manuscript identifies `C_i` by “tte partie … est de longueur ≤ i” and `C_\infty` by a bounded set of lengths, then gives a natural-number-valued function strictly decreasing along moves. This supplies a bound depending on the starting position, whereas merely requiring each individual play to be finite supplies no such bound in an infinitely branching game. The finite-C converse and `C=C_{N-1}` on 003 are therefore a separate finite-set assertion, not permission to suppress the distinction in the later question on 013.

### G03 — Set-valued strategies make permissiveness a substantive issue

**File/scan:** 68/003, strategy definition; 025, lines 2–10. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.3, H-II.1; **restates** Hua's interpretation.

The conditions `\varnothing\ne\Sigma(x)\subset R(x)` allow several moves at a controlled position, and winning quantifies over **all** compatible maximal plays. Thus adding an allowed move can destroy victory even when that move enters a position that is individually winning; this is the relevant setting for “une plus grande” on 006. Hua calls these positional multi-strategies, but the manuscript's definition, rather than the modern name, is what supports the distinction.

### G04 — The return-to-the-start difficulty is repaired in the text

**File/scan:** 68/004–005, compared with 026–027. **Kind:** withdrawn in the same text. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.4, H-II.2; **restates and supports** Hua's account of the repair.

After changing the move at `x` to a winning successor `y`, scan 005 explicitly separates the possibility that a `\Sigma`-compatible path from `y` returns to `x`, concluding in that case “alors par le lemme on a aussi x∈G(j).” The second draft makes the same division cleaner: if `x_0` is already winning for the selected strategy there is nothing to patch, and otherwise its first return would contradict the propagation lemma. This is a repaired proof step, not a surviving gap that should be reported without the later paragraphs.

### G05 — The first universal-successor proof admits finiteness

**File/scan:** 68/004, left margin; 006, lines 13–14. **Kind:** self-flagged: a step he himself marks as unverified. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.4; **adds direct wording to and supports** Hua's criticism.

### G06 — A minimal test of the admitted step

**File/scan:** 68/006, the strategy `R(z)\cap G(j)`. **Kind:** own idea, based on Hua's diagnostic example. **Confidence:** high, because the complete transition set can be inspected. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.4; **adds a smaller witness** to the same obstruction.

Take one j-controlled position `a`, one winning terminal `t`, and `R(a)={a,t}`. Position `a` is winning by choosing `t`, but the all-winning-successors strategy permits the infinite play `a,a,a,…`. This tests failure of that proposed strategy only; the separate question whether a greatest winning strategy exists needs G08.

### G07 — “Stable … par succession” is essential to the greatest-strategy proof

**File/scan:** 68/006, lines 21–25; 008–009. **Kind:** unproved claim. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.5; **supports** Hua's formalization and **adds** the explicit inserted phrase.

The insertion says `\Gamma\subset G(j)` is “stable (dans G(j)) par succession,” before asserting a strategy winning for every point of Γ and a greatest such strategy. Hua supplies explicit successor-closure conditions, which explain why a path starting in Γ stays where the restricted policy is applied; they are a formal unpacking, not a displayed definition in the manuscript. Even with that closure, the proof in 008–009 still needs termination, and its literal `\Sigma(x_i)=R(z)\cap G(j)` is a separate variable mismatch left unchanged in the transcription.

### G08 — Two winning strategies can have no winning common upper bound

**File/scan:** 68/006 and 008–009, “il y en a une plus grande.” **Kind:** own idea, adapting Hua's counterexample. **Confidence:** high, by direct enumeration of the allowed transitions. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.5; **restates the obstruction and simplifies** its witness.

Let `a,b` be j-positions, `t` a winning terminal, and `R(a)={b,t}`, `R(b)={a,t}`. The strategies `(Σ(a),Σ(b))=({b,t},{t})` and `({t},{a,t})` both win from `a` and `b`, but any common upper bound allows `a,b,a,b,…`. Hence the unrestricted greatest-strategy assertion fails even for a finite game graph; the example adapts Hua's two-terminal example by identifying the winning terminals and is not a novelty claim.

### G09 — The cancelled well-order draft lacks the right accessibility condition

**File/scan:** 68/007, wholly cancelled draft; 027–029. **Kind:** withdrawn in the same text. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.4, H-II.2; **supports** Hua's identification of the repair.

### G11 — A loop isolates the auxiliary-game qualification

### G12 — The common-play lemma has a choice dependency

**File/scan:** 68/010, contradiction argument; 025, inserted lemma; 027, “Choisissons un bon ordre.” **Kind:** unproved claim. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.6, H-II.1, H-II.2; **restates** Hua's foundational qualifications.

### G15 — Every play can be finite while finite iteration misses the root

**File/scan:** 68/002–003 and 013. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.2, H-I.7; **restates and checks** Hua's example.

### G16 — Finite branching is only needed at universal predecessor positions

**File/scan:** 68/013, the two predecessor clauses. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.7; **restates and explains** Hua's sufficient condition.

Hua observes that finite-stage iteration suffices when every opponent position has finitely many successors. The reason is visible in the two clauses: one witness in an increasing union occurs at some finite stage, while a universal requirement can be moved to one stage only when finitely many successor-stage bounds need to be maximized. Thus finite branching at the j-controlled existential positions is unnecessary for this particular continuity argument, although that sharpening is contextual analysis rather than manuscript wording.

### G17 — Hua's transfinite indices require a consistent limit convention

**File/scan:** 68/013, compared with context only. **Kind:** reading question about the contextual interpretation. **RS:** Ø. **Context:** H-I.7; **differs from** its displayed/prose indexing.

### G19 — The path category must be separated from infinite completed plays
