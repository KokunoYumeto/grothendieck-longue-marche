# Further universal B2 reductions attempted

## Solvable common-stabilizer theorem

The finite theorem B.SV1 has been proved and checked directly on a semilinear Q8-module in characteristic three with Frobenius of order three. Together with the original Feit–Thompson theorem (Pacific J. Math. 13 (1963), Chapter I, §1, p. 775), it implies that in characteristic two any failure of all-level norm freeness already has one finite quotient and one nonidentity deck element fixing every rational norm coordinate. It does not prove that such a quotient exists for each curve.

## Raynaud nonordinarity does not yet give that obstruction

For genus two each nontrivial cyclic character has a one-dimensional coherent cohomology space. A zero Cartier transition therefore kills the entire corresponding orbit of characters under multiplication by p. The missing p-primary representation component has that character's exact kernel. However, other p-Frobenius orbits can have the same exact kernel, and a surviving component in any of them still detects the full cyclic quotient. The common-stabilizer test needs all components of that exact-kernel packet to disappear. Raynaud's nonordinary-cover theorem and Yang's prime-to-p torsion argument supply a deficient component, not this stronger simultaneous disappearance.

If multiplication by p and inversion are transitive on primitive characters, hyperelliptic symmetry would remove this distinction. In characteristic two, powers of three have this transitivity. What remains unproved is the existence of a suitable nonzero 3-primary Raynaud-theta point for every curve. Prime-to-p torsion density does not imply density, or even nonemptiness, of the 3-primary intersection. In genus two a nonzero order-three point on a theta embedding based at a Weierstrass point is already excluded by the pole-order gap, so even the first torsion level cannot simply be assumed.

We also tried enlarging the deck group so that a solvable symmetry permutes all primitive components. This would turn one vanished component into the whole packet. Constructing a cover with the required action on a prescribed theta-torsion line is a new unproved geometric step; it is not supplied by the finite representation argument.

## Good twists over an intermediate curve do not automatically give a normal obstruction

The twist bound B.TW1 gives good unpointed twists, and after changing the point B.TW2 gives a relative class-number witness. The specified point of the original curve cannot be changed. Passing to an auxiliary cover and choosing a lift of that point might vary the twist, but a resulting cyclic relative cover need not be normal over the original curve. Its Galois closure contains mixed character components. Vanishing for one cyclic subquotient does not force vanishing of the primitive exact-kernel packet at a normal quotient. Thus taking the Galois closure is not a justified transfer to B.NM4 or B.SV3.

These attempts sharpen the exact missing geometric statement but do not settle universal B2.
