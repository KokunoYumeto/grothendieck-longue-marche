## CF4: formal arc

For a rational irreducible correspondence surface Z different from the diagonal, the two-parameter polynomial jet family has zero-dimensional generic intersection with Z. On xx'(x−x')≠0, its evaluation matrix has determinant x^N(x')^N(x'−x), so both parameter values are determined. On x=x'≠0, the relevant part of Z lies in Z intersect Diag, of dimension at most one, and one evaluation condition leaves total incidence dimension at most two. On x=0,x'≠0, the fibre above the fixed first point has dimension at most one: a two-dimensional fibre would be the entire irreducible surface, contradicting dominance. The reversed and double-zero cases are analogous. Thus the claimed generic zero-dimensional intersection really covers every stratum.

Its finite coordinate algebra produces a nonzero elimination polynomial R(x) in the ideal of the substituted equations. In a longer cylinder, evaluation at(t,h) makes every substituted error have valuation at least M; the coefficients A_j(t,h) have nonnegative valuation. Choosing M larger than the order of R(t) contradicts the identity uniformly for all rank-one complete valued extensions. The countable avoidance proof and recursive version consequently work. Dominance of both projections of the final Zariski closure follows exactly from the two algebraic-independence hypotheses; no generic smoothness is being silently assumed.

## CF5: all finite-extension centralizers

The constructed K has residue Q and value Z because it contains Q(t) and lies in Q((t)); henselization is immediate and algebraic. Its completion remains Q((t)). The union K Qbar(t^(1/n)) is henselian with residue Qbar and value group Q, hence algebraically closed by the cited characteristic-zero criterion. Constants are linearly disjoint from K, and adjoining t^(1/n) has degree n by its valuation, so the semidirect product G_K=Zhat(1) semidirect G_Q has the stated splitting.

An element of G_K centralizing an open subgroup projects to the trivial centralizer in G_Q. Its remaining inertia coordinate is fixed by an open cyclotomic image; in each ell-adic coordinate some character value differs from one, and multiplication by that nonzero ell-adic integer is injective. Hence the element is zero.

If sigma in Aut(Kbar) centralizes G_E, it preserves E. Continuity and rank-one discreteness preserve its normalized valuation; henselian uniqueness extends that equality to Kbar. Its restriction to Qbar centralizes an open subgroup of G_Q and is trivial. Continuity of the rational truncations therefore gives sigma u(t)=u(sigma t). CF4 applies to this pair, showing that sigma fixes t and u; uniqueness of the valued henselization makes it fix K. The preceding internal-centralizer calculation then makes it the identity. This handles every finite E/K, not just K itself.

## CF6: log fibres and coherence

The supported scaling is an isometry that preserves every initial difference. Inside the positive ball it is one scaling by lambda, outside it is the identity; crossing the boundary only adds a strictly higher-valuation term. Consequently it preserves all cluster depths and residue configurations, not just the combinatorial tree.

The toric node uv=epsilon*pi^d is fs log regular even when d>1 and the underlying total surface is singular. The residue of epsilon is computed from the same initial differences; it is preserved over the fixed base log point. For uniqueness of a node log lift with fixed characteristic monoid and base, the conditions (u1−1)x=0,(u2−1)y=0,u1u2=1 place u1−1 simultaneously in(x) and(y), whose intersection is zero in the reduced nodal ring. At a smooth marking the horizontal parameter is a non-zero-divisor, so there is no extra unit freedom there either.

Uniqueness alone would not establish forgetting-mark compatibility. The explicit transition (b−a)/pi^m+pi^(n−m)w does: its characteristic exponents and normalized residual unit factors are unchanged. Node and marking charts are built from these linear expressions and reciprocals. This also checks generators vanishing identically on contracted components. Base change replaces pi by epsilon'*pi'^e on both sides, preserving the unit comparison; uniqueness after the explicit comparison gives Galois equivariance.

At a cusp, K(a)=K(F(a)) as actual subfields, and the local comparison fixes that field. Every principal unit has a unique n-th root congruent to one for each n, by henselianity and residue characteristic zero. These roots are compatible as n varies. Thus parameter changes do not hide a nontrivial Kummer arithmetic twist; the complete inertia/decomposition pair is compared over the same G_{K(a)}.

## CF7: the entire small etale topos, not only finite covers

After including the images of removed points in S, the full-site argument is valid. The filtered union of the finite-cover categories FEt(P1−S), with all finite stable S, is the category of finite separable K(x)-algebras. The coherent log isomorphisms give an equivalence of this entire category, with compatible restrictions. Choosing quasi-inverses to equivalences with their unit and counit gives the requisite coherent natural isomorphisms; no separate arbitrary family of unrelated outer automorphisms is being used.

A quasi-compact separated etale object is an open subset of its finite normal compactification in its generic finite separable algebra. This follows from normality and Zariski's Main Theorem for curves; normalization is finite because the base is a finite-type curve over a field. At a puncture, inertia orbits in a finite generic fibre identify the geometric points of the compactification above that puncture; the action of the quotient decomposition group gives their residue extensions. Inertia triviality identifies exactly which of these points are unramified and can belong to the etale object. The comparisons preserve these actions functorially in the covering object. Therefore the removed point sets, including unramified ones, are transported.

A morphism between etale objects is determined by its map of generic algebras, which extends uniquely to normal compactifications. Whether its image is in the designated opens is a condition on those functorially transported points. The equivalence thus preserves all morphisms. A covering family is jointly surjective exactly at the geometric generic points and the geometric closed points; both conditions are preserved. Quasi-compact separated objects form a basis of the entire small etale site, so its topos, and not just its locally constant part, is reconstructed from the transported basis.

The comparison is over the whole BG_K. It preserves the constant finite-cover subcategory FEt(K) by the base log-trait comparison. Every continuous discrete G_K-set is a union of finite orbits. Since geometric inverse-image functors preserve colimits, the identification on finite G_K-sets extends to the structural inverse-image functors on all of BG_K, supplying the relative 2-cell. The point map is F, not merely an unspecified permutation of closed supports, because the cusp quotient identifications preserve the actual fields K(a). It fixes infinity and restricts to A1. A polynomial giving its point function would fix every nonzero rational number and hence be the identity; its value at t is different. This proves nonalgebraicity even after the stated weak-normalization localization.

## CF8 and CF9

CF8's counterexample uses exactly the same field in CF5 and CF7. It therefore does not confuse absence of centralizing coefficient automorphisms with absence of all pinned topos automorphisms. The necessary direction is a separate assertion, consistent with the already opened CHW point-function result.

For CF9, if P(t,u_*)=0 then P(t,u_N) has order at least(N+1)! unless zero, but degree at most deg_t(P)+deg_U(P)*N!, so it must vanish for every sufficiently large N. Infinitely many distinct polynomial truncations cannot be roots of one nonzero polynomial over Q(t). Thus the factorial series is transcendental and the stated defect-one field follows. CF7 applies; no centralizer statement for this particular factorial series is needed.

The geometric structural 2-cell at a finitely generated source level is unique because the image of its component fundamental group in G_F is open, and G_F is slim. Relative algebraic constants are finite in a finitely generated field extension. The inverse-image functors preserve qcs separated etale schemes by exactly CHW A.8–A.9. Over a reduced noetherian source, their natural-isomorphism spaces are closed products of finite sets: maps into a separated etale target are determined by finite generic fibres. The closure of finite-stage comparison isomorphisms is genuinely needed. Its finite-coordinate approximation property allows a whole functor comparison to be chosen with the prescribed component on the slicing object. Common upper fields make every finite collection of global cocycle equations satisfiable. Thus compactness supplies coherent choices without changing the ordinary pro-category input.

For a slice, descend the chosen component on the slicing object first; then approximate a selected parent comparison by one whole comparison with that same component. Equality of the finite-presented component maps descends after another common source field. This yields an isomorphism of entire slice functors, not merely an objectwise isomorphism. Approximating on the slicing object and a finite list of slice objects also preserves the closure property and permits the chosen limit realization to be the actual slice of alpha_k. The same argument applies to finite constant base change, whose field systems are cofinally obtained by adjoining a fixed finite field extension.

A pinned pro-point has closed image at all sufficiently large target stages because its finite closed limit subscheme descends. The map then factors through the closed point subtopos. The finite-etale slice over BG_F identifies relative maps to that subtopos with actual F-embeddings of its residue field into the source field. Pro-compatibility forces one actual k-point. ### PRO3: the completion intersection

A normal projective model of B over the relative algebraic closure A' of A shows that the divisor of b vanishes: every divisorial valuation is zero on A, and its completed valuation is therefore zero on the restricted class. Normality and properness make b a constant in A'. Restriction from A' to the regular extension B is injective on each power quotient, hence on its inverse limit. ### PRO4: reconstruction over the same E

For arbitrary targets, agreement of every geometric point implies agreement of inverse images of affine opens; on those opens reducedness forces equality of coordinate functions. Slicing each qcs etale target object and reconstructing its point function gives a map h_V between etale schemes over X_E, with no extension depending on V. ## Independent audit of B.CF16–17

The boundary-residue reduction is valid: Riemann–Roch constructs a map with the chosen boundary point as sole pole and all other boundary points above zero. Finite étale corestriction has residue equal to the residue-field-degree-weighted sum; no ramification factor is inserted. Comparing values at t0 and p^n t0 isolates n times the desired residue, so the scalar-detection theorem gives rationality.

The actual-point-function argument in B.CF17 also passes an independent check. For a real-analytic F the mixed derivative of log|F-a|² is twice Re(F_zbarz/(F-a)−F_z F_barz/(F-a)²). Varying the centre through a dense set forces both coefficients to vanish. Real-analytic continuation fixes a single holomorphic or antiholomorphic orientation on the connected punctured plane. Rational exponents do not obstruct the removable-singularity argument, since centres with fibres avoiding the puncture give positive bounded distance functions there. Three such centres identify the filled value with the actual algebraic point value. Polynomial growth gives a polynomial or antipolynomial; interpolation at rational integers puts its coefficients in E. In the antipolynomial case the Galois commutator h satisfies P(hz+n)=P(z+n) for every integer n, and the coefficient of n^(deg(P)−1) forces hz=z. Thus complex conjugation centralizes G_E, contrary to the previously checked valuation argument. This proves the stated actual point function; it supplies no unproved equality of full étale inverse-image functors.
