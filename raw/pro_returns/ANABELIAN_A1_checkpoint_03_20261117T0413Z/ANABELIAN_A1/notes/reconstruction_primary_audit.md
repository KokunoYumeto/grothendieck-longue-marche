## B.CF4: the formal-arc argument

The dimension and elimination argument passes an independent check. For a non-diagonal irreducible rational surface Z dominant over both A2 factors, varying P=J+a t^N+b t^(N+1) imposes two independent linear evaluation conditions whenever xx'(x-x') is nonzero. On x=x' nonzero, graph membership forces membership in Z intersect Diag, of dimension at most one, and leaves one evaluation condition. On x=0, x' nonzero, the first point is fixed; a fibre of a dominant morphism from an irreducible surface has dimension at most one, and the remaining evaluation imposes one condition. At x=x'=0 the points are fixed. The incidence variety has dimension at most the two-dimensional parameter space, giving a dense open set of polynomial choices with zero-dimensional intersection.

That affine zero-dimensional intersection has finite coordinate algebra. A nonzero R(x) belongs to the specialized defining ideal, with polynomial coefficient witnesses. If u agrees with P through order M and v(h)=1, substitution of u(t),u(h) for P(t),P(h) changes every defining equation by valuation at least M, uniformly in every complete valued extension. Polynomial witness coefficients have nonnegative valuation. Choosing M greater than the order of R(t) yields the required contradiction. This proves the claimed cylinder exclusion uniformly, not just for one fixed Puiseux extension. Countably many rational surfaces and algebraicity equations can then be excluded by nested finite jets or by Baire's theorem.

## B.CF5: all finite-extension centralizers

The field K=Q(t,u)^h is algebraic over the indicated transcendence-degree-two subfield and immediate in Q((t)); its completion is Q((t)) because it contains Q(t). Its relative algebraic constants are Q. The union K Qbar(t^(1/n)) is algebraic and henselian, has residue Qbar and value group Q, and is algebraically closed by ADH Corollary 3.3.22. The displayed semidirect product G_K=Zhat(1) semidirect G_Q follows by acting on constants and compatible uniformizer roots. The splitting fixes the chosen roots, and the inertia action is cyclotomic.

For any open H in G_K, an element centralizing H maps to the centralizer of an open subgroup of G_Q, hence maps to 1 by Hoshi–Tsujimura and the normal-core reduction. Its inertia component is fixed by an open subgroup of G_Q. Each l-adic cyclotomic character has nontrivial image on every number field's absolute Galois group, and Z_l is torsion-free; thus every component vanishes.

If an arbitrary field automorphism sigma of Kbar centralizes G_E for finite E/K, it preserves its fixed field E. This field is henselian discretely valued and is not separably closed, since its residue field is a number field. Khassa–Khanduja therefore makes sigma continuous. For rank one, equivalent valuation topologies give the same valuation ring; the induced ordered automorphism of the discrete value group is the identity. Uniqueness of valuation prolongation extends valuation preservation to Kbar. Restriction to Qbar centralizes an open subgroup of G_Q and is trivial. Continuity then gives sigma(u(t))=u(sigma(t)). Both entries are algebraic over Q(t,u), and algebraic independence is preserved. The B.CF4 lemma forces sigma(t)=t and sigma(u)=u. The unique valued embedding property of henselization makes sigma fix K, and the earlier open-centralizer calculation kills it. Every finite E has been covered.

## B.CF6–7: all-prime log comparison and noncomplete bases

The relevant comparison is the finite Kummer-étale category, not a tempered quotient. Theorem 1.6 requires a log-regular fs scheme and invertibility of the chosen primes. Theorem 1.8 requires a noetherian henselian base, a connected fs log scheme, and properness of its underlying scheme over the base. Every DVR is noetherian; completion is not required in either theorem. Residue characteristic zero makes every integer invertible in the valuation ring and in the marked model, so the prime set may be all primes. Stable marked models have toric node charts uv=epsilon*pi^d and the divisorial boundary log structure, which satisfy log regularity. The resulting comparison therefore retains every finite étale cover of the punctured generic curve.

The same pair of theorems applies to Spec R with its vertical log structure: it is proper over itself and has generic log-trivial locus Spec K. Thus covers of the fixed log point identify with FEt(K), and comparisons over that log point are over the entire BG_K. Finite splitting extensions and Galois descent are compatible with this base identification. At a mark, the local parameter comparison fixes the actual field K(a)=K(F(a)) and its arithmetic quotient D/I. This preserves the finite residue-field extensions as well as inertia; the distinction from merely preserving an abstract cusp is essential.

The contraction-coordinate calculation and the whole étale-site argument are detailed in the companion independent audit. They pass. For fully explicit categorical coherence, choose every extended/transported cover together with its specified restriction identification. Full faithfulness of the restriction equivalences makes the groupoid of such extensions contractible: there is a unique isomorphism extending the specified identity. The comparison isomorphisms for inclusions, base changes and descent therefore satisfy their cocycle laws. The directed system defines a site functor, not just unrelated equivalences of fundamental groups.

Normal compactifications of quasi-compact separated étale objects, generic finite algebra maps, and retained unramified point sets recover a basis of the entire small étale site. Pointwise surjectivity, hence covering families, is preserved. On Kbar-valued points the induced function is the supported scaling F; the generic geometric points are not themselves parametrized by Kbar. The nonalgebraicity proof by infinitely many fixed rational units is valid and does not use reconstruction circularly.

## B.CF8–9 and the exact scope of the conclusion

B.CF8 combines the same field from B.CF5 with B.CF7 and therefore refutes sufficiency of the centralizer condition. Its necessary direction is valid in the category of relative topoi with the structural 2-cell included: a centralizing automorphism induces the corresponding relative coefficient automorphism, including a possible twist of the structural 2-cell when it already fixes E. Proposition 3.6 identifies its map on algebraic points. The regular-unit sufficient-and-necessary replacement quoted from Carlson–Haine–Wolf is exactly Theorem 4.18(5), not a new scalar-automorphism criterion.

The lacunary series in B.CF9 is transcendental by the supplied order-degree argument: if P(t,u_*)=0 then P(t,u_N), when nonzero, has order at least (N+1)! and degree at most m+e N!, impossible for large N. Infinitely many distinct u_N would otherwise be roots of a fixed polynomial in U over Q(t). The henselization has the asserted residue field and value group, and transcendence defect 2-0-1=1. B.CF7 applies directly. No centralizer assertion about this particular factorial series is needed or has been inferred.

The construction proves the DVR residue-characteristic-zero cases stated. It does not by itself decide every arbitrary-rank or nondiscrete henselian case possibly included elsewhere in the B8 outcome table.
