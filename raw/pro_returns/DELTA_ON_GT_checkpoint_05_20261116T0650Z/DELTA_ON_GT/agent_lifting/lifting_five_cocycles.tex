\subsection{Explicit five-point cocycles and their forgotten images}

\paragraph{LF.1 (Proved): the two cocycles as words in $f$.}
Use the positive braid generators $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_4$ of
$G_5=\widehat\Gamma_{0,[5]}$ and put
\[
 b=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3,\qquad
 d=\sigma_1^2\sigma_2\sigma_3.
\]
Let $\Phi=(1,f)\in\widehat{\mathrm{GT}}$ and set
\[
 F_2=f(\sigma_1^2,\sigma_2^2),\qquad
 F_3=f(\sigma_2\sigma_1^2\sigma_2,\sigma_3^2).
\]
Then the actual cocycles of which the pure $b,d$ frames are primitives
are
\begin{align*}
 c_b&=\Phi(b)b^{-1}\\
 &=\sigma_1F_2^{-1}\sigma_2F_2F_3^{-1}\sigma_3F_3
       \sigma_3^{-1}\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{-1},\\
 c_d&=\Phi(d)d^{-1}=\sigma_1c_b\sigma_1^{-1}.
\end{align*}
Equivalently, writing $\operatorname{Ad}_g(h)=ghg^{-1}$,
\[
 c_b=\operatorname{Ad}_{\sigma_1}(F_2^{-1})\,
      \operatorname{Ad}_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}(F_2F_3^{-1})\,
      \operatorname{Ad}_{b}(F_3).
\]
They lie in the pure group and satisfy
\[
 \prod_{j=0}^{3}\operatorname{Ad}_{b}^{,j}(c_b)=1,
 \qquad
 \prod_{j=0}^{2}\operatorname{Ad}_{d}^{,j}(c_d)=1.
\]
For the uniquely normalized pure frames $\alpha_b,\alpha_d$, their
primitive equations are exactly
\[
 c_b=\alpha_b\operatorname{Ad}_{b}(\alpha_b)^{-1},\qquad
 c_d=\alpha_d\operatorname{Ad}_{d}(\alpha_d)^{-1}.
\]

\emph{Proof.}
Schneps's actual braid formula is
\[
 \Phi(\sigma_i)=f(\sigma_i^2,y_i)\sigma_i
                         f(y_i,\sigma_i^2),\qquad
 y_i=\sigma_{i-1}\cdots\sigma_1^2\cdots\sigma_{i-1}
\]
\cite[Eq.~(1.3)]{LiftSchneps2006}. By the two-cycle relation the
left factor is the inverse of the right factor. Thus
$\Phi(\sigma_1)=\sigma_1$,
$\Phi(\sigma_2)=F_2^{-1}\sigma_2F_2$ and
$\Phi(\sigma_3)=F_3^{-1}\sigma_3F_3$. Multiplication gives the
displayed $c_b$. The identities $d=\sigma_1b$ and
$\Phi(\sigma_1)=\sigma_1$ give $c_d=\operatorname{Ad}_{\sigma_1}c_b$.
The alternative product follows by inserting cancelling adjacent
conjugators. These are free-word identities; the accompanying program
checks them by exact free reduction.

The two arguments of each $F_i$ are pure, so $F_i$ is pure; alternatively
the GT action fixes the permutation homomorphism, which directly shows
that $c_b,c_d$ are pure. Since $b^4=d^3=1$ and $\Phi$ is an actual
automorphism, expanding $\Phi(b)^4=(c_bb)^4$ and
$\Phi(d)^3=(c_dd)^3$ gives the respective ordered norm identities.
Finally $\Phi(t)=\alpha_t t\alpha_t^{-1}$ is equivalent to the stated
primitive equation. This calculation identifies the cocycles and uses
the separately established existence and uniqueness of the pure frames;
it does not infer that existence merely from an abstract norm identity.

\paragraph{LF.2 (Proved): the exact images of these cocycles.}
Let $q:(G_5)_5\to\widehat B$ be the map which forgets the fifth point
and then divides by the geometric Klein four-group. In the positive
normalization,
\[
 q(\sigma_1)=u,\quad q(\sigma_2)=v,\quad q(\sigma_3)=u,
 \quad q(b)=s=uvu,\quad q(d)=r^{-1},\quad r=uv.
\]
The central full twist is killed, so $(uv)^3=1$ and $sr=u^{-1}$.
Consequently
\[
 q(F_2)=f(x,y),\qquad q(F_3)=1,
 \qquad x=u^2,quad y=v^2,
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{q(c_b)=f^{-1},\qquad q(c_d)=r^{-1}fr.}
\]
In particular $q(\alpha_b)=\beta_\Phi$ and
$q(\alpha_d)=A_\Phi$ with the exact frames of R4.

\emph{Proof.}
The braid relation implies
\[
 (vu^2v)u^2=(vu)^3=1.
\]
Thus $q(F_3)=f(u^{-2},u^2)=1$: the two arguments commute, and
$f$ is in the closed commutator subgroup of the free profinite group.
The normalized quotient action of R4 has
$\Phi(s)=sf$ and $\Phi(r)=f^{-1}r$. Hence
\[
 q(c_b)=sf s^{-1}=f^{-1},\qquad
 q(c_d)=\Phi(r^{-1})r=r^{-1}fr.
\]
Here conjugation by $s$ interchanges $x,y$, so the first equality uses
the two-cycle relation. Applying $q$ to the primitive equations now
gives the pure $s$ and $r^{-1}$ conjugators. A conjugator of $r^{-1}$
also conjugates $r$, and uniqueness of the pure frames proves the two
frame identities.

The identity $c_d=\operatorname{Ad}_{\sigma_1}c_b$ becomes
\[
 r^{-1}fr=\operatorname{Ad}_{u}(f^{-1}).
\]
It already follows from $u=r^{-1}s$ and the two-cycle relation for every
gentle pair. Thus this particular relationship between the five-point
cocycles adds no condition on their modular images beyond the gentle
relations. In particular the element of R2 satisfies the projected
identity and has unequal CM parities. This is not a statement that the
element of R2 has five-point frames or satisfies the pentagon: a further
five-point constraint would be needed to force parity agreement.
