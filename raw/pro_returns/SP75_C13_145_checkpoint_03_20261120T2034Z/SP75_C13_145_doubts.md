## Legibility and witness comparison

These are handwritten mathematical working notes, with substantial revision on the page. Most running prose and uncancelled formulas can be followed at native resolution, but the confidence is uneven. Small indices, retraced exponents, compressed marginal writing and text underneath repeated hatching remain the least secure. The most difficult clusters include the cancelled computations on scans013–015 and018–020, the four tiny inverse-exponent positions on024 and six on025, the multiple versions of(24) on025, and the overlapping oblique note on026. The final factor on025 also approaches the clipped right edge. These limitations remain marked in the text; a plausible formula has not been substituted for hidden ink.

Ornate permutation-group S forms often do not distinguish a conventional Fraktur from a calligraphic rendering securely. Their preferred rendering and alternatives are kept consistent. Capital Pi, small cursive pi, doubled-left Gamma, dots, hats, bars and upper waves are treated from their local strokes: their mathematical roles alone do not authorize a uniform typographic replacement. Punctuation and the endpoints of corrections are less secure than the main letters where writing directions intersect.

Witness C usually supplies a fuller account of cancellations and insertions and is useful for locating the difficult material. It nevertheless contains unmarked errors, omitted traces, doubtful case choices and supplied punctuation. Witness H sometimes preserves a better local word or sign, but frequently omits or rearranges cancelled and marginal material, expands abbreviations, or gives a mathematically smoother expression than the ink. Agreement between them was not accepted as verification. Counts in the changes log describe documented loci, not independent error rates or a guarantee that every residual ambiguity has been settled. Source slips and incomplete formulas remain in the transcription; their mathematical implications are separated into the notes and the actual check-event record.
