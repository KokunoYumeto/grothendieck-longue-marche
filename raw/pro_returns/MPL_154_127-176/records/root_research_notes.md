## 1. What these pages are about

### P01 — A direct match to the relative-position surface in the Esquisse

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 151, opening paragraph (TeX line 2); scans 153–155 for the ensuing local constructions. **Kind:** unpursued implication / identification of the project. **Quote:** “surface cellulaire des positions relatives”, \(X^*=\operatorname{Rel}(\underline\Sigma)\).

**Context:** `Esquisse_cote119_copy1_API_transcription.tex`, §8, marker 119/50, manuscript page 48, lines 2212–2223; also §5, 119/28, manuscript page 26; `README.md`, the three proposed readings of cote 154. **Relation:** adds working definitions and explicit difficulties to a closely matching retrospective description; no specific settled-results ID claims this identification.

The strongest identification is with the Esquisse’s combinatorial surface classifying stable and unstable positions of one additional pseudoline relative to a fixed system, a description confirmed in the published text (Grothendieck, 1997, §8, p. 41). The present pages supply frames, orientations, critical and semistable positions, local moves, and a still problematic endpoint construction for that project. This is strong evidence about the subject of the notes, rather than a proof that every construction here agrees with a completed or uniquely defined object elsewhere in the archive.

### P02 — The tame-topology option is supported, with a more specific meaning

**Source:** 154.pdf, scans 151, 154–158 and 171. **Kind:** unpursued implication / answer to the classification question. **Quotes:** “positions relatives”; “balayages élémentaires”; “plans projectifs réels”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §5, 119/27–38, especially 119/28 and 119/33–36; `notes/RESULTS_STATE.md`, **MPL_150_044-084 Q9** (150/073–076; earlier research-note lead), and **WG2 B15 (§1.2)** (unchecked planning question). **Relation:** confirms the pseudoline and stratified-moduli branch of the first proposal; does not supply the tame axioms or the open-test realization theorem discussed in those entries.

Among the proposed alternatives, these scans support tame topology in the sense of a concrete programme of topological and combinatorial classification, particularly stratified relative-position spaces. The supplied Esquisse explicitly includes projective pseudoline systems among the examples motivating that programme, so the connection is textual and does not depend merely on calling the drawn curves tame. The phrase “curves in the plane” is too narrow for these pages: projective duality, local orientations, cellular surfaces and immersed configurations are central, whereas no axiomatization of a tame category or comparison of tame theories is established here.

### P03 — A specific precursor of the 1986 geometry of forms is not established

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 160, cancelled heading and date; scans 151 and 171, construction headings and conditions. **Kind:** unpursued implication / bounded historical judgment. **Quotes:** “1.1.84”; “Compléments et rectifications sur les facettes exceptionnelles”.

**Context:** `README.md`, proposed comparison with the 1986 geometry of forms; searches of `notes/RESULTS_STATE.md` found no entry establishing a specific genealogy from these pages to cote 156. **Relation:** the supplied context poses the genealogy but supplies no comparison text from cote 156.

The material shares a concern with reconstructing geometric objects from combinatorial data, but the present fifty scans do not demonstrate a specific derivation of the later *Vers une géométrie des formes*. The official catalogue distinguishes cote 154, dated 1983–1984, from the separately titled 1986 sequence in cotes 156-1–9, whose headings include networks, cuttings and figures (Université de Montpellier, n.d.). Chronological priority and broad thematic resemblance leave a precursor hypothesis possible; they do not establish it, while “neither” misses the much more precise identification in P01–P02.

### P04 — Choosing the added pseudoline as infinity supplies a useful interpretation

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 151, opening definition; scan 158, first paragraph. **Kind:** unpursued implication. **Quotes:** “\(X^*=\operatorname{Rel}(\underline\Sigma)\)”; “\(X,\check X\) plans projectifs réels duaux”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §8, 119/50, manuscript page 48, lines 2214–2218, where relative positions are also described as affinizations. **Relation:** restates a contextual interpretation and gives concrete projective and orientation data to examine; no result-register entry proves an equivalence for these particular constructions.

Regarding the added pseudoline as the line at infinity would interpret relative-position changes as changes of the affine presentation of the fixed projective arrangement. This makes the recurring cyclic orders and choices of side natural quantities to preserve when comparing the drawings. It remains necessary to specify what is fixed, how the projective or antipodal convention is handled, and which isotopies are admitted before treating this interpretation as a bijection between formally defined moduli problems.

### P05 — The elementary sweeps belong to an explicitly described dynamic programme

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 155, heading; scans 133, 156 and 176, local sequences. **Kind:** unpursued implication / literature and context comparison. **Quote:** “balayages élémentaires de positions relatives”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §8, 119/48–49, manuscript pages 46–47, on sweeping a branch past a double point and creating or removing a bigon; `notes/RESULTS_STATE.md`, **MPL_148_029-072 R29** (148/058–059, 061) and **R34** (148/055, 061, 064), both first-pass leads about the scope of different cutting and erasing operations. **Relation:** adds examples in the pseudoline setting; the operations in the other cote are not identified with these solely by their names.

The heading is a close lexical and geometric match to the Esquisse’s dynamic study of immersed curves and pseudoline systems. The diagrams provide candidate elementary moves and short circuits, but this window does not prove that the moves generate every admissible deformation or give all relations among move sequences. A comparison must retain the ambient surface, endpoint conditions, labelled background arrangement and allowed singularities; changing any of them changes the equivalence relation under study.

## 2. Frames, orientations and combinatorial counts

### M01 — Reversing a circuit changes two pieces of data

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 133, paragraphs following the circuit (TeX lines 125–139). **Kind:** unproved claim with a local explanatory model. **Quote:** “C'est en changeant à la fois la rive \(\rho\), et l'orientation \(\omega\).”

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §3, 119/12, manuscript page 10, distinguishes oriented and nonoriented cartographic operators; no supplied entry gives this precise reversal rule. **Relation:** adds a local geometric interpretation to that operator framework.

The claim concerns the inverse of the displayed circuit with its side and orientation data retained, not merely reversing the written list of relative positions. Its verification requires following the ordered triangle sweeps and checking that simultaneous reversal returns the same states in reverse order, including the initial marked point and any endpoint identification. The subsequent assertion that \(\sigma_2\) fixes the ambient local orientation while changing the orientation of \(D\) is a useful consistency condition, but should not be conflated with the two-data circuit reversal itself.

### M02 — The cartographic dictionary is a direct contextual match

**Source:** 154.pdf, scans 135–136 and 157, especially scan 157, TeX lines 9–13. **Kind:** unpursued implication / notation comparison. **Quote:** \(\rho_s=\sigma_2\sigma_1,\ \rho_f=\sigma_1\sigma_0,\ \rho_s\rho_f=\sigma\).

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §3, 119/12, manuscript page 10, gives the cartographic presentations \(\sigma_i^2=(\sigma_0\sigma_2)^2=1\) and \(\rho_s\rho_f=\sigma,\ \sigma^2=1\); no listed research return supplies the local fuseau interpretation. **Relation:** restates the operator algebra while these pages work out its geometric meaning.

The matching names and product relation are stronger evidence than a general resemblance to a flag calculus. Under the contextual involution convention, the displayed products give \(\rho_s\rho_f=\sigma_2\sigma_0\), so identifying this product with the manuscript’s \(\sigma\) is an algebraic consistency check to be made within the same action convention. It does not settle the geometric questions about quotient objects and the roles of \(\sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_1\) raised on scan 135, nor authorize changing any product order in the frozen text.

### M03 — The duality calculation records a sign distinction that matters

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 157, TeX lines 185–192; scan 158, TeX lines 23 and 119–120. **Kind:** unproved claim / unpursued implication. **Quotes:** \(\omega^*=-\alpha^*\wedge\beta^*\); “mais \(\alpha',\beta',\omega'\) non associés”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §3, 119/12, has an oriented cartographic framework; searches for duality, associated orientations and triangles find no exact contextual counterpart to this sign formula. **Relation:** adds local convention-sensitive information, without changing a result-register status.

The minus sign and the explicit nonassociation are part of the proposed duality rule, rather than discrepancies to repair by imposing a preferred orientation convention. A proof should identify the two orientation torsors, the ordered pair of tangent or transverse directions, and the point-line correspondence, then calculate how each changes under duality. The local nature of the orientations is essential on a real projective plane; replacing them by an unexplained global surface orientation would alter the problem.

### M04 — Cyclic words are a promising comparison format, not a verified identification

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 132, lower labelled diagrams and displays (TeX lines 303–329); scans 137 and 143–150, numbered sequences. **Kind:** own idea. **Quotes:** “\(\mathrm{Circ}\)”; the displayed powers of \(u\).

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §8, 119/49–50, locates the work in topological and combinatorial pseudoline classification; searches give no definition of this particular `Circ` expression or these numbered sequences. **Relation:** proposes a comparison not supplied by a settled entry.

A useful next representation would retain the cyclic order, distinguished starting point, orientation and each local exchange as separate fields, then test whether a sequence fits an allowable-sequence or wiring-diagram model. Such a test must allow simultaneous events or higher criticality rather than assuming every move is a simple adjacent transposition. **Confidence: medium**, because the visible sequences and sweeps motivate the comparison but the definitions of \(u\), the underlying set and its marked lifts are outside this window or remain uncertain.

### M05 — A conditional combinatorial explanation of the quartic count

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 142, final displays (TeX lines 236–240). **Kind:** own idea / reading-sensitive implication. **Quote:** \(\frac12\frac{n(n-1)}2(\frac{n(n-1)}2-1)-\frac{n(n-1)(\unsure{n-1|n-2})}{2}\sim\frac18n^4\).

**Context:** searches for the factors, quartic term and counting vocabulary found no matching line in the supplied context; no result-register ID is claimed to settle this count. **Relation:** a new interpretive test for these pages, not a correction of the exponent or factor.

If the second factor were \(n-2\), the identity \(\binom{\binom n2}{2}-n\binom{n-1}{2}=3\binom n4\) would count pairs of edges with disjoint endpoints in the complete graph on \(n\) labels, by subtracting the pairs sharing a vertex. Its leading term is \(n^4/8\), matching the secure asymptotic term, but the same leading term also survives the alternative \(n-1\), so the asymptotic cannot decide the ink. **Confidence: high for the conditional identity, medium for its relevance**, because the manuscript does not specify the counted objects here and the ambiguous factor remains frozen.

### M06 — A finite permutation check could expose missing geometric identifications

**Source:** 154.pdf, scans 135–140 and 157, operator diagrams and relations. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “sur les repères, dont deux consécutifs commutent”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §3, 119/12, manuscript page 10, cartographic operators and their relations; no supplied result-register entry gives the complete frame action for this pseudoline construction. **Relation:** adds a finite consistency-test method to that explicit contextual framework.

After the frame set and all four actions have been defined geometrically, one can tabulate the actions as permutations, test the stated involutions and commuting pairs, and compare the cycles with the drawn stable-position circuits. Failed tests would distinguish an incomplete geometric definition, a convention mismatch and a reading question, rather than justify silently changing a label. **Confidence: medium**, because finite permutation verification is decisive for a specified finite model, but this package does not itself give an unambiguous exhaustive frame table.

### I02 — Normalizing the four-valent graph needs its local branch pairing

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 171, conditions (a) and the following sentence, TeX lines 280–284. **Kind:** own idea / explanation of an unproved construction. **Quotes:** “d'ordre \(4\)”; “Il en résulte un \(\unsure{dépliement|déploiement}\)”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §8, 119/48–49, manuscript pages 46–47, on cellular immersions with ordinary double points and their decomposition. **Relation:** adds a local reconstruction test to that programme; no listed return proves this particular normalization.

For a graph embedded in a surface with a specified cyclic order at each four-valent vertex, pairing opposite half-edges produces the local branches of a normalized immersed curve; a boundary vertex of valence one supplies an endpoint. An abstract four-valent graph alone has three pairings at each vertex, so the embedding or an equivalent pairing datum is part of the input. **Confidence: high for this conditional construction, medium for identification with the uncertain symbol on the page**, because the text does not separately spell out the branch-pairing convention.

### I03 — A section of the side cover is global structure

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 171, circled (b), TeX line 286. **Kind:** own idea / hypothesis audit. **Quote:** “On a une \(\unsure{section}\) du \(\unsure{rev.}\) à deux feuillets”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §6, 119/40–41, manuscript pages 38–39, the principle of choices made in contractible spaces; no supplied entry establishes triviality of this side cover. **Relation:** adds a concrete possible obstruction to treating the indicated side choice as automatic or harmless.

If this is the two-sheeted cover of local sides of the normalized immersion, a section exists exactly when the side monodromy is trivial on every component, so local four-valence alone cannot supply it. A Möbius-band core illustrates the obstruction to a continuous choice of side, while a two-sided component still has two section choices. **Confidence: high for the covering-space criterion, medium for the manuscript identification**, because both “section” and “rev.” are uncertain readings and (b) may be an additional assumed datum rather than a claimed consequence of (a).

### I04 — Even valence does not determine the immersed curve structure of L

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 171, condition (c), TeX line 290. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “Tout sommet de \(L\) d'ordre \(\uline{\unsure{pair}}\)”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §8, 119/48–49, immersed curves and decomposition; **MPL_148_029-072 R29** in `notes/RESULTS_STATE.md` concerns different marked-circle cutting and erasing questions and is an unreviewed lead. **Relation:** adds a parity-versus-pairing distinction; does not identify the two sets of operations.

For a finite graph with every vertex of even degree and no unmatched boundary ends, edge pairing can decompose the graph into closed walks, but this does not by itself determine the geometric branch pairing of an immersion. At higher-valent vertices, different pairings may produce different component counts and traversal words while preserving all vertex degrees. **Confidence: high for the distinction, medium for its application**, because the reading “pair” and the relevant boundary convention must be confirmed from the surrounding definitions before using it as a theorem about \(L\).

### I05 — The face equation has an immediate conditional parity consequence

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 171, marginal condition (h), TeX lines 303–309. **Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Quote:** \(\sum_s(2\nu_s+\varepsilon_s)+\sum_a\nu_a=2n\), with the manuscript's uncertain \(\nu/\ell\) letters understood as labels.

**Context:** searches for multiplicity, parity and face independence find no existing statement of this equation in the supplied context or a result-register item settling it. **Relation:** adds a small necessary test; the letters and membership signs in the transcription remain unchanged.

If the multiplicities and \(n\) are integers, reducing the displayed equation modulo two gives \(\sum_{s\in S(K)\cap\overline F}\varepsilon_s+\sum_{a\subset\overline F}\nu_a\equiv0\pmod2\) for every face. This provides a quick obstruction to proposed numerical data, but cannot establish that the graphs, their intersection pattern or an immersion realizing them exist. **Confidence: high under the stated integrality assumption**, because it is a direct algebraic consequence independent of whether the multiplicity glyph is \(\nu\) or \(\ell\).

### I06 — Summing the face equations gives a stronger test under explicit incidence assumptions

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 171, conditions (a), (g) and (h). **Kind:** own idea. **Quotes:** “\(n\) indép. de \(F\)”; “d'ordre \(4\)”.

**Context:** searches for Euler, multiplicities and face counts find no exact contextual result; **MPL_150_044-084 N18** is only a general first-pass warning about retaining attachment data. **Relation:** adds a conditional numerical check and explicitly retains the distinction between a set of incident cells and repeated boundary incidences.

Suppose, additionally, that the surface is closed, every vertex of \(K\) meets four distinct faces, every edge meets two distinct faces, and the sums in (h) count each of those incidences once; summing (h) then gives \(nF=4\sum_s\nu_s+2\sum_s\varepsilon_s+\sum_a\nu_a\). If \(K\) is a finite cellular four-valent graph, \(E=2V\) and Euler's equation yields \(F=V+\chi(X)\), hence \(n(V+\chi(X))=4\sum_s\nu_s+2\sum_s\varepsilon_s+\sum_a\nu_a\). **Confidence: high for the conditional calculation, low that all its hypotheses are intended globally here**, since the manuscript allows a boundary and its written sums over cells in \(\overline F\) need not count repeated corners or repeated edge incidences.

## 4. Bounded comparisons with curve-move results

### L01 — Innermost-triangle lemmas offer a concrete model for a sweep proof

**Source:** 154.pdf, scans 135, 153 and 155. **Kind:** own idea / literature comparison. **Quotes:** “deux triangles fermés”; “fuseau”; “balayages élémentaires”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §8, 119/48–49, branch sweeps and bigons; no return ID supplies an innermost-triangle theorem for these fuseaux. **Relation:** a possible proof method, not a retrospective proof of the manuscript's full claims.

Hass and Scott (1994, Lemmas 1.2, 1.4 and 1.6) use innermost triangles and bigons to organize controlled curve moves in disks. Their hypotheses about embedded arcs, intersections and the disk model suggest exactly what should be checked before iterating a sweep inside a manuscript fuseau, especially if the background arrangement must remain fixed. **Confidence: medium**, because the local configurations are closely comparable but an allowed homotopy of several curves need not preserve Grothendieck's particular relative-position data.

### L02 — The permitted singularities determine what the move system can classify

**Source:** 154.pdf, scan 155, heading; scan 176, four-arrow circuit. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “balayages élémentaires de positions relatives”.

**Context:** supplied Esquisse, §8, 119/48–49; **MPL_148_029-072 R29/R34**, first-pass leads where changes in allowed operations matter. **Relation:** adds a scope test, without equating unrelated move systems.

Triangle moves, bigon changes and removal of a monogon should be distinguished when specifying the equivalence relation: Hass and Scott (1994, proof of Lemma 1.6) explicitly identify the monogon-removal exception to regular homotopy. **Confidence: high for this distinction, medium for its use here**, because the manuscript's local sweeps are visible but the full class of admissible deformations is not defined in these scans.
