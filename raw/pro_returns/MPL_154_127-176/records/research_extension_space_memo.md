## X01 — The rank-three sphere theorem concerns an order complex

**Source:** cote 154, scan 151, frozen page lines 2–9 and 76–82; scan 154, line 44. **Kind:** unpursued implication / literature comparison. **Anchor:** “surface cellulaire des positions relatives”; “cube”. **Context:** supplied Esquisse 119/50, typescript 48, PDF 51, §8, announces the relative-position “surface modulaire” (unchecked API transcription); RESULTS_STATE, Hua findings register 154-generisation-cube, conjecture/unsearched, no verdict; no matching extension-space theorem ID.

Sturmfels and Ziegler (1993, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 4.5 in the consulted author version) prove \(|\Delta\mathcal E(M)|\simeq S^2\) for every rank-three oriented matroid, where \(\mathcal E(M)\) consists of all oriented one-element extensions with the added element neither a loop nor a coloop, ordered by specialization/weak maps. This **adds a precise external comparison**, but its homotopy-sphere conclusion does not identify the order complex with Grothendieck’s cellular surface or with an embedding space. As a purely logical illustration of the distinction, attaching an interval to one point of a sphere preserves its homotopy type while destroying the surface-manifold property at that point. A comparison therefore needs an explicit map between the manuscript’s model and the extension poset, and a separate argument for any claimed surface structure.

## X02 — Fixed straight-line geometry and all realizable extensions are different

**Source:** cote 154, scan 154, lines 31–44; comparison158, opening projective-duality discussion. **Kind:** unpursued implication / reading question. **Anchor:** “générisations stbles”; “cube”; “plans projectifs réels”. **Context:** RESULTS_STATE, Hua findings register 154-generisation-cube, conjecture/unsearched, whose straight-line comparison is truncated after “2ν stable…”; supplied Esquisse 119/28, typescript 26, PDF 29, §5, lists both straight-line and pseudoline systems, and 119/50, typescript 48, PDF 51, §8, fixes a system before adding another pseudoline.

For a rank-three oriented matroid \(M\), three objects must be distinguished: extensions over one fixed linear realization \(X\), extensions realizable over any realization of \(M\), and all combinatorial extensions of \(M\). Sturmfels and Ziegler (1993, §1, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 1.1/Proposition 2.2) show that the first has spherical order complex, whereas the second is connected but can fail to be a homotopy sphere even in rank three. This **adds a qualification** to the Hua comparison: a count for perturbing a straight line against a fixed geometric arrangement does not range over arbitrary re-realizations of that arrangement, and neither count identifies the full pseudoline extension space.

### Independent checks and useful limits

**Orientation quotient.** For a nonzero sign localization \(\sigma\), changing only the added orientation gives \(-\sigma\); the two cannot be comparable in the pointwise sign order because any nonzero coordinate would have to agree with its own opposite. Thus no chain is preserved as a set by this involution, so the induced action on its order complex is free. This elementary observation does not identify the manuscript’s chosen orientation cover, its equivalence relation, or its cellular quotient; no projective-plane conclusion is being asserted for the manuscript.

**Dimension versus homotopy type.** In any successfully constructed independent-resolution model on \(k\) distinct projective contact orbits, resolving one zero at a time gives a chain of \(k+1\) extensions, hence a \(k\)-simplex in the order complex. Accordingly, a higher-dimensional local resolution poset can be compatible with a global homotopy-sphere theorem: homotopy equivalence may collapse cells, and it does not require every link to be a circle. This is an inference from the conditional local model, not an assertion that all such chains have been identified in Grothendieck’s \(X^*\).

**Higher-rank boundary.** Liu (2020) constructs a realizable uniform oriented matroid of high rank and corank three with disconnected extension space; consequently the unrestricted sphere conjecture is false. This does not contradict the rank-three result or decide the topology of the present surface, and is included only to prevent extrapolation beyond the applicable rank.
