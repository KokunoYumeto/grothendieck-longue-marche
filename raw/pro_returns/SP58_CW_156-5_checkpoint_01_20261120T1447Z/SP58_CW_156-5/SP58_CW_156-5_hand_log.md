# Hand log

Recorded by the primary reader in scan order. Scope: scans 034–049 only.

## Scan 034

The raised star is commonly a small angular k-like mark (I*, I′*, φ*) rather than a symmetric asterisk. It holds at the clear occurrences on this sheet; the crowded inline target has an additional unresolved mark. I has heavy top and bottom bars, while J has a left-curved foot: the apparent doubled stroke on the upper J′ is retracing, not evidence for a separate alphabet. Both witnesses retain ordinary capitals. The shorthand = after “Mais” consists of two horizontal strokes and stands where “même” would read naturally; C offered si and H omitted it. The long descenders of j and f can resemble a cancellation: H took the live “p bijectif” as struck. These are page-local observations to compare on subsequent sheets.

## Scan 035

The subdivision sign has a curved wedge and lower curve, distinct from the ordinary ≤ used later; C flattened both opening occurrences. Latin f, g, h overwrite φ, ψ, χ in several diagrams, but old Greek names remain live in the prose. Raised residual blots are not reliably distinguishable from live stars at all occurrences: the ambiguity is explicitly retained rather than propagated as certain notation. H omits most old letters and reverses the roles in the explanatory sentence. The reduced final -on in surjection is visible as a detached oval and n; both witnesses preferred the grammatical surjective.

## Scan 036

A small dotted curl labels the upper arrow i; it resembles a 2-shaped stroke but has no descending j. The final -t in induit and -ter in expliciter are abbreviated in height. The abbreviation for identity ends in a tall loop, hence idel/ident. remains open. The ordinary order signs inside the construction are ≤, while the cancelled opening still uses the curved subdivision sign ≼. The author uses sss consistently on this sheet.

## Scan 037

The short cross at the left of the square’s J′ may be a surplus plus or an earlier start; its interpretation remains open. The dotted ultr. abbreviation appears twice and should not be expanded to ultra-. Double underlining applies only to pré in préordonnées. The footnote-like inserted closedness clause uses a clear short raised prime at both S occurrences, even where the first is paired with T. The sign = again abbreviates même.

## Scan 038

The subscript J has a compact top bar and a left-curving low foot; it must not be silently harmonised to the later I. A bare line may end in a dark nib turn or dot and should not automatically become a hooked inclusion arrow. Two close stems in a T-like glyph may reflect retracing; Π remains a visible alternative in the pair. Heavy ultr. retracing has no independent cross-out stroke.

## Scan 039

Across this sheet the curved ≼ appears at the top, in the first vertical array and between F′ and F. Straight ≤ compares the topologies and points of preorders. The topology glyph is a looped τ, unlike a capital T. Its raised decoration in the second array is a two-stroke +. The marginal word notion is highly reduced but its not- and -tion strokes are separable. Long insertion guides pass through neighbouring writing and should not be treated as blanket deletions.

## Scan 040

Membership ∈ has a very short internal bar; without magnification it can look like ⊂. The rightmost J is undotted and has an extended foot. Straight brackets below the factors are not braces. The oblique note crosses normal baselines but does not delete them. The added closure gloss has an elbow guide, distinct from the boxed condition itself.

## Scan 041

The large underlined family T has two conspicuous loops; a script capital is used consistently, separate from lower-case τ. The a index is a small rounded oval with a right tail, not an asterisk. The repeated earlier B beneath live T retains both bowls. A small prime-like curve above C could also be a placement guide to the raised phrase; it is explicitly left unresolved.

## Scan 042

The topology letter can acquire a triangular interior when the returning lower curve is retraced. Its cap is not automatically an overbar: compare the two retraced letters in (e) with the detached bars in the diagram and bottom paragraph. The small u index is lower and rounder than full U at every visible occurrence. The repeated sss abbreviation retains three s forms. The cancelled capital M in Mais has two strong peaks, unlike the initial A of Alors later in the same paragraph.

## Scan 043

Waves over τ show two clear crests in (i), contrasting with detached straight overbars. In the large family T the cap may be fully looped (upper margin) or partly open (proof); its underline and family role recur, so it remains script T, not H’s C. Retouched Roman iv has extra uprights that do not safely establish iiv. A final e in munie is visible even though the agreement with I′ is unusual. Both shorthand equals signs in prose are literal =.

## Scan 044

The prime at the last order subscript is detached and higher than tau’s lower bowl. Membership has a separate middle arm, whereas a subset sign elsewhere has none. A broad correction can hide an older topology cap beneath a large S, so the old T/τ remains unresolved. The word pour in a raised addition is not crossed merely because its long joined baseline resembles a stroke.
