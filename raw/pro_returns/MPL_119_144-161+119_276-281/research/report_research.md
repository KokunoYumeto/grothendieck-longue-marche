### R01 — Section names and the unexplained “Institut d'Enseignement”

**Kind:** reading question. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan276.tex:12–18`, I.1–2; `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan277.tex:23`, I.4(a). **Hua:** “B. The report assigns the first two teaching cycles and non-classical courses developed with other departments to the “Ecole de Mathématique,” and third-cycle teaching plus the Department's research environment to the Laboratory. This supports Hua's architectural comparison while adding a more explicit division of responsibilities than his emphasis on “pré-licence” teaching. Yet I.4(a) also says “dans le cadre de l'Institut d'Enseignement,” a name not defined on these six pages; Hua's “Institut de Mathématique” names the research section, so the two expressions cannot safely be substituted for each other. The terminology should remain a question about these related texts, without changing the frozen reading.

### R02 — Quantified teaching duties distinguish the two senior roles

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan277.tex:15–29`, I.4; `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan278.tex:4`. **Hua:** “B. Demain : les propositions,” points 1–2, lines 1080–1096; “Les pièces de la commission d'Orsay,” lines 1184–1189. **Relation:** adds operational detail absent from Hua's account. **RESULTS_STATE:** no topical ID found.

The report specifies “trois heures hebdomadaires” for Laboratory maîtres de conférences/professors, half obligatorily a third-cycle course, with research and research supervision also obligatory. The School service is “6 heures hebdomadaires,” together with pedagogical advice to young assistants and maîtres-assistants. These provisions add quantified duties to Hua's institutional division: both groups teach, while their specified teaching-hour loads differ by a factor of two. This ratio concerns the stated teaching service alone and does not measure total workload.

### R03 — Representation and decision rules give “cogestion” a precise constitution

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan277.tex:7–13`, I.3; `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan279.tex:9`, III.3. **Hua:** “B. Demain : les propositions,” point 1, lines 1085–1087, and point 8, lines 1155–1169. **Relation:** adds exact shares and thresholds to Hua's account of representation and “cogestion.” **RESULTS_STATE:** no topical ID found.

The Assembly reserves “un tiers” to elected student representatives but “un sixième au plus” to assistants/maîtres-assistants or their elected representatives. Thus students plus that junior-teacher category occupy at most one half, not one half each; this elementary bound is an analyst's calculation from the stated shares. The report also distinguishes an ordinary absolute majority from two-thirds decisions, while its phrases “votes exprimés,” “membres votants,” and “membres de la Commission” do not all state the same denominator. Hua's “représentation massive” does not settle the resulting questions about abstentions, participation, or rounding.

### R04 — The School veto is an additional mechanism, not merely a large student bloc

**Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan277.tex:11–13`, I.3; `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan279.tex:9–11`, III.3–4. **Hua:** “B. Demain : les propositions,” point 4, lines 1116–1119, and point 8, lines 1164–1168; “Les pièces de la commission d'Orsay,” lines 1186–1188. **Relation:** restates the student veto reported by Hua and adds its threshold and a formal consequence. **RESULTS_STATE:** no topical ID found. **Own-inference confidence:** high for the finite arithmetic under the explicit all-vote assumptions; no claim about actual institutional operation.

The School senior-appointment rule makes the veto effective when “deux tiers au moins des représentants d'étudiants” support it. With exactly one third student seats, its numerical lower bound is two ninths of the Assembly before any required integer rounding, separate from the two-thirds appointment-approval rule. For example, in an eighteen-member Assembly with six student representatives, four student veto supporters could block an appointment even if all fourteen others approved; conversely, six student “no” votes alone leave twelve “yes” votes, exactly the general two-thirds threshold, unless the veto is exercised. This makes the special veto a substantive addition to ordinary weighted representation, not a consequence of the one-third student share alone.

### R05 — The junior-appointment commission has a distinct student-participation rule

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan279.tex:7`, III.2. **Hua:** “B. Demain : les propositions,” point 5, lines 1121–1131; “Les pièces de la commission d'Orsay,” lines 1185–1187. **Relation:** adds the commission's composition, promotion scope, and voting denominator. **RESULTS_STATE:** no topical ID found. **Own-inference confidence:** high for the six-seat arithmetic example below, because it uses the explicit member-based threshold.

The Assembly may delegate assistant/maître-assistant appointments and assistant promotions to a commission whose student representatives form “au moins un tiers et au plus une moitié,” with approval from at least two thirds of all commission members. This is more specific than Hua's general mention of Assembly commissions and should not be conflated with the School's explicit veto. In a six-member example, two student seats allow the other four members to reach approval alone, whereas three student seats require at least one student affirmative vote if the other three approve. The report's instruction to give the students' opinion the greatest consideration adds a qualitative direction but does not itself specify another veto threshold.

### R06 — Laboratory recruitment is a delegated mixed commission, not an automatic closed cooptation rule

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan279.tex:5,13`, III.1 and III.5; `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan280.tex:4`. **Hua:** “B. Demain : les propositions,” point 4, lines 1116–1119; “Les pièces de la commission d'Orsay,” lines 1185–1187. **Relation:** differs from the B. Demain formulation while supporting Hua's broader reference to Assembly commissions. **RESULTS_STATE:** no topical ID found.

Hua's B. Demain describes research appointments by cooptation in an assembly restricted to the Institute's members. The report instead starts with Assembly authority and activates a commission designated by that Assembly if a majority of Laboratory members requests it. All Laboratory maîtres de conférences/professors belong to that commission and form “au moins deux tiers,” but it may also include detached CNRS researchers and qualified School maîtres de conférences/professors; appointment then requires at least two thirds of commission members. The composition share and approval share are separate rules, and the text does not specify students among the optional commission members.

### R07 — The leave entitlement differs numerically from the neighbouring proposal

### R08 — Discretionary renewable detachments are separate from the periodic entitlement

**Kind:** reading question / unpursued implication. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan280.tex:8–12`, IV.1–2; `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan281.tex:5`. **Hua:** “B. In addition to the periodic semester, the report provides requested, Assembly-approved one-year detachments in both directions, with “renouvelable” clear in the Laboratory-to-School clause and marked doubtful in the frozen School-to-Laboratory clause. The latter is offered particularly as a route toward the qualification for permanent Laboratory membership, whereas staff detached to the School assume “les mêmes obligations” as its teaching staff. Hua mentions both directions of mobility but does not give these separate authorization and service conditions. The explicit teaching exemption grammatically belongs to the periodic semester, so extending it automatically to the discretionary annual option would go beyond the selected wording.

### R09 — Existing staff choose their section, subject to the teaching demand

**Kind:** unpursued implication. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan278.tex:6`, I.5. **Hua:** “B. Existing mathematician maîtres de conférences/professors in post before the October 1968 rentrée choose their permanent section, but the Laboratory vocation is respected only insofar as it does not conflict with “les impératifs de l'enseignement à assurer” during a temporary imbalance. Hua's B. Demain instead reports a minimum one-third senior staffing share for each section and a majority of new posts directed to teaching during transition. The selected report does not restate those staffing quotas; it supplies individual allocation plus an overriding teaching-demand condition. This is a difference of institutional instruments, not evidence that the earlier proposal was formally rescinded.

### R11 — Permanent transfer returns to the first-appointment procedure

**Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Source:** `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan281.tex:7`, IV.3; compare `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan278.tex:10`, II, and `pages/MPL_119_144-161+119_276-281_119_scan279.tex:9–13`, III.3–5. **Hua:** “B. The report says permanent transfer follows “les mêmes modalités qu'une première nomination,” distinguishing it from a renewable detachment. Read together with III, this suggests that a permanent move to the School again encounters the senior-appointment vote and student-veto mechanism, while a move to the Laboratory encounters its possible delegated commission. This procedural inference should remain explicit rather than silently treating transfer as an automatic consequence of temporary attachment. Whether “modalités” is intended to restate every qualification condition of II as well is a scope question that the final clause does not elaborate.
