## Legibility and reliability

All 41 retained scans were read by the primary reader as whole sheets and at the supplied half-sheet resolution, then read again against the page files; additional crops and rotations were used for crowded and upside-down material, without OCR. The typed passages are generally more legible than the rapid handwritten argument, but overtyping, handwritten completions, cropped continuations, and page-wide cancellation lines remain genuine sources of uncertainty. The cancelled matter on008,014,018–019,025,036,038–039, the fine marginal additions on021–022/028–029/035/037, the filing note on002, and the lower-edge calculations on010/041–045 are the least secure portions.

The doubt table groups repeated occurrences of the same problem rather than assigning a row to every repeated glyph. Confidence describes the reading of the visible ink, not the truth of the mathematics. Explicit source anomalies—such as the021/022 sign discrepancy, the domain symbols on036 and043–044, an unfinished equality or bracket, and the zero in014—have been left in the transcription; their mathematical assessment belongs solely in the research notes. An `\illegible{n}` count estimates unreadable words under physical obliteration; it does not substitute for a mathematical symbol. The precise scope of very large cancellations and some insertion connectors is less certain than their individual legible contents.

Pencil foliation has no independently established authorship. Reused typed leaves and their own page numbers are distinguished from the handwritten RR units and their title sheets; a visible foliation number is not reported as a statement of the length of a text. TikZ captures drawn outlines and relative arrangements approximately; it is not a facsimile of pen width or paper texture. Earlier readings and their revisions are preserved separately in `records/doubt_register_history.md` and `records/visual_audit.md`.
