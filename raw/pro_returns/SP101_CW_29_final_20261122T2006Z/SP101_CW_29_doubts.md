## Legibility and witness comparison

The typewritten main lines are substantially more legible than the corrections, superposed keystrokes and handwritten signs. The least secure areas are long x-cancelled passages, the identity and chronological order of letters sharing one cell, and the surviving fragments at clipped right edges, especially scan033. The archival number on scan080 remains a local numerical doubt; expected foliation was not used to settle it. A doubtful partial letter is distinguished from a wholly invisible completion.

The final apparatus records 130 substantive locus differences against C and 593 against H, across 631 documented decisions. Counts are witness-relative: a decision changing only certainty against C adds nothing to the C total, even when that same locus changes H substantively; a decision changing only certainty against both adds to neither. C more often preserves the blank mathematical slots and correction layers; H frequently supplies expected relations, smooths spelling or spacing, or leaves cancelled material illegible. These are observations on this selected packet, not comparable whole-archive error rates. Both witnesses also omit individual overprint layers. Agreement was not treated as independent evidence.

The blind audit reads difficult loci rather than constituting another full line-by-line reading. Its exact token differences include macro syntax, prime notation, spacing commands and crop-edge context; they must not be read as error counts. All differing boxes have a main-reader crop decision. The edition remains an AI reading of supplied scans, with its unresolved readings visible in the text and the records.
