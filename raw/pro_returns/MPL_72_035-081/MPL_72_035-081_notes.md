#### B.4 Projective and quadratic conventions over a base ring

Here \(\mathbf P_{\rm lines}(V)\) parametrizes rank-one locally direct summands, so it is \(\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{Sym}V^\vee)\). Over a general ring, the line in an \(R\)-point need not have a global generator; for \(R=\mathbf Z/N\), the Chinese remainder decomposition into local rings makes every invertible module free, and the primitive-column description is valid.

A quadratic equation over an arbitrary ring is a homogeneous degree-two polynomial section \(q\in\operatorname{Sym}^2(V^\vee)\otimes M\), with \(M\) invertible; equivalently, it is the corresponding homogeneous quadratic polynomial law. Its vanishing scheme in \(\mathbf P_{\rm lines}(V)\) is the quadric. The map \(L_0\mapsto L_0\otimes L\) gives a canonical isomorphism \(\mathbf P_{\rm lines}(V)\to\mathbf P_{\rm lines}(V\otimes L)\), and the transported equation is the section corresponding to \(q\) under

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2((V\otimes L)^\vee)\otimes(M\otimes L^2)
\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^2(V^\vee)\otimes M.
\]

These maps identify the zero schemes. Multiplication of a local equation by a unit generates the same ideal. This polynomial-section formulation and the tensor-line identification work in characteristic 2 as well; they give the precise dictionary for the ambiguities written on scan 076.

### C. The marked Farey map of the principal modular curve

#### C.1 Groups, matrices, and the dart bijection

Fix an integer \(N\ge2\) and set

\[
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z)/\{\pm I\},\qquad
H_N=\ker\bigl(\Gamma\longrightarrow
G_N:=\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z/N)/\langle-I\rangle\bigr).
\]

Thus \(H_N\) is the image of the principal congruence subgroup of \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z)\); a class belongs to it exactly when an integral lift reduces to \(I\) or \(-I\). The denominator \(\langle-I\rangle\) is the **single global sign subgroup**. Define

\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Y=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S=XY=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Their classes satisfy \(X^3=S^2=1\), and \(Y\) has order \(N\) in \(G_N\). The oriented Farey darts are indexed by \(\Gamma\), and the descended darts by \(H_N\backslash\Gamma\); the actual bijection is

\[
H_N\gamma\longmapsto\overline\gamma\in G_N.
\]

It sends right multiplication by each of \(X,Y,S\) to right multiplication by its displayed reduction. Consequently triangle boundaries, vertex rotations, and edge reversals are all preserved, not merely their counts. This is the algebraic regular-map construction used by Stanier, pp.1–3, and the Farey quotient on \(X(N)\) described by Singerman–Strudwick, pp.39–42.

For completeness, the reduction is surjective. A primitive first column \((a,c)\) over \(\mathbf Z/N\) admits \(t\) with \(a+tc\) a unit: for each prime dividing \(N\), one avoids the single forbidden residue when \(c\) is nonzero and uses the Chinese remainder theorem. Elementary row operations then reduce an arbitrary determinant-one matrix to \(\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})\); this last matrix is a product of elementary matrices because

\[
W(u)=E_{12}(u)E_{21}(-u^{-1})E_{12}(u)
=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-u^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
W(u)W(-1)=\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1}).
\]

Every elementary matrix lifts integrally, and \(Y\) and \(S=XY\) generate them. The kernel and the dart bijection above are therefore exact.

#### C.2 Surface, cells, and dessin convention

The Farey tessellation lives in \(\mathfrak H\) with ideal vertices in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf Q)\). Taking the quotient by \(H_N\), and filling its cusp punctures by those vertices, gives

\[
X(N)=H_N\backslash\bigl(\mathfrak H\cup\mathbf P^1(\mathbf Q)\bigr)
\]

with the usual cusp charts. This is the compact principal modular curve in Milne's definition, and the same quotient operation gives its Farey triangular map. Its vertices, edges, and faces are respectively the coset sets

\[
G_N/\langle Y\rangle,\qquad G_N/\langle S\rangle,
\qquad G_N/\langle X\rangle,
\]

where \(g\langle h\rangle\) denotes a left coset and the cycles on darts are right multiplication. A full flag has one additional choice of side of a dart; there are twice as many flags as darts.

The associated clean dessin is obtained by joining each triangle centre to the centres of its three edges. On the same dart set its black and white permutations are right multiplication by \(X^{-1}\) and \(S\), and their product, read in the order applied for this right action, is right multiplication by \(Y\); the inverse face permutation is therefore \(Y^{-1}\). This gives the exact conversion: dessin black vertices are Farey triangles, white vertices are Farey edges, and dessin faces surround Farey cusp vertices.

#### C.3 Counts proved from these sets

There are \(N^2\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2})\) primitive columns, by the Chinese remainder theorem. Each primitive first column has exactly \(N\) determinant-one second columns: the solutions of \(ad-bc=1\) form one translate of the cyclic free module generated by \((a,c)\). Hence

\[
|\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z/N)|
=N^3\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2}).
\]

Write \(\epsilon_N=|\langle-1\rangle|\), equal to 1 at \(N=2\) and 2 at \(N>2\). Then

\[
D=\frac{N^3}{\epsilon_N}\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2}),\quad
V=D/N,\quad E=D/2,\quad F=D/3,\quad
g=1-\frac{V-E+F}{2}.
\]

These formulas also follow directly from the three marked cycle decompositions, which the code checks independently for every listed level.

### E. The actual morphism, its kernel, and the split conic

#### E.1 Vertices: primitive columns to nilpotent vectors

A Farey vertex \(g\langle Y\rangle\) is exactly the first column \(v=(a,c)^t\) of a determinant-one matrix, modulo simultaneous sign. Indeed, the second columns of two such matrices with the same first column differ by a multiple of it, which is right multiplication by \(Y^t\). Its geometric image is

\[
M(v)=\operatorname{Ad}(g)(-E_{12})
=\begin{pmatrix}ac&-a^2\\c^2&-ac\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This formula proves independence of the second column and sign. More generally, for any invertible \(2\times2\) matrix \(g\),

\[
M(gv)=\det(g)\,gM(v)g^{-1}.
\]

To check the identity, write \(M(v)=v(c,-a)\) and use \(((gv)_2,-(gv)_1)=\det(g)(c,-a)g^{-1}\). For the determinant-one rotations it is equivariance under \(\operatorname{Ad}\); for each \(R_i\) it is equivariance under \(T_i=-\operatorname{Ad}(R_i)\). This is a map of the marked vertex actions, with the flag/dart map supplied in E.2.

Its fibres are exact. The image module of \(M(v)\) is \(Rv\), because the row \((c,-a)\) is unimodular; equality \(M(v)=M(w)\) therefore gives \(Rw=Rv\), hence \(w=uv\) for a unit \(u\). The entries of \(M(v)\) generate the unit ideal, so \(M(uv)=u^2M(v)=M(v)\) gives \(u^2=1\). Consequently the nilpotent vertex orbit is precisely

\[
\operatorname{Prim}((\mathbf Z/N)^2)/\mu_2(\mathbf Z/N),
\]

and each fibre from the Farey vertices has size \(|\mu_2(\mathbf Z/N)|/\epsilon_N\).

#### E.2 Darts and the central kernel

The actual marked dart map is

\[
G_N\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ad}(G_N),\qquad[g]\longmapsto\operatorname{Ad}(g).
\]

It intertwines right multiplication by \(X,Y,S\) with right multiplication by \(T_0T_1,T_1T_2,T_0T_2\), respectively. A matrix in its kernel commutes with \(E_{12}\) and \(E_{21}\); writing out these two commutators forces its off-diagonal entries to vanish and its diagonal entries to agree. The determinant condition then says \(g=uI\), \(u^2=1\), proving

\[
\ker\bigl(G_N\to\operatorname{Ad}(G_N)\bigr)
=\mu_2(\mathbf Z/N)/\langle-1\rangle.
\]

For odd \(N\), the full flag map is equally explicit. Set

\[
\widehat G_N=\{g\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf Z/N):\det g\in\{1,-1\}\}/\langle-I\rangle,
\qquad\kappa(g)=\det(g)\operatorname{Ad}(g).
\]

The base Farey flag is the vertex \(\infty\), edge \((\infty,0)\), and triangle \((\infty,0,1)\); its three column representatives are \(e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2\), and their images are exactly \(s_0,s_1,s_2\) from D.3. The map on every flag is \([g]\mapsto\kappa(g)\cdot(s_0\subset\overline{s_0s_1}\subset\overline{s_0s_1s_2})\), and \(\kappa(R_i)=T_i\) proves that it intertwines each of the three flag changes. The determinant separates the two cosets, so \(|\widehat G_N|=2|G_N|\); D.3 and the scalar-kernel calculation prove that every flag fibre has size \(|\mu_2(\mathbf Z/N)|/2\). The support maps send an edge to the affine span of \(M(ge_1),M(ge_2)\), and a triangle to the affine span of those two points and \(M(g(e_1+e_2))\). Writing \(A_N=\operatorname{Ad}(G_N)\), their exact factorizations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_N/\langle S\rangle&\longrightarrow
A_N/\langle\operatorname{Ad}S\rangle\longrightarrow
\{aL:a\in A_N\},\\
G_N/\langle X\rangle&\longrightarrow
A_N/\langle\operatorname{Ad}X\rangle\longrightarrow
\{aP:a\in A_N\},
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(L=\overline{s_0s_1}\), \(P=\overline{s_0s_1s_2}\), and E.4 computes the fibres of the second arrows.

No nonidentity element of the adjoint kernel lies in \(\langle X\rangle\), \(\langle Y\rangle\), or \(\langle S\rangle\): the nontrivial powers of \(X\) and \(S\) have a unit off-diagonal entry, and \(Y^t\) is scalar exactly at \(t=0\bmod N\). Thus each map between the labelled coset cell sets has the same degree as the dart map; these are the first arrows in the displayed support factorizations. Equivalently, the adjoint map is the Farey quotient by the explicitly defined larger subgroup

\[
H_N^{\rm ad}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:
\gamma\bmod N=uI\text{ for some }u^2=1\},
\qquad H_N\subset H_N^{\rm ad},
\]

and the corresponding compact surfaces are related by the actual quotient covering \(X(N)\to H_N^{\rm ad}\backslash\mathfrak H^*\). For odd \(N\), D gives its marked geometric realization. The adjoint matrix map itself is integral and the finite calculation is valid at even levels too; the basis change \(P\) used in D has determinant \(-2\), so D's manuscript-coordinate identification is stated over \(\mathbf Z[1/2]\) and its odd reductions.

#### E.3 Projectivization and a verified split B12 morphism

The formula

\[
\nu:\mathbf P^1_R\longrightarrow
Q_C=\{[x:y:z]:x^2+yz=0\}\subset\mathbf P^2_R,
\qquad[a:c]\longmapsto[ac:-a^2:c^2]
\]

defines an isomorphism over every base ring \(R\), in particular over \(\mathbf Z[1/2]\). Its inverse on \(D(y)\) is \([-y:x]\), and its inverse on \(D(z)\) is \([x:z]\); the relation \(x^2+yz=0\) proves that these maps compose to the identity and agree on their overlap. These two opens cover the conic, since \(x\) is nilpotent on the closed locus \(y=z=0\). This proof is scheme-theoretic, so it includes rings with nilpotents.

For \(R=\mathbf Z/N\), the map from Farey vertices to \(\mathbf P^1(R)\) divides primitive columns by all units; it factors through the nilpotent orbit just computed. Its total fibre has size \(|R^\times|/\epsilon_N\), and the second stage has size \(|R^\times|/|\mu_2(R)|\). The displayed formula for \(M(gv)\) proves projective equivariance under every \(g\in\operatorname{GL}_2(R)\). This gives the requested concrete relation among cusp vertices, the nilpotent orbit, and the split conic; a general descent equivalence with all rank-four Azumaya algebras is not asserted from the supplied pages.

#### E.4 Labelled map cells and their geometric supports

Write \(A_N=\operatorname{Ad}(G_N)\), and continue at odd levels so that D gives the source-shaped realization. Its labelled edges and triangles are \(A_N/\langle\operatorname{Ad}S\rangle\) and \(A_N/\langle\operatorname{Ad}X\rangle\). For the base line \(L=\overline{s_0s_1}\) and plane \(P=\overline{s_0s_1s_2}\), the maps to their actual supporting affine subspaces are

\[
a\langle\operatorname{Ad}S\rangle\longmapsto aL,
\qquad
a\langle\operatorname{Ad}X\rangle\longmapsto aP.
\]

These are the maps to \(A_N/\operatorname{Stab}(L)\) and \(A_N/\operatorname{Stab}(P)\) induced by the displayed subgroup inclusions. Their fibres can therefore be computed exactly, with the marked full flag orbit still identified with the geometric flag triples by D.3.

At \(N=15\), Chinese remainders give \(A_{15}=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\times\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf F_5)\). Over either field, the base line is \((x,y,z)=(0,t-1,t)\), and its nilpotent points satisfy \(t(t-1)=0\); a stabilizer permutes its two endpoints, and its endpointwise stabilizer is trivial by D.3. Since \(\operatorname{Ad}S\) swaps those endpoints, the local line stabilizer is \(C_2\), and \(\operatorname{Stab}_{A_{15}}(L)=C_2\times C_2\) contains the diagonal \(\langle\operatorname{Ad}S\rangle\) with index 2.

The base plane has equation \(x+y-z=-1\). Its normal functional is \(B\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(KB)\), where

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1\\1&-1/2\end{pmatrix}=\tfrac12I-X.
\]

The trace pairing is nondegenerate over both fields, so its stabilizer in \(\operatorname{Ad}(\operatorname{SL}_2)\) is the centralizer of \(X\), modulo sign. Every matrix commuting with \(X\) is \(aI+bX\), of determinant \(a^2+ab+b^2\); the determinant-one solutions over \(\mathbf F_3\) and \(\mathbf F_5\) are exactly \(\{\pm I,\pm X,\pm X^2\}\). Thus the local plane stabilizer is \(C_3\), and \(\operatorname{Stab}_{A_{15}}(P)=C_3\times C_3\) contains the diagonal \(\langle\operatorname{Ad}X\rangle\) with index 3.

Actual additional stabilizers are

\[
g_L=\begin{pmatrix}6&10\\5&6\end{pmatrix},\qquad
g_P=\begin{pmatrix}6&10\\5&1\end{pmatrix}\pmod{15}.
\]

Both have determinant 1; their reductions modulo 3 and 5 are respectively \((S,I)\) and \((X,I)\). The resulting support counts are \(720/4=180\) lines and \(720/9=80\) planes, with the exact labelled-cell fibres 2 and 3. The map's genus in F is calculated from its labelled cycle sets, while these support counts record the further explicit quotient maps.

### 2. Determinants, permutations and matching counts

Let $A_r$ have diagonal 2, upper diagonal $-B_1,\ldots,-B_{r-1}$, and lower diagonal $-1$. Expansion at its first row gives $\det A_r=L_r$, with initial determinants 1 and 2.

Take the smallest moved label $i$ in a contributing tridiagonal permutation. All earlier labels are fixed, forcing $i\mapsto i+1$; the only remaining row able to map to $i$ is $i+1$, forcing the adjacent transposition. Removing those two labels proves inductively that every contributing permutation is a product of disjoint adjacent transpositions.

Write $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r-1\}$ for the left edge labels, with no consecutive labels, and $p=|J|$. Each transposition contributes $-B_j$ and each unmatched vertex contributes 2, giving

$$
L_r=
\sum_{p=0}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}
(-1)^p2^{r-2p}
\sum_{\substack{J\subseteq[1,r-1],\ |J|=p\\
J\text{ has no consecutive labels}}}
\prod_{j\in J}B_j.
$$

The map $j_k\mapsto j_k-(k-1)$ is a bijection to increasing p-tuples in $[1,r-p]$, with inverse $y_k\mapsto y_k+(k-1)$. Therefore the exact count is $\binom{r-p}{p}$. The monomial recovers J, proving the source's noncancellation statement in the formal polynomial ring.

The complete gap coordinates are

$$
a_1=j_1-1,\quad
a_k=j_k-j_{k-1}-2\ (2\leq k\leq p),\quad
a_{p+1}=r-1-j_p,\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^{p+1}a_k=r-2p.
$$

Their inverse is $j_k=2k-1+\sum_{h=1}^{k}a_h$. The empty matching has the single gap $a_1=r$. Thus the equality version uses p+1 gaps; retaining only the first p gaps gives $\sum_{1}^{p}a_k\leq r-2p$. For r=3,p=1, the tuples (0, 1),(1, 0) give edges 1 and 2, whereas the source's equality in one gap selects only edge 2.

For $T_r(C)$, unmatched vertex i contributes $2C_i$ and edge j contributes $-C_j^2$, so

$$
\Delta_r=\sum_J(-1)^p2^{r-2p}
\prod_{i\text{ unmatched}}C_i\prod_{j\in J}C_j^2.

### Appendix A — Definitions, exact maps and proof

#### A1. The coordinate model and its source map

Fix a field $k$, an integer $n\ge1$, coefficients $\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{n-1}\in k$, and coefficients $\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_n$ with $\lambda_i=1+\mu_i\in k^\times$. Define

\[
 E=k a_{-1}\oplus k a_0\oplus\cdots\oplus k a_n,
 \qquad V=\operatorname{span}(a_0,\ldots,a_n),
 \qquad \pi(a_{-1})=1,\quad \pi(V)=0.
\]

Define the linear functional $\phi_i$ by the following complete list:

\[
 \phi_i(a_{i-1})=1,\qquad
 \phi_i(a_i)=\mu_i,\qquad
 \phi_i(a_{i+1})=\beta_i\quad(i<n),
 \qquad \phi_i(a_j)=0\text{ at all other positions}.
\]

The map to the manuscript notation is $a_j\mapsto a_j$ and $\phi_i\mapsto a_i'$ in the pairing table on 059: its rows say $\langle a_i,a'_j\rangle=1$ for $j=i+1$, $\mu_i$ for $j=i$, and $\beta_j$ for $j=i-1$. Evaluation therefore agrees entry by entry, including the unit joining $a_{-1}$ to $a_0'$ in the diagram. The operator

\[
 T_i(x)=x+\phi_i(x)a_i
\]

is the evaluation of $a_i'\otimes a_i$ in scan 074; in column-matrix notation its added matrix is $a_i\phi_i$. The equality $N_i^2=\mu_iN_i$ for $N_i=a_i\phi_i$ gives the explicit inverse

\[
 T_i^{-1}=I-\lambda_i^{-1}N_i.
\]

For $|i-j|\ge2$, both $N_iN_j$ and $N_jN_i$ vanish by the displayed pairing, so $T_iT_j=T_jT_i$. In the reflection specialization $\mu_i=-2$, the equality $T_i^2=I+(2+\mu_i)N_i=I$ verifies the involution relations directly, including characteristic two. Thus $s_i\mapsto T_i$ defines an actual representation of the group with generators $s_0,\ldots,s_n$, relations $s_i^2=1$, and $s_is_j=s_js_i$ for $|i-j|\ge2$ in that specialization.

The orbit contains

\[
 v_0=a_{-1},\qquad v_j=T_{j-1}\cdots T_0a_{-1}
      =a_{-1}+a_0+\cdots+a_{j-1}\quad(1\le j\le n+1),
\]

because $\phi_j(v_j)=1$. These $n+2$ vectors are independent: $v_0=a_{-1}$ and $v_{j+1}-v_j=a_j$ give an explicit triangular inverse between their coordinates and the $a$-basis. This is the precise nondegeneracy verified here; comparison with all clauses of the archive's earlier definition remains the task recorded in IS12.

Put $X=\mathbf P(E)$, $h_i=\mathbf P(ka_i)$, $H_i=\mathbf P(\ker\phi_i)$ and $C=\mathbf P(V)$. The first $n+1$ columns of the matrix $(\phi_i)$ form an upper-triangular matrix with diagonal $1$, so the functionals are independent and their common kernel is one-dimensional. Its projectivization is the manuscript centre $\{c\}$.

#### A2. The two flags and all invariant spaces

Use the following names for actual vector subspaces:

\[
 A_i=\operatorname{span}(a_i,\ldots,a_n)\quad(0\le i\le n),
 \qquad A_{n+1}=0,
\]
\[
 K_i=\bigcap_{0\le j\le i}\ker\phi_j\quad(0\le i\le n),
 \qquad K_{-1}=E.
\]

Their dimensions are $\dim A_i=n+1-i$ and $\dim K_i=n+1-i$ for $0\le i\le n$, with $A_0=V$ and $K_n=kc$. Under the notation in the large diagram on 080,

| Actual subspace | Diagram label | Projective dimension |
|---|---|---:|
| $\mathbf P(K_i)$, $-1\le i\le n$ | $Z_i'$ | $n-i$ |
| $\mathbf P(A_{i+1})$, $-1\le i\le n$ | $Z_i^{\sharp\mid!}$ | $n-i-1$ |

Here $\mathbf P(0)=\varnothing$ has dimension $-1$. This explicit table follows the diagram, and does not replace the different preliminary labels in the frozen text.

For a vector subspace $W$, the formula $T_i(w)-w=\phi_i(w)a_i$ proves the exact criterion

\[
 T_iW=W\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 W\subset\ker\phi_i\ \text{ or }\ a_i\in W.
\]

Indeed, a nonzero value of $\phi_i$ on $W$ gives $a_i\in W$ by division in $k$, and either alternative makes every added term belong to $W$; invertibility of $T_i$ upgrades the resulting containment to equality. This is an equality of actual invariant-subspace conditions, not a dimension-only comparison.

An invariant $W$ containing none of the $a_i$ lies in $K_n$, giving $0$ or $K_n$. For every other invariant $W$, let $r$ be the least index with $a_r\in W$; the equality $T_{j+1}(a_j)-a_j=a_{j+1}$ propagates membership to all $a_r,\ldots,a_n$, while the criterion puts $W$ in $K_{r-1}$. Thus

\[
 A_r\subset W\subset K_{r-1}.
\]

The two bounding spaces have dimensions differing by one. Their only intervening vector subspaces are the two bounds, and, for $r\ge1$, their containment is equivalent to $\beta_{r-1}=0$: the only possibly nonzero evaluation on the tail is $\phi_{r-1}(a_r)=\beta_{r-1}$. For $r=0$ the bounds are $V$ and $E$.

Conversely, the four spaces $0,K_n,V,E$ are invariant. At $\beta_i=0$, every generator preserves $A_{i+1}$ and $K_i$: generators on the appropriate side add their own vector inside the space, and the other generators vanish there by the pairing table. This proves the complete list

\[
 \{0,E,K_n,V\}\ \cup\
 \{A_{i+1},K_i:0\le i<n,\ \beta_i=0\}.
\]

The map $W\mapsto\mathbf P(W)$ and its inverse, the linear cone over a projective linear subspace together with $0$ for the empty set, identify this list with the projective subspaces in the manuscript theorem.

#### A3. Coincidences and the exact total

Both $A$ and $K$ are strict flags. Across the two flags, equal dimensions force the pair $A_i,K_i$ for a unique $0\le i\le n$, and equal dimensions turn equality into the containment $A_i\subset K_i$. Evaluating $\phi_j$ on the displayed basis of $A_i$ gives

\[
 A_i=K_i
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mu_i=0,\qquad
 \beta_{i-1}=0\ (i>0),\qquad
 \beta_i=0\ (i<n).
\]

These are exactly the three local vanishings, with endpoint omissions, at the top of scan 081. Each such equality identifies two members of the invariant list, and every duplicate has this form. Define

\[
 z=\#\{i:0\le i<n,\ \beta_i=0\},
\]
\[
 c_{\mathrm{eq}}=
 \#\{i:0\le i\le n,\ \mu_i=0,
       \ (i=0\text{ or }\beta_{i-1}=0),
       \ (i=n\text{ or }\beta_i=0)\}.
\]

The notation $c_{\mathrm{eq}}$ denotes a count and keeps it separate from the centre point $c$. The resulting count is

\[
 \boxed{N=4+2z-c_{\mathrm{eq}}.}
\]

The four base spaces are distinct for $n\ge1$, as their vector dimensions are $0,1,n+1,n+2$. The maximum $2n+4$ requires $z=n$ and $c_{\mathrm{eq}}=0$; at $z=n$ the second condition is exactly $\mu_i\ne0$ for all $i$. This proves the manuscript's maximum criterion within the model.

For the minimum, a block of $\ell$ consecutive zero $\beta$ edges contributes at most $\ell-1$ interior coincidences plus one at each chain endpoint it meets. A proper block therefore contributes at most $\ell$ coincidences; the block comprising the entire chain contributes at most $n+1$. With at least one zero edge and $n\ge2$, these bounds give $c_{\mathrm{eq}}<2z$, hence $N>4$. For $n=1$ and $\beta_0=0$, the formula becomes $N=6-[\mu_0=0]-[\mu_1=0]$, giving the extra four-space case stated in IS08.

In the characteristic-two reflection specialization, all $\mu_i=0$. Writing $b$ for the number of nonempty maximal zero-edge blocks and $e\in\{0,1,2\}$ for the number of chain endpoints incident to a zero edge gives $c_{\mathrm{eq}}=z-b+e$ and

\[
 N=4+z+b-e.
\]

For $b>0$, at least $b+1-e$ nonzero edges separate the blocks and occupy their unfilled endpoints, so $n-z\ge b+1-e$ and $N\le n+3$. For $b=0$, $N=4\le n+3$ directly. At $\beta_0=\cdots=\beta_{n-1}=0$, the equalities $A_i=K_i$ hold for every $i$, and the list is the full flag with exactly $n+3$ members, attaining this bound.

#### A4. The finite-centre localization and the replacement (d)

Normalize a common-kernel vector $q$ by $q_n=1$ and use the recurrence

\[
 q_{n+1}=0,\qquad
 q_{i-1}=-\mu_iq_i-\beta_iq_{i+1}\quad(0\le i\le n),
\]

with the final $\beta_nq_{n+1}$ term omitted. This constructs $q$ explicitly and proves $\phi_i(q)=0$. For the restricted pairing matrix $B=(\phi_i(a_j))_{0\le i,j\le n}$, determinant expansion gives

\[
 d:=q_{-1}=(-1)^{n+1}\det B.
\]

In the reflection specialization $\mu_i=-2$, this is the positive-diagonal continuant $\rho$ used in the earlier calculations. More generally, the zero locus of $d$ is exactly the locus where the centre lies in $C$, without a choice of sign convention for the symbol $\rho$.

The formulas also work over the polynomial coefficient ring with the $\lambda_i$ inverted. Over its localization at $d$, put $c_0=q/d$ and define

\[
 E\longrightarrow Rc_0\oplus V,
 \qquad x\longmapsto
 \bigl(\pi(x)c_0,\ x-\pi(x)c_0\bigr).
\]

Addition is the inverse. The equalities $T_i(c_0)=c_0$ and $\pi T_i=\pi$ show directly that this map intertwines every generator. The restriction of $\phi_i$ to $V$ is exactly the $a_i^\vee$ expression on 073; this supplies the explicit morphism from the full coordinates on 059 to the centred coordinates on 073–074.

On the locus $d\ne0$, each $K_i$ contains $q$ outside $V$, whereas every $A_j$ lies in $V$, so there is no cross-flag equality or containment $K_i\subset A_j$. Every proper nonzero invariant subspace inside $V$ is consequently one of the distinct $A_{i+1}$ with $\beta_i=0$. The replacement (d)'s exact bijection is

\[
 \{i:0\le i<n,\ \beta_i=0\}
 \longrightarrow
 \{\text{proper nonempty invariant projective subspaces of }C\},
 \qquad i\longmapsto\mathbf P(A_{i+1}).
\]

Its inverse sends a subspace to the least index of a basis vector it contains, minus one, as proved in A2. At $d=0$, the common fixed line is contained in $V$ and is proper for $n\ge1$; together with the finite-centre list, this also verifies the crossed-out irreducibility criterion in this model, while retaining its cancelled documentary status.

### Explicit algebra map

The integral polynomial \(q_{\mathbf Z}=x^2+yz\) base-changes along \(\mathbf Z\to\mathbf Z[1/2]\) to the form in the centred reflection example. Its base changes along \(\mathbf Z\to R\) are the all-ring objects computed below. The identification with the manuscript-shaped centred coordinates uses the bridge appendix's change of basis of determinant \(-2\), hence is made over \(\mathbf Z[1/2]\).

Let \(e_x,e_y,e_z\) be the Clifford generators with
\[
e_x^2=1,\qquad e_y^2=e_z^2=0,\qquad
e_xe_y+e_ye_x=e_xe_z+e_ze_x=0,\qquad
e_ye_z+e_ze_y=1.
\]
These are obtained by expanding \(v^2=q(v)\) with the convention \(q=x^2+yz\). Set
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Y=E_{12},\qquad Z=E_{21}.
\]
The assignment \(e_x\mapsto D,\ e_y\mapsto Y,\ e_z\mapsto Z\) respects every relation: in particular
\[
(xD+yY+zZ)^2=(x^2+yz)I.
\]
This identity is integral, including after reduction modulo \(2\).

The relations move \(e_x\) before \(e_y\) before \(e_z\), eliminate repeated generators, and express every even word in the span of
\[
1,\qquad i=e_xe_y,\qquad j=e_xe_z,\qquad k=e_ye_z.
\]
Their four images are \(I,E_{12},-E_{21},E_{11}\). They form an \(R\)-basis of \(M_2(R)\): the inverse coefficient map sends
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
\longmapsto d\cdot1+b\,i-c\,j+(a-d)k.
\]
The spanning statement gives surjectivity from these four generators to the even algebra, and the displayed independent images show that their only linear relation has all coefficients zero. Thus the induced homomorphism
\[
C^0(x^2+yz)\ \longrightarrow\ M_2(R)
\]
is an algebra isomorphism, with the written inverse.

The algebra \(M_2(R)\) is free of rank \(4\), its centre consists exactly of scalar matrices (commutation with \(E_{11},E_{12},E_{21}\) gives this entrywise), and its canonical action map is
\[
M_2(R)\otimes_R M_2(R)^{\mathrm{op}}
\longrightarrow \operatorname{End}_R(M_2(R)),\qquad
A\otimes B\longmapsto(T\mapsto ATB).
\]
For the matrix units this sends
\[
E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\ :\ E_{mn}\longmapsto
\delta_{jm}\delta_{nk}E_{il}.
\]
These are precisely the sixteen elementary endomorphisms, each occurring once, so the action map is an isomorphism. This proves the split rank-four Azumaya assertion by the defining map.

### The conic and the right-ideal scheme

The morphism
\[
\nu:\mathbf P^1_R\longrightarrow Q,\qquad
[a:c]\longmapsto[ac:-a^2:c^2]
\]
has inverse \([-y:x]\) on \(D(y)\) and \([x:z]\) on \(D(z)\). The equation \(x^2+yz=0\) proves agreement on the overlap and both inverse identities; these opens cover \(Q\), since a homogeneous prime containing \(y,z\) would also contain \(x\). Hence this is an isomorphism of schemes over \(R\).

For a line subbundle \(L\subset\mathcal O_T^2\) on any \(R\)-scheme \(T\), define
\[
I_L=\{A\in M_2(\mathcal O_T):\operatorname{im}A\subset L\}
=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal O_T^2,L).
\]
It is a rank-two locally direct-summand right ideal: right multiplication \(A\mapsto AB=A\circ B\) precomposes with an endomorphism of the domain and preserves the image, and a local basis for \(L\) gives the two columns independently. Conversely, for such a right ideal \(I\), define \(M\) to consist of the columns \(v\) for which \((v,0)\in I\). Right multiplication by the four matrix units extracts each column and moves it to either position, proving \(I=M\oplus M\) and \(M_2(\mathcal O_T)/I=(\mathcal O_T^2/M)^{\oplus2}\). Thus \(M\) and \(\mathcal O_T^2/M\) are direct summands of locally projective rank-two modules; their fibre ranks are one, because twice each fibre rank is two as an equality of integers. Consequently \(M\) is a line subbundle.

The two assignments \(L\mapsto I_L\) and \(I\mapsto M\) are inverse and commute with arbitrary base change: the first is \(L\otimes(\mathcal O_T^2)^\vee\), and the second is the image of the idempotent \(I\to I\), \(A\mapsto AE_{11}\), identified with its first column. The inclusions are locally split, so these pullbacks require no flatness assumption. Together with \(\nu\), they supply the promised scheme isomorphisms
\[
Q\ \simeq\ \mathbf P^1_R\
\simeq\ \{\text{rank-two locally direct-summand right ideals of }M_2(R)\}.
\]
Under \(g\in\operatorname{GL}_2(R)\), conjugation sends \(I_L\) to \(I_{gL}\), while the trace-zero matrix map of the bridge appendix satisfies \(M(gv)=\det(g)\,gM(v)g^{-1}\). The projective scalar factor disappears through the explicit quotient to \(Q\), proving compatibility with the projective conic action used there.
