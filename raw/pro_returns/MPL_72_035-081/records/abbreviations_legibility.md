## Legibility and limits of confidence

Every whole scan and both supplied halves were read directly, and every page received a second comparison against its image before the transcription was frozen. Enlargements and rotations were used for small handwriting and sideways typed sheets; no OCR was used. The pages range from sparse cover sheets and relatively clear typed notices to densely revised calculations with several layers of ink.

The least secure readings are the opening correction on 035; the isolated script alphabets on 037–038; crossed prose and small bounds in 046–055; overinked determinant indices and centre symbols on 049–054; several dual-map coefficients and primes on 057 and 063–071; the diagonal margin on 073; the compressed final paragraph on 075; and the raised Z mark and parameter parenthesis on 080–081. Scan 070 has cropped or torn word endings, preserved as tentative fragments. Typed proper names were read from the supplied images, with local doubt retained wherever the print or scan did not decide the letters.

The doubts table's confidence labels describe confidence in the marked reading, with a conservative page-level default where several doubtful marks share the same handwriting conditions. They do not certify the mathematical truth of the text. A mathematically natural alternative has not displaced a less natural visible reading. The page observations below the doubts table retain the reasons for the local uncertainties; cancellations, incomplete formulas, incomplete sentences, and apparent contradictions remain in the transcription.
