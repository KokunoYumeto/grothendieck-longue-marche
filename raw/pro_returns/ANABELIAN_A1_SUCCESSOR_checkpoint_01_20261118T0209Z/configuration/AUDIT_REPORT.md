## Statement-by-statement mathematical audit

**A.CF1 (A.2).** Sound. Iterated Frattini subgroups are characteristic, open and cofinal in a finitely generated pro-r group. The finite automorphism kernel is an r-group by its free action on ordered generating lifts. The Magnus argument is sound: nilpotence of the augmentation ideal follows by induction through a central subgroup of order r, so substitutions into every finite r-group algebra recover every quotient. The intersection of central preimages in finite quotients is the trivial centre of the free pro-r group. The blind proof uses this last compactness argument directly; the inherited proof uses the full profinite automorphism group. Both detect every finite quotient, and neither needs any condition on the discrete quotient B0.

**A.CF2 (A.3).** Sound for complex curves and every prime set Sigma with r in Sigma. The deck action on C' is free, so its graphs are disjoint. The cover U_A is connected, normal of group A×A, and its fibre subgroup is exactly Lambda intersect H. The peripheral module is F_r[A]/F_r(sum e_a), not an augmentation summand obtained by dividing by |A|. Its single relation is nonzero even when r divides |A|. Monodromy fixes both the peripheral submodule and compact quotient; an arbitrary vector-space splitting gives an elementary abelian r-group of shears. The finite core is finite because J is normal in H. Its H-quotient is an r-group by the product embedding. Joint finite images justify passing the kernel containment from the dense discrete fibre to its completion. Right exactness uses the universal property and does not assert that completion preserves arbitrary kernels.

**A.CF3 (A.4).** Sound for Sigma excluding p. The opened SGA 1 XIII 2.9 is a cohomological properness/local-constancy theorem for a smooth proper pair with relative normal-crossings boundary and finite invertible-order coefficients; it is not itself worded as a configuration theorem. XIII 2.10 constructs specialization through strict localization and a path, with inner ambiguity. XIII 2.11 transports inertia. Applied to constant finite groups these statements give the torsor comparison required in the proof. III 7.3 and 7.4 provide smooth proper/projective lifts; formal smoothness lifts the marked point. The diagonal and point fibre are transverse with multiplicity one. The countable-field descent and subsequent algebraically closed base change are valid. The arithmetic comparison is an isomorphism on geometric kernels and identity on the full G_k, hence an isomorphism of extensions.

**A.CF4 (A.5).** Sound, with its quotient-of-boundary argument expanded in A.DC4. SGA XIII 5.2 states extension over Kummer charts, not root stacks; the stack assertion is derived by unique extension and effective étale descent. A finite stabilizer of a root gerbe is not presumed injective. The inverse-limit right exact sequence remains right exact after the geometrically pro-Sigma quotient, by the same finite-quotient argument. Actual inertia is shown to survive by the fibre injection; A.DC3 now gives a wholly algebraic proof of its infinite r-adic order. The second conjugation by a geometric element of the Q-fibre cancels the inner automorphism on the first proper coordinate. No lifting of a point section to a global configuration section is used.

**A.CF5 (A.6).** The group law and orders are correct: 3^9·25=492075, fibre 3^5·5=1215, quotient A of order405, total quotient A×A. The coefficient 2 is the inverse of2 in F3 and the commutator is the alternating form J. The central element chosen for sigma has square1 in the central F3 coordinate; the surface relation is the product of two inverse commutators, also1. The full braid presentation maps to G semidirect C2; its pure subgroup maps to the odd group G. This distinction is essential. Bellingeri Theorem1.2 was opened and its commutator convention checked. Detailed finite computations are owned by the finite-checks agent and are recorded there; this audit does not relabel their work as its own computation. The old positive quotient is nilpotent and therefore is not the requested S3 test; the new order75 nonnilpotent quotient and explicit configuration domination are in the finite-checks deliverables.

**A.CF6 (A.7).** The mathematical obstruction is sound. The subgroup generated by the fibre and C^3,D^3 preserves K and N; invariance under all pure braids is unnecessary. If a finite odd total quotient dominated F4/N, the action on K/N would factor through an odd group, contradicting the exhibited nonzero eigenvector of eigenvalue−1 modulo5. The Schreier rank82 and order27·5^82 are correct. The inherited program and an independently written verifier were rerun by the finite-checks agent. Bellingeri's exact relations, not a guessed braid action, support the conjugation formulas.

**A.CF7 (A.8).** Sound. A subgroup containing a normal subgroup of finite Sigma-index inherits its full pro-Sigma topology by finite cores. The nonidentity graph meridians generate a fibre-normal subgroup stable under the total group because the punctures are individually labelled. Filling those graphs kills exactly their total normal closure by van Kampen; total normality identifies this with the fibre normal closure. Right exactness on the two fillings gives the asserted passage to a proper cover. No new left-exactness assumption is inserted.

**A.CF8 (A.9).** Sound after explicit check of the characteristic-two issue. The difference cycles u_s−u_zs are independent by pairing with individual handle duals; the pairing restricted to the difference subspace can be degenerate in characteristic2 and is not used. The r-th point-push lifts have r-fold short twists and one-fold long twists. The lifted pair-of-pants relation gives the long class as the sum over the central orbit; its intersection with every difference cycle vanishes. Thus the restricted action is the transvection of coefficient±r. Since r is invertible modulo ell, powers produce every coefficient and primitive simple curves produce all directions. Standard elimination with symplectic transvections gives the full symplectic group in every characteristic, including2. The normalizer argument on the finite compact homology quotient turns this finite monodromy group into a quotient of a finite Sigma-group if full fibre injectivity were assumed.

**A.CF9 (A.10).** Sound with the stated scope. For complex curves the empty set and singletons follow directly; the total set is supported by the opened Mochizuki–Tamagawa Proposition2.2. For a proper set with two primes r,ell, an omitted prime q divides ell^(2d)−1, with d its multiplicative order moduloq (d=1 forq=2). Cyclic proper r-power covers make genus large enough for this factor to occur in Sp. A.CF7 and A.CF8 then give a contradiction. In characteristicp this classification is transferred only for Sigma excludingp. The statement does not claim a full-fibre classification for prime sets containingp. The relative single-cusp-prime repair has broader scope and survives p in Sigma.

**A.CF10 (A.11).** Sound with the nonempty-section convention made explicit. If m=0 is allowed, the total space is the product and the conclusion is immediate; the free-fibre argument is for m≥1. The common base cover trivializes the finitely many pullback A-torsors, has group in a finite product of A, and hence is a Sigma-cover. Labelled punctures then give shears. In positive characteristic the inherited proof assumes a simultaneous lift for a general family; A.DC1 removes this lifting assumption for constant proper compactifications with labelled sections over a proper base of positive genus. Arbitrary morphisms of proper curves are not claimed to lift.

**A.CF11 (A.12).** The finite obstruction is sound; the finite-checks agent independently verified the rank41, order8·3^41, symplectic group order51840, and an acting element of order5. As in A.CF6, a finite {2,3}-total quotient dominating the fibre quotient would force the finite preserving action to have only primes2 and3. This contradicts order5. The general A.CF8 proof does not depend on this test.

**A.CF12 (A.13).** The assertion is true, including p in Sigma, but the inherited phrase saying that all remaining kernel identifications are unchanged omits a necessary justification in characteristicp. An unrestricted étale homotopy sequence for an open morphism is not automatic. The repair is A.DC2: every finite E_cr cover is tame along the diagonal because its inertia maps into the retained r-group kernel; its normalization is smooth proper over the second curve by local Kummer charts, and the finite étale Stein factor proves middle/right exactness. A.DC1 independently supplies the finite-r fibre detection through abelian localization and central nonabelian torsor induction. A.DC4 reconstructs the common boundary and arithmetic transport. Thus the statement remains Proved with an expanded proof, not on the strength of the omitted assertion.

## Exact dependency changes

The original Stix route is a valid algebraic argument without constructing a characteristic-zero lift, but Stix §2.3 invokes SGA XIII4.3–4.4, and XIII4.4 explicitly invokes XIII2.9. Therefore that route alone does not meet G1(ii)'s literal exclusion of XIII2.9. The replacement proof avoids this transitive citation by proving the required finite-r torsor extension directly. Its algebraic inputs are Milne12.7,14.1–14.2,14.9–14.10,16.7,17.10; SGA X1.2 for Stein factors; and SGA XIII5.2 for tame Kummer charts.

For the direct route, A.13 uses A.DC1 and A.DC2 plus the finite-core algebra written out in its proof for the geometric clauses, and A.DC4 for its inherited boundary/arithmetic clause. A.DC4 independently repeats the finite-core argument using A.DC1 and A.DC2, then uses A.DC3 for actual inertia; it does not depend on A.13. Thus the statements remain intact and the dependency graph is acyclic. The original A.5 may be used through A.DC4 with no A.4 dependency. The characteristic-zero proof A.2–A.3 and its specialization A.4 remain independently verified results. The p-primary-inclusive arithmetic transport is valid for every Sigma containing at least one r different from p; no claim is made here for Sigma contained in {p}.
