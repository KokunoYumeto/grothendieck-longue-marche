## Principal results and corrections

### R2: the centralizer theorem and the missing orbit argument

Statements A1–A7 prove the number-field result, and AE1 carries it to finitely generated fields over Q. For a hyperbolic curve and an actual continuous section, the centralizer of the restricted cyclotomic-kernel image is nontrivial exactly for a cuspidal section. In that event it is the whole inertia group at the unique fixed lifted cusp. The proper case has trivial centralizer.

The direct proof repairs the original fourth step at its exact failure. Distinct cusps need not remain distinct in a covering whose subgroup contains the entire centralizer: they can lie in the same centralizer orbit. A5 first proves that all relevant cusps cannot form one orbit, using the finite derangement count for a union of conjugates of a proper subgroup. It then separates two distinct orbits at a finite level. A compact etale double cover handles the two-cusp case while retaining the centralizer. In the resulting boundary abelianization, the two products zz′ and z²z′ give a contradiction with only three cusps. The argument works over Zhat without treating that ring as an integral domain.

The second proof uses the opened Stix uniqueness theorem. Its hypothesis is verified over every finite extension of the cyclotomic field by the explicit divisible-point argument, followed by the Jacobian Kummer comparison. The arithmetic proof begins with Ribet's actual number-field theorem, descends finite cyclotomic extensions to number fields, and writes out the restriction cocycle. It does not reinterpret the phrase “number field” in the 2008 Stix lemma as an infinite algebraic field.

### R3 and the function-field/model comparison

B1 checks the independent Kummer/valuation proof, including the equality of stabilizers, the exponent in the Kummer relation, the choice of an unramified auxiliary prime, the cyclotomic value group, and the final passage through every finite extension. B2 gives a stronger criterion from the opened Jarden and Haran–Jarden results: the centralizer of every nontrivial closed normal subgroup of a Hilbertian absolute Galois group is trivial. It covers every proper Galois extension M/Q in the requested question. B3 verifies the named examples and the finite certificates for their properness. B4–B6 supply the finitely generated-field and inverse-limit passages.

B7 proves the birational criterion by constructing a valuation from compatible lifted cusps at all open models. The local inertia maps are isomorphisms onto the cusp inertia groups at every model deleting the place; this verifies both inclusions in the centralizer equality. B8 then refutes the literal zero formulas (32) and (33): for a decomposition section both groups written with L are I_v. The two formulas remain equivalent. Under the quotient E_L → E_U, inertia at a point of U is killed, and both model-group centralizers are trivial. The example L=Q(t), x=0, U=P1 minus {1,2,infinity} makes this comparison explicit. Both letters read L in scan 127; the mathematical correction is not silently put into the transcription.

The packet reconstruction B10–B11 uses the subset of section classes with nonzero centralizer, already characterized by B7. Its fibres are the splitting packets. A colimit of packet labels recovers the geometric points and their Galois action. Killing the labelled inertia at precisely the points retained in an open model gives its fundamental-group quotient. Tamagawa's quoted Isom theorem is applied to actual isomorphisms of reconstructed affine hyperbolic models, with common refinements proving compatibility. No exhaustion of arbitrary birational sections is needed for these reconstruction statements.

### Explicit cyclotomic coverings and the failure of blanket surjectivity

D1 determines exactly which pairs of point sections agree after geometric abelianization. In positive compact genus, equality forces equality of points. In genus zero, distinct points can agree only when the boundary splits over the cyclotomic field and all the specified cross-ratios are roots of unity; the converse is proved as well. D2 and D0b use positive-genus finite covers and the divisible-point theorem to prove full point injectivity and at most one fixed pro-point in the entire completed cover.

D3 proves that the requested two-step covers w^n=z, u^m=1−ζw give isomorphic Galois fibres at ζ6 and its inverse. The program checks 7,800 branch cases, and the root-of-unity rescaling proves the unbounded assertion. D4 separates the points by a different connected etale cover of degree 81. On the Fermat cubic, exact arithmetic over Q[ζ18] shows that all nine differences from the chosen point to the inverse fibre have order 9. A Frobenius/complex-conjugation calculation proves that cyclotomic rational 3-primary torsion on Y²=X³−432 has exponent at most 9. Pullback by multiplication by 3 and translation consequently gives a rational point in one fibre and none in the other.

D5 refutes Conjecture A over the cyclotomic field with the smooth proper genus-four normalization of t^6+2y^3+4=0. It maps to the diagonal cubic X³+2Y³+4Z³=0. The three term valuations have distinct residues modulo 3 at 2 in every finite cyclotomic field, so no rational point exists. The paper separately proves section existence: Brauer vanishing over every finite cyclotomic extension gives H²-vanishing for finite modules, then weak solutions of finite embedding problems and compatible splittings of the arithmetic extension. This is a continuous section construction by finite lifting, not an invocation of a section conjecture and not a computer claim about an infinite Galois group. The counterexample persists over pure transcendental extensions.

AE2 gives the primewise correction to H1: a nonzero centralizer is the subgroup of its unique inertia group fixed by the cyclotomic image.
