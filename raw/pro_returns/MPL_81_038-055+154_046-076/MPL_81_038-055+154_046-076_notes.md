**Source:** 154/047:36–44; 061:9–35; 072:4–21; 076:10–18. **Kind:** unpursued implication / historical identification. **Quote:** “faire une surface avec les p.r. stables”; “Description combinatoire”. **Search terms:** positions relatives, surface modulaire, pseudodroites.

The ESQ passage describes a combinatorial surface classifying stable and unstable positions of one added pseudoline, which matches the present surface, its face adjacency, and the attempted transport rules much more specifically than the word “topology” does. The published passage is also in Grothendieck (1984/1997, §8, p. 41). These scans add the construction’s local experiments and objections; the match does not identify every trial gluing with one successfully completed surface.

### C02 — Tame topology is a supported setting, with a narrower concrete subject

### C03 — The 1986 geometry of forms is a separate, unproved genealogy

**Source:** 154/047:39–63; 073:54–62; 076:10–18. **Kind:** reading question / bounded historical judgment. **Quote:** “Description combinatoire”. **Search terms:** formes, réseaux, ateliers.

## 5. Conditional deductions and proposed work — explicitly the present reader’s ideas

### O01 — The even-polygon character identity can be checked on two generators

**Source:** 154/061:35–49; 063:7–28. **Kind:** own idea / algebraic verification method. **Confidence:** high for the abstract calculation, medium for the source identification, because its characters still require a convention dictionary. **Search terms:** caractères, coloration, diédral.

**Context and relation:** N077 R09–R11, 082; H154, Michel Hua’s project, pp. 60–63; RS **Hua `154-dihedral-monodromy-half-turn`**. Adds a bounded algebraic check to the recorded interpretation.

Write the symmetry group of the labeled $2n$-gon as $D_{2n}=\langle r,q\mid r^{2n}=q^2=1,qrq=r^{-1}\rangle$, with $r(t)=t+1$ and $q(t)=-t$, so its order is $4n$. Vertex parity changes under r and is preserved under q, edge parity changes under both, and polygon orientation is preserved under r but reversed under q, giving $\chi_{\mathrm{or}}=\chi_{\mathrm{vertices}}\chi_{\mathrm{edges}}$. This checks the algebra behind 063 once its torsors are identified, while leaving the surface-orientation comparison and the literal sign wording on 061 untouched.

### O02 — Test the parity formula as a coboundary on the actual move graph

**Source:** 154/063:30–43; 064:22–31. **Kind:** own idea / proof strategy. **Confidence:** high for the criterion, medium for applicability, because the complete admissible move set is not secured. **Search terms:** parité, sing, exceptionnels.

**Context and relation:** N077 R31–R32, 093–094; SS **STRATA C.8** and RS **follow-ups @944**. Adds an explicit finite check of the manuscript’s proposed trivial character.

For each directed admissible move e from D to D′, record its elementary-step weight $w(e)\in\mathbb Z/2$ and test $w(e)=\mathrm{sing}_{D'}-\mathrm{sing}_D$. If this identity holds for every ordinary and exceptional move, its sum along a path is the endpoint difference and vanishes on closed paths. A stable-only edge compressing two earlier steps must carry their summed weight, so this check also detects an illicit transfer of unweighted path length from one graph to another.

### O03 — Trivial sign characters do not imply trivial polygon holonomy
