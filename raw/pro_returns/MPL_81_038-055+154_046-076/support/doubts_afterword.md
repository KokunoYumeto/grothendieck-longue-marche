## Legibility, trust, and limits

The main ink text is often readable but extremely compressed. The least secure passages are the opening and marginal material on scans 046–050, overwritten formulas and endpoint labels on scans 052–057, the warning on scan 063, and several retrospective or parenthetical clauses on scans 068, 070–072, 075–076. The reused printed sheets are mostly clearer than the handwriting, although pen strokes obscure isolated printed characters and the faint pencil constructions are much less secure than the printing.

All 31 whole-page images and their 62 supplied halves were examined directly in the sequential transcription; each page was subsequently reread against its image. No OCR or other reading software was used. Additional crops and rotations were used for small or inverted writing. A notation comparison was performed across the package before freeze, including primes, stars, alphabets, boundary marks, orientation symbols, inequalities, headless links, and the prose equality shorthand.

The register is deliberately conservative. In particular, a low-confidence multiword `\unsure` passage is a best attempt at the visible ink, not a claim that a fluent sentence has been recovered. No `\illegible` macro was needed because a tentative reading could be attempted at each difficult passage; this should not be mistaken for complete legibility. Unfinished expressions, surprising formulas, and cancelled assertions remain present without mathematical repair.

TikZ drawings preserve the observed objects, labels, crossings, colored groups, and relative placement as closely as the readings allow. They are vector transcriptions of freehand drawings, not facsimiles: curvature, hatch density, exact metric proportions, and faint overlapping pencil continuations carry less assurance than prominent labeled incidence. The compile-and-render check establishes that the supplied LaTeX and figure code can be typeset; it is not independent evidence for any paleographic or mathematical reading.
