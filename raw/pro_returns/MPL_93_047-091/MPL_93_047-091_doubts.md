# Legibility and limits of confidence

All 42 supplied whole-page images and all 84 supplied overlapping half-page images were visually read by the same reader, without OCR. Each page was reread against its initial transcription, then received a separate full visual audit after the initial sequence was complete; magnified and rotated crops were used for the smallest or slanting writing. The inventory records 1,020 transcribed elements; it is an aid to coverage, not a certificate that every pen stroke has an infallible interpretation.

The typed administrative pages are generally the most legible, although clipped edges and overtyping retain local uncertainty. The mathematical typescripts contain genuine typing errors and overlapping revisions that are sometimes harder to distinguish than ordinary handwriting. The least secure areas are the crowded marginal additions on049,053,072–074, the diagrams and overlapping pencil/blue-ink formulas on081–082, the cancelled line on087, and a few compressed phrases on088–091. Names and the address on069–070 are not normalized from external information. Strong mathematical expectations were deliberately kept separate from ink: surprising signs, missing exponents, unclosed delimiters and authorial errors remain as read, with doubtful alternatives marked locally. TikZ reproduces the structure and relative placement of diagrams and nonverbal marks; it does not reproduce the exact pen ductus.

The confidence labels qualify readings, not mathematical truth. A high-confidence reading can be a mistaken statement in the manuscript; a low-confidence reading is a genuine unresolved visual question. The notation comparison was completed before the transcription was frozen and before the context archive was opened.
