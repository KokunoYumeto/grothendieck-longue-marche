### BR05 — Connectedness does not replace irreducibility in the section argument

**Witness:** 29/067, margin “peut-on remplacer ‘irréductible’ par ‘connexe’ ? non”. **Kind:** own idea, worked test of his negative answer. **Confidence:** high, by explicit projective linear subspaces. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Le lemme de Bertini de Zariski”, modern p66 = scan067. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

Take the two planes L₁={x₃=x₄=0} and L₂={x₁=x₂=0} in P⁴, meeting only at [1:0:0:0:0]; their union is connected. A generic hyperplane avoids that point and cuts one line in each plane, so its section of the union is disconnected. This independently verifies the margin's “non” and explains why the following finite-étale-cover argument uses normality to turn connectedness of a cover into irreducibility.

### BR06 — The arbitrary-field Bertini formulation needs geometric irreducibility

**Witness:** 29/067, “Soit k corps” and “X irréductible”. **Kind:** own idea, counterexample and refined formulation. **Confidence:** high, from a split quadratic base extension. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Le lemme de Bertini de Zariski”, modern p66 = scan067, and “Théorème de Bertini et variantes — Les deux énoncés de base”, modern pp112–115 = scans113–116. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

Over k=R, the integral R-scheme X=P²_C with its finite projection to P²_R satisfies the displayed irreducibility and image-dimension conditions, but after the algebraically closed generic parameter extension its inverse image of a line is two disjoint lines because C⊗_R K≅K×K. Thus irreducibility alone does not justify the geometric generic conclusion over an arbitrary field; geometric irreducibility is a necessary repair in this example, consistent with [S4]. Hua's report of the later typed Bertini theorem explicitly includes that stronger hypothesis, although those later scans were not visually checked here. The application in scans066–068 has algebraically closed k, so this counterexample does not invalidate that application.

### BR07 — The surjectivity criterion after Bertini is sound under its hypotheses

**Witness:** 29/067, “étale connexe … normal … irréductible”. **Kind:** unpursued implication / targeted proof check. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Le lemme de Bertini de Zariski”, modern p66 = scan067. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

### BR10 — Purity and normality do different jobs in the rational-map passage

**Witness:** 29/069, “par le th. de pureté” and “surjectif (car X normal)”. **Kind:** unpursued implication / targeted proof check. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Le passage à la limite sur les modèles”, modern p68 = scan069. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

Properness of the target extends the rational map from regular U across its codimension-one points [S7], while purity identifies π₁ of U with π₁ of the remaining domain of definition [S8]. Normality of the target then supplies surjectivity by the connected-cover criterion, because the dominant birational pullback has the same field extension and remains connected [S2–S3]. This supports Hua's separation of the two mechanisms, but his footnote's quoted “codim ≥1” differs from the frozen scan069 reading “codim ≥2”, so that quotation should not be imported into the transcription.

### BR11 — A fixed regular open repairs the birational finite-generation argument

**Witness:** 29/069–070, “d'où à la limite un hom surjectif” and “U peut être pris complémentaire … codim ≥2”. **Kind:** own idea, restricted repair. **Confidence:** high for this birational application, because one projective model suffices and avoids BR04's false arbitrary-pullback step. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Le passage à la limite sur les modèles”, modern pp68–69 = scans069–070. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

Choose one projective normal model Z of K/k and its regular locus U, so the projective argument BR08 bounds the generators of π₁(U). For every proper normal model X, BR10 produces a compatible surjection from this same π₁(U) to π₁(X). Compactness now makes π₁(U)→lim_X π₁(X) surjective: for a compatible tuple, the nonempty closed fibres in π₁(U) have the finite intersection property, using a common dominating model. Thus the birational finiteness conclusion can be justified without claiming codimension preservation for every proper replacement.

### BR13 — The regular-locus question has a negative answer for arbitrary normal models

**Witness:** 29/070, “U l'ouv de ses pts réguliers … bijectif” and “non ramifiée sur U … sur un modèle X′”. **Kind:** own idea, explicit counterexample. **Confidence:** high, by the quotient chart and the valuation proof in BR14. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Le passage à la limite sur les modèles”, modern p69 = scan070; the modernised body omits this marginal question, and searches for “réguliers”, “essentiellement constant”, and “éclat” give no supplied contextual answer. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

Over C, let X=(P¹×P¹)/⟨(x,y)↦(−x,−y)⟩ and U=X_reg: the four fixed points become four A₁ singularities, and V=(P¹×P¹) minus those four points is a connected finite étale double cover of U [S10]. Its field extension is K′=C(x,y) over K=C(x²,y/x), a rational function field. BR14 shows that this extension is ramified at a divisorial valuation and cannot become étale on any proper model, so it directly answers the manuscript's proposed extension criterion negatively. This does not refute essential constancy of the model system: here K already has the proper regular model P².

### BR14 — A valuation makes the obstruction persist on every modification

**Witness:** 29/070, “déduit de X par éclatemts”. **Kind:** own idea, independent proof of BR13's obstruction. **Confidence:** high, because x²=a is Eisenstein at a discrete valuation with v(a)=1. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Le passage à la limite sur les modèles”, modern p69 = scan070; compare the contextual normal/regular distinction there. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

At one singular point the invariant chart is C[a,b,c]/(ac−b²), where a=x², b=xy and c=y²; on its resolution use t=y/x, so b=at, c=at² and K=C(a,t). The a-adic valuation on C(t)(a) has v(a)=1 and v(t)=0, and K′=K(√a) has ramification index two. Every proper model of K has a centre for this valuation by the valuative criterion [S11], and a finite étale extension on that model would remain finite étale after pullback to the valuation ring, which cannot have ramification index two. Thus blowups cannot remove this obstruction, although the original cover was étale over all regular points of X.

### BR15 — Codimension-two kernel finiteness remains a different question

**Witness:** 29/066, margin “a un noyau de type fini ???”. **Kind:** conjecture / own-idea diagnostic. **Confidence for diagnostic:** high, from the explicit profinite semidirect product below. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “π₁ birationnel — Le théorème”, modern p65 = scan066; the margin's kernel question is not pursued in that account. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

Finite generation of π₁(X−Y) and π₁(X) does not by itself imply finite generation of their kernel. For a group-theoretic test, the profinite group F_p[[T]]⋊Z_p, where a topological generator acts on the additive power-series group by multiplication by 1+T, is generated topologically by 1 in the module and 1 in Z_p, but its kernel F_p[[T]] has quotients (Z/p)^m of unbounded rank. Indeed conjugating 1 gives (1+T)^n, whose linear combinations contain all powers T^n and are dense in F_p[[T]]. This is not claimed to arise from the manuscript's geometric pair, and BR13's finite double-cover obstruction does not settle the kernel question either.

### BR16 — Genus must be fixed once, not declared model-independent

**Witness:** 29/068 and 29/070, the generic curve and its use in the model limit. **Kind:** own idea, correction of contextual explanation. **Confidence:** high, since two embeddings of the same P² already give different genera. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Le passage à la limite sur les modèles”, final footnote, modern p69 = scan070. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

Hua's explanation that the genus of the generic curve does not depend on the chosen model is too strong if read literally: a generic hyperplane section of P² embedded by O(1) is a line, while one for its O(3) embedding is a smooth plane cubic, of genera zero and one respectively [S12]. A usable uniform bound instead comes from selecting one model, one regular open U and one curve once, then mapping its π₁ onto the groups of all models as in BR11. This preserves the manuscript's uniform-bound strategy without making the auxiliary curve's genus a birational invariant.

### BR17 — A concrete full-Künneth obstruction in characteristic p

**Witness:** 29/063, final corollary restricted to “rev. étales premiers à la car”. **Kind:** own idea, test of the need for that restriction. **Confidence:** high, from an explicit Artin–Schreier torsor. **Context:** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29, “Fin du théorème de finitude relatif : deux corollaires sur les produits”, modern p62 = scan063; the comparison with his report of the 1959 letter, modern p128 = scan129, concerns a different total/infinitesimal group and is not a proof of this claim. **RESULTS_STATE:** no matching mathematical ID.

On A² over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, z^p−z=xy is a finite étale C_p-torsor whose restrictions to both coordinate axes are trivial. It is nontrivial because xy is not g^p−g for a polynomial g: the leading degree excludes p>2, and in p=2 a squared linear form has no xy term. If π₁(A²) were π₁(A¹)×π₁(A¹), a character trivial on both factors would be trivial, a contradiction. This supplies a concrete warning against dropping the prime-to-p condition in the nonproper setting, without challenging the proper Künneth theorem.

## Verification details for the negative regular-locus answer

The construction in BR13–BR14 is an independent example proposed in this run; no claim of novelty is made. All four standard affine charts of P¹×P¹ are stable under the involution; near each of its four fixed points the action is (s,t)↦(−s,−t), so the invariant ring is C[s²,st,t²]≅C[a,b,c]/(ac−b²). This ring is normal (the invariant subring of a normal domain under a finite group) and its Jacobian (c,−2b,a) vanishes only at the origin, giving precisely four singular points on the quotient. On a≠0 the cover is obtained by adjoining s with s²=a and then t=b/s, so the derivative 2s is invertible; the analogous calculation on c≠0 verifies étaleness away from the fixed point without relying only on the abstract quotient theorem. The quotient is a normal projective surface; it can be constructed as the finite projective quotient, or by gluing the four stable invariant affine charts.

The original cover is connected because removing finitely many points from the integral surface P¹×P¹ leaves an integral open. Its generic extension has degree two: writing u=x² and v=y/x gives C(x,y)=C(x,v), and the involution fixes v and negates x. The valuation ring R=C(v)[u]_(u) has fraction field C(u,v); adjoining x with x²=u gives an Eisenstein degree-two extension, so its normalization has ramification index two. If a finite étale model of that field extension existed over any proper normal K-model W, the valuative criterion would give Spec R→W, and its pullback would be finite étale over R with generic fibre K′, contradicting that ramification.

An optional strengthening is π₁(U)=C₂ and π₁(K/C)=1: purity gives π₁(V)=π₁(P¹×P¹)=1, while the rational field K has proper regular model P² and the proper regular birational invariance argument applies. These strengthenings use [S8–S9] as background; BR13's negative extension answer needs neither strengthening.
