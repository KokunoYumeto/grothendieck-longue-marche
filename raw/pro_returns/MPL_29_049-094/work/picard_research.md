### P04 — Tangent spaces do not by themselves finish the nonreduced group-scheme argument

**Witness:** cote 29, scan 072, “Il reste à prouver … un isom sur les espaces tangents.” **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the abstract counterexample, by its coordinate Hopf algebras; no claim of a counterexample among the stated birational Picard schemes. **Context:** Hua, “Le théorème,” p.71/scan072, repeats the same proposed endpoint. **RESULTS_STATE:** no direct ID.

A surjective closed immersion of proper group schemes that induces an isomorphism on tangent spaces need not be an isomorphism: over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, the inclusion `α_p ⊂ α_{p²}` has these properties, but the two coordinate rings have lengths p and p². Both have a single geometric point and tangent space of dimension one, since their augmentation ideals modulo their squares are generated by t. Thus the stated endpoint needs additional geometric or deformation-theoretic information in characteristic p; this refutes the bare group-scheme criterion, not the manuscript's birational Picard theorem. A promising repair would compare all infinitesimal test schemes or the completed local Picard functors, rather than only dual numbers.

### P06 — The fixed-vector comparison has a precise Artin–Schreier scope

**Witness:** cote 29, scans 072–073, “Le premier hom s'écrit” and purity reference. **Kind:** unproved claim, with targeted check. **Context:** Hua, “Le corollaire en caractéristique p,” pp.71–72/scans072–073, Artin–Schreier footnote. **RESULTS_STATE:** no direct ID.

For a proper connected reduced scheme over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, the Artin–Schreier long exact sequence identifies `H¹_et(X,Z/p)` with the F-fixed vectors in `H¹(X,O_X)`, because `F−1` is onto on `H⁰(X,O_X)=k`. This checks the qualification supplied by Hua and explains why extending the ground field is part of the argument; [S2] gives the exact sequence and semilinear surjectivity used here. It does not independently check the separate purity assertion identifying the two étale fundamental groups.

### P07 — Normality really does prove the torsion-free half of the root-quotient argument

**Witness:** cote 29, scan 073, “sans torsion.” **Kind:** own idea / independent local proof. **Confidence:** high, by the integral-closure argument. **Context:** Hua, “Le corollaire en caractéristique p,” p.72/scan073. **RESULTS_STATE:** no direct ID.

For a normal domain R of characteristic p and q a p-power, `R^{1/q}/R` is torsion-free: if `a·b^{1/q}∈R` for `0≠a∈R`, then `b^{1/q}` lies in the fraction field of R and is integral over R, hence lies in R. This supplies a short local justification for the manuscript's injectivity of restriction from X to its dense open part. It should not be confused with surjectivity of restriction across a codimension-two complement, which needs the separate extension argument in P08.

### P08 — Regularity supplies the missing extension step on Y

**Witness:** cote 29, scan 073, the isomorphism label on `T(Y)→T(U)` and margin “Y lisse sur k (!).” **Kind:** own idea / conditional proof completion. **Confidence:** high under the explicitly stated regular finite-type perfect-field hypotheses. **Context:** Hua, “Le corollaire en caractéristique p,” p.72/scan073, attributes the whole triangle to normality. **RESULTS_STATE:** no direct ID.

When Y is regular of finite type over a perfect field, Frobenius is finite flat [S3–S4], and locally `O_Y^{1/q}/O_Y` is finite locally free: over a local ring the section 1 is unimodular in the finite free Frobenius module and extends to a basis. Such a sheaf extends uniquely across a codimension-two complement by the reflexive-sheaf extension property [S5], giving the needed `T(Y)≅T(U)`. Thus the triangle can be justified using normality of X for injectivity and regularity of Y for extension; normality alone has not been proved to supply both parts.

### P10 — A fixed smooth model explains the conditional stabilization argument

**Witness:** cote 29, scan 075, Corollaire 2, “le système … inductif est essentiellement constant.” **Kind:** unpursued implication, conditional on the theorem. **Context:** Hua, “Les deux corollaires et la question,” p.74/scan075. **RESULTS_STATE:** no direct ID.

If the scheme-level birational theorem is available and a fixed proper smooth model exists, normal closures of graphs give models dominating it and any other given proper normal model. Those dominating the fixed smooth model form a cofinal subsystem, and the theorem identifies their `Pic^τ` through the fixed model, which is the mechanism behind essential constancy. This is a conditional explanation of the corollary, not an independent repair of the gaps in the preceding Picard argument.

### P11 — The three bounds do not control infinitesimal thickness in abstract systems

**Witness:** cote 29, scan 075, Question and bounds (i)–(iii). **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the abstract counterexample; applicability to the special system of birational models remains open here. **Context:** Hua, “Les deux corollaires et la question,” p.74/scan075. **RESULTS_STATE:** no direct ID.

The chain `α_p ⊂ α_{p²} ⊂ α_{p³} ⊂ …` has scheme dimension zero, tangent dimension one, trivial geometric-point torsion and trivial component group, yet it never stabilizes because its lengths grow as `pⁿ`. Consequently bounds on dimension, discrete torsion and `h¹(O)` do not imply essential constancy for arbitrary systems of proper group schemes. The geometry of Picard schemes of models of one function field may impose additional restrictions, but those restrictions are not furnished by the three numerical bounds alone. In particular, an order bound for a finite group **scheme**, including its nonreduced length, is materially different from a bound for torsion in a group of geometric points or in the Néron–Severi group.

### P12 — The rank formula requires a prime different from the characteristic

**Witness:** cote 29, scan 076, displayed half-rank formula. **Kind:** own idea / counterexample to the unrestricted displayed equality. **Confidence:** high, using the standard fundamental-group/Tate-module calculation and an explicit supersingular curve. **Context:** Hua, “La borne uniforme, et la question finale,” p.75/scan076, repeats the formula without `ℓ≠p`. **RESULTS_STATE:** no direct ID.

For a supersingular elliptic curve E in characteristic p, `dim Pic_E=1` but `T_{p,et}(E)=0`, hence the pro-p factor of its geometric fundamental group has rank zero [S6–S7]. Thus the equality on the page is false if the prime ℓ is allowed to equal p; the usual prime-to-characteristic restriction is necessary and belongs in a research refinement, not in the transcription. This does not refute the dimension bound, for which one can choose any `ℓ≠p`.

### P16 — Dimension, tangent dimension, and stabilization are distinct outputs

**Witness:** cote 29, scans 075–077, “(i) résulte de (iii)” and the uniform-dimension theorem. **Kind:** unpursued implication, with targeted check. **Context:** Hua, “Les deux corollaires et la question” and “La borne uniforme, et la question finale,” pp.74–76/scans075–077. **RESULTS_STATE:** no direct ID.

The marginal implication `(iii)⇒(i)` is correct because the tangent space of the Picard scheme at the identity is `H¹(X,O_X)` and local dimension is at most tangent dimension [S8]. The theorem on scan076 proves only the numerical dimension bound, conditional on its rank argument, whereas the tangent bound, discrete torsion bound, and full infinitesimal stabilization remain different questions. In positive characteristic the distinction matters because even smooth projective varieties can have nonreduced Picard schemes [S1, Remark 5.15].

## Explicit checks underlying the counterexamples

### C1 — Hopf-algebra check for P04 and P11

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, define `G_n=Spec k[t]/(t^{p^n})` with comultiplication `t↦t⊗1+1⊗t`; the ideal is a Hopf ideal since `(x+y)^{p^n}=x^{p^n}+y^{p^n}`. The quotient map `k[t]/t^{p^{n+1}} → k[t]/t^{p^n}` defines a closed group immersion `G_n→G_{n+1}`, which is surjective on the singleton underlying topological spaces and induces the identity on their one-dimensional augmentation ideals modulo squares. Their ranks are `p^n` and `p^{n+1}`, so the map is not an isomorphism; all G_n are finite, hence proper, connected, and have only the identity geometric point. This is a complete algebraic counterexample to the general criteria in P04 and P11, with no assertion that these G_n are the Picard schemes arising from the manuscript's fixed function field.

### C2 — Frobenius-linear check for P05

Let `F(ae₁+be₂)=b^p e₁` on W and let V be `ke₁`. A fixed vector has e₂-coordinate zero and then e₁-coordinate zero, so `V^F=W^F=0`; both first kernels equal `ke₁`, but `ker(F²|W)=W`. Thus the injective inclusion induces isomorphisms on fixed spaces and first kernels and nevertheless fails to be surjective. Requiring comparisons for all sufficiently large powers detects this particular defect; a full semilinear criterion additionally uses the stable invertible part, for which [S2, Lemma 59.63.2] supplies the fixed-vector dimension calculation.

### C3 — Supersingular elliptic witness for P12

For `E/F₃: y²=x³−x`, the affine partial derivative with respect to x is 1, and the usual point at infinity is nonsingular, so E is a smooth plane cubic. Each x in F₃ gives `x³−x=0` and therefore exactly y=0, giving three affine points and the point at infinity, hence `#E(F₃)=4≡1 mod3`. By [S7] it is supersingular, and the triviality of geometric p-torsion propagates to every p-power torsion group; [S6, Corollary 10.37] then makes its p-primary fundamental-group factor zero. Its Picard dimension remains one, so it is a counterexample precisely to the missing prime restriction in the displayed equality.
