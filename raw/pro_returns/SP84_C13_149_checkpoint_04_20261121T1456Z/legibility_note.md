The large central lines are generally readable, and the repeated definitions, diagram labels and nearby examples provide useful comparisons of the hand. Enlarging the native scans makes many short abbreviations and crossed words readable, but it cannot recover a stroke hidden by another stroke or replace detail absent from the scan. The confidence labels describe local readings, not a probability that an entire page is correct.

The least secure places are the compressed oblique marginal notes, especially on scans022 and024; words under multiple cancellations; the boundaries between a cancelled insertion and its surviving context; and very small primes, bars, indices and arrow terminals. Rounded E/epsilon-like and Z forms require care about alphabet as well as letter identity. These are kept distinct from clear but mathematically surprising letters or indices, which remain literal in the transcription.

Both witnesses were useful locators of trouble. C often preserves abbreviations, cancellation and incomplete syntax more closely. H sometimes recovers a word or alphabet better, but also expands short forms, supplies punctuation, moves additions and regularizes diagram signs. Neither pattern establishes any particular reading. The logs include places where both witnesses agree and the visible ink nevertheless supports a different result, including unsupported stops and altered deletion boundaries.

The edition preserves unresolved alternatives in place and records why they remain unresolved. It is a set of model readings checked against saved image crops; it is not claimed to be a human-verified transcription. The independent reading records and every adjudicated difference are retained so that a later reader can examine the evidence directly.
