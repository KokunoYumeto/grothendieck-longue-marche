WORKING PROOF FOR INDEPENDENT AUDIT, 2026-11-16 04:21 UTC
Pro-p pentagon integrality: nonzero quadratic parameter is a square unit.
This is not yet a promoted statement. The full profinite hexagon is not inferred.

INPUT. p>=5, f in the closed commutator of the free pro-p group F=<x,y>. Suppose f satisfies the pro-p pentagon. Put D=1+24c2(f) in Z_p. Furusho2010 Theorem2/Cor12 gives, in the free prounipotent group over E=Q_p(mu), a root mu^2=D and the equations
 f(x,y) f(y,x)=1,
 f(x,y) x^m f(z,x) z^m f(y,z) y^m=1,
where z=(xy)^(-1), m=(mu-1)/2. For the present proof assume D!=0. Then mu!=0 and mu is integral over Z_p, so mu,m belong to O_E. At p2,3 the square-unit conclusion follows directly from D=1+24c2(f); D=0 for p>=5 is treated separately by the relative pro-p braid argument, not by a limit.

1. THE INTEGRAL METABELIAN COEFFICIENT.
Use [x,y]=xyx^(-1)y^(-1). Put Lambda=Z_p[[A,B]], a=1+A,b=1+B, and consider the pro-p semidirect product Lambda^2 semidirect Z_p^2. Write its elements as (d,v), with d=a^s b^t and v in Lambda e1+Lambda e2, and multiplication
 (d,v)(e,w)=(de,v+d w), (d,v)^(-1)=(d^(-1),-d^(-1)v).
There is a continuous homomorphism from F sending x to (a,e1), y to (b,e2). Their commutator is
 (1,(1-b)e1+(a-1)e2)=(1,w0).
The image of f has the form (1,F(a,b)w0) for one unique F(a,b) in Lambda. Indeed F' is the closed normal closure of [x,y]; conjugation multiplies w0 by a^s b^t; the Lambda-multiple submodule Lambda w0 is compact and hence closed. Existence follows, and uniqueness follows since Lambda is a domain and w0 has a nonzero coordinate. No assertion that an arbitrary closed subgroup is freely generated is used.
Under the swap of x,y, the commutator image is -w0 with variables swapped. The duality equation therefore gives F(a,b)=F(b,a). This identity already holds in the integral Magnus module; alternatively compare its injective image over E.
For any two affine matrices with diagonal entries d,e and translation entries v,w, the image of f is the translation
 F(d,e)((1-e)v+(d-1)w).
This follows first for discrete commutator words and then by continuity. All substitutions below put d-1,e-1 in T E[[T]], so the series is well-defined. The comparison with the canonical prounipotent image follows from the same Magnus expansion; it can also be checked coefficientwise on discrete words before taking the pro-p limit.

2. EVERY AFFINE MULTIPLICATION.
Set a=1+T and evaluate in matrices (d,v)=[[d,v],[0,1]] over E[[T]]:
 x=(a,T), y=(a^(-1),0), xy=(1,T), z=(1,-T).
They are all congruent to the identity modulo T, so this is a valid evaluation of the free prounipotent identities, using logarithms in T M_2(E[[T]]).
Define
 t(a)=(1-a^(-1)) F(a,a^(-1)),
 V(a)=(a-1)F(1,a).
The general commutator formula is
 [(d,v),(e,w)]=(1,(1-e)v+(d-1)w).
Consequently:
 f(x,y)=(1,T t(a));
 f(z,x)=(1,T(a-1)F(1,a))=(1,T V(a));
 f(y,z)=(1,T(1-a^(-1))F(a^(-1),1))=(1,-T V(a^(-1))).
The last equality uses F symmetric. Powers are
 x^m=(a^m,T(a^m-1)/(a-1)), z^m=(1,-mT), y^m=(a^(-m),0).
Multiplication of the six hexagon factors gives diagonal1 and translation
 T t(a) + T(a^m-1)/(a-1) + a^m T V(a) - a^m m T - a^m T V(a^(-1)).
Dividing by T a^m gives
 a^(-m)t(a)+(1-a^(-m))/(a-1)+V(a)-V(a^(-1))-m=0.    (I)
Duality symmetry gives t(a^(-1))=-a t(a). Apply (I) under a->a^(-1):
 -a^(m+1)t(a)+a(a^m-1)/(a-1)+V(a^(-1))-V(a)-m=0.   (II)
Add (I)+(II), put d=a^(-m)-a^(m+1), and use
 1-a^(-m)+a^(m+1)-a = 1-a-d.
The result is d(t(a)-1/(a-1))=2m+1=mu. Since mu!=0, d is a nonzero formal series and division in E((T)) is legitimate. It follows that
 t(a)=1/T - mu a^m/(a^mu-1),
and therefore the exact identity is
 R(T):=1-T(1-a^(-1))F(a,a^(-1))
      =mu a^((mu-1)/2) T/(a^mu-1).                 (III)
Its left side lies in 1+T^2 Z_p[[T]]. This is the crucial integral object. No gamma factorization, logarithmic-coefficient integrality or Bernoulli cancellation is assumed.
As a sign check its quadratic coefficient is -c2(f)=-(mu^2-1)/24, in agreement with the right side.

3. THE FIRST POSSIBLE DENOMINATOR.
Put H_mu(T)=(a^mu-1)/(mu T), which has constant1, so (III) says
 R(T) H_mu(T)=a^m.
For 0<=k<=p-2,
 [T^k]H_mu = (mu-1)...(mu-k)/(k+1)!
belongs to O_E, because mu is integral and (k+1)! is a p-unit.
Also [T^(p-1)]a^m=binom(m,p-1) lies in O_E: its denominator is (p-1)!, a p-unit. No binomial-integrality theorem for arbitrary quadratic m is being assumed; only these degrees below p are used.
Since every coefficient of R is in Z_p, the coefficient comparison at degree p-1 forces
 [T^(p-1)]H_mu = product_{j=1}^{p-1}(mu-j)/p! in O_E.
Equivalently product_{j=1}^{p-1}(mu-j) belongs to p O_E.
Modulo p O_E, the polynomial product_{j=1}^{p-1}(X-j) is X^(p-1)-1. Thus
 mu^(p-1)-1 = D^((p-1)/2)-1 belongs to p O_E.
This expression belongs to Q_p, and p O_E intersect Q_p=p Z_p, so
 D^((p-1)/2)=1 modp.
Euler's criterion gives D modp a nonzero square, and Hensel's lemma gives a square root in Z_p^times. Hence both chosen roots mu lie in Z_p^times. The conclusion includes exclusion of every nonzero p-divisible D and every nonsquare D.
