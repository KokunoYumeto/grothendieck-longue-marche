# Root review: A.51, an actual nontrivial second scalar refinement

Reviewed 16 November 2026, approximately 06:07–06:10 UTC. Accepted
after reading the complete working proof, the full exact program,
and the independent CE review. We reran the program successfully.

The odd filiform head has order 100842 and is generated by the specified
order-two and order-three elements. The program enumerates the entire
head and verifies the representation, pairings, cocycles and source
relations. Its 84-dimensional paired module has exterior-square
coinvariant dimensions two over the odd subgroup and one over the
whole head. The difference alone would be insufficient, but the
two-center hyperbolic cover is an actual quotient of Pi: the s lift
has order two and the explicit r correction (1,0) makes its cube one.
The image of the actual residual R is the full two-center group P.
Thus the image of W is its center, and its commutator with the head
is the nonzero diagonal central line. The next commutator is zero.
This already proves strictness before any universal upper bound.

The identification of the actual scalar refinements uses relative
perfectness of the initial R, not of the smaller W1. Vanishing of
H1(C,Vbar-dual) is valid for the entire seven-dimensional cocycle
space: every nonzero class gives a full affine quotient and restricts
nontrivially to R. There is no unsupported inference from one nonzero
class to the whole cohomology space.

For successive finite scalar extensions, the kernel on abelianizations
is the image of the NEW globally central kernel. The previous
abelianization is already Vbar. The finite dual argument therefore
continues to apply without incorrectly treating the entire old center
as a trivial module. At the second stage the old center is trivial,
so its commutator pairing with Vbar vanishes. At the third stage the
old center is a nontrivial two-dimensional module for H/J, but is
trivial under the odd normal N. Since (Vbar)_N=0, its pairing again
vanishes. Odd-order semisimplicity bounds each resulting wedge
quotient by the two already attained central dimensions.

The upper bounds hold in every finite quotient retaining the displayed
cover; they derive finiteness rather than presupposing it. They identify
the first two actual refinements, give W1/W2=C2, and give W3=W2.

The result refutes a blanket immediate-stabilization inference. It
does not refute the involution conjecture. The single head need not
be c-stable; a negative norm obstruction is neither required for the
strictness statement nor supplied by it. A paired c-stable extension
is being treated separately.
