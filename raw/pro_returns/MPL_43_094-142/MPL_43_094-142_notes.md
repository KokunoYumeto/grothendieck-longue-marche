### A03 — A fully computed arithmetic test for the finite-generation premise

**Location:** cote 43, scans 109 and 112, \(G_m\) calculations; scan 120, divisibility assertion. **Kind:** own idea. **Quote:** “\(G=G_m\)”. **Context:** README, assertion that finite generation of \(G(K)\) fails for the toric part; CT 16/19, prime-to-characteristic divisibility of \(\operatorname{Pic}(U)^0\), and CT 16/21, the explicitly imposed finite-generation premise for \(\operatorname{NS}(X)\). **Register:** bears on WG2 §1.1 #3; arithmetic bridge B11 is named only in the README. **Confidence:** high, by the following complete elementary calculation.

The homomorphism \(q\mapsto(\operatorname{sgn}q,(v_p(q))_p)\) is an isomorphism \(\mathbb Q^\times\simeq\{\pm1\}\times\bigoplus_{p\text{ prime}}\mathbb Z\), with inverse \((\varepsilon,(a_p))\mapsto\varepsilon\prod_p p^{a_p}\), by unique prime factorization of numerator and denominator. Every finite set of rational numbers has nonzero valuations at only finitely many primes, so a prime outside their union cannot lie in the subgroup they generate and \(G_m(\mathbb Q)\) is not finitely generated. An element of \(\bigcap_{n\ge1}(\mathbb Q^\times)^n\) has every \(v_p\) divisible by every positive integer, hence all valuations zero, hence lies in \(\{\pm1\}\), and its membership in the subgroup of squares leaves only \(1\). This exact example verifies both the failure of the README's proposed finite-generation premise in a toric case and the relevant all-orders nondivisibility conclusion, without attributing either calculation to these manuscript pages.
