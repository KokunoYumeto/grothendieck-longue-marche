Here is a complete elementary proof, including the coefficient issue.

Fix a prime p.  A free group on finitely many generators is residually
finite p by its noncommutative Magnus expansion over Fp.  To see directly
that the expansion detects a nontrivial reduced syllable word

  x_(i1)^e1 ... x_(ik)^ek,   adjacent i_j different,

write d_j=p^(v_p(e_j)).  In (1+X_i)^e_j, including negative e_j, the
lowest nonzero positive-degree term over Fp has degree d_j and nonzero
coefficient.  The monomial

  X_(i1)^d1 ... X_(ik)^dk

has k runs of distinct adjacent variable names.  It can arise in the
product only by choosing a positive-degree term from EVERY one of the
k factors; omitting a factor gives fewer than k runs.  Its coefficient
is therefore the product of the nonzero leading coefficients.  The word
is nontrivial already after truncation above this total degree.  The
units1+augmentation in that finite truncated Fp algebra form a finite
p-group.  This yields a finite p-group separating the word.  A finite
set of distinct words is separated by the product of finitely many such
quotients.  The same conclusion holds for an arbitrarily generated free
group, because a finite set of words uses finitely many generators and
the other generators may be killed.  It holds for E, the product of two
free groups, by taking products of the separating p-quotients.

For a finite p-group P, its augmentation ideal over Fp is nilpotent.
Induct on |P|: a central element z of order p has (z-1)^p=0, and the
quotient by(z-1) is Fp[P/<z>], whose augmentation ideal is nilpotent by
induction.  Therefore the original ideal is nilpotent.  Over Z/p^k, if
its reduction modulo p has m-th power0, its m-th power lies in p times
the group ring and its mk-th power is0.

Now let c!=0 be an integral finite-support group-ring element in Z[E].
Choose a finite p-group quotient of E separating every element of its
support, and choose k so that one nonzero coefficient of c remains
nonzero modulo p^k.  Its image in (Z/p^k)[P] is nonzero.  Since the target
augmentation ideal is nilpotent, c cannot lie in every power I_E^n.
This proves the required separation.  Notice that nilpotence was used
over Z/p^k, NOT over Z[P].  No invariant finite p-quotient under F5 is
needed: equation(11) reduces the separation in Z[G] coefficientwise to
the already separated filtration of Z[E].  Thus intersection_n J^n=0.
