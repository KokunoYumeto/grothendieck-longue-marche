9. A sufficient whole-subgroup normalizer, and its present gap.

For the full actual I, proving both C and D=BC normalize I would suffice.
Indeed alpha0(I)=I and alpha0(D)=AC, hence AC would normalize I too;
then A=(AC)C^-1 and B=DC^-1 normalize I. Since I contains the deleted
free generators, every generator of F7 would normalize I. The inherited
internal normal-generation theorem would then force I=R.

The exact actions on C are favorable but do not yet prove that premise:

 alpha2,...,alpha5(C)=V_j C V_j^-1.
The known C-strip and D-strip establish normalization only on their
respective free-factor filling kernels. One cannot conclude whole-I
normality by applying the earlier one-axis induction separately. In
particular alpha1^-1(C)=D^-1 C D, and a C-normal finite stage need not
remain C-normal after D-conjugation. A simultaneous invariant-stage
argument or an additional genuine normalizer identity is still required.
