Research-only finite perfect-kernel countermodel to abstract fixed lifting
CE, 2026-11-16. No formal numbered statement and no actual-surface claim.

1. Exact purpose and scope.

The following finite example simultaneously has
  N_A intersect N_B = [N_A,N_B] = M,
  M/[M,N] = 1,
and a fixed-diagonal image I which normally generates the entire diagonal
head R. Nevertheless, fixed lifting onto that diagonal head fails. It
therefore excludes an abstract inference from mutual-commutator equality,
internal normal generation, and the vanishing of the first relative
abelian obstruction to full nonabelian fixed lifting. Every group below
is finite, so the same example is already a profinite example. It is not
asserted to be a quotient of the actual surface or to preserve its source
marking, divisor incidence, or arithmetic structure.

2. Group and involution.

Let R=S3, let t=(12) in R, and let A=A5. Choose u=(12)(34) in A. Set
  M=A^R,
  N=M semidirect (R x R).
The action on the six coordinate indices is
  (a,b): x |-> a x b^{-1}.
Equivalently, on functions f:R->A it is
  ((a,b) f)(x)=f(a^{-1} x b).
Let sigma0 act by swapping the two R factors and by index inversion on M:
  sigma0(a,b)=(b,a),   (sigma0 f)(x)=f(x^{-1}).
Compatibility follows from
  (a x b^{-1})^{-1}=b x^{-1} a^{-1},
so sigma0 is an involutory automorphism of N.

Let m in M have value u at t and identity at every other coordinate.
Since t=t^{-1}, sigma0(m)=m. Thus the element
  sigma=m sigma0 in N semidirect <sigma0>
has square one. In the sequel sigma also denotes its conjugation action
on N. This action induces the swap on the quotient R x R.

The exact orders are
  |M|=60^6=46,656,000,000,
  |N|=60^6 * 36=1,679,616,000,000.
Neither M nor N is enumerated in the certificate.

3. The factor kernels and the first relative module.

Let N_A and N_B be the inverse images of R x 1 and 1 x R, respectively.
Their intersection is M. Their images in R x R commute, hence
  [N_A,N_B] is contained in M.
Both contain M, and A5 is perfect, so
  M=[M,M] is contained in [N_A,N_B].
This proves the exact equality N_A intersect N_B=[N_A,N_B]=M.
Similarly [M,N] contains [M,M]=M, and consequently M/[M,N]=1.
Every repeated relative scalar commutator beginning with M remains M.
The obstruction found below is therefore invisible to these relative
abelian quotients. This is a limitation of that abstract mechanism, not
an assertion that all other finite or nonabelian tests are ineffective.

4. Exact fixed-lift image.

An element n=(f,(a,b)) fixed by sigma must have a=b=r, because sigma
induces the swap on the head. Commutation of n with m sigma0 is exactly
the six-coordinate system
  f(x) m(r^{-1} x r) = m(x) f(x^{-1})  for every x in R.       (1)

There are four inversion-fixed indices: 1 and the three transpositions.
The remaining two indices, the 3-cycles, form one inversion pair.
If r does not centralize t, evaluating (1) at x=t gives
  f(t)=u f(t),
because m(t)=u but m(r^{-1} t r)=1. This is impossible since u is
nonidentity. Hence every fixed lift has head in C_R(t).

Conversely, if r centralizes t, the pure diagonal element
  (1,(r,r))
commutes with m sigma0: diagonal index conjugation preserves the unique
nonidentity coordinate t. Thus every such r has a fixed lift, and
  image(N^sigma -> (R x R)^swap) = diagonal C_R(t).
Here C_R(t)=<t> has order two. Its normal closure in S3 is all S3,
because the conjugates of t are all transpositions. It is proper and
nonnormal. Therefore internal normal generation of the fixed image does
not give surjectivity of fixed lifting.

For completeness the exact fixed-fiber sizes can be read from (1).
For r in C_R(t), the coordinate f(t) must lie in C_A(u), of order four.
At the other three inversion-fixed indices f is arbitrary (60 choices
each). On the remaining inverse pair the two values agree (60 choices).
Each of the two allowed diagonal heads therefore has
  4 * 60^4 = 51,840,000
fixed lifts. The total fixed subgroup has order 103,680,000. The other
four diagonal heads have no fixed lifts.
