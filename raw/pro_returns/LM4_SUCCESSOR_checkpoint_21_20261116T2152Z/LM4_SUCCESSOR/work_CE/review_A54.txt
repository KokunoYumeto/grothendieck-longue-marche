Independent review of A.54: cohomology after replacing the initial base
Reviewer: CE agent. Date: 2026-11-16 UTC.
Disposition: PASS.

Reviewed source: work_A/A54_new_base_cohomology_working.md.
The initial base C=Pi/R has its actual normal pronilpotent subgroup N coming
from the relative-pronilpotent construction. The coefficient hypothesis is
on the actual normal pro-p-prime subgroup N_(p'), not merely on a finite
odd-order subgroup in a quotient. Averaging gives vanishing in every
positive degree on that subgroup, and its invariant coefficient space is
zero. Hochschild--Serre therefore gives H^j(C,T)=0 for all j, including
j=0, under the stated hypothesis.

For C'=Pi/W_m with kernel P=R/W_m, the coefficient action factors through C,
so P acts trivially. The low-degree exact sequence gives
H^1(C',T) = Hom_C(P_ab,T) because both H^1(C,T) and H^2(C,T) vanish.
A.53 identifies P_ab with the original finite abelian residual module V.
No splitting of C' -> C is required. Thus H^1(C',T)=Hom_C(V,T) is the
correct exact next-base formula.

For the concrete A.51 module V=U+U^dual, the summands are nonisomorphic
simple modules with endomorphism ring F2. The initial H^1(Pi,U) and
H^1(Pi,U^dual) have dimension seven, while the next-base inflated spaces
have dimension one. A cocycle outside the latter line has nonzero
restriction to the stabilized residual R'=W_m. That restriction is an
equivariant homomorphism, because R' acts trivially on its coefficient
module. Simplicity forces surjectivity, and its kernel is normal in Pi.
It therefore produces an actual next residual quotient U^dual. The dual
coefficient U has nonzero H^1(C',U), precisely the hypothesis which the
initial-base averaging had made zero.

The conclusion is a genuine failure of automatic propagation of the
cohomological hypothesis to arbitrary successive bases. It is not a norm
obstruction or an impossibility result. Simultaneously collecting finitely
many affine layers over the initial base retains an abelian residual
module, so A.53 remains applicable there. The source maintains this scope.
