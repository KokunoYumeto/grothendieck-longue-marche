# Additional full profinite recognition source audit

These readings were made after checkpoint 08. None supplies the missing full free-product recognition theorem. Their scopes are recorded to avoid reusing an inapplicable result as a solution. No new formal theorem relies on an unopened step below.

1. Douboula, S., Temate, N., Mantika, G., and Tieudjo, D. (2026). *On a cohomological characterization of free profinite products of three profinite groups with amalgamated subgroups*. Extracta Mathematicae, 41(1), 111–132. https://doi.org/10.17398/2605-5686.41.1.111. The primary PDF abstract and introduction were opened at https://revista-em.unex.es/index.php/EM/article/download/2407/2142/7522. They explicitly restrict the class C to finite solvable groups. Subsequent retrieval returned access errors, so the exact main theorem was not verified at its statement and proof. No full profinite application is made.

2. Wykowski, J. (2026; copyright 2025). *Profinite subgroup accessibility and recognition of amalgamated factors*. Groups, Geometry, and Dynamics, 20, 1071–1097. https://doi.org/10.4171/GGD/851. The primary paper https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/53276 was opened, including Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 and the discussion on pages 1078–1079. Theorem 1.1 starts with an accessible subgroup and constructs a continuous derivation; it does not recognize the required splitting from the present section data. Theorem 1.2 assumes an already discrete finitely generated virtually free group. The discussion also explains why a profinite quotient graph need not have the spanning-tree property used in the discrete argument. No application to an arbitrary semidirect product Fhat_2 by C2 follows.

3. Haran, D., and Jarden, M. (1986). *The absolute Galois group of a pseudo real closed algebraic field*. Pacific Journal of Mathematics, 123(1). Primary PDF: https://msp.org/pjm/1986/123-1/pjm-v123-n1-p05-s.pdf. We read Lemma 2.2, Proposition 2.3, Lemma 3.4, and Lemma 4.1. The exact page extent was not checked, so it is not supplied here. The results give sections under real projectivity, embeddings into a universal real-free group, and classifications under the stated solvability or generation assumptions. They do not remove the missing generation condition in our extension.

4. Jarden, M. (1982). *Torsion-free profinite groups with open free subgroups*. Archiv der Mathematik, 39(6), 496–500. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01899653. Publisher metadata was accessible, and Haran's primary discussion at https://www.tau.ac.il/~haran/Papers/non-free.pdf was reopened. The actual Jarden theorem PDF was not accessible; its exact theorem was therefore not treated as primary-verified. In any case our extensions contain involutions, so the torsion-free hypothesis would require a further proved reduction.

5. The rank-at-most-two finite-extension results found for Herfort, Ribes, and Zalesskii (1998), Israel Journal of Mathematics, 107, 195–227, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02764009, concern pro-p groups. No full profinite recognition follows from their title or from the pro-p conclusion.

The remaining target is the full generation/recognition statement for the specified involution extension with its required peripheral information. An embedding, a retract, a maximal pro-two quotient, or a theorem conditional on an existing discrete graph-of-groups structure does not supply it.
