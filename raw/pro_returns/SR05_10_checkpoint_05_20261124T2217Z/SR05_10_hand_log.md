### Local graphic variants and limits worth retaining

These are attested local facts, not additional universal habits.

- **Doubled V versus plain V on the same line:** at 053-b32, the first V and two later formula-internal V signs have separate descending left strokes joined by an upper cap, supporting `\mathbb{V}`. At 053-b33, the terminal `sur V` has a single typed left diagonal and ordinary serifs. Its nearby pale curve belongs to the cancellation. The alphabet must not be harmonized across the sentence.
- **Sloping inequality bar:** 051-b20 supports `\leqslant`, extending the earlier log's attention to slanted lower bars. This is a local shape identification; it does not change the relation.
- **A sigma may have an extra closing stroke without a recoverable underletter:** 052-b22 has the sum's two diagonal arms and long top/bottom bars, plus a right vertical that closes its outline. Root retained the sum and did not infer a separate earlier character. By contrast, 047-b36 has a genuinely separable small serifed typed x beneath the handwritten sum, with exposed terminals. Do not use the first case to suppress the second, or the second to invent a substrate in the first.
- **A lambda's upper hook is not a detached tilde:** 053-b36 and b37 have a heavy hooked lambda and a thinner crossing arm possibly belonging to an older chi. The hook belongs to the lambda stroke. The possible chi underlayer remains uncertain; the cancellation at 053-b41 still admits chi/lambda. These observations support removing a claimed detached accent, not declaring every thin arm a securely identified chi.
- **Repeated strokes need not mean a double arrow:** 045-b27 has two actual shafts on the left vertical, the shorter ending above a single open V head. The single right arrow at 053-b15 instead has a retraced upper head and detached short stroke, without a second shaft. The four connections at 046-b11 are single-shaft arrows. Preserve the specific geometry rather than deciding from ink darkness alone.
- **Accent-shaped ink requires local discrimination:** the extra detached grave-like mark above m at 048-b35 and V-shaped accent in `opěre` at 048-b38 are retained as written. The circumflex in `interprête` at 053-b30 is independent of the cancellation. Conversely, the second dot at 050-b31 accompanies a displaced second i stem: root identifies an overlapped typed i, not an intentional diaeresis. These are different graphic phenomena, not one accent habit.
- **Ellipsis count varies:** 054-b28 contains exactly two points between the commas; 054-b29 has three between the wedge factors. Do not normalize both to a three-dot macro.
- **Capital G can retain a decisive inner spur:** 049-b15 identifies the old subject G, distinct from the new interlinear `il`; 050-b27 identifies an inserted G with that spur; 051-b22 retains literal typed `Gn`, whose initial has the inward bar and short right upright.
