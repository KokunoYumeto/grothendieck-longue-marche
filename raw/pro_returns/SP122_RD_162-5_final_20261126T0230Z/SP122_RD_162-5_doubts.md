## Cross-page notation review

The recurring forms were compared across the completed first readings and reviewed again after all 21 blind audits were closed. The supplied prose and diagram crops support the following distinctions: plain keyed C/R/Z/G in the Adams typescript; C followed by an underline under H alone; underlined f as a separate pro-object symbol; rounded hand-drawn cup versus serifed typed U; keyed baseline x in the fiber constructions; and dollar sigla alongside literal SF letters. The source's different completion-wedge positions remain different. The unresolved dollar/phi reading on 061 stays explicitly uncertain with dollar first, consistent with the clearer local notation but without claiming the congested ink is fully separated.

A static search of the final typescript files found no surviving underline over both C and H and no introduced blackboard-bold alphabet in scans 060–069. The cup and keyed-x occurrences were matched to their inspected crops. The audit distinctions between a wavy relation over a plain lower bar and a wavy label over a right arrow were preserved on 061, 064 and 065. The first lower suffix of X on 066 remains 0/C with the closed form first; the i-star/1-star labels on 067 remain uncertain. The middle lower et on 067 carries its local acute/plain alternative, without propagating that doubtful accent to other indices. The final SF_2-SF_1 on 067 remains literal SF despite dollar signs in the neighboring relations.

Every retained alternative was reviewed for its order before freezing. The preferred reading follows the visible stroke and sentence; a familiar title or expected identity was not used to remove a doubt. In particular the three disputed circled operations on 069 remain locally marked despite the conditional algebra check recorded separately. The printed article's typography is a different source layer; its E-tilde notation and indices were not forced into the typescript's machine conventions. This is a consistency review of readings, not an independent verification of the mathematics.

## Legibility and witness comparison

The printed offprint (051–056) is the clearest part of the packet. Its prose is generally sharp; the main reading risks are small indices, primes, and the extent or position of tildes and bars. The preserved index discrepancy on 054 is a visible source reading, not an unresolved character.

The French typescript leaves (047–049) are appreciably harder. The machine text is often clear, but local overtyping, keyed cancellation strokes, a whole abandoned passage, and handwritten additions occupy the same small areas. The least secure material lies under the densest cancellations, at the cut right edge, and in the long marginal note on 049. An unreadable underlying letter is distinguished from a legible replacement letter. A large cancellation across a passage does not make every underlying word unreadable.

The Adams typescript (058–069) has variable reproduction density. Small inserted symbols are sometimes pale; other cells are filled by duplicate or smeared ink. Short arrows, completion marks, circled operations, and the overprinted second SF on 066 need particular caution. The struck funding phrases and the replacement grant number on 058 remain low-confidence readings. Broken ring fragments and scattered dots can resemble punctuation or isolated letters. The small marks retained with doubt on 067 have unresolved textual status: their representation does not establish that they are authorial additions. The pale paths crossing the diagram on 066 are reproduced geometrically, with possible obscured labels explicitly recorded as a classification doubt. The notes record places where no textual mark was added, as well as the doubtful marks retained in the text. No word has been reconstructed from a faint nontext outline alone.

C provides the more continuous record across this packet, but its unmarked prose, underline extents, arrow directions and mathematical signs still required correction. H sometimes supplied a useful competing word or ending on the French leaves, and sometimes supplied text absent from the ink. Its files for 059, 062 and 066 deliberately concentrate on selected dark additions. The large omissions counted against those files describe coverage, not an error rate for a claimed full-page transcription. H's editorial explanations were not copied into the source text.

The confidence labels describe particular readings, not a measured overall accuracy. High confidence identifies a clear local reading; medium confidence allows congestion or competing stroke interpretations; low confidence is reserved for a reading that remains weak. Agreement between the two witnesses was not treated as independent evidence. The first and independent readings, literal token comparisons, root decisions, and per-page audit totals are preserved in `second/` and the progress log. The remaining-doubts table is the place to find the final unresolved readings and their alternatives; no doubt is silently completed from an expected mathematical statement or a familiar title.
