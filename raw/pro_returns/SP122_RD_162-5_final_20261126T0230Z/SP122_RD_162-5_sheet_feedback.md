## Refinements for later runs

## Recurring patterns in this packet

The category counts above are counts of distinct logged places, with one primary category per row. A place that differs from both witnesses contributes to both witness columns but only once to the distinct-place total. Wholesale missing blocks in H are grouped by source block, not inflated into one row per absent word. These counts are not word error rates.

### Patterns confirmed from the supplied images

- **Underline extent is a recurring reading issue.** On scans 060–065 the category notation has a separate C followed by an underlined H. Several C readings underlined both capitals. The boundary of the line, rather than the choice of a LaTeX macro, decides these changes. The same distinction applies to the separately underlined f in the cone notation. This is a useful specific subtype to add to the sheet's general instruction to check bars.
- **Typed and handwritten symbols coexist in the Adams typescript.** Cups before the Thom classes on scans 064–065 have rounded, un-serifed sides; the typed U elsewhere has clear top serifs. Baseline typewriter x signs in the fiber constructions on scan 063 were transcribed as keyed x, without silently substituting a multiplication sign. The evidence is local to these pages, not a universal rule for the author's hand.
- **The sphere-fibration siglum must be read occurrence by occurrence.** A compact looped sign crossed by an upright is the dollar glyph on scans 061–068. The source also uses the separate letters SF, including the final equality on scan 067. Those visible forms must not be made uniform merely because they play related mathematical roles. The notation table distinguishes source variation from a mistaken reading.
- **Broken ring fragments can resemble letters or accents.** Scans 063 and 067 contain isolated curved fragments near formulas and prose. Alignment, darkness, repetition away from text, and attachment to an actual character all matter. A doubtful punctuation or underline decision is not evidence that every nearby fragment belongs to the text.
- **A lower stroke beneath a wave can be an arrow shaft.** The blind audit distinguished short rightward arrowheads on scans 064–065 from the plain lower bars of nearby wavy relations. Inspect the terminal projection, not just the two-stroke silhouette. The added lower tilde beneath the i-shriek arrow on 061 is also a separate physical mark.
- **Raised stars can merge into the top of a typed capital.** All three E_3 stars on 069 have several projecting rays, although the blind reading initially omitted two. Conversely, the tiny isolated point after U on 065 remains doubtful. A star, prime, exponent, and reproduction spot need separate local decisions.
- **The French cancellations have two levels.** A whole abandoned passage can contain an older word correction within it. The outer cancellation and the underlying local overtype must both survive. On scans 047–049, close attention to separate typewriter cells prevents a corrected cell from becoming an invented doubled letter.
- **Blank symbol slots are real evidence.** Scan 047 leaves some coefficient positions uninked. Mathematical familiarity and the corresponding passage of H do not authorize filling those spaces.
- **Typed slips remain source readings.** Examples include the printed index mismatch on scan 054, the English wording “induced a map” on scan 063, and “nivertible” on scan 066. The notes distinguish a visible slip from an unresolved glyph; they do not silently repair either.

### Witness comparison and limits

C is much more complete on the typescript pages. H covers six scans, but its last three supplied files deliberately select dark additions rather than reproduce the full underlying text. Their omissions must be logged to make the consolidated edition complete; they must not be interpreted as evidence that H attempted and failed a full transcription of those leaves. H's editorial synopses and page labels are not source prose.

On the French leaves, both witnesses supplied useful alternatives, but neither was used as a deciding vote. The difficult places are concentrated in local overtyping, dense cancellations, the long handwritten marginal note, and the last letters of words cut at the edge. In the printed Quillen article the text is substantially clearer; the small number of corrections does not mean those leaves were sampled.

The new practical emphasis suggested by this packet is to record exactly **which letters a short underline spans**, to distinguish **keyed x, hand-drawn cup, and typed U** within the same typescript, and to inventory **multiple physical ink layers of a diagram** independently of their mathematical interpretation. The broader error sheet already names omissions, overtypes, slips and reproduction artifacts; this packet confirms those warnings rather than introducing a new general error class.
