## C.13: exact invariants under the outer action comparison

The relevant map is the explicit centre-free pullback isomorphism from the arithmetic surface extension to `Aut(Delta) ×_{Out(Delta)} C2`. An outer conjugator therefore induces an actual isomorphism of these extensions, including in the fixed-point-free case. C.4 supplies section classes and their geometric centralizers. The number of section classes recovers the number of real ovals. The unique nonzero class in each `H_i/2H_i` maps to its oval's modulo-two homology class, with no generator choice. The sum vanishes precisely for dividing real structures; the proof is given by a triangulated two-chain argument. Together these invariants distinguish all allowed real topological types, including all six types in genus three.

Gross–Harris's primary text was read at Proposition 3.1, the realization statement immediately following it, and the discussion of connected real moduli loci in Section 9. Local text extraction: `source_text/gross_harris_C_extra.txt`. The proof invokes the same quotient-surface classification already proved as C.1. It requires no goodness statement for a mapping class group.

## C.14: the finite-cover comparison needed for signs

The proof retains graph flags, so a one-edge loop has two distinct cusp flags even when both map to the same interval section class. Filling is the actual quotient by the closed normal subgroup generated by peripheral inertias. Every open normal, real-stable subgroup of the filled geometric group gives a finite compact etale real cover, with the inverse-image punctured cover. The group isomorphism sends these cover diagrams to the corresponding diagrams.

For a selected oval with centralizer H and a finite quotient P/N, the real fibre is the fixed-coset set. The selected connected real oval has fibre exactly the orbit of the actual oval loop, equal to `H/(H intersect N)`. The group H acts by real deck transformations on that component. The maps of cusp section torsors and global section classes give the actual maps between incidence cycles, commuting with deck actions and with selected sheets. These cyclic graph covers are cofinal. An isomorphism over the base cycle sends one circuit to its positive or negative circuit at every level, forcing the exact same exponent +1 or -1 in H. Cofinality alone would have allowed an arbitrary profinite unit; the proof uses the graph-cover maps to exclude those units.

In the dividing case the integral homology relation is computed from the relative sequence of the pair consisting of the surface and its oval union. Its kernel is exactly the diagonal copy of Z, and tensoring with the flat Z-module Zhat gives the completed relation. This forces all marked-oval signs to agree. Unmarked-oval multipliers are allowed to be units initially, and the same diagonal relation then makes them agree with the marked signs. Labelled real configurations and unordered boundary/interior configurations are compared with the exact topological equivalences of C.11.

## C.18: the remaining fixed-point-free genus-three case

The quotient is the closed nonorientable surface N4. C.18 gives a canonical, explicit comparison with automorphisms of its completed fundamental group. An outer automorphism of the orientation-double-cover group centralizing the deck involution extends uniquely: if e is in the nonidentity coset, a is conjugation by e, and z=e², write b a b⁻¹=Inn(d)a. Centerlessness turns the squared identity into b(z)=d a(d)z, which is exactly the relation needed for e↦de. Quotienting by inner automorphisms first divides the extended centralizer by the deck involution; the canonical positive representative then gives the orientation-preserving centralizer. ## C.19: the finite-index hyperelliptic route is refuted

The parent proposed looking for a finite-index subgroup of Mod(N4) whose unique positive lifts lie in a genus-three hyperelliptic centralizer. An explicit signed two-pants decomposition rules this out. Its connected orientation double is the four-pants graph written in C.19. Each base gluing curve is two-sided and its positive lifted twist has the two opposite exponents +1 and −1. Finite index supplies powers of all three base twists. A positive finite-order class centralizing the subgroup must preserve each of the six lifted curves individually by signed multitwist uniqueness. The six edge labels distinguish the four vertices, so the cut map lands in the trivial product of four pure three-punctured-sphere groups. Its kernel is the injected Z^6 twist group, which has no torsion. This excludes every nonidentity positive finite-order class, including hyperelliptic involutions, without requiring it to commute with the free deck involution beforehand.

The precise primary facts were read in Farb–Margalit, author manuscript Version 5.0: Proposition 2.3, Lemma 3.17 and Proposition 3.20, including the cutting-kernel proof. Stukow 2016 page 1030 supplies the actual positive-lift map for the standard nonorientable hyperelliptic subgroup. Stukow's earlier primary paper arXiv:1402.3905, Theorem 2.1 and its proof, was also inspected: it gives the hyperelliptic subgroup as a C2-extension of the appropriate five-punctured-sphere mapping class group at genus four, not a statement of finite index in Mod(N4). No group-goodness conclusion is drawn from its finite presentation.

The F contributor independently checked the stronger graph proof and specifically verified that signed exponents prohibit exchanging the paired lifts, that edgewise fixation forces vertex fixation, and that the pure cut kernel is torsion-free. The written proof retains connectedness of the orientation double and two-sidedness of all three base curves as requested by that review. The new script code/B/n4_pants_check.py and its output check the finite graph automorphism calculation and the primitive rank-three exponent matrix. The density problem in C.18 remains unfinished.

### Further unsuccessful attacks on the free genus-three density problem

Adding one unordered conjugate pair to a fixed-point-free real curve gives the actual configuration space parametrized by x modulo x∼τ(x), namely its quotient surface N4. The finite-cover comparison therefore recovers the completed group of N4 already present in C.18. This construction supplies no distinguished cyclic generator analogous to an oval circuit, so it has not produced the missing density assertion.

The odd-prime linear representation was also examined. On H1 of the genus-three orientation cover, τ is anti-symplectic, and its two eigenspaces over Z_l are rank-three Lagrangians. Its symplectic centralizer is the image of A↦diag(A,(A^{-1})^t), isomorphic to GL3(Z_l). The discrete Mod(N4) action on the torsion-free integral homology of N4 has determinant ±1, and the same restriction holds in its closure. A matrix in the displayed linear centralizer with another determinant would obstruct density only after lifting it to the actual procongruence centralizer. No surjectivity of that centralizer-to-linear-centralizer map has been established. The lifting obstruction is an instance of the nonabelian fixed-kernel/cohomology calculation in C.15. Consequently this experiment supplies neither an extra profinite reflection nor a counterexample to the density equality in C.18.
