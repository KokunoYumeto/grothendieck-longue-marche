## Independent check of D14

The refinement is valid under the finite-model hypotheses stated at the start of D and the solution-line construction in D10/D11. Let

\[
B=A[t]/(t^q-\alpha),\qquad
K=\ker\bigl(A^4\longrightarrow B,\ (a,b,c,d)\longmapsto s(ct+d)-at-b\bigr).
\]

The monic root extension `A' = A[u]/(u^q-alpha)` is finite free and faithfully flat. After this extension, the previously proved normalized calculation identifies `K tensor_A A'` with a free line generated by a matrix having unit determinant and unit denominator. Flat base change commutes with this kernel. Descent therefore makes K an invertible A-module; no reducedness or noetherian assumption is required for that finite-projective descent statement.

1. On a local generator M of K, determinant is a unit. Replacing M by vM multiplies its determinant by `v^2`. Thus determinant glues to an isomorphism `K^{tensor 2} -> A`. This uses the quadratic homogeneity on a line and does not require polarization or division by 2.
2. The denominator is a B-linear map `K tensor_A B -> B`, sending a matrix to `ct+d`. It is an isomorphism after the faithfully flat root extension, because a normalized generator has unit denominator. It is consequently an isomorphism before that extension.
3. Taking **A-module** determinants and using that B is free of rank q yields

   \[
   K^{\otimes q}\otimes_A\det_A B\simeq\det_A B,
   \quad\text{hence}\quad K^{\otimes q}\simeq A.
   \]

4. If q is odd,

   \[
   K\simeq K^{\otimes q}\otimes_A
   (K^{\otimes 2})^{\otimes -(q-1)/2}\simeq A.
   \]

A global generator then gives the required matrix with unit determinant and unit denominator. This removes the Picard-triviality hypothesis for the stated models with odd p, including q=9 in characteristic 3. It gives no such conclusion for q=4 in characteristic 2, and does not establish necessity of a Picard hypothesis there.

The argument does **not** produce a square root of the determinant of a chosen global generator. D09's example `s=2t` over `F_5[t]/t^5` therefore still rules out a general determinant-one normalization.

The opening D paragraph and D10/D11 remain true sufficient-hypothesis statements. After adding D14, a brief forward reference from that opening paragraph would make the stronger odd-characteristic conclusion easier to find; this is coherence advice, not a further mathematical defect.
