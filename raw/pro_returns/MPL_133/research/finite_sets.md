## Independent calculations and precise limits

### A. The Burnside-ring test and the two degree-two operations

Let $G=C_2$, $1$ the one-point fixed set, and $u$ the free orbit of two points. The additive group $A(C_2)$ is freely generated by $1,u$, and the diagonal action on $u\times u$ has two free orbits, so $u^2=2u$. The two-element-subset functor gives $\mathcal P_2(u)=1$, whereas the symmetric square consists of the free orbit of doubled points plus the fixed unordered pair:

\[
[\operatorname{Sym}^2(u)]=u+1.
\]

Consequently $\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)=\mathcal P_2(E)\amalg E$ is an equivariant decomposition, but the proposed $E^2=\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)+\mathcal P_2(E)$ is not. For the second identity the ordinary set cardinalities agree, which explains why the numerical calculation at the bottom of scan 019 misses the obstruction. Applying the $C_2$-fixed-point mark detects it immediately: the asserted $2+u=2u$ would yield $2=0$.

There is also an objection independent of the uncertain subset symbol: the boxed identity applied to $u$ would require $2\lambda^2(u)=u$, and $u$ is not divisible by two in the free abelian group with basis $1,u$. Thus no integral Burnside-ring value of $\lambda^2(u)$ can satisfy that boxed formula.

If one **defines** the opposite symmetric-power operation by $\lambda_t(x)\sigma_{-t}(x)=1$, its degree-two coefficient is instead

\[
\lambda^2(u)=u^2-[\operatorname{Sym}^2(u)]=u-1.
\]

Bouc and Rökaeus use this opposite-symmetric-power convention, state that it is generally non-special, and give a formula in terms of tuples of disjoint subsets (2009, introduction; Theorem 1.1). Their usage of “λ-ring” should therefore not be silently equated with the manuscript's stronger “λ rather than pre-λ” terminology on scan 006. This citation supplies the convention and comparison; the explicit counterexample above is independently calculated.

### B. What polynomiality survives on scan 021

For $G=1$, the $S_n$-action on injections is free. For any subgroup $H\leq S_n$, not just $H=S_n$,

\[
q_H^n(r)=\frac{r(r-1)\cdots(r-n+1)}{|H|}
=[S_n:H]\binom rn.
\]

Thus there is a degree-$n$ integer-valued polynomial, but for $n\geq2$ it has lower ordinary-degree terms. The degree bound also has a direct finite-difference explanation for actual $G$-sets: in the alternating sum over subsets of $n+1$ disjoint summands, each injection uses at most $n$ summands, and its contributions cancel. This supports numerical-polynomial degree at most $n$, without assuming an ordinary homogeneous polynomial law.

For comparison, quotienting **all** maps by $S_n$ gives multisets and the cardinality $\binom{r+n-1}{n}$. The two constructions should remain separate even after passing to numerical polynomials. None of these counting identities establishes the quasi-isomorphism questioned in PS VI §119, original p. 503.

### C. The partition stabilizer in the uncancelled decomposition

For a partition type $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)$, let $m=\sum_i\alpha_i$ and $\sum_i i\alpha_i=n$. Fix a partition $\pi_\alpha$ of $I_n$ with exactly $\alpha_i$ blocks of size $i$. Its stabilizer is

\[
K_\alpha=\prod_i(S_i\wr S_{\alpha_i})\leq S_n,
\]

and its action on the $m$ blocks factors through

\[
H_\alpha=\prod_i S_{\alpha_i}\leq S_m.
\]

The maps with exact kernel $\pi_\alpha$ are injections from its block set into $E$. Hence on actual finite $G$-sets the corresponding contribution is

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_{G\times K_\alpha}^{G\times S_n}
\operatorname{Inf}_{G\times H_\alpha}^{G\times K_\alpha}
\operatorname{Res}_{G\times H_\alpha}^{G\times S_m}
\tau^m(E),
\]

and $T^n(E)$ is the sum over all such types. The index of $K_\alpha$ is exactly the number written on scan 023,

\[
[S_n:K_\alpha]=\frac{n!}{\prod_i\alpha_i!(i!)^{\alpha_i}}.
\]

This is why cardinality alone does not justify the cancelled induction map: for $n=3$, $E$ a two-point set, the maps using exactly two values form two orbits of size three, whereas inducing the free two-point $S_2$-set directly to $S_3$ gives a single free orbit of size six. The formula here is a research reconstruction from the kernel decomposition, not text to insert into the diplomatic transcription.

### D. Species with support in a subset

Given species data $X_n$, define

\[
F_X(E)=\coprod_{n\geq0}\operatorname{Inj}([n],E)\times_{S_n}X_n.
\]

Separating an injection by its image $S\subseteq E$ gives a canonical decomposition

\[
F_X(E)\cong\coprod_{S\subseteq E}X[S].
\]

This calculation proves both its left-Kan-extension description and pointwise finiteness when each $X_n$ is finite. If the species has finite support, its cardinality is the polynomial

\[
|F_X([r])|=\sum_n |X_n|\binom rn.
\]

Here the diagonal action on the injection factor is free even if $X_n$ has stabilizers. Decomposing each $X_n$ into its finitely many transitive $S_n$-sets identifies finite-support data with finite coproducts of the $h_n/H$ used to define distinguished functors. If instead $X_n=1$ for every $n$, the same formula is the finite sum $\sum_{n=0}^r\binom rn=2^r$, proving that finite values do not imply finite support or finite-degree polynomiality.

For the special functors, the separate component decomposition

\[
H_I(E)=\operatorname{Hom}(I,E)
\cong\coprod_{\pi\in\operatorname{Part}(I)}h_{I/\pi}(E)
\]

is natural for injective target maps. It validates the connected-component observation across scans 024–025 but does not validate the stronger claim that every finite coproduct of components embeds as a summand in one $H_I$; the singleton evaluation in F05 is decisive.

### E. The exact $\mathbb F_2$-representation calculation

Let $M=\mathbb F_2e_1\oplus\mathbb F_2e_2$, with the nonidentity element of $S_2$ interchanging the two basis vectors. Its invariant line $L=\mathbb F_2(e_1+e_2)$ and its quotient (M/L) are both trivial modules, giving the exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbf1\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow\mathbf1\longrightarrow0.
\]

The sequence is not split as an $\mathbb F_2[S_2]$-module sequence, but it is an allowed relation in the exact-sequence Grothendieck group. The induction product of two degree-one trivial representations is $M=\operatorname{Ind}_{S_1\times S_1}^{S_2}\mathbf1$, so $\rho_1^2=2\rho_2$. The homogeneous polynomial $t_1^2-2t_2$ is therefore in the kernel of the proposed map $\mathbb Z[t_1,t_2,\ldots]\to\bigoplus_nR_{\mathbb F_2}(S_n)$. This disproves both the asserted coefficient independence in this example and the proposed sufficiency of locality/$K_0(k)=\mathbb Z$, while leaving the broader classification question open.

Over $\mathbb C$, the Frobenius map sends the integral character lattice with irreducible Specht basis to the lattice with Schur-function basis. That lattice is $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$; the Jacobi–Trudi determinants express its Schur basis integrally in the $h_i$, and the $h_i$ are algebraically independent, giving the polynomial description with integral coefficients. The modern source cited in F12 uses completed rational symmetric functions, so its convention alone should not be quoted as an integral-base theorem; the preceding lattice observation is the additional check. No assertion is made here about every characteristic-zero coefficient ring, integral lattices over arbitrary orders, or the split representation Green ring.

### F. Finite limits and composition of special functors

For products, a pair of injections $I\hookrightarrow E$, $J\hookrightarrow E$ determines a partial bijection between a subset of $I$ and a subset of $J$, recording which images coincide. If $K$ is the quotient of $I\amalg J$ by that partial bijection, the pairs with precisely this pattern form $h_K(E)$; there are only finitely many patterns. Distribution over the finite coproducts proves the product assertion, and $h_\varnothing$ supplies the terminal object. For equalizers, Yoneda sends a natural transformation $h_I\to\coprod_jh_{J_j}$ to a choice of $j$ and an injection $J_j\hookrightarrow I$. Two such transformations agree after evaluation on an injection $I\hookrightarrow E$ precisely when those choices already agree, since postcomposition by an injection cannot identify two different maps. Applying this argument separately to each source summand proves the equalizer assertion and hence all finite limits.

For composition it suffices to consider $h_I\circ F$ with $F=\coprod_{j=1}^a h_{J_j}$. An element chooses, for every $i\in I$, a component $j(i)$ and an injection $J_{j(i)}\hookrightarrow E$, with distinct choices as elements of $F(E)$ for distinct $i$. The kernel partition of the induced map from the finite labelled disjoint union $\coprod_{i\in I}J_{j(i)}$ must be discrete on each individual $J_{j(i)}$ and must satisfy those distinctness constraints. For each allowable partition with quotient $K$, the resulting choices form exactly $h_K(E)$, naturally in injective maps of $E$. There are finitely many component choices and partitions, including the cases of empty $I$ or empty $J_j$, so the composition is special. This argument is independent of any presentation as a summand of a single $H_I$.
