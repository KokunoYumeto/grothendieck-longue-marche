2. The short index-two positivity proof is complete.

Maschke applies since p does not divide 2|Q|.  Restriction of an E-simple
is supported on a single b-orbit of Q-simples: the sum of the corresponding
isotypic spaces is E-invariant and therefore is the whole simple module.
For an orbit {S,S^b} of size two, Ind_Q^E S has division endomorphism ring
End_Q(S), by restriction-induction and reciprocity.  It is simple and
restricts to S+S^b.  Reciprocity shows that every E-simple supported on
this orbit occurs in that induction, so this is the sole restriction type.

For an invariant S, write Ind S as the sum of T_i with multiplicities n_i,
and write Res T_i=S^(e_i).  Restricting gives sum n_i e_i=2.  Again every
supported E-simple occurs, by reciprocity.  Thus the available restriction
multiplicities are all 1, or solely 2.  Every nonnegative integer in the
image lattice is consequently a nonnegative sum of actual restrictions.
Applying this independently to each orbit proves precisely the required
saturation.  It is not a positivity assertion for arbitrary subgroup
restrictions, and no finite-field crossed-product classification is needed.

3. The signed boundary action preserves the actual cusp vector.

Put t=q(tau), m=ord(t), and r=|Q|/m.  The oriented surface cover has r
boundary components of width m.  Their classes in M have exactly the one

This also treats r=1, when the unique boundary class and P are zero.

Since b_Q(t)=t^-1, minus the b_Q-permutation of the cusp basis gives an
actual E-extension of P.  In particular it sends the identity cusp class

  tau^m = sum_(j=0)^(m-1) t^j a = m a_bar,
with a_bar the cyclic average.  Here m is a unit in k.  Thus the signed
action sends the literal averaged vector a_bar to -a_bar; there is no
unproved scalar or basepoint identification.

4. Actual group splitting and cusp norm.

The finite extension 1->M->G->Q->1 splits by coprime averaging (equivalently
Schur--Zassenhaus); its use is explicit and need not be canonical.  Given B,
the automorphism Inn(k)B sends (a,t) to
  (Ba+(1-t^-1)k,t^-1).
The equation for literal cusp inversion is therefore
  (1-t^-1)k=-t^-1 a-Ba.
The right side has zero cyclic average, so it is in the image of 1-t^-1.
If k solves it, the resulting square is Inn(v), v=k+Bk.  Cusp preservation
after squaring implies v in M^t, and Bv=v.  The correction Inn(-v/2) keeps
literal cusp inversion and changes the square to identity.  Division by
two is valid.  This proves existence on the actual G.

6. Consequence for A79 at p=5.

The A79 Q is factor-faithful and noncyclic, and 5 does not divide its order.
The theorem therefore constructs an actual involutory negative action on
G5=Pi/Phi5(L) above its stored negative action, with literal cusp inversion
and the prescribed separate lower frames.

This G5 action has no full negative involution inducing it, even when the
full lift is not required in advance to invert tau literally.  Indeed a
full involution alpha with alpha(tau)=a tau^-1 a^-1 has a_G5 centralizing
tau_G5.  The odd cusp detector gives a_Q in <tau_Q>.  Also alpha(a)a lies
in C_Pi(tau)=<tau>; applying alpha shows this procyclic element equals its
inverse.  Torsion-freeness forces alpha(a)=a^-1.  Replacing a by a tau^u
preserves that norm-one equation.  Choose u to kill its Q-head.  Then
Inn((a tau^u)^-1) alpha is still involutory, inverts tau literally, and
has exactly the stored Q-action.  Composing with canonical c produces the
positive literal-cusp action excluded by A79.  This is a contradiction.

The odd detector itself is correctly typed: a centralizer fixes the class
of tau^m, hence the corresponding cusp coset.  The boundary classes are
distinct for an odd p when r>=2, including the r=2 opposite-class case.
Thus its image in Q is exactly <tau_Q>.

The consequence concerns full actual involutions, using the established
source normalization.  It does not prove that the newly constructed G5
action has an ordinary full positive lift or passes its own central tests.
That distinction must remain explicit in the formal statement.
