Exact research consequence of B88: the product-one / zero-source subgroup
CE,2026-11-16. Separate research note; no formal row added after freeze16.

Notation is the actual common marking of B83-B88. Let b be the product-one
meridian, z the source meridian at v=0,w=2, H the actual source, and R the
ambient both-family filling kernel. Put L0=<b,z> inside pi1(Y).
Then
  L0 is free of rank2,
  L0 intersect H=<z>,
  L0 intersect R=1.
The same conclusions hold for the actual compact closures, with Fhat2 and
Zhat in the first two assertions. This is a source-intersection result,
not a marginal-kernel obstruction: it explicitly has trivial R-intersection.

Proof.
Use the actual maps f1=v and f2=v(w-1)/(1-v) from Y_b, hence Y, to
M=P1 minus{0,1,infinity}. They share the literal base point and agree
pointwise on the source. Let Z=f1*(z)=f2*(z), B=f2*(b). The element Z is
a conjugate of a primitive0-meridian and B a conjugate of a primitive
1-meridian; f1*(b)=1 literally. Their abelianization vectors are signed
(1,0) and(0,1), so they cannot commute in the free group pi1(M): commuting
nontrivial elements of a free group are powers of one element, which would
make those vectors proportional.

The subgroup K=<B,Z> of freeF2 is thus noncyclic. Its finite core covering
graph has free fundamental group, generated by2 and not by1 elements,
hence rank2. The epimorphism from the abstract F(b,z) to K is an
isomorphism by the Hopfian property of finitely generated free groups.
Equivalently this is the ordinary rank-two Nielsen argument. Because this
map factors through the actual subgroup L0, it proves that L0 itself is
free rank2 and that f2* embeds L0 as K.

For w in L0, f1*(w)=Z^{e_z(w)}, while f2*(w) is the image of its full word
under the injective F(b,z)->K. If w belongs to H, the two images are equal,
so injectivity forces w=z^{e_z(w)}. The reverse inclusion is literal because
z is a source word. This proves L0 intersect H=<z>.

The full-topology assertion is elementary and uses these DISCRETE words,
not an arbitrary two-generated closed subgroup of a profinite free group.
A finite connected core graph for K immerses into the two-petal rose.
Complete it to a finite cover by pairing the missing incoming/outgoing
edges of each label; the two deficit counts agree. The original graph is
a subgraph and its fundamental group is a free factor of the finite-cover
group (choose its spanning tree and extend). A retraction therefore extends
any finite quotient of K to that finite-index group. Taking its core in
F2 proves full induced topology, and the retraction embeds K-hat into the
actual Fhat2. Pulling these finite quotients back along f2* proves full
induced topology on L0 in pi1(Y). In particular the map from its actual
closure to K-hat is injective.

For completeness, the Hopfian input can also be proved from the same
finite-cover residual-finiteness argument. A surjective endomorphism of a
finitely generated residually finite group has no nontrivial kernel:
choose a finite-index normal subgroup omitting a hypothetical kernel
element; the repeated inverse images all have the same finite index and
therefore repeat, forcing the kernel element into the original subgroup.

On the actual compact closure of L0, f1* is the continuous z-exponent map
followed by Z-powering, while f2* remains injective. Equality of the two
maps on the compact source then gives precisely the procyclic <z> closure.
Both maps factor through Y_b. Since f2* is injective on L0 and its closure,
their intersections with R and its actual closed filling kernel are1.

Consequences and limits.
For every nonzero integer n, b^{-n}zb^n is outside H. For every nonzero
alpha in Zhat, b^{-alpha}zb^alpha is outside the compact source. Thus b
does not normalize H, and no b-normal subgroup contained in H can contain
z. This prevents enlarging the B83/B84 b-normal SOURCE stage by simply
adjoining the zero source meridian. It does not prevent b from normalizing
the DELETION kernel J_c, and it does not decide arbitrary mixed conjugates
of that kernel. The stage gap from the earlier free-group counterexample
is not removed.

There is also a direct finite detector for each nonzero alpha: choose m
with alpha modm nonzero and use UT3(Z/m). The0/1 meridian images Z,B have
commutator with central coordinate a unit, so the two images of
b^{-alpha}zb^alpha differ by the nonzero central coordinate alpha times
that unit. This is compatible with the mod2 B88 detector and needs no
profinite centralizer theorem.
