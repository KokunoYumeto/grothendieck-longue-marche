2. Actual homotopy calculation.

Write C=Ztilde, A=Ytilde, B=Vtilde, W=A union B, with the actual
connected maps C->A,B and actual groups from B89. Since A and B are
K(free,1)'s, the fibers F(C->A) and F(C->B) have fundamental groups
N_A and N_B. Their second homotopy groups map isomorphically onto
pi2(C). The source hypotheses therefore supply the connectedness
required for the tensor theorem and the hypotheses used previously
for the intersection-commutator conclusion.

Use the two fibration sequences for the homotopy-fiber square. The
sequence F(A->W)->A->W identifies pi2 of its fiber with pi3(W).
The other sequence has pi2(F(C->B))=pi2(C), pi1 of the square fiber
equal to N_A tensor N_B, and pi1(F(C->B))=N_B. Exactness gives

  pi2(C) -> pi3(W) -> N_A tensor N_B -> N_B -> 1.

The tensor map's image is [N_A,N_B]=M. Replacing its target by this
actual image gives exactly the sequence in the note. This derivation
does not require the full deleted complement C to be aspherical.

The contractible real-four-dimensional Xtilde minus its locally finite
node set is2-connected: homotopies needed for pi1 and pi2 have dimension
at most3 and can avoid a discrete codimension-four subset. Relative
excision gives H3(W)=the free abelian group on its lifted nodes, with
finite support for compact singular chains. Degree-three Hurewicz
applies because W is2-connected.

For the classifying-space pushout W_cl, the already proved identities
K_s=N_A N_B and M=[N_A,N_B] make pi1(W_cl) and pi2(W_cl) zero.
Its two other vertices have free fundamental groups. Mayer–Vietoris
therefore gives H3(W_cl)=H2(K_s), and Hurewicz identifies this with
the tensor-kernel. Naturality of the two pushouts and their MV maps
makes the node-sphere map precisely the ordinary Hopf quotient

  pi2(C) -> H2(C) -> H2(K_s) -> 0.

Both N_A and N_B act trivially on this tensor-kernel; they generate
K_s. Thus this ordinary quotient cannot be substituted for the full

retained correctly in the note.

4. Exact cellular quotient and degree shifts.

Assuming the actual equivariant nodal model of Sections2–4, the
product-tree augmented cellular complex is exact:

  0 -> P -> C1 -> C0 -> Z -> 0.

The node complex has degree-two boundary partial*d. The inclusion
of D=[P_node -> P], in cochain degrees-2 and-1, into the reduced
node cellular complex has quotient C1/P -> ker(C0->Z). This last
map is an isomorphism, so D is genuinely quasi-isomorphic to that
reduced complex, with all E and F actions retained.

The complete C2 resolution has finite free terms. Applying its Hom
complex to this bounded cellular complex and totalizing therefore
preserves this quasi-isomorphism: at each total degree the cellular
direction has only finitely many entries. No infinite-product
completion or unsupported interchange of limits is used.

P and P_node have zero even Tate groups and their odd groups come
from negative fixed abelian basis indices. In the two-term complex,
total degree0 can only receive P in degree-1 with Tate degree1.
The incoming map is the corresponding odd group of P_node, whose
image is J_I by B92. Consequently TH^0(D)=I_F2[R]/J_I exactly.

The homology truncation triangle is

  Z2[2] -> D -> M_ab[1] -> Z2[3].

Its long exact Tate-hypercohomology sequence in degree0 is therefore

  H^2(C2,Z2) -> TH^0(D) -> H^1(C2,M_ab)
      -> H^3(C2,Z2) -> TH^1(D).

These are the degree shifts claimed in the note; no edge is omitted.
For a fixed representative z of H^2(C2,Z2), its image in P_node is
a norm because H^2(C2,P_node)=0. Write z=(1+F)p. Then
(1+F)dp=0. The edge class is exactly the diagonal of dp modulo J_I.
This is the already defined kappa, with the same actual coefficient
convention, rather than a newly identified abstract differential.

5. Fixed graph and the correct limitation.

Once the relative geometric comparison is accepted, equivariant
subdivision of the node squares gives a fixed graph and free C2
orbits of cells off it. Relative cellular chains are direct sums
of free Z[C2] modules. Boundedness and their zero Tate groups give
zero relative Tate hypercohomology. Infinite ranks cause no problem:
the finite group's standard resolution has finitely generated terms,
so all relevant kernels and images commute with these direct sums.

At a fixed based fiber the fixed E labels are diagonal R. Lifting
the actual source paths changes those labels by their actual image I.
Thus the fixed components are R/I. This is a component statement,
not an assumption that the source group embeds. Each fixed component
is a graph up to homotopy. Its integral H1 is free, and trivial F
action gives zero odd integral Tate group. Hence degree-zero reduced
Tate hypercohomology is exactly the augmentation kernel of F2[R/I],
which is I_F2[R]/J_I. The right-defect convention gives r^-1 I-I.

This confirms the fixed-space interpretation of the SAME middle term
in the exact sequence. A fixed graph does not imply H^2(C2,Z2)=0,
and its dimension supplies no reason for the edge kappa to vanish.
The newly proved nonasphericity likewise does not alone produce a
nonzero kappa class. Evaluating the actual edge, then the nonlinear
and compact lifting gates, remains a separate problem.
