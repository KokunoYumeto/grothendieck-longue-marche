An actual shared five-generator neck subgroup and its regional detectors
CE research proof, checkpoint21. Uses the actual free quotient Q of B106
and the regional half-plane splitting of B107. No full global gluing or
completion of an arbitrary amalgam is asserted.

1. A boundary marking selecting a single actual family

Write u_i=exp(2*pi*i*i/5), i=1,2,3,4, and choose the literal regular
base value q_*=exp(pi*i/5), in the open unit-circle arc from1 to u_1.
Put Z=(t+1)/(t-1) and Q=(1+q)/(1-q). For the A puncture t=T_u(q),
the exact B106 formula gives

  Z_A=lambda_u*Q+nu_u,
  lambda_u=1-(u+u^-1)/2>0,
  nu_u=(u^-1-u)/2.

If u=exp(i*theta_u), 0<theta_u<2*pi, and q=exp(i*theta), then

  Z_A=i*lambda_u*(cot(theta/2)-cot(theta_u/2)).

At theta=pi/5, theta_u=2*pi*i/5. Strict decrease of cot on(0,pi)
therefore gives Im(Z_A)>0 for every one of the four u. Since

  t=(Z+1)/(Z-1),

each actual A point T_u(q_*) lies in the LOWER open t semicircle.
The lower branch puncture t_*=exp(-pi*i/10), satisfying qt^2=1,
lies there as well. All five are distinct and avoid t=1,-1 by the
exact collision factorization; their partners under F:t->-t lie
in the upper semicircle. This selects A, not a mixture of A and B.

Use the orbifold quotient coordinate y=t^2. The two cone points
are y=0,infinity. There are six ordinary deleted points:

  1, 1/q, T_(u_1)^2, ..., T_(u_4)^2.

At q_* they are distinct on the unit circle. Choose a small open
annular-sector disk D around the five points other than1, cutting
the annulus through a small gap at1. It avoids 0,infinity and1.
The unramified preimage of D under t->t^2 is two disks. Choose D^-
whose arc lies in the lower t semicircle. It contains exactly
T_(u_1),...,T_(u_4),t_*, with no B point and no other retained point.

The five ordinary small meridians, with disjoint arcs in D^-, give
a group K=pi1(D^- minus its five punctures)=F5. All these elements
have trivial orbifold orientation character. Choose the basepoint
and the connector in the small gap adjoining the retained puncture
pair; choose the lift through D^-. This fixes the actual sheet.
Replacing the connector globally conjugates K and makes none of
the injectivity assertions depend on an arbitrary individual
cone conjugation of one generator.

2. The SAME subgroup on both sides of one radial neck

No critical value lies on the unit circle away from mu5. Choose a
sufficiently small disk N about q_* avoiding all critical values
and old punctures. The five moving y punctures and retained y=1
stay distinct there. An isotopy in the annular sector transports
D and its five disjoint arcs over N. Its lower lift continuously
transports D^- and still selects the same four A punctures plus
the same branch puncture. This is one based marking, not two
independently chosen free factors.

For q_-=r_-*q_* with r_-<1 sufficiently close to1, all five moving
y punctures have modulus greater than1. Thus D(q_-) can be chosen
inside the outside bad piece, containing the cone at infinity
only in its complementary region. The orbifold fiber of that bad
piece is C2_infinity * K. For q_+=r_+*q_* with r_+>1 sufficiently
close to1, all five moving punctures have modulus less than1; the
same disk transported through N lies in the inside bad piece,
whose orbifold fiber is C2_0 * K. The disk avoids both cones in
both cases. More precisely, transport the punctured disk and its
arc system through the regular neck, then isotope it at the two
endpoints within the lower t half-plane into the respective bad
side. Keep the connector in the fixed gap throughout. No disk is
required to lie in a bad side at the middle value |q|=1, where its
punctures lie on the separating circle. These endpoint isotopies
preserve the actual based subgroup. The collars can be narrowed
variably as in B107.

Equivalently, at q_* the six complementary corridors between the
two cone disks give the common orbifold fiber marking

  H=C2_0 * C2_infinity * K.

The two bad fiber factors are C2_infinity*K and C2_0*K. They SHARE
K; they are not complementary free factors. The annular-sector
construction makes their shared K literal under the chosen neck
transport. This construction uses the first unit-circle arc. At
an arbitrary other arc, the lower lift can select a mixture of
A and B, and the detector below cannot simply be reused without
another actual comparison.

3. Actual ordinary detector, and the necessary orientation typing

Let Q_A be Q with the four B divisors t=-T_u(q) filled. It is the
free coordinate-swap quotient of the original one-family Y. Its
q projection is an actual bundle over the old base: the generic
fiber is the sphere punctured at

  t=1,-1, +q^-1/2,-q^-1/2, T_(u_1),...,T_(u_4),

and has fundamental group F7. All collisions of these punctures
lie over the removed fifth roots, so this bundle remains genuine
over either complete regional base, including every mixed critical
value. The disk D^- contains five of its punctures and the outside
contains three. Its meridian subgroup K is a free factor F5 of the
fiber F7: choose the omitted sphere meridian outside the disk and
a disjoint geometric arc basis for the other seven punctures.

Consequently K injects into pi1(Q_A over the region), because this
is a bundle over a surface with pi2=0. It also has its full induced
profinite topology there. Indeed its fiber F7 has characteristic
finite-index subgroups cofinal among all finite-index subgroups.
The regional base group is free (cyclic for the complete inner or
outer annulus, trivial for the slit regions used below), so the
bundle group splits over it. For each such characteristic subgroup
L, the action on F7/L has finite image and gives a finite quotient
of the semidirect product retaining the whole F7/L. The free factor
K has its full topology in F7 by the retraction onto K. Combining
these statements separates every prescribed finite-index subgroup
of K by an actual finite quotient of the one-family bundle group.

FILLING B IS NOT F-EQUIVARIANT. There is no asserted homomorphism
from the whole orbifold extension to pi1(Q_A). The map is used only
on the ordinary orientation kernel pi1(Q_region). The subgroup K
lies in that kernel. If an element of K becomes trivial in the bad
orbifold vertex group, it becomes trivial in the whole regional
orbifold group, hence in its embedded ordinary index-two subgroup,
and then in the one-family detector. Therefore it was trivial in K.

For full topology, pull a separating finite quotient back from Q_A
to the ordinary subgroup. Intersect its finite-index kernel with
its conjugate under the external involution. This finite-index
normal subgroup is invariant under the involution and hence gives
a finite quotient of the whole orbifold extension. Equivalently,
use the elementary finite-index core argument. Restricting this
quotient to the actual bad vertex separates K there. Thus

  K -> R_inner and K -> R_outer

are injective and induce the full profinite topology on K. The
corresponding maps of full completions of K into the vertex
completions are injective. The reverse inclusions needed in this
argument are simply the actual ordinary index-two embedding and
the B107 vertex embeddings; neither is an equivariant B filling.

4. A genuine discrete amalgam over a simply connected one-neck base

Choose a slit of the punctured inner disk from0 to its outer
boundary, and a slit of the outer annulus to infinity. They can be
chosen away from q_* and all critical values; their complements
V_- and V_+ are simply connected and retain respectively all nine
inner and all nine outer critical values. Join the two regions by
the single small neck N. Enlarge them over opposite parts of N,
using the above critical-free bundle transport. This creates an
actual open cover of the resulting connected base U, with one
contractible overlap. No other unit-circle neck or old base handle
is included in U.

Over V_-, the empty inside fiber piece contracts to its selected
cone, and B107 van Kampen gives

  E_-=C2_0 * R_-.

Over V_+, the empty outside piece contracts to its other cone,
giving E_+=C2_infinity * R_+. The based common neck group is H
of section2. Its C2_0 factor maps to the free cone in E_- and
the actual cone in R_+; its C2_infinity factor maps to the actual
cone in R_- and the free cone in E_+. Its K maps are precisely
the two injective maps proved in section3.

The group pushout therefore simplifies, by eliminating the two
redundant free cone generators, to the ACTUAL discrete amalgam

  pi1(Q_U) semidirect C2 = R_- *_K R_+.

This algebraic pushout calculation does not assume that the full
fiber H injects into either filled regional group. Only K must
inject, and that has been independently detected. Every critical
fiber in U remains present throughout.

Since K is torsion-free, an involution in one vertex group fixes
only that vertex in the ordinary amalgam tree. Its centralizer in
the amalgam equals its centralizer in that vertex group. In
particular the selected cone's centralizer receives no additional
elements from the opposite regional vertex in this one-neck model.
The selected cone is in R_+, whose own centralizer is NOT evaluated
by this argument. The other cone is handled symmetrically.

5. Exact limits

The construction omits the other four unit-circle necks and the
old q-base handles. Their transports and relation kernels may
introduce further identifications. No whole-surface amalgam, full
nonlinear M freeness, selected fixed-source equality, or kappa
vanishing follows yet. The normal closures need not be complementary.

Although K has its full topology in each vertex group, this alone
does not establish efficiency or a profinite centralizer theorem
for their amalgam. Section4 asserts the discrete group comparison
only. No profinite tree or completion of an infinite-index subgroup
is assumed. The ordinary finite-index detector in section3 is a
proved separate full-topology assertion.
