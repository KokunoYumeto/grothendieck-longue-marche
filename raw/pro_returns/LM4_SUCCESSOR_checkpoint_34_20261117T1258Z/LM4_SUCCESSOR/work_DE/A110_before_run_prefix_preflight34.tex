\statement{A.110}{Proved}{The central modular quotient creates incompatible pure frame choices and a nontrivial cyclic lifting obstruction}
Retain the actual signed congruence map and first relative-central
quotient of A.106--A.107:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 q:F\longrightarrow C\leq\mathrm{SL}_2(\widehat{\mathbf Z}),
 \qquad N=\ker q,\quad N_1=\overline{[N,F]},\\
 B_1=B/N_1,\qquad E_1=F/N_1,\qquad \rho=\operatorname{Ad}(r).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The original \(B=C_2(s)\amalg C_3(r)\), signed matrices and
based free generators are those of A.105 and A.109.
There exists an element
\[
 h\in(E_1')^\rho,\qquad q_4(h)=I,\qquad q_3(h)=-I.
 \tag{A.110.1}
\]
Thus the identity automorphism of \(B_1\) has the two equally pure
quotient \(r\)-frames \(1,h\), with the same \(s\)-frame \(1\).
They have identical abelian normalization. Applying the literal
original LM7\_PUSH~E.65.2--4 recipe to these two choices gives
respectively zero and one.

Consequently that recipe does not descend to an unframed quotient
automorphism when arbitrary pure quotient frames are allowed.
This does not disprove an extension with additional coherent frame
data, or decide whether the actual GT character factors through a
suitably specified action image.

The element \(h\) has no \(\rho\)-fixed lift to \(F'\). For any
lift \(w\in F'\), the cocycle
\[
 w^{-1}\rho(w)\in Z^1(C_3,N_1)
 \tag{A.110.2}
\]
represents a nonbase cohomology class. In particular
\[
 H^1(C_3,N_1)\ne\{*\},\qquad N_1
       \text{ is not a pro-two group}.
 \tag{A.110.3}
\]
The actual full-GT CM parity remains open.
\begin{proof}
\emph{A.110.1. A central pro-two kernel with the exact actual marking.}
Write
\[
 M_2=\ker(F^{(2)}\to C_{(2)}),\qquad
 M_{2,1}=\overline{[M_2,F^{(2)}]},
\]
where \(F^{(2)}\) is the maximal pro-two quotient and \(C_{(2)}\)
is the actual dyadic congruence image. A.106's exact character
comparison gives
\[
 N_1=N\cap F'\cap\pi_2^{-1}(M_{2,1}).
 \tag{A.110.4}
\]
The actual derived images are
\(E_1'=F'/N_1\) and \(C'=q(F')\). Consequently
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow E_1'
       \longrightarrow C'\longrightarrow1,\\
 K=(N\cap F')/N_1
       \hookrightarrow M_2/M_{2,1},\qquad
 nN_1\longmapsto\pi_2(n)M_{2,1}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{A.110.5}
\]
Injectivity is exactly (A.110.4). Hence \(K\) is a compact pro-two
group. It is central in \(E_1\), since \(N_1=\overline{[N,F]}\).
The actual geometric stability proved in A.107 makes these groups
and maps \(\rho\)-equivariant. In particular it uses the full
congruence kernel: the two independent modulo-four characters
force the central sign in the integral cyclic substitution to
vanish on \(N\). The unstable kernel of \(q_3\) on all \(F\)
is not substituted for \(N\).

\emph{A.110.2. An explicit supported three-adic commutator.}
The actual product decomposition proved in A.106 is
\[
 C=C_{(2)}\times\prod_{p\ {\rm odd}}\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z_p).
 \tag{A.110.6}
\]
It is supplied by supported idempotent powers of the original
unipotents, so the following are elements of this actual image.

Choose \(b\in\mathbf Z_3\), \(b\equiv1\pmod3\), with \(b^2=-2\).
For completeness one constructs it successively: if
\(b_n^2+2\equiv0\pmod{3^n}\), exactly one choice
\(b_{n+1}=b_n+3^nt\), \(t\bmod3\), solves the next congruence,
because \(2b_n\) is a unit. The inverse limit is the required \(b\).
In the three-adic factor put
\[
 i_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 j_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\b&-1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{A.110.7}
\]
Both determinants are one, and direct multiplication gives
\[
 i_3^2=j_3^2=-I,\qquad i_3j_3=-j_3i_3,\qquad
 [i_3,j_3]=-I.
 \tag{A.110.8}
\]
Support \(i_3,j_3\) only on the three-adic factor of (A.110.6),
with identity at every other prime. Their commutator
\[
 c_*=(I\text{ at }p\ne3,\ -I\text{ at }p=3)
 \]
therefore lies in \(C'\). It centralizes the integral cyclic
matrix \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
The action on \(C'\) is exactly \(\operatorname{Ad}(J)\), because
the central sign character of the actual cyclic substitution
kills \(F'\). Thus \(c_*\in(C')^\rho\).

\emph{A.110.3. Fixed lifting through the central pro-two kernel.}
Choose a lift \(g\in E_1'\) of \(c_*\). Its crossed defect
\(z=g^{-1}\rho(g)\) belongs to \(K\) and satisfies the order-three
cocycle identity. A.109.4 proves \(H^1(C_3,K)=\{*\}\) for this
actual compact pro-two group. Thus \(z=k^{-1}\rho(k)\) for some
\(k\in K\), and
\[
 h=gk^{-1}
 \]
is fixed by \(\rho\). It has the same congruence image \(c_*\).
This proves (A.110.1). No surjectivity of a map from an abstract
discrete kernel completion is used.

\emph{A.110.4. The exact failure of the unframed E.65 recipe.}
This paragraph uses the original LM7\_PUSH definitions, including
their signed lift. For the identity quotient automorphism, take
\(\beta=1\), so the frame coordinate is \(\rho_F=0\) and the
original shear \(V=U^{8\rho_F}\) is \(I\). Both choices
\[
 A=1\quad\hbox{and}\quad A=h
 \]
are pure \(r\)-frames for that same automorphism: \(h\) centralizes
\(r\). Since \(h\in E_1'\), both have zero abelian vector. The
same \(s\)-frame \(\beta=1\) is retained, and both choices satisfy
the original modulo-four normalization.

The literal original quantities are
\[
 T=q_{12}(A)^{-1}V,\qquad W=q_{12}(\beta)^{-1}V.
 \tag{A.110.9}
\]
For \(A=1\) both are \(I\). For \(A=h\), one has
\[
 T_4=I,\quad T_3=-I,\qquad W=I.
 \]
Equivalently \(T=5I\) modulo twelve. This is not a choice between
two global signed lifts: the original convention fixes the lift
equal to \(I\) modulo four in both cases.
The original rational line
\(\mathbf F_3(1,-1)=\ker(C-I)\) receives the scalar \(-1\) from
the second \(T\). Hence the original
\(\epsilon_3(T)+\epsilon_2(W)\) is zero for \(A=1\) and one for
\(A=h\).

The source audit identifies the precise obstruction to copying
the original proof. LM7\_PUSH~E.65 uses
\(C_B(s)\cap F=C_B(r)\cap F=1\) to obtain unique continuous frames.
Uniqueness then gives its multiplication law (E.65.12), and again
the normalized non-pure-frame laws and transport (E.65.14--17).
Here \(C_{B_1}(r)\cap E_1\) contains the nontrivial \(h\).
Purity, zero abelian vector, and the fixed signed congruence lift
do not restore uniqueness, as the identity example proves.
Consequently arbitrary quotient frames do not supply the
choice-independent character used in that proof.

This is a limitation of that proposed descent, not a new actual
GT element of odd CM parity. A coherently chosen extra framing,
or a different proof that the actual character factors through
an action image, is not excluded. Nor is the original referee's
frame-determination statement for its distinct finite \(G_6\)
quotient contradicted. The already proved conjugacy of
\(\alpha\) and complex conjugation on \(B_1\) alone therefore
does not assign the original CM parity.

\emph{A.110.5. A genuine nonlifting class, and non-pro-two kernel.}
Lift \(h\) to \(w\in F'\). Its defect (A.110.2) lies in \(N_1\)
because \(h\) is fixed, and has order-three norm one.
If it were a coboundary \(n^{-1}\rho(n)\), \(n\in N_1\), then
\[
 \rho(wn^{-1})=wn^{-1}.
 \]
But \(wn^{-1}\in F'\) still has modulo-three image \(-I\), because
\(N_1\le N\). This contradicts \((F')^\rho=1\) from A.109,
or directly the original finite-factor centralizer theorem.
The class is nonbase. If \(N_1\) were pro-two, the compact
coprime-action argument of A.109.4 would make this cohomology
set trivial. This proves (A.110.3).

The nonlifting class detects failure of arbitrary quotient-frame
lifting. It does not identify the original \(A_r\) with either
of the two new quotient choices and does not prove or refute the
actual full-GT CM equality.
\end{proof}
