# Retained-deck group ring and exact fixed-coefficient criterion

This proves the coefficient bookkeeping and cohomological criterion for
the proposed retained-deck enlargement. It does not claim a completed
module comparison, an old-base orbit closure, or global vanishing of
kappa. Numerical elimination over this larger ring is a separate input.

## 1. Actual common Schreier basis and deck action

Let T=F5 be the retained fiber free factor and let beta be its actual
involution. The common free basis of R is indexed by (g,i), where g is
a reduced word of T and i is one of the eight deleted slots. For the A
family this denotes g a_i g^-1. The B common index uses the beta-twisted
head, so the actual involution exchanges the two copies of this same R
basis literally. Write E=R_A times R_B.

Define S_t on the free R basis by S_t(g,i)=(t g,i). The retained deck
element t acts on E by

    theta_t(u,v)=(S_t(u), S_beta(t)(v)).

This is an actual action: S_t S_s=S_(ts). It comes from the literal
retained free-factor section, so no additional unbased transport or
inner correction is omitted. Put G=E semidirect_theta T, with

    (u,v,t)(u',v',s)
      =(u S_t(u'), v S_beta(t)(v'), ts).

The involution is

    F_G(u,v,t)=(v,u,beta(t)).

Indeed swapping E intertwines theta_t with theta_beta(t). Thus this
formula is an automorphism of order two.

## 2. Unique row normalization, hence no retained-deck omission

A basis square of P is determined by vertices u,v in R and edge labels
(g,i),(h,j). It is uniquely

    (u,v,g) · e_(i,j,z),   z=beta(g)^-1 h,

where e_(i,j,z) is the square with identity vertices, A edge (1,i) and
B edge (z,j). The uniqueness follows first from its A retained head g,
then from z, and finally from its two vertices. Consequently P is a
free left Z[G]-module on the rows (i,j,z), with i,j among the eight
deleted slots and z in T.

Every finite original chain therefore has a unique finite Z[G] row
expression. The Z[G] span of the64 seed columns and their F-images is
exactly the submodule generated by all their E and retained-deck
translates. This is not a finite-depth truncation of the T action. It
still omits any additional old-base translate not separately shown to
belong to this span.

The row involution follows directly from swapping the two oriented
edges and reversing the square orientation:

    F(e_(i,j,z)) = -D_z e_(j,i,beta(z)^-1),
    D_z=(1,1,z).

The coefficient and row formulas give F squared equal to1 exactly.

## 3. Private binomial rows remain sufficient

The group G is torsion-free. A finite-order element would have trivial
projection to the torsion-free free group T, hence lie in the
torsion-free product E. This proves the claim without a group-ring
zero-divisor theorem.

For distinct g,h in G, right multiplication by epsilon*g+delta*h,
where epsilon,delta are signs, is injective on Z[G]. If a finite-support
coefficient x satisfies x(epsilon*g+delta*h)=0, its support is invariant
under the nontrivial right translation h g^-1. That translation has
infinite order, so a nonempty finite support is impossible.

Thus a residual column with a private nonzero two-term unit-binomial
row is independent of the other residual columns. Together with exact
ordered unit pivots this proves a free image/kernel calculation over
Z[G]. The noncommutative coefficient order must be preserved in every
recorded elementary basis change.

## 4. A G-valued cocycle need not have a half

Suppose a=(u,v,t) satisfies F_G(a)a=1. Its tail satisfies

    beta(t)t=1.

For a negative fixed module generator with F(e)=-a e, an underlying
abelian basis vector g e is fixed up to that negative sign precisely
when

    F_G(g)a=g.

There need not be such a g. Its T projection w would have to satisfy
beta(w)t=w. This is the exact old-fiber coboundary test, not a condition
that may be discarded because the E-only cocycles always split.

Conversely, if such a w exists, the explicit element

    g=(S_beta(w)(u),1,w)

satisfies F_G(g)a=g. To check the E coordinates, the cocycle equation
gives v S_beta(t)(u)=1, equivalently u=S_t(v^-1); using beta(w)t=w
then gives S_beta(w)(u) S_w(v)=1. Thus the T condition is also sufficient.

## 5. Exact old-fiber solver and cohomological orbit types

In the literal retained basis beta inverts the axis and exchanges the
other two pairs. Hence T^beta=1: every letter of a nonempty reduced word
is changed to a different letter, with no cancellation introduced.
It follows that G^F is exactly the diagonal copy of R in E.

The old-fiber semidirect product with the involution is the explicit
orbifold free product C2*C2*F2. Write its two reflections as tau,tau1,
the axis as tau1*tau, and the paired ordinary generators as y_i and
tau*y_i*tau. For a cocycle t, the element tau*t has order two. Free
product normal form reduces it to a conjugate of exactly one of the
two reflection factors. It is a conjugate of tau exactly when t is
the coboundary beta(w)^-1 w. If tau*t=q tau q^-1, choose the unique
orientation-even q by multiplying by tau on the right when needed;
then w=q^-1 is the required solution. The other reflection class has
no solution. This gives a finite exact word algorithm.

For a monomial module basis, exchanged generator pairs always give
free C2 orbits on the underlying abelian basis. A fixed generator with
coefficient cocycle a contributes fixed basis indices only when the
criterion above succeeds. In that case those indices are a torsor for
G^F=diagonal R. A negative fixed generator contributes one F2[R] to H1
and zero to H2; a positive fixed generator contributes one F2[R] to H2
and zero to H1. If the cocycle does not split, both groups vanish.

Thus a signed-permutation kernel with no positive fixed basis orbit
has H2=0. More generally, finitely many surviving positive fixed
summands can be evaluated by explicit half/norm lifts and actual
source-ideal certificates. These criteria require the complete actual
monomial action, not merely the signs of the module generators.
