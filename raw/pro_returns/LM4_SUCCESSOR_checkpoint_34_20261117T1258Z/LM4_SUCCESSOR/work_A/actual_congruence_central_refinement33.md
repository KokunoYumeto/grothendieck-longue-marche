## 1. Exact marked objects and conclusions

Let \(F=\widehat F(x,y)\), let \(\alpha=(-1,\phi)\) be the actual negative involution in the **full** profinite Grothendieck--Teichmüller group, and let \(u\in F'\) be its canonical solution of \(u=\phi\alpha(u)\). Use the original signed homomorphism
\[
q:F\longrightarrow {\rm SL}_2(\widehat{\mathbb Z}),\qquad
X=q(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
Y=q(y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Write \(C=q(F)\) for the actual compact image and \(N=\ker q\). In the full dihedral quotient, \(x\mapsto s,\ y\mapsto sT\), write \(u\mapsto T^k\), so \(k\in2\widehat{\mathbb Z}\), and put \(d=1+2k\). With \(z=(xy)^{-1}\), retain the exact ordered word
\[
H_d=u(x,y)x^k u(z,x)z^k u(y,z)y^k.                    \tag{1}
\]
No different root or independently chosen conjugator is substituted.

Set
\[
N_0=N,\qquad N_{r+1}=\overline{[N_r,F]}\quad(r\geq0),
\qquad N_\infty=\bigcap_{r\geq0}N_r.
\]
All closures and commutators are in the actual full profinite \(F\). We prove:

1. \(H_d\in N_1=\overline{[N,F]}\); thus (1) is one in \(F/N_1\).
2. The actual normalized endomorphism
   \[
   \psi_d(x)=x^d,\qquad \psi_d(y)=u^{-1}y^d u
   \]
   induces a continuous automorphism of every \(F/N_r\), including \(F/N_0=C\), and of \(F/N_\infty\).
3. The actual involutions \(\alpha\) and \(c(x)=x^{-1},c(y)=y^{-1}\) are conjugate by that induced automorphism on each of these quotients.
4. On the full group,
   \[
   \psi_d\text{ is onto }F
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \psi_d(N_\infty)=N_\infty.                         \tag{2}
   \]

The first conclusion concerns the actual marked \(H_d\); the tower conclusions concern the actual endomorphism. We do not assert \(H_d\in N_r\) for \(r>1\), \(N_\infty=1\), full surjectivity of \(\psi_d\), or triviality of full \(H_d\), \(\Omega_\alpha\), or the remaining CM sign.

## 2. The actual congruence image and its dyadic factor

Let \(C_{(2)}\) be the closed subgroup of \({\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z_2)\) generated by \(X_2,Y_2\). It is pro-two because both generators are identity modulo two and that reduction kernel is pro-two. We do not replace it by all of \({\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z_2)\).

For each prime \(p\), the idempotent \(e_p\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}=\prod_\ell\mathbb Z_\ell\), one in its \(p\)-coordinate and zero elsewhere, gives
\[
X^{e_p}=I+2e_pE_{12},\qquad Y^{e_p}=I-2e_pE_{21}.
\]
These elements of \(C\) are supported on the single \(p\)-coordinate. For odd \(p\), their powers give all upper and lower elementary matrices over \(\mathbb Z_p\), since two is a unit. These generate \({\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z_p)\): a determinant-one matrix has a unit in its first column; elementary row operations, with a row exchange if necessary, reduce it to a diagonal matrix. The diagonal matrices are elementary products, using
\[
w(a)=E_{12}(a)E_{21}(-a^{-1})E_{12}(a)
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\-a^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad w(a)w(-1)=\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1}).
\]
At two the supported generators retain precisely \(C_{(2)}\). Their finite products are dense in the full product, and \(C\) is closed. Hence
\[
C=C_{(2)}\times C_{\rm odd},
\qquad C_{\rm odd}=\prod_{p\text{ odd}}{\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z_p). \tag{3}
\]
Thus the finite image at \(m=2^rn\), \(n\) odd, is the actual image \(C_{2^r}\) times \({\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z/n)\). No approximation theorem about an unrelated abstract subgroup is required.

Each \(G_p={\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z_p)\), \(p\) odd, has pro-\(p\) abelianization: any finite abelian quotient is generated by the images of two pro-\(p\) unipotent cyclic subgroups, so is a \(p\)-group. The abelianization of \(C_{(2)}\) is pro-two.

## 3. The precise external input and a direct product argument

All cohomology here is continuous, with the **discrete trivial** module \(A=\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\). The sole new external theorem is Beyl (1986), Proposition5.7(b), printed page41:
\[
H^2_{\rm cont}({\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z_p),A)=0
\quad\text{for every prime }p.                        \tag{4}
\]
Only the odd-prime cases are used. Its whole statement and proof were freshly read at the original scan. The proof uses the direct limit over finite congruence quotients and vanishing transition maps on degree-two homology. It expressly allows nonzero individual finite-level multipliers at two. We do not infer \(H^2(C_{(2)},A)=0\).

The product step has a direct cocycle proof. Suppose profinite \(P,Q\) satisfy \(H^2(Q,A)=0\), and every continuous bicharacter
\[
P_{\rm ab}\times Q_{\rm ab}\longrightarrow A
\]
is zero. Then projection induces
\[
\mathrm{inf}:H^2(P,A)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2(P\times Q,A). \tag{5}
\]
Restriction to \(P\) proves injectivity. For surjectivity subtract the inflation of a given class's restriction to \(P\); its restrictions to both factors are now trivial. Choose continuous homomorphic sections of the two factors in the corresponding central extension by \(A\). Their commutators lie in \(A\) and form the displayed bicharacter. If it is zero, the sections commute, and their product splits the entire extension. This proves (5).

For pro-\(p\) and pro-\(q\) abelian groups with distinct primes, every such bicharacter is zero. Its image is finite by compactness, and evaluation in its two variables forces each value to have both \(p\)-power and \(q\)-power order. Equations (4)--(5) consequently give \(H^2=0\) for each finite product of odd factors.

A continuous two-cocycle on \(C_{\rm odd}\) depends on only finitely many prime factors: finite image and compactness give a common open normal coordinate neighborhood on which it is constant, containing every factor outside a finite set. The descended function is still a cocycle on that finite product. Thus
\[
H^2(C_{\rm odd},A)=0.                                \tag{6}
\]
Similarly, a continuous bicharacter from \(C_{(2),\rm ab}\times C_{{\rm odd},\rm ab}\) to \(A\) has finite odd-order image from its second variable and two-power order from its first, so vanishes. Applying (5) gives the exact natural isomorphism
\[
\mathrm{inf}:H^2(C_{(2)},A)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2(C,A). \tag{7}
\]
Any degree-two cohomology of the actual dyadic image remains present.

## 4. Five-term comparison with the actual pro-two kernel

Let \(F^{(2)}\) be the maximal free pro-two quotient, let \(\pi_2:F\to F^{(2)}\), and put
\[
N^{(2)}=\ker(F^{(2)}\longrightarrow C_{(2)}).
\]
The actual factorization at two gives
\[
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
1&\to&N&\to&F&\to&C&\to&1\\
 &&\downarrow&&\downarrow\pi_2&&\downarrow\\
1&\to&N^{(2)}&\to&F^{(2)}&\to&C_{(2)}&\to&1 .
\end{array}                                         \tag{8}
\]
No surjectivity assertion for \(N\to N^{(2)}\) is needed.

Both free groups have continuous degree-two cohomology zero with coefficients \(A\). For the free pro-two group, this follows from cohomological dimension one on two-primary torsion and vanishing on odd-primary torsion. Continuous cochains commute with the filtered union of finite torsion submodules of \(A\). The five-term sequences therefore contain
\[
H^1(F,A)\longrightarrow H^1(N,A)^F
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{tr}}H^2(C,A)\longrightarrow0,
\]
\[
H^1(F^{(2)},A)\longrightarrow H^1(N^{(2)},A)^{F^{(2)}}
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{tr}_2}H^2(C_{(2)},A)\longrightarrow0.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
Use the same transgression convention in both rows.

Given a continuous \(F\)-invariant character \(\chi:N\to A\), (7) and the lower surjectivity in (9) supply an invariant \(\chi_2:N^{(2)}\to A\) whose transgression inflates to \(\operatorname{tr}(\chi)\). Naturality under (8) gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\chi-\chi_2\circ\pi_2|_N\bigr)=0.
\]
Exactness then gives a continuous character \(\lambda:F\to A\) with
\[
\chi=\chi_2\circ\pi_2|_N+\lambda|_N.                  \tag{10}
\]
This concerns actual continuous kernels, with no comparison to an abstract discrete kernel completion.

## 5. The same marked hexagon is killed

Three established facts apply to exactly (1):

* A.103 gives \(q(H_d)=1\), hence \(H_d\in N\).
* Each substituted \(u\) is in \(F'\), and the total exponent vector of \(x^kz^ky^k\) is zero, so \(H_d\in F'\).
* A.89, proof §3, proves **the entire image of this particular \(H_d\) is one in \(F^{(2)}\)**. It uses A.42 for the same canonical \(u\), A.45's identification of the local roots with \(\pm d_2\), and the actual prescribed positive pro-two hexagon with root \(d_2\). This is stronger than a dyadic Alexander coefficient equality.

The complete relevant A.42 and A.45 proofs and the complete A.89 source were freshly reopened for this application. The last input has full-GT scope and is not inferred merely from a negative GT0 involution.

Equation (10) now gives \(\chi(H_d)=0\) for every invariant \(\chi\). These are precisely the continuous characters of the profinite abelian group \(N/\overline{[N,F]}\). Such characters separate points, since any nonidentity element survives in a finite abelian quotient and a character of that quotient into \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) detects it. Hence
\[
\boxed{H_d\in\overline{[N,F]}.}                       \tag{11}
\]

## 6. Automorphy on the entire relative-central tower

A.104 proves \(d\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}\), \(\psi_d^{-1}(N)=N\), and automorphy on \(C=F/N\). The same endomorphism preserves every \(N_r\) by its definition using closed commutators.

Inductively suppose \(\psi_d\) is an automorphism on \(F/N_{r-1}\), \(r\ge1\). Write
\[
E_r=F/N_r,\qquad Z_r=N_{r-1}/N_r,\qquad H_r=\psi_d(E_r).
\]
The compact \(Z_r\) is central, and the induction hypothesis gives \(E_r=Z_rH_r\). Thus \(H_r\) is normal and \(E_r'=\overline{[H_r,H_r]}\subseteq H_r\). Since \(N_r\subseteq F'\), one has \(E_{r,\rm ab}=F_{\rm ab}\); the actual map acts on this abelianization by the unit scalar \(d\). Therefore \(H_rE_r'=E_r\), forcing \(H_r=E_r\). A surjective endomorphism of a finitely generated profinite group is injective, by the finitely-many-subgroups-of-each-index argument. Hence it is an automorphism.

Compactness identifies \(F/N_\infty=\varprojlim_rF/N_r\). Compatible automorphisms have compatible inverses, giving automorphy on that limit and
\[
\psi_d^{-1}(N_r)=N_r,\qquad
\psi_d^{-1}(N_\infty)=N_\infty.                       \tag{12}
\]
A.104 gives actual \(\alpha\)-stability of \(N\). The standard \(c\) also preserves \(N\), since \(q\circ c\) is conjugation by \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\). Both preserve all \(N_r\). The exact original identity \(\alpha\psi_d=\psi_dc\) descends, and quotient automorphy gives \(\alpha=\psi_dc\psi_d^{-1}\) there. This is a group-automorphism conjugacy on these quotients; no missing full third-cusp or GT membership is inserted.

Finally \(L=\psi_d(F)\) has full image modulo \(N_\infty\), so \(F=N_\infty L\). Equation (12) gives
\[
L\cap N_\infty=\psi_d(N_\infty).
\]
This proves (2). Surjectivity on the full finitely generated profinite \(F\) would imply automorphy, but the restriction to \(N_\infty\) remains undecided. Nothing in this tower argument puts \(H_d\) in every \(N_r\).
