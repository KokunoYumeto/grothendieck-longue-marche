% Proposed B.92; equivariant node presentation and exact residual syzygy map.
\statement{B.92}{Proved}{The actual node-module contribution is the source left ideal and every additional diagonal fixed lift is measured by a syzygy map}
\label{stmt:B92}
Retain the discrete modules and actions of B.91. The actual node whiskers
can be chosen equivariantly so that the free $\mathbb Z[E]$-module
$P_{\mathrm{node}}$ on the lifted nodes has the action
$F(e_\nu)=-e_{F(\nu)}$, semilinear under the exchange of the two
factors of $E=R_A\times R_B$. Its actual column map
\[
 d:P_{\mathrm{node}}\longrightarrow P,\qquad e_\nu\longmapsto z_\nu
\]
is equivariant and has image $\overline L$. Put $Z_2=\ker d$ and
\[
 J_I=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb F_2[R](i-1)\subseteq I_{\mathbb F_2[R]},
\]
where $I\subseteq R=R_A$ is the actual source kernel. The node indices
are the nodes in the universal filled-surface cover $\widetilde X$;
the separate $E$ coefficients index lifts of their attaching cells
in the $E$-cover, not another copy of the same node index set. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B92-node-image}
 H^2(C_2,P_{\mathrm{node}})=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{im}\bigl(H^1(C_2,P_{\mathrm{node}})
                         \longrightarrow H^1(C_2,P)\bigr)=J_I.
\end{equation}
There is a canonical $R$-linear map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B92-kappa}
 \begin{gathered}
 \kappa:H^2(C_2,Z_2)\longrightarrow I_{\mathbb F_2[R]}/J_I
       ,\qquad \operatorname{im}\kappa=U/J_I,\\
 I_{\mathbb F_2[R]}/J_I
       \cong\ker\bigl(\mathbb F_2[R/I]\xrightarrow{\mathrm{aug}}\mathbb F_2\bigr).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $U$ is the exact obstruction submodule of B.91. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B92-coset-test}
 \begin{gathered}
 (r,F(r))\text{ has an $F$-fixed lift in }K_s/[M,M]
 \\
 \Longleftrightarrow\quad r^{-1}I-I\in\operatorname{im}\kappa .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
If $\kappa=0$, the image of $(\Gamma/[M,M])^F$ in $G_1$ is the actual
source image. Vanishing is not proved here, nor asserted to be necessary
for that projected subgroup equality. No compact coefficient
identification or nonlinear lifting conclusion is included.

\begin{proof}
\emph{B.92.1. Actual equivariant transport.}
On the universal product-pencil base choose the analytic branch
\[
 a(q)=1+\sqrt{1-q},\qquad b(q)=1-\sqrt{1-q},
 \qquad a(q_*)=399/200.
\]
Thus $a+b=2$ and $ab=q$. The actual sphere-fiber coordinate
\[
                         z=\frac{v-a(q)}{v-b(q)}
\]
sends the selected fixed source to $0$ and the other fixed sheet
to $\infty$. Since the actual involution is
$F(v)=(v-q)/(v-1)$, direct substitution gives
\[
 \frac{F(v)-a}{F(v)-b}
       =\frac{1-a}{1-b}\frac{v-a}{v-b}
       =-\frac{v-a}{v-b}.
\]
In this coordinate the involution is the fixed map $z\mapsto-z$.

Along a compact regular base path, all original old and new puncture
trajectories form a finite configuration invariant under this map.
Choose an ambient isotopy vector field giving their prescribed
velocities, extend with compact support, and average under the
involution. The average still has each prescribed velocity, because
the trajectories are paired equivariantly. It can be taken zero
at $0,\infty$. Its flow is an equivariant finite-family transport.
Lifting the transport to the universal old-fiber cover at the chosen
fixed source point preserves equivariance by uniqueness of lifts.
This is used only on regular paths; the actual finite local nodal
models of B.91 handle their endpoints.

The fixed locus of the chosen lift of $F$ on $\widetilde X$ is
exactly $\widetilde S_0$. To verify the nontrivial inclusion, lift a
path in $S_0$ from the basepoint to the projection of a fixed point.
The selected endpoint is fixed and every other endpoint differs
by a deck element $g\in Q$. Such an endpoint is fixed only if
$F(g)=g$. B.87 gives $Q^F=H_b$, which stabilizes precisely the
chosen lifted source component. Thus no additional fixed component
occurs. A lifted node is therefore fixed exactly when it is an
intersection point with $\widetilde S_0$.

\emph{B.92.2. Exact node whiskers and source meridians.}
The simply connected surface $\widetilde S_0$ is a disk, and its
intersection punctures are locally finite. Choose compatible planar
peripheral-tree paths to them. The punctured $\widetilde S$ is the
pullback cover of $S$ associated with $\ker(\pi_1(S)\to\pi_1(S_0))$.
The paths give a free meridian basis of $\pi_1(\widetilde S)$,
whose image is the actual subgroup $I$;
this is subgroup generation, not merely normal generation.
All these source whiskers are fixed pointwise by $F$.
Choose them to avoid the other critical base values, and extend them,
on successive compact disks, to one common peripheral tree for all
critical values. The additional values are not source punctures, so
this retains the chosen source free basis. At nonfixed nodes use
paired fiber whiskers over that same tree. Thus the complete van
Kampen marking of B.91 is retained; independently based lassos are
not being substituted for its actual transports.

At a fixed node the two branches are exchanged. With the transported
generic-fiber marking, its relation is
\[
                         r_\nu=[p_\nu,F(p_\nu)].
\]
The based local source comparison is explicit. The model is
$\{z^2-t^2\ne0\}$, with $F(z,t)=(-z,t)$ and source $z=0$.
A positive source lasso in $t$ winds once positively in both branch
coordinates $z-t,z+t$. It is the product of the two positive
meridians in the local normal-crossing group. The source itself
does not extend as a section of the complement through $t=0$.
Eliminating a base lasso by a regular local section gives the
relation between the two meridians; it does not kill the source
product. Hence the actual source peripheral word at this node is
$p_\nu F(p_\nu)$ after imposing the local commutator.
More precisely, the source whisker ends at the common nearby
basepoint $(z,t)=(0,\epsilon)$, never at the deleted node. Choose
the two local meridian arcs there in an $F$-paired way. Their
words are exchanged literally, and the source whisker is fixed
pointwise. Transporting them back in the equivariant generic-fiber
marking therefore introduces the same actual whisker for both,
with no residual conjugator. The abelian local group identifies
the source lasso with their product before that same based map
is applied globally.

For a nonfixed node choose one whisker and use its $F$-image for
the mate. Order each commutator with the $A$ branch first. The
equivariant transports of B.92.1 and the actual covering-local
presentation of B.91 then give, literally in the generic free group,
\[
                         F(r_\nu)=r_{F(\nu)}^{-1}.
\]
Changing any local whisker only conjugates its actual relation;
the chosen paired paths specify that conjugation. Thus no
uncontrolled based conjugator is discarded.

\emph{B.92.3. The free node cohomology and its image.}
As an abelian signed permutation module, $P_{\mathrm{node}}$ has
basis indexed by $(e,\nu)\in E\times\{\text{lifted nodes}\}$.
Its index involution exchanges the factors of $e$ and sends
$\nu$ to $F(\nu)$, with a minus sign on the basis element.
The fixed indices are exactly the fixed nodes with
$e=(g,F(g))$ diagonal. Every other index orbit has two elements.
The elementary norm calculation therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B92-node-Tate}
 H^1(C_2,P_{\mathrm{node}})
       \cong\bigoplus_{\nu\text{ fixed}}\mathbb F_2[R],
 \qquad H^2(C_2,P_{\mathrm{node}})=0.
\end{equation}

It remains to identify the image of each fixed generator with the
actual source, rather than only its augmented incidence tensor.
Write $p_\nu=n a$ in the generic free product, with $n\in N$
and $a$ in the pure first free factor; its $E$-image is $(a,1)$.
Put $b=F(a)$ and $c=[n]\in P=N^{\mathrm{ab}}$.
Expanding the commutator in the extension with kernel $P$ gives
\begin{align}
 z_{[p_\nu,F(p_\nu)]}
  &=z_{[a,b]}+(1-b)c+(a-1)F(c) \notag\\
  &=z_{[a,b]}+(1-F)\bigl((1-b)c\bigr).
                                                        \label{eq:B92-node-coboundary}
\end{align}
The second equality uses $F(b)=a$ and semilinearity.
The added term is a $C_2$-coboundary. Under B.91's natural
$H^1(C_2,P)\cong I_{\mathbb F_2[R]}$, the pure rectangle
$z_{[a,b]}$ represents $a-1$. But $a$ is exactly the
first-coordinate image of the actual source meridian
$p_\nu F(p_\nu)$. Its diagonal translates give $g(a-1)$.
The chosen source peripheral meridians generate $I$ as a subgroup.
The identities for products and inverses in an augmentation ideal
then show that the generated left ideal is exactly $J_I$.
This proves both assertions of \eqref{eq:B92-node-image}.

\emph{B.92.4. The canonical residual map.}
The equivariant exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow Z_2\longrightarrow P_{\mathrm{node}}
                \longrightarrow\overline L\longrightarrow0
\]
and \eqref{eq:B92-node-Tate} give a surjection
\[
 H^1(C_2,\overline L)\twoheadrightarrow H^2(C_2,Z_2)
\]
whose kernel is the image of $H^1(C_2,P_{\mathrm{node}})$.
The map from $H^1(C_2,\overline L)$ to $H^1(C_2,P)$, followed
by quotienting by $J_I$, therefore descends to the unique map
$\kappa$ in \eqref{eq:B92-kappa}. Its image is $U/J_I$ by
definition of $U$. All these maps commute with diagonal left
multiplication, proving $R$-linearity.

An explicit representative description retains the syzygy contribution.
Represent a class of $H^2(C_2,Z_2)$ by $z\in Z_2$ with $Fz=z$,
modulo norms. Since $H^2(C_2,P_{\mathrm{node}})=0$, choose
$p\in P_{\mathrm{node}}$ such that $z=(1+F)p$. Then $d(p)$
is anti-invariant and
\[
 \kappa([z])=
  \text{the diagonal Tate class of }d(p)\pmod{J_I}.
\]
A different $p$ changes this by the image of an anti-invariant
node chain, which is in $J_I$ by B.92.3. Changing $z$ by a norm
from $Z_2$ leaves $d(p)$ unchanged. This also proves
well-definedness directly.

\emph{B.92.5. The exact coset criterion and its limits.}
The quotient $\mathbb F_2[R]/J_I$ is the permutation module on
the cosets $rI$, and $I_{\mathbb F_2[R]}/J_I$ is its augmentation
submodule. Reducing the right-defect criterion of B.91 through
this quotient proves \eqref{eq:B92-coset-test}.

If $\kappa=0$, the displayed difference of coset basis vectors is
zero exactly when $r\in I$. For any projected fixed target over
$H_b$, choose an actual fixed source lift of its $H_b$ head and
multiply by its inverse. The remaining target lies in $R$, where
this criterion applies. Thus the projected fixed subgroup is the
actual source image under the stated vanishing hypothesis.

This is a sufficient condition, not a necessity assertion: a
nonzero submodule of the coset augmentation module might contain
no difference of two distinct basis vectors. The exact proved
criterion is membership in $\operatorname{im}\kappa$.
No syzygy group, map value, or compact analogue is calculated
here. The actual unaugmented node columns and their relations,
rather than internal normal generation alone, are the remaining
objects to determine. The separate nonlinear kernel $[M,M]$
also remains.

\end{proof}
