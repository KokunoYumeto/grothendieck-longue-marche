## Legibility and the witnesses

The four inverted typescript leaves are substantially easier to read than the manuscript leaves. Their ordinary machine text is sharp enough for letter-by-letter reading, but the handwritten mathematical signs, small indices and the overtyped cell on scan 070 require separate inspection. They are a distinct text, with their own numbering. The two printed stationery leaves are clear; their pale background marks do not support additional text.

The manuscripts vary considerably within a single page. Uncancelled prose is often legible, while compressed abbreviations, detached primes, low torsion indices and short connectors can remain ambiguous. Cancellation strokes sometimes preserve enough of a word to prefer a reading without settling every letter. Such readings remain marked, including inside deleted passages. Scan 081 is a whole cancelled draft: the large crossing strokes are distinct from its smaller local revisions, and both layers matter. Scan 083 has crowded alterations, a margin note and a damaged lower edge. Lost material at a paper edge is not completed from either witness or from the expected argument.

The least secure readings are therefore not distributed evenly. They concentrate in the heavily struck or overwritten passages of scans 065, 067, 069, 071, 074–077, 079, 081, 083 and 084; in very short words whose terminal return could be an s; and in faint residual letters beneath a live replacement. The long displaced tildes over Tate expressions, and indices apparently attached to the next term, need particular care. A formula can be mathematically familiar and still contain a clearly written anomalous index. Clear anomalies have been retained, with their mathematical implications discussed separately.

Neither witness supplies a dependable default. C provides useful local doubt marks, but unmarked words, connectors and indices also needed correction. H sometimes preserves a word or sign better, but also expands abbreviations, supplies expected wording, normalizes notation, or changes the scope of a cancellation. Agreement between C and H was not treated as independent evidence: both derive from the same scans. Changes of confidence are distinguished from changes of the reading itself in the log’s counting description. The remaining uncertainty table should be used with the page text, because a low-confidence old layer can coexist with a high-confidence live replacement.

Only the supplied raster images were read. Enlarging them makes existing pixels easier to inspect; it does not recover detail absent from the scans. The crop filenames and original pixel coordinates allow every local decision to be checked against the supplied image. No OCR, external edition, published reconstruction or TeX compilation was used.

The completed independent adjudications show why a first pass can remain overconfident even after enlarged-crop inspection. They recover short written abbreviations and old layers, but also introduce narrower doubts where a letter’s alphabet, a terminal mark or a cancellation boundary cannot be secured. Scan 070 now has pi at seven projection loci, an uncertain old type character, and the pencil-folio reading 69|63. Scan 082’s lower added word and its cancellation layer remain explicitly uncertain. The typescript body can be clear while its faint foliation is not. Retained readings and broadened doubts are both legitimate audit outcomes; disagreement counts alone do not measure either witness’s accuracy.

The completed scan 077 decisions distinguish strong local readings from unresolved structure. The additive-group G_a and the closing CQFD are secure, while a reduced dotted i needed same-page comparison, several short forms can be full words or abbreviations, and the insertion over connexe remains lisse versus a mathematical group glyph. In the crowded corrections, uncertainty concerns which marks belong to the live or cancelled layer as well as the letters themselves. At the actual right paper edge, widening the crop leaves the final es of topologies uncertain. These cases explain why a familiar argument cannot justify a globally high confidence rating for the leaf.

The 079 audit similarly separates clear live replacements from less readable old letters. Several new doubts concern a possible prime, a connector endpoint, compressed short forms and the blotted torsion prefix; the clipped residue-field letter at the physical top edge remains uncertain even after the frame reaches that edge. The leader and the insertion’s own punctuation are treated separately. The margin pen mark is retained graphically, without promoting neighbouring baseline text into an invented label.

Whole-page cancellation on 081 does not make every underlying sign equally doubtful. Root B025 secures a double-stroked G from its separate left uprights, while the ornate O elsewhere retains its calligraphic/script alternatives and the preceding t/ell remains uncertain. The lower prime placements and the differing equality/isomorphism signs are retained as written; a separate limit shaft still lacks a decisive arrowhead. Short prose under cancellation and in the margin has broadened or retained doubts. The two aligned lower vertical strokes preserve layout without adding a relation that the ink does not establish.

Scan 083's final audit distinguishes the low-armed k in its H0 formula from three high-armed K occurrences, while keeping the tiny degree-field subscript explicitly doubtful. The three capital-K decisions are medium-confidence local readings, not a normalization from mathematical usage.
