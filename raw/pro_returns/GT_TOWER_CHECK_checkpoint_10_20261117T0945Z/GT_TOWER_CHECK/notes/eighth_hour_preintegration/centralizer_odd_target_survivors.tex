\subsection{Genuine parity-one survivors in a large family of finite five-point targets}

Use the actual source
\[
 B=\langle s,r\mid s^2=r^3=1\rangle^\wedge,\qquad
 F=\widehat F(x,y),\quad
 \tau=sr,\quad x=\tau^{-2},\quad y=sxs,\quad z=(xy)^{-1}.
                                                               \tag{OGS.1}
\]
For a finite group $Q$, the following two specified
conditions will be used separately:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \mathrm{(i)}& |Q'|\text{ is odd};\\
 \mathrm{(ii)}&
 O\triangleleft Q\text{ is a specified odd-order subgroup,
 and }\exp(Q/O)\mid4 .
 \end{array}                                                    \tag{OGS.2}
\]
All finite maps below are actual continuous homomorphisms
from the displayed full profinite source.

\begin{result}{A.4.41}{Proved}{Actual gentle survivors for odd-derived and specified odd-normal finite targets}
For every finite family of maps $\rho_i:F\to Q_i$
whose target satisfies either condition in (OGS.2),
there is an actual $g=(1,f_g)\in\widehat{\mathrm{GT}}_0$
with
\[
 \Delta(g)=1,\qquad
 \rho_i(f_g)=1\quad\hbox{for every }i.
                                                               \tag{OGS.3}
\]
The construction retains the signed original modular
frames:
\[
 \vartheta_{12}(\beta_g)=I,\qquad
 \vartheta_{12}(A_g)=5I,\qquad
 \mathcal V_{12,g}=I.
                                                               \tag{OGS.4}
\]
It also retains every one of the six source-deck
conjugates of every $\rho_i$.

Consequently, for every specified finite quotient
$q:\widehat\Gamma_{0,5}\twoheadrightarrow Q$ satisfying
(OGS.2), there is an actual gentle element of parity one
whose literal pentagon is one in $Q$.
For an arbitrary directed family of such five-point
quotients, one actual gentle element of parity one
satisfies all their pentagon equations simultaneously.
It can moreover be chosen in $\ker D_P$, with $\rho_g=0$.

For the concrete capped mixed target $C$ of order $216$
from A.4.28, there is an actual normalized element with
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \Delta(g)=1,\qquad c_2(f_g)=0\pmod3,\\
 f_g(D,V^2)=f_g(V,D^2)=1,\qquad
 f_g(D,V^2)D^{-4c_2(f_g)}
       f_g(V,D^2)V^{-4c_2(f_g)}=1 .
\end{gathered}                                                   \tag{OGS.5}
\]
Here $D,V$ are precisely the two retained generators
of that target.  These are values of a genuine full
gentle element with the signed frames (OGS.4).
\end{result}
\begin{proof}
\paragraph{The auxiliary frame and its six exact source maps.}
Put
\[
             A_0=[[x^4,y^4],z^4]^2\in F',
 \qquad [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}.
                                                               \tag{OGS.6}
\]
Let $\mathcal T=\{1,s,r,r^2,sr,sr^2\}$ be the named
transversal for $F$ in $B$.
In case (i), each $\rho_i(bA_0b^{-1})$ belongs to
$Q_i'$, since $F'$ is preserved by conjugation by $B$.
It therefore has odd order.
In case (ii), each fourth power of an element of
$Q_i$ belongs to $O_i$.
Applying $\rho_i$ to the three fourth-power inputs
of $bA_0b^{-1}$ consequently puts the entire word in
$O_i$, so its order is again odd.
Choose the explicit odd integer
\[
 m=\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}_{i,\ b\in\mathcal T}
                    \operatorname{ord}\rho_i(bA_0b^{-1}),
 \qquad A=A_0^m.
                                                               \tag{OGS.7}
\]
Every test coordinate of $bAb^{-1}$ is one.
This is a finite calculation in the named target
groups, with their actual evaluation maps.

The original signed pure map is
\[
 \vartheta_{12}:F\to\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/12),
 \qquad x\mapsto U^2,\quad y\mapsto L^2,\quad
 U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \vartheta_{12}([[x^4,y^4],z^4])
       =\begin{pmatrix}9&4\\8&9\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \vartheta_{12}(A_0)=5I .
                                                               \tag{OGS.8}
\]
The projective extension
$q_{12}:B\to\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/12)/\{\pm I\}$
is the original modular map of (B.M1); its
restriction to $F$ is the projectivization of
$\vartheta_{12}$.
For every $b\in B$, the projective value of
$bA_0b^{-1}$ is thus $[5I]$.
Its signed value is $5I$, as follows without
extending the signed pure map to $B$:
the image of $F$ modulo four generated by $U^2,L^2$
is abelian, so the image of $F'$ modulo four is
$I$.  The two possible signed lifts of $[5I]$
are $5I$ and $7I$; their reductions modulo four
are $I$ and $-I$, respectively.
Hence
\[
          \vartheta_{12}(bAb^{-1})=5I
                       \quad(b\in B),
                                                               \tag{OGS.9}
\]
using also that $m$ is odd.

\paragraph{The actual finite joint source.}
Form the finite quotient $E_0$ of $B$ by intersecting
the following actual open normal subgroups:
\[
 B',\qquad \ker q_{12},\qquad
 \operatorname{core}_B\ker\vartheta_{12},\qquad
 \operatorname{core}_B\ker\rho_i\quad\text{for every }i.
                                                               \tag{OGS.10}
\]
Each core is the intersection of the six conjugates
indexed by $\mathcal T$, because its original kernel
is normal in $F$.
For specificity these quotients have the usual
induced permutation representations on six copies
of the finite target, with the literal coset transitions;
their restrictions at the identity coset recover the
specified $F$ maps.
The quotient (OGS.10) also retains $B/F$ and $B^{\rm ab}$.

The image of $A$ in this source is central of order
two.  On the induced test coordinates it is the
identity, by (OGS.7).  On the six induced signed
coordinates it is the constant central tuple
$(5I,\ldots,5I)$ by (OGS.9); this tuple commutes
with both coordinate permutations and all coordinate
weights.  Its projective coordinate is the nontrivial
central element $[5I]$, and its abelian coordinate
is zero.  These are all the defining joint coordinates,
proving centrality and exact order two in $E_0$.

Choose
$N=\mathrm{lcm}(12,\exp(E_0))$ and add the actual
quotient $B/(F'F^N)$ to the joint source, obtaining
\[
 E=B/K,\qquad
 K=\ker(B\to E_0)\cap F'F^N,\qquad P=\operatorname{im}(F\to E).
                                                               \tag{OGS.11}
\]
The subgroup $F'F^N$ is characteristic in $F$ and
normal in $B$, so this is again an actual finite
source quotient.  Its pure projection to
$F/F'F^N$ is the named $(\mathbb Z/N)^2$.
The elements $x_E,y_E$ have order exactly $N$:
their orders divide $N$ in each old factor and
are $N$ in this abelian factor.
Since they generate $P$, this proves
\[
                 P^{\rm ab}\cong(\mathbb Z/N)^2
                                                               \tag{OGS.12}
\]
with exactly the two named basis elements.
The added coordinate of $A$ is one because $A\in F'$;
its image $a\in E$ therefore remains central of order two.
The intersection with $B'$ in (OGS.10) ensures
$K\subseteq B'$, retaining the finiteness hypothesis
of the canonical central-cover theorem.

\paragraph{A literal frame in the full canonical central cover.}
Use the full finite canonical cover
\[
 E^c=B/\overline{[B,K]}\twoheadrightarrow E,\qquad
 M=K/\overline{[B,K]}\leq Z(E^c).
                                                               \tag{OGS.13}
\]
Its finiteness is B.2.7--B.2.9.
Let $\widetilde a$ be the image of the actual source
word $A$ in $E^c$.
Since $a$ is central in $E$, the commutator
$t=[\widetilde a,r]$ lies in $M$.
It is central.  Also $\widetilde a^2\in M$ and
$r^3=1$, so the literal commutator identities give
\[
       t^2=[\widetilde a^2,r]=1,\qquad
       t^3=[\widetilde a,r^3]=1,\qquad t=1.
                                                               \tag{OGS.14}
\]
In particular $\widetilde a$ is a pure positive
$r$-frame for the identity automorphism of $E^c$.
Take that identity automorphism, its pure $s$-frame
one, its pure $r$-frame $\widetilde a$, and cusp
conjugator one.
This is an actual member of the finite framed
automorphism group, preserving $M$.
The exact finite-certificate image equality
B.2.7, in its general finite-source form B.2.9,
gives a full positive automorphism $\Phi$ of $B$
fixing $\tau$, whose retained source record is
\[
        \Phi_E=\operatorname{id},\qquad
        \beta_E=1,\qquad A_{\Phi,E}=a .
                                                               \tag{OGS.15}
\]
Thus its raw pair $f=s\Phi(s)$ has $f_E=1$.
The lift used here is supplied by the full canonical
cover itself; no elementary central quotient or
formal pair is substituted for it.

\paragraph{Normalization and the signed character.}
Write $\beta^{\rm ab}=(u,v)$.
The raw pair has abelianization $(v-u,u-v)$.
Put $t_0=v-u$ and make the exact correction
\[
 \Phi_0=\operatorname{Inn}(x^{t_0})\Phi,\qquad
 f_0=y^{t_0}fx^{-t_0},\qquad
 \beta_0=x^{t_0}\beta,\quad A_0^{\rm frame}=x^{t_0}A_\Phi .
                                                               \tag{OGS.16}
\]
This is the normalized multiplier-one gentle element
$g=(1,f_0)$ of B.2.7.
By $\beta_E=1$ and (OGS.12), both $u,v$ belong
to $N\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.
Consequently $t_0\in N\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ and
$x_E^{t_0}=y_E^{t_0}=1$.
Every retained coordinate in (OGS.15) is unchanged:
\[
             (f_0)_E=1,\qquad(\beta_0)_E=1,\qquad
                          (A_0^{\rm frame})_E=a .
                                                               \tag{OGS.17}
\]
This identifies the normalized output pair and its
two frames separately in the same actual source.
The harmonic abelian parameter is $\rho_g=-v$,
so it belongs to $N\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.
Since $12\mid N$, its signed intertwiner
$U^{8\rho_g}$ is $I$ modulo twelve.
Equations (OGS.9) and (OGS.17) give the other
two signed values in (OGS.4).
Their R1 normalized frames are $T=5I$ and $W=I$.
Modulo four both are $I$; modulo three they are
$-I,I$.  Therefore $\epsilon_3(T)=1$ and
$\epsilon_2(W)=0$, proving $\Delta(g)=1$.
Every $\rho_i$ factors through the retained pure
source $P$, so (OGS.17) proves (OGS.3) and all
its six deck-conjugate versions.

$\{g\in H:\Delta(g)=1,\ p(f_g)=1\text{ in the retained targets}\}$

The literal descent formulas (B.C12)--(B.C13)
therefore give $D_P(g)=1$.
