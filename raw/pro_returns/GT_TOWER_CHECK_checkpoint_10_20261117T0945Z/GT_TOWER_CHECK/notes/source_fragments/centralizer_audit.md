## C1. The actual prime-power centralizer statement

Let M be a discrete finite-index subgroup of the orientation-preserving mapping class group Γ0,[n], n≥3. Let J≤M be a finite p-group. Write M→M̂ for full profinite completion. Then BZ Theorem A gives

    N_{M̂}(J)=closure(N_M(J)),   C_{M̂}(J)=closure(C_M(J)).

Derivation of hypotheses: Γ0,n is the fundamental group of the ordered configuration space of n−3 points in P1 minus {0,1,∞}. Its forgetting fibrations have finitely generated free kernels. The associated profinite Birman sequences are exact, with the full profinite topology on those kernels. Free groups are good; the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre comparison and finite cohomology of finitely generated free groups inductively give goodness for finite coefficients. Ordered configuration spaces have finite CW models, so Γ0,n has type FP∞ and finite cohomological dimension. Finite extensions preserve goodness and FP∞ and yield finite virtual cohomological dimension. Thus the stated mixed M is residually finite, good and of finite virtual p-cohomological type.

To address BZ's arbitrary discrete continuous Fp[[M̂]]-module formulation, each such module is the filtered union of its finite M̂-stable submodules: the orbit of any vector is finite, and the Fp-span of finitely many finite orbits is finite. Cohomology of M commutes with these filtered unions because M is FP∞. Continuous cochains of M̂ commute with these unions because every continuous map from a compact profinite Cartesian power to a discrete module has finite image. The finite-module comparison therefore gives the required comparison for every such module. BZ Theorem A applies with R=M̂.

At the end of §4.3, BZ obtains normalizer density from hereditary subgroup-conjugacy separation and Lemma 2.3. Centralizer density then follows without any closure/intersection operation: conjugation N_{M̂}(J)→Aut(J) is continuous with finite target. Its kernel is open, and its intersection with dense N_M(J) is C_M(J), hence is dense in C_{M̂}(J). This also fills the terse reference to Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3 in that paragraph.

## C2. Exact audit of the disputed step

In Boggi Proposition 2.4, the first two equalities

    C_{M̂}(J)=intersection_i C_{M̂}(x_i)
            =intersection_i closure(C_M(x_i))

follow respectively from generation and the individual prime-power result. The last equality with closure(intersection_i C_M(x_i)) is an additional assertion. It is precisely the simultaneous-centralizer density claim. No justification of that additional assertion occurs in the supplied proof or in the opened published proof. The corresponding last equality in BZ Theorem 5.1(iii) has the same issue for general finite J. Neither audit produces a counterexample to the statements of those theorems.

For the finite solvable case under BZ Theorem 5.1's hypotheses, the authors separately prove normalizer density. The open-kernel argument above proves centralizer density from it and supplies a complete repair of that part of the proof. This repair is valid for all finite J for which the relevant normalizer density has been established; it does not derive normalizer density in a new class of examples.

In E.44 there is no application to a nonsolvable group. The specific group is J=H=C2², and direct Theorem A(iii) is enough. Thus Lead 2's protective conclusion is correct, but its description of the dependence is imprecise: E.44 avoids the disputed equality, rather than invoking it only after a solvability repair.

## C3. The solvable genus-zero repair, with topology distinguished from density

The following supplies the route indicated by LM4_SUCCESSOR B.30/B.39.

For finite p-subgroups J of Γ0,[n], both C=C_Γ(J) and N=N_Γ(J) have their full profinite topology induced from Γ. Here is the proof of the topology statement, whose conclusion is stronger than density alone. Remove the unmarked ramification points R of the J-action to obtain S°→T° with free deck action J. The ordinary quotient surface T° is again a punctured sphere with at least three punctures. The discrete centralizer upstairs maps to a finite-index subgroup of Γ(T°) with finite kernel Z(J). A level subgroup avoiding the finite kernel identifies with a finite-index subgroup of Γ(T°). The genus-zero congruence theorem makes its completed action on π̂1(T°) faithful. Restriction to the open normal subgroup π̂1(S°) is faithful for automorphisms: an automorphism restricting identically to an open normal subgroup has each a^{-1}f(a) centralizing that subgroup; its centralizer in a nonabelian free profinite group is trivial. The latter follows, for example, by intersecting the procyclic centralizers of noncommuting powers of two primitive free generators. After adjusting an inner representative upstairs, the same argument shows faithfulness for the relevant outer actions. Hence the completed centralizer embeds in Γ̂(S°).

Filling R has the full profinite configuration kernel K̂, where K=π1(Conf_|R|(S)) (unordered configuration at the orbit R). The extension K⋊J is residually finite, good and of finite virtual cohomological type; an ordered configuration subgroup has finite index and is an iterated extension of finitely generated free groups. The discrete branch-filling centralizer comparison C_Γ(S°)(J)→C_Γ(S)(J) is an isomorphism: both are lifts of the same quotient mapping classes, with the same finite deck kernel. Therefore C_{K⋊J}(J)=Z(J). Direct application of BZ Theorem A(iii) to the one p-group J yields C_{K̂⋊J}(J)=Z(J), and consequently C_{K̂}(J)=1. This proves that the completed centralizer remains embedded after filling R. It gives its full induced topology in Γ̂(S).

The centralizer is finite index in the normalizer. It is open for the induced topology: choose an ambient finite-index normal subgroup U avoiding all nonidentity elements of J; an element of N∩U centralizes J because its commutators with J belong to U∩J. A finite extension of a subgroup with full induced topology has full induced topology when that subgroup is induced-open. This proves the normalizer topology statement. These steps use exactly the prime-power replacement for Proposition 2.4 in Boggi §§3.1–3.2; the nonsolvable statement is unused.

For a finite solvable subgroup H of a finite-index genus-zero mixed mapping class group M, proceed by induction on |H|. A nontrivial finite solvable H has a nontrivial characteristic elementary abelian p-subgroup A: take the p-torsion in the last nontrivial derived subgroup. This subgroup is a p-group, so the preceding topology proof and BZ A give

    N_{M̂}(A)=N_M(A)^.

Put N=N_M(A), Q=N/A. The quotient by the actual A-cover identifies Q with a finite-index subgroup of a genus-zero mapping class group, and N̂/A=Q̂. The group H/A is finite solvable of smaller order. The induction assertion applies to H/A inside Q. Since A is characteristic in H, every normalizer of H normalizes A. The inverse image in N̂ of N_{Q̂}(H/A) is exactly N_{M̂}(H): H is the full inverse image of H/A under N→Q. The same assertion holds discretely. Thus induction proves normalizer density and full induced topology for N_M(H).

For clarity on the topology transfer through the finite kernel A, choose a finite-index subgroup V≤N avoiding A. It maps isomorphically to a finite-index subgroup of Q. Restriction to V reduces the induced-topology comparison to that for Q, and the remaining finite-index extension restores the complete subgroup. Finally the finite conjugation map N_{M̂}(H)→Aut(H) and its open kernel give centralizer density; finite index gives the full topology of C_M(H). This proves the solvable centralizer comparison and its full-topology refinement. In the tetrahedral case H=A4, the induction is exactly A=V4 and H/A=C3, as in LM4_SUCCESSOR B.30.

The proof uses as known inputs genus-zero congruence faithfulness and the profinite Birman exact sequence. The exact source places for those inputs must be fully opened before the main paper marks this complete derivation Known/Proved. The above supplied-source proof has been audited independently at all transfer steps.

## C8. Repair of the determinant-minus-one correction in E.49
