\subsection{The actual mixed mod-four target and common mod-twelve source}

Keep the named groups, source maps, and bases of A.4.52.  Thus
$B_0=C_2*C_3$, $F_0=\langle x,y\rangle$, the full modular kernel
$K$ is free of rank $97$, and the common base kernel $J$ has
$[F_0:J]=288$.  The actual signed five-point quotient has kernel
$N\triangleleft\Gamma_{0,5}$ and quotient $Q_{192}$ of order $192$.
For the two new moduli define the literal maps
\[
 \Gamma_m=\Gamma_{0,5}/(N'N^m),\qquad
 J_m=\bigcap_{i=1}^8\ker\bigl(F_0\xrightarrow{\iota_i}\Gamma_m\bigr),
 \qquad E_m=B_0/J_m\quad(m=4,12),
                                                               \tag{CF4.1}
\]
using precisely the eight ordered insertions (SVM.1).

\begin{result}{A.4.58}{Proved}{The common modulus-four and modulus-twelve kernels retain a free rank-ninety-seven source over mixed target modules}
The exact target kernels and orders are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 N/(N'N^4)&\cong C_4^{100}\oplus C_2^2,
 &|\Gamma_4|&=192\,4^{100}2^2,\\
 N/(N'N^{12})&\cong C_{12}^{100}\oplus C_6^2,
 &|\Gamma_{12}|&=192\,12^{100}6^2.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{CF4.2}
\]
The actual common source kernels have the formulas
\[
                 J_m=(K'K^m)\cap(F_0'F_0^{6m})
                                     \quad(m=4,12).
                                                               \tag{CF4.3}
\]
In particular $J_m$ is normal in $B_0$, and
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 J/J_m\cong C_m^{97},\qquad |E_m|=1728\,m^{97},\qquad
 |F_0/J_m|=288\,m^{97},\\
 (F_0/J_m)^{\mathrm{ab}}=C_{6m}\overline x\oplus
 C_{6m}\overline y,\qquad |\overline x|=|\overline y|=6m.
 \end{gathered}                                                   \tag{CF4.4}
\]
The coefficient maps give the specified actual fibre products
\[
 \Gamma_{12}\xrightarrow{\sim}
        \Gamma_4\mathbin{\times}_{Q_{192}}\Gamma_3,
 \qquad
 E_{12}\xrightarrow{\sim}
        E_4\mathbin{\times}_{B_0/J}E_3.
                                                               \tag{CF4.5}
\]
Both maps send every original source generator to that same generator
in each coordinate.  The coefficient-four common source, its two
directions of factorization through the eight mixed target modules,
its $s,r$ action, and its reduction to the earlier binary source are
retained by explicit matrices in the certificate below.
\end{result}

\begin{proof}
\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The exact target relation module.}\quad
The actual $192$-sheeted cover of the five-generator, six-relator
presentation of $\Gamma_{0,5}$ has $960$ directed generator edges.
The named positive tree removes $191$, leaving $769$ free
Schreier generators.  Rewriting all six relators at every base
state gives the integral $1152$-by-$769$ relation matrix $R$
already retained in A.4.52.  Hence
\[
 N^{\mathrm{ab}}=\mathbb Z^{769}/\operatorname{row}_{\mathbb Z}(R),
                                                               \tag{CF4.6}
\]
with the actual source words and quotient maps.

Perform Gaussian elimination modulo four using only odd pivots.
There are exactly $667$ such pivots.  Eliminating these pivot
coordinates leaves $102$ generators and the actual relation
matrix $2B$, where the binary rank of $B$ is exactly two.
Binary row reduction and its two pivot columns give an invertible
change of those $102$ generator coordinates, after which the
remaining relation subgroup is exactly twice the last two
coordinates.  Consequently its quotient is
$C_4^{100}\oplus C_2^2$.

This is a complete coefficient-four relation calculation.
Odd row operations are invertible modulo four.  On the even
residual, every binary row operation lifts to the corresponding
operation on twice that row, and the full binary rank is retained.
The generator change is triangular after the pivot columns are
ordered first, with unit diagonal.  Thus no residual relation is
discarded.  The program saves $R$, the full odd-pivot reduction,
its pivot columns, the initial projection, the mixed generator
change, both residual rows, and the resulting $769$-by-$102$
projection $P_N$.  It verifies
\[
       RP_N^{\mathrm{free}}=0\pmod4,\qquad
       RP_N^{\mathrm{tors}}=0\pmod2,
                                                               \tag{CF4.7}
\]
where the superscripts designate the first $100$ and last two
columns.  The pivot description proves surjectivity and the exact
kernel in addition to these identities.

The previously executed binary and ternary dimensions $102$ did
not give a free $102$-dimensional coefficient-four module.  The
first attempted lift kept those $102$ coordinates free and failed
on the nonzero even residual.  That failed program and output
are preserved.  The calculation above retains this residual and
therefore proves the first line of (CF4.2) with its actual maps.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Both directions of the common-source map.}\quad
Use the same $289$ named Schreier generators of $J$.  Substitute
each of the eight actual insertion pairs in each generator word,
rewrite it in the target cover, and apply $P_N$.
Its first $100$ coordinates are reduced modulo four, and its
last two modulo two.  Encode each target's latter coordinates
by multiplication by two in $\mathbb Z/4$; this is the injective
map $C_2\to C_4$ onto $2C_4$.  The eight blocks form an exact
$289$-by-$816$ matrix $T_4$ over $\mathbb Z/4$.

Independently rewrite the same $289$ source words in the actual
$97$-generator free modular kernel $K$.  Their exponent rows
modulo four form $Q_4$, of size $289$-by-$97$.
The program retains matrices $L_4,F_4,G_4$ and checks entrywise
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 L_4Q_4=I_{97},\qquad T_4=Q_4F_4,\qquad Q_4=T_4G_4\pmod4,\\
 L_4:\ 97\times289,\qquad
 F_4:\ 97\times816,\qquad G_4:\ 816\times97.
 \end{gathered}                                                   \tag{CF4.8}
\]
These are both actual factorization directions for the mixed
target.  The first map is surjective and has free image
$C_4^{97}$, and the other two equalities identify its kernel with
that of all eight insertion maps.  Since $J'J^4$ is killed by
both actual abelian maps, they give
\[
                   J_4=J\cap(K'K^4),\qquad J/J_4=C_4^{97}.
                                                               \tag{CF4.9}
\]

For the explicit action and coefficient changes, let $S_J,R_J$
be the source substitution matrices of $s,r$ on the $289$ words.
The retained matrices $S_4,R_4$ satisfy
\[
 S_JQ_4=Q_4S_4,\qquad R_JQ_4=Q_4R_4\pmod4.
                                                               \tag{CF4.10}
\]
Let $Q_2,S_2,R_2$ be the earlier binary common-coordinate matrices.
An explicitly retained invertible $97$-by-$97$ matrix $C$ gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 (Q_4\bmod2)C=Q_2,\\
 (S_4\bmod2)C=CS_2,\qquad
 (R_4\bmod2)C=CR_2.
 \end{gathered}                                                   \tag{CF4.11}
\]
Thus a row $v$ in the new common coordinates reduces to
$(v\bmod2)C$ in the old ones.  The program also saves the actual
$102$-coordinate target reduction: reduce its $100$ free and two
binary coordinates modulo two, then apply the saved invertible
target change matrix.  This retains the two torsion coordinates;
reducing the encoded columns $2C_2$ themselves would lose them.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The kernel formula, orders, and CRT.}\quad
The common base satisfies
\[
                         J=K\cap(F_0'F_0^6).
                                                               \tag{CF4.12}
\]
Indeed the fifth retraction and the base abelian quotient give
the inclusion.  The exact exponent lattice (CFK.8) is
$L=\{(a,b):a,b\equiv0\pmod2,\ a+b\equiv0\pmod3\}$.
Its image modulo $6\mathbb Z^2$ has order three.  Therefore the
right side of (CF4.12) has index $96\cdot3=288$ in $F_0$, equal
to the actual common base index, proving equality.

The exponent image of $K'K^4$ is $4L$.  Its intersection with
$6\mathbb Z^2$ is exactly $24\mathbb Z^2$: each coordinate
is divisible by both eight and six, and the converse vectors
already satisfy the remaining sum congruence.  Equations
(CF4.9)--(CF4.12) consequently give
$J_4=(K'K^4)\cap(F_0'F_0^{24})$.
The words $x^{24},y^{24}$ belong to $J_4$, since $x^6,y^6\in K$.
They realize both basis vectors of its exponent lattice; hence
that lattice is exactly $24\mathbb Z^2$.  This proves the
modulus-four abelianization and the exact orders in (CF4.4).
Normality follows from the displayed kernel formula: $K$ is
normal in $B_0$, and $F_0'F_0^{24}$ is characteristic in its
normal subgroup $F_0$.

For any abelian group $M$, the coefficient map
$M/12M\to M/4M\times M/3M$ is an isomorphism.  Applying it to
$N^{\mathrm{ab}}$ gives the first actual fibre product (CF4.5).
The known ternary kernel is $C_3^{102}$.  Combining it with the
first line of (CF4.2) gives the second line, and multiplying by
the unchanged base order $192$ gives the exact target order.

Taking all eight source kernels gives $J_{12}=J_4\cap J_3$.
The two formulas already proved give
\[
 J_4\cap J_3=(K'K^{12})\cap(F_0'F_0^{72}),
                                                               \tag{CF4.13}
\]
because the intersections on each of the two abelianizations
are exactly their coefficient-$12$ and coefficient-$72$ kernels.
The two normal subgroups $J_4,J_3$ have relatively prime indices
$4^{97}$ and $3^{97}$ in $J$, so $J_4J_3=J$.
This proves the second actual fibre product (CF4.5) and
$J/J_{12}=C_4^{97}\times C_3^{97}\cong C_{12}^{97}$.
Multiplication by the fixed base indices gives all stated orders.
Formula (CF4.13) forces the exponent lattice into $72\mathbb Z^2$,
while $x^{72},y^{72}$ belong to its kernel and realize its two
basis vectors.  The modulus-twelve abelianization and the two
exact generator orders follow.
\end{proof}
