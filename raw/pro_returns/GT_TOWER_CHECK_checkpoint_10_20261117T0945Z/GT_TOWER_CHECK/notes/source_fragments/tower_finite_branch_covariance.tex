\subsection{The finite branch-forgetting covariance on every centralizer element}

We make the kernel comparison following (DDP.11) explicit.
Keep $K_*=F(A,B,C)$ and $K=\Pi_{1,9}$ as the two different
surface groups of (FBO.8)--(FBO.11).
For an integer $s\geq2$ define
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 W_s=K_*/\kappa_s(K_*),\qquad
 w_s=(s!)^{(s!)^3},\qquad t=2w_s,\\
 Q_t=\Omega/\kappa_t(\Omega),\qquad
 q_s=(t!)^{(t!)^4}.
 \end{gathered}                                                   \tag{BCV.1}
\]
The kernel $\kappa_s(K_*)$ is normal in $\Omega$, because
$K_*$ is normal and the kernel is characteristic in it.
Thus $\Omega/\kappa_s(K_*)$ is an actual group of order
$2|W_s|\leq t$.  Its regular representation proves
\[
                    \kappa_t(\Omega)\subseteq\kappa_s(K_*).
                                                               \tag{BCV.2}
\]
Use $D_0=Q_t$ in the cone and square-root constructions of
(OBT.1)--(OBT.5).  One uniform numerical choice is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 r=1+576q_s,\qquad r'=1+2^r(r-1),\\
 m=\max\{128q_s2^{r+r'},\;2q_s^2 3^{1+12q_s^2},\;2\},\\
 P=\mathcal E/\kappa_m(\mathcal E),\qquad
 B=(m!)^{(m!)^7},\qquad v=B3^{1+5B},\\
 Z=K/\kappa_v(K),\qquad z=(v!)^{(v!)^{10}}.
 \end{gathered}                                                   \tag{BCV.3}
\]
All comparisons in (FBO.19)--(FBO.25) hold for these actual
groups.  In particular the named outer deck involution is
faithful in $\operatorname{Out}(Z)$, and its normalizer
has the actual inner-action descent to
$\operatorname{Out}(P)$.

Let $q_Z$ be the actual joint map
\[
 \mathcal M\longrightarrow
     \operatorname{Out}(Z)\times\mathfrak S_9,
       \qquad Q_Z=\operatorname{im}(q_Z),\quad
       |Q_Z|\leq9!z^{10}.
                                                               \tag{BCV.4}
\]
The proof of (FBO.27), now using the retained square-cusp
detector over $Q_t$, gives the literal homomorphism
\[
 \theta_s:q_Z(\mathcal C)\longrightarrow
 J_s=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\Gamma_{1,2}
                             \to\operatorname{Out}(W_s)\bigr),
 \qquad\theta_s q_Z|_{\mathcal C}=\rho_s\operatorname{mon}\delta.
                                                               \tag{BCV.5}
\]
Here is the exact kernel argument in these targets.  The two
surface lifts of a based ordinary sphere representative
differ by the named deck involution.  If its $q_Z$ value
is one, either lift lies in the full inverse image of that
deck group.  Hence its based action on $P$ is inner.
Choose the ordinary boundary representative fixing $b_0$
as an element.  Its inner conjugator centralizes $b_0^2$;
(OBT.2) makes its $Q_t$ image even.  Its restriction to
$K_*$ and then to $W_s$ is inner, proving (BCV.5).
This checks the required kernel containment for the exact
quotients in (BCV.1)--(BCV.4), not just for an abstract
isomorphic image.

Apply A.4.54 at $n=9$ to $q_Z$ and denote the resulting
actual quotient and projection by
\[
 \eta_I:\mathcal M\longrightarrow E_I
          \xrightarrow{p_I}Q_Z,
 \qquad p_I(C_{E_I}(\eta_I\iota))=q_Z(\mathcal C).
                                                               \tag{BCV.6}
\]
All finite groups in (BCV.1)--(BCV.6) are fixed before
selecting a gentle parameter.

Take a common universal finite tower level $U_S(M)$ of
A.4.40 with
\[
                  M\geq\max\{|E_I|,m_{v,9},|J_s|\}.
                                                               \tag{BCV.7}
\]
For the actual finite tower action supplied by its five-point
input, choose the nine-point quilt of (DDP.7)--(DDP.10).
Its pure core correction is $c_F$, and put
$\Psi_F^0=\operatorname{Ad}(c_F^{-1})\Psi_F$.
This is an automorphism of the actual common finite group
and fixes the named involution there.
For every ordinary $a\in\mathcal C$, (BCV.6) therefore
places $\pi_Z\Psi_F^0(\bar a)$ in $q_Z(\mathcal C)$.
Consequently
\[
       \mathcal B_F(a)
            =\theta_s\bigl(\pi_Z\Psi_F^0(\bar a)\bigr)
                               \quad(a\in\mathcal C)
                                                               \tag{BCV.8}
\]
is an actual homomorphism $\mathcal C\to J_s$.
Bars and $\pi_Z$ in this formula denote the specified
ordinary quotient maps from $\mathcal M$ through
$U_{1,[9]}(M)$.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The actual finite branch kernel is preserved at the stated output.}\quad
There is an automorphism $\gamma_s$ of the actual surface
group $W_s$ for which
\[
 \mathcal B_F(a)=
      [\gamma_s]\,\rho_s(\operatorname{mon}\delta(a))
                       [\gamma_s]^{-1}\quad(a\in\mathcal C).
                                                               \tag{BCV.9}
\]
Thus $\mathcal B_F$ kills $\ker\delta$.
The conjugation action in (BCV.9) preserves $J_s$, and on
its three specified braid generators it is exactly the
same-parameter action (DDP.5).  This conclusion is a
finite output identity for every element of $\mathcal C$.


\begin{proof}
At the surface quotient $Z$, A.4.40 supplies the actual
peripheral transport automorphism and the covariance
with the nine-point action.  Conjugating it by the based
ordinary lift of the same core multitwist $c_F^{-1}$ gives
the covariance for $\Psi_F^0$.
Because this action fixes the named involution, its outer
surface transport normalizes the specified deck subgroup
in $\operatorname{Out}(Z)$.
The intrinsic quotient (FBO.25) therefore supplies an
actual automorphism $\gamma$ of $P$.

Conjugate the based surface representative inside $Z$ so
that its origin peripheral loop $b_0^2$ maps to the literal
$\lambda$th power.  This does not change the outer
covariance.  The restriction assertion in the inner-action
reconstruction makes
$\gamma(b_0^2)=b_0^{2\lambda}$ in $P$.
The four $t_j$ are original surface peripheral loops, so
$\gamma(t_j)$ is conjugate to $t_j^\lambda$ in $P$.
All these conjugators are images of the actual characteristic
surface group.  The cone and root lemmas (OBT.4)--(OBT.8),
with the containment (BCV.2), consequently give the
automorphism $\gamma_s$ of $W_s$ and its two ordered
peripheral classes.

Choose $a'\in\mathcal C$ with
$q_Z(a')=\pi_Z\Psi_F^0(\bar a)$; it exists by (BCV.6).
The universal-curve covariance gives the equality of the
two actual outer actions on $Z$ indexed by
$\Psi_F^0(a)$ and by the conjugation of $a$ with the
surface transport.  Apply the inner-action descent
homomorphism to $\operatorname{Out}(P)$.
Use ordinary based sphere representatives of both $a$ and
$a'$ fixing $b_0$ as an element.
Conjugating the first by $\gamma$ fixes $b_0^2$, since
$\gamma(b_0^2)=b_0^{2\lambda}$ and $\lambda$ is a unit.
The second also fixes $b_0^2$.
The residual inner conjugator in $P$ therefore centralizes
$b_0^2$.  Its image in $Q_t$ is in $\langle b_0\rangle$
by (OBT.2), and so is even for $\chi_T$.

Both ordinary representatives descend to the actual
$\Omega$ quotient after forgetting the three cone points.
The transport does so by the independently proved cone
permutation and the characteristic-coordinate argument
(OBT.7).  The residual even inner conjugator becomes an
inner automorphism of $W_s$.
Thus the resulting equality in $\operatorname{Out}(W_s)$ is
\[
 \rho_s\operatorname{mon}\delta(a')
       =[\gamma_s]\,
          \rho_s\operatorname{mon}\delta(a)\,[\gamma_s]^{-1}.
                                                               \tag{BCV.10}
\]
By (BCV.5) its left side is precisely (BCV.8).
This proves (BCV.9), including independence of the chosen
ordinary representative $a'$.

The diagonal disk map of (DDP.1) sends the three braid
generators into $\mathcal C$ and its branch projection is
the actual standard four-strand map.
Equation (DDP.6), followed by (BCV.5), shows that (BCV.9)
sends their $J_s$ images to the displayed words (DDP.5).
These three images generate $J_s$.
Moreover (BCV.8) takes its values in $J_s$, while
conjugation by $\gamma_s$ is injective on this finite
subgroup.  Its image has the same order and is contained
in $J_s$, so it equals $J_s$.
This proves that the resulting same-parameter action on
$J_s$ is an actual automorphism.

Finally for any $a\in\mathcal C$ and any braid word $w$
with $\operatorname{mon}\delta(a)=\operatorname{mon}\delta\Delta(w)$,
their quotient lies in the kernel of the original branch
map to this ordered-pole group.  Equation (BCV.9) makes
its finite output one, so the disk-generator computation
gives the same value for $a$ as for that word.
The kernel statement, rather than surjectivity of the
disk map alone, is the reason this comparison applies to
every ordinary normalizer element used in the CM square.
\end{proof}
