\subsection{A common finite peripheral input and its exact geometric output}

\begin{result}{A.4.24}{Proved}{The joint peripheral input has a complete geometric image at the retained output}
Keep the literal surface group and marking
\[
 \Pi=F(A,B,C_1,\ldots,C_8),\qquad
 C_9=([A,B]C_1\cdots C_8)^{-1}
\]
of A.4.8.  Define the following finite quotients, always by the
identity map on these ten named generators:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 G_3&=\Pi/(\gamma_4\Pi\,(\gamma_2\Pi)^3\,\Pi^9),\\
 Q_L&=\Pi/(\gamma_3\Pi\,(\gamma_2\Pi)^3\,\Pi^9),\\
 K_c&=\langle\!\langle C_1,\ldots,C_8\rangle\!\rangle
          \gamma_3\Pi\,\Pi^9,\qquad H_c=\Pi/K_c,\\
 Q_3&=\Pi/\bigl((\gamma_4\Pi\,(\gamma_2\Pi)^3\,\Pi^9)
                         \cap K_c\bigr).
\end{aligned}                                                    \tag{JP.1}
\]
Then $|H_c|=3^6$, $|Q_3|=3^{396}$, $|Q_L|=3^{65}$, and there
are the literal surjections $Q_3\to G_3\to Q_L$ and $Q_3\to H_c$.
The kernels in (JP.1) are preserved by every pure peripheral
automorphism preserving each of the nine named classes up to a
common invertible power.  The verbal output $Q_L$ is also preserved
by permutations of the peripheral labels.

The image in $\operatorname{Out}(Q_L)$ of positive pure peripheral
automorphisms of $Q_3$, with common peripheral power $1$ modulo nine,
is exactly the actual image of the pure orientation-preserving
nine-point mapping class group.  Its order is
$24\cdot3^{126}$.  The corresponding statement for common power
$-1$ follows by the literal reflection (TPL.8).  For every such
input automorphism with power $\lambda$, its ordinary compact
period matrix $C$ modulo nine satisfies $\det C=\lambda$.
Thus the power has been retained in this comparison.

Let $Q_f$ be the image of $\Pi\subset\Sigma=F(a,b)$ in the
characteristic group $\mathcal E^-$ of A.4.18.  There are exact
marked splittings and centers
\[
 Q_f\cong A_f\rtimes G_2,\qquad A_f=C_3^{36},\qquad
 G_2=Q_8\mathbin{\times}_{C_2^2}(\mathbb Z/4)^2,
 \qquad Z(Q_f)\cong C_2^2.                              \tag{JP.2}
\]
Here the action on $A_f$ is the faithful quaternion action, and
the notation $G_2$ in (JP.2) means the order-$32$ signed quaternion
group of (EX.1), not the class-two surface quotient in (TPL.1).
The latter will be written $G_{\mathrm{surf},2}$ in this proof.
The actual joint maps from $\Pi$ onto both
\[
 Q_{\rm in}=Q_f\times Q_3,
 \qquad Q_{\rm out}=Q_f\times Q_L                         \tag{JP.3}
\]
are surjective.  Both factors in each product are characteristic,
and restriction gives the exact product isomorphisms on
$\operatorname{Aut}$ and on $\operatorname{Out}$.  The map
$Q_{\rm in}\to Q_{\rm out}$ is the identity on $Q_f$ and the
specified map $Q_3\to Q_L$.

These marked quotients are obtained from a characteristic finite
input of $\Pi$ by intrinsic, peripheral-preserving quotient maps.
One explicit choice is $\Pi/\kappa_m(\Pi)$, where
$m=32\cdot3^{432}$ and $\kappa_m$ is the intersection of the
kernels of all homomorphisms to $\mathfrak S_m$, as in (DR.3).

Finally, in the actual geometric normalizer of the nine-point
translation subgroup on the retained output, the permutation of
labels is affine.  After removing its actual translation part,
its linear label map $\nu$ and compact period $C$ satisfy
\[
                 \nu=J(C\bmod3)J,
 \qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.           \tag{JP.4}
\]
This is the comparison in the literal downstairs labels of
(TFC.3) and the upstairs handle basis $(A,B)$; the intervening
integral covering map remains the previously specified $3J$.
\end{result}

\begin{proof}
\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The two three-group maps.}\quad
The compact quotient $H_c$ is the free class-two group on $A,B$
of exponent nine.  It has the unique forms
$A^iB^j[A,B]^k$, with $i,j,k\in\mathbb Z/9$, and hence order
$3^6$.  The upper bound follows by collection; equality follows
in the group of upper unitriangular $3$-by-$3$ matrices over
$\mathbb Z/9$, with $A=I+E_{12}$ and $B=I+E_{23}$.
In $H_c$ the image of the kernel of $\Pi\to G_3$ is exactly
$\langle[A,B]^3\rangle$, of order three: the weight-four terms
and ninth powers vanish, and the cubes of the commutator
subgroup generate this displayed subgroup.  Thus the joint
image $Q_3$ adds exactly one factor of three to $|G_3|=3^{395}$.
Equivalently it is the fibre product of $G_3$ and $H_c$ over
\[
 H_0=\Pi/(\langle\!\langle C_1,\ldots,C_8\rangle\!\rangle
                \gamma_3\Pi\,(\gamma_2\Pi)^3\,\Pi^9),
 \qquad |H_0|=3^5.                                    \tag{JP.5}
\]
All maps here send each named generator to the same named
image; this also proves their surjectivity onto the stated
fibre product.

Write $V_3=H_1(\Pi,\mathbb F_3)$ and
$H_9=H_1(\Pi,\mathbb Z/9)$.  Commutator collection in $Q_L$
gives ten generators of order nine and forty-five independent
central commutators of order three.  The exact comparison is
\[
 Q_L\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 G_{\mathrm{surf},2}\mathbin{\times}_{V_3}H_9,
 \qquad x\longmapsto(x\bmod G_{\mathrm{surf},2},\ x\bmod H_9).
                                                               \tag{JP.6}
\]
For completeness, the commutator quotient has the model
$(v,z)\in(\mathbb Z/9)^{10}\times\bigwedge^2\mathbb F_3^{10}$
with multiplication
$(v,z)(v',z')=(v+v',z+z'+2(\bar v\wedge\bar v'))$.
Its ten standard generators generate all central commutators,
and its order is $3^{20+45}$.  This realizes the collection
bound, proving $|Q_L|=3^{65}$ and (JP.6).

The restrictions of an automorphism of $Q_L$ to the two quotients
in (JP.6) determine it.  The same is true after retaining the
outer class on $G_{\mathrm{surf},2}$ and the full map on $H_9$:
an automorphism trivial in both can first be corrected by an
inner automorphism lifted from $G_{\mathrm{surf},2}$; its two
remaining quotient maps are then identities, and (JP.6) makes
it the identity.  Thus
\[
 \operatorname{Out}(Q_L)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Out}(G_{\mathrm{surf},2})
                   \times\operatorname{GL}(H_9)
 \quad\hbox{is injective}.                              \tag{JP.7}
\]
Conversely, compatible automorphisms of the two factors in
(JP.6) act on that fibre product.  This proves the exact meaning
of the joint outer target, including its inner ambiguity.

The compact quotient kills the eight independent puncture generators
and retains the ninth boundary class as $[A,B]^{-1}$.  Thus $K_c$
is preserved by a pure peripheral automorphism: each of those eight
conjugacy classes is separately preserved.  A permutation moving
the ninth label instead carries this quotient to the compact
quotient with that different omitted label.  We retain this based
choice, and apply the $H_c$ projection only to the pure input.
The verbal projections $G_3\to Q_L$ are preserved by every
peripheral permutation.  The characteristic input constructed
below supports all these actions before projection.

In $H_c$ the ninth peripheral word has central image
$[A,B]^{-1}$.  An input automorphism with common peripheral
power $\lambda$ therefore satisfies
$[\varphi(A),\varphi(B)]=[A,B]^\lambda$ in this quotient.
The left side is $[A,B]^{\det C}$, with $C$ the compact
abelian matrix modulo nine.  Since the commutator has exact
order nine, $\det C=\lambda\pmod9$.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The complete actual joint image.}\quad
For power $1$ modulo nine, A.4.9 identifies the
$G_{\mathrm{surf},2}$ projection with the actual positive pure
geometric image, of order $24\cdot3^{107}$.  On $H_9$ the input
has the actual geometric block form
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}C&0\\B&I_8\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/9),\quad
 B\in M_{8,2}(\mathbb Z/9).                              \tag{JP.8}
\]
Choose an actual pure mapping class with the prescribed
$G_{\mathrm{surf},2}$ projection.  The remaining homology
correction is in the kernel of reduction from (JP.8) to its
modulo-three version.  This kernel is elementary abelian of
rank $3+16=19$.  The three actual handle words
$U^3,L^3,UL^3U^{-1}$ have trivial action on
$G_{\mathrm{surf},2}$ and span its three trace-zero compact
entries.  The cubes of the sixteen actual pointpushes have
trivial outer action on $G_{\mathrm{surf},2}$ and span its
sixteen puncture entries.  Thus all nineteen corrections are
realized while retaining the prescribed outer image.  This
proves equality with the complete fibre product in (JP.7),
and gives its order $24\cdot3^{107+19}$.

The certificate further verifies that the $120$ commutators of
the sixteen pointpushes and the $28$ pure pair twists have
\emph{integral} homology identity and span all $91$ actual outer
IA directions from A.4.9.  Hence this realization does not rely
on an undetected integral homology change.  It retains the
$148$ words in the eleven literal free-group automorphisms,
their full $148$-by-$440$ matrix, and $91$ independent row
indices.  The nineteen correction words and their rank-$19$
matrix are retained separately.  The full executed source is
\path{translation_mod9_joint_certificate.py}; its JSON, NPZ,
and text output retain every entry used here.
For power $-1$ the explicit reflection (TPL.8) first changes
both signs, after which the positive argument applies.
No other residue of the common power is identified with one
of these signs.

It is also possible to prescribe the compact period modulo
four independently of this entire $Q_L$ output.  The actual
handle words $U^9,L^9$ act identically on $Q_L$, because the
named handle generators have order nine.  Their matrices
modulo four are $U,L$, generating all
$\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/4)$, of order $48$.
The certificate checks this finite enumeration.  Any remaining
inner quaternion discrepancy is removed by an actual inner
automorphism of $\Pi$: its quotient map onto $Q_8$ is surjective,
and such an inner correction is invisible in (JP.7) and in
ordinary periods.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The faithful factor and the direct products.}\quad
The restriction $\Pi\to\mathcal E^-$ kills the ordinary
modulo-three quotient $H$, so (EX.1)--(EX.4) give exactly (JP.2).
The center of $G_2$ is the preimage of $Z(Q_8)$ and has order
eight.  Its kernel on $A_f$ is the order-four subgroup
$\ker(G_2\to Q_8)$; the central element mapping to $-1\in Q_8$
acts as $-I$.  Since $A_f^{G_2}=0$, a central element of
$A_f\rtimes G_2$ has zero $A_f$ coordinate and lies precisely
in that order-four kernel.  This proves the stated center.

Every homomorphism from $Q_f$ to a three-group kills its
order-$32$ complement.  The central quaternion involution
therefore both acts trivially and inverts the image of $A_f$;
that image is trivial as well.  Thus $Q_f$ has no nontrivial
three-group quotient.  The joint image of $\Pi$ in $Q_f\times Q_3$
is subdirect.  Its two kernels would yield isomorphic common
quotients of $Q_f$ and $Q_3$; any such quotient is a three-group
and hence trivial.  Therefore the joint image is the full
product.  The same argument applies to $Q_L$.

In either product the intersection of kernels of all
homomorphisms to three-groups is exactly $Q_f$.  It is therefore
characteristic.  Its centralizer is $Z(Q_f)\times Q_3$, or
$Z(Q_f)\times Q_L$; the unique Sylow-three subgroup of this
centralizer recovers the other factor as a characteristic subgroup.
Consequently every automorphism restricts separately to the
two factors, and every pair of automorphisms acts on their
product.  Since inner automorphisms also form the corresponding
product, both asserted isomorphisms follow on passage to outer
automorphisms.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The characteristic finite refinement.}\quad
The kernel of $\Pi\to Q_{\rm in}$ has index
$32\cdot3^{432}=m$, so it contains $\kappa_m(\Pi)$.
The following construction records the relative quotient maps
inside $\Pi/\kappa_m(\Pi)$, not just their abstract isomorphism
types.  There is a unique kernel of an epimorphism to $Q_8$
sending all nine named peripheral elements to $-1$.  Indeed
$C_1,\ldots,C_8$ must map to $-1$, and the ninth condition is
$[A,B]=-1$.  It says that the two images of $A,B$ generate
$Q_8$; its $24$ generating pairs form one orbit under
$\operatorname{Aut}(Q_8)$.  The kernel is consequently the
literal $T=\Pi\cap N_8$ already used in A.4.15.
The same argument applies to the finite characteristic input,
since its projection to this $Q_8$ is retained.

Take the modulo-three abelianization of this recovered kernel
and the negative eigenspace of the unique central involution
of its quaternion quotient.  This recovers exactly $A_f$:
the image of $\kappa_m(\Pi)$ in $H_1(T,\mathbb F_3)$ lies in
the positive eigenspace, because $\kappa_m(\Pi)$ lies in the
already specified kernel of $Q_f$.  Quotienting it out therefore
does not change the negative eigenspace.  Joining this quotient
with the marked compact abelian quotient modulo four recovers
$Q_f$, with its actual quotient map.  The compact abelian
quotient is obtained by killing all peripheral classes in
abelianization, so it is invariant under their permutations.
The action of the involution is independent of the chosen lift,
because inner automorphisms of its kernel act trivially on
that abelianization.

The quotient $G_3$ is verbal, and the compact class-two
quotient in (JP.1) is invariant under the pure peripheral
input.  If the translations or arbitrary peripheral
permutations are to act on this strengthened input as well,
replace its kernel by the intersection of its nine conjugates
under actual deck translations, or, uniformly, by all nine
choices of the omitted peripheral label.  This finite
intersection is contained in the same characteristic
refinement after increasing $m$ to the explicit bound
\[
                    m_*=32\cdot3^{36+9\cdot396}.        \tag{JP.9}
\]
The actual product maps (JP.3) remain the projections to the
specified original factor.  Each translated compact quotient
has the same order and the same literal change of marking.
The verbal output $Q_L$ and the faithful output $Q_f$ are
already stable under every peripheral permutation.  Thus all
normalizer and fusion calculations at $Q_{\rm out}$ retain
well-defined projections from this common finite input.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{All label and period residues.}\quad
The nine distinct peripheral homology vectors retain the full
permutation of the punctures.  A permutation normalizing the
regular translation group $H=C_3^2$ is $v\mapsto\nu v+t$:
its value $t$ at zero and its conjugation action $\nu$ on the
translations determine its value at every $v$.  Removing the
actual translation by $t$ leaves the matrix $P_\nu$ on the
rank-eight puncture lattice.  A positive geometric normalizer
has a matrix $g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}C&0\\B&P_\nu
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ modulo nine.  Write the actual deck
matrices from (TFC.4) as
$h_i=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\t_i&P_i
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$h_{\nu i}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\t_{\nu i}&P_{\nu i}
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.  Then $g h_i=h_{\nu i}g$ is exactly
\[
 (I-P_{\nu i})B=t_{\nu i}C-P_\nu t_i,
 \qquad i=(1,0),(0,1).                                 \tag{JP.10}
\]
No quotient of a period matrix by three is used in this equation.

The complete finite calculation enumerates all $48$ possibilities
for $\nu\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)$ and all $648$
possibilities for $C\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/9)$.
For each $\nu$, the $32$-by-$16$ coefficient matrix of (JP.10)
has twelve unit pivots and four pivots equal to three under
invertible row and column operations over $\mathbb Z/9$.
The program retains both operation matrices and checks their
product with the original coefficient matrix.  Applying them
to all $648$ right sides proves that solutions exist exactly
for (JP.4).  Thus precisely $24$ label matrices survive, each
with $27$ ordinary period lifts.  Each such right side has
$3^4=81$ choices of $B$.  Before removing the label translation,
the order of this complete homological normalizer is
$9\cdot24\cdot27\cdot81$.
The executed source
\path{translation_label_period_mod9.py} and its JSON/NPZ output
retain all $31\,104$ tests, all $48$ coefficient matrices,
and their exact diagonalizations.  These are equations in the
literal integral deck matrices, so (JP.4) identifies the label
map required in (NJ.7) with the signed ordinary period map by
an actual morphism.  It makes no assertion that the ambient
transporter itself is an ordinary geometric normalizer.
\end{proof}
