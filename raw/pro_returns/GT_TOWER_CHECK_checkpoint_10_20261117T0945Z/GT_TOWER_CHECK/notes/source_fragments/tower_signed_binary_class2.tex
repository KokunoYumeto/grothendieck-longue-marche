\subsection{The signed exponent-four class-two target and its exact common source}

Put $\Gamma=\Gamma_{0,5}$ and retain the actual signed period
map $q:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow Q_{192}$ on the five positive band
words $(A_{13},A_{23},A_{14},A_{24},A_{34})$.  Write $N=\ker q$.
Retain also the marked free groups $F_0\triangleleft B_0=C_2*C_3$,
$K\triangleleft B_0$, and $J=K\cap(F_0'F_0^6)$ of A.4.60.
Thus $K$ is free of rank $97$, $J$ is free of rank $289$,
and $[B_0:J]=1728$.

\begin{result}{A.4.62}{Proved}{The actual signed class-two target and its marked common source}
The explicitly collected quotient
\[
 T_2=\Gamma/(\gamma_3(N)N^4)
\tag{SBC.1}
\]
has order $192\cdot2^{4870}$.  Its normal kernel over $Q_{192}$
has abelianization $C_4^{100}\times C_2^2$ and commutator subgroup
$C_2^{4668}$.  Let $\iota_1,\ldots,\iota_8:F_0\to\Gamma$ be the
same eight insertion maps as in (SVM.1).  Their exact common kernel is
\[
 \bigcap_{i=1}^8\ker(F_0\xrightarrow{\iota_i}\Gamma\to T_2)
       =J\cap\bigl(\gamma_3(K)K^4\bigr).
\tag{SBC.2}
\]
In particular it is normal in $B_0$.  The resulting actual common
source has the order
\[
 \left|B_0/\bigl(J\cap\gamma_3(K)K^4\bigr)\right|
        =1728\cdot 2^{4850}.
\tag{SBC.3}
\]
Its image of $J$ is identified, by the inclusion $J\hookrightarrow K$
and the maps in (SBC.2), with the free exponent-four class-two quotient
$K/(\gamma_3(K)K^4)$, of order $4^{97}2^{4656}$.

\end{result}

\begin{proof}
We first give the precise collection construction of the target.
The six defining relations in the specified free-by-free presentation
of $\Gamma$ lift along the positive-generator spanning tree in
$Q_{192}$ to $1152$ actual relators in $769$ Schreier generators of $N$.
The retained abelian map from these generators to
$C_4^{100}\times C_2^2$ is the exact map of A.4.58, not a newly chosen
linear quotient.  Its matrix $P$ is recorded with $102$ columns;
its last two columns are interpreted modulo two.

For clarity, the collecting group before imposing the remaining
relations is the free exponent-four class-two group on $102$
generators.  Its normal coordinates are
\[
 (v,z)\in(\mathbb Z/4)^{102}\times
           (\mathbb F_2)^{\binom{102}{2}},\qquad
 (v,z)(w,t)=(v+w,z+t+C(v,w)),
\quad C(v,w)_{ij}=v_jw_i\pmod2\ (i<j).
\tag{SBC.4}
\]
The inverse is $(-v,z+C(v,v))$.  The sign in collecting a commutator
is immaterial modulo two, but the order of the normal factors is
fixed by (SBC.4).  Every fourth power is one, and every commutator
has order two.  Conversely, in any class-two exponent-four group,
$[x,y]^4=1$ and $(xy)^4=x^4y^4[y,x]^6$ imply $[x,y]^2=1$;
thus these are exactly the universal power relations.

We keep the last two coordinates of $v$ modulo two and move their
even parts to two explicit central square coordinates.  The resulting
central list has dimension
\[
 2+\binom{102}{2}=5153.
\tag{SBC.5}
\]
This retains both squares; it does not divide by two in a coefficient
ring.  The integer $v_j/2$ is used only when the selected even
representative is $0$ or $2$ in $\mathbb Z/4$.

Every lifted relator is central after the displayed abelian
projection.  Collect it by (SBC.4), retaining both square entries,
to obtain the matrix $B$ with $1152$ rows and $5153$ columns.
Let $R$ be its literal exponent-sum matrix modulo two.  A central
correction $Z_a$ of the image of a Schreier generator changes the
relator column by $\sum_aR_{ra}Z_a$.  The exact simultaneous row
reduction of $(R,B)$ has $667$ pivots in the first block.  Setting
the corrections of its $102$ free generators to zero determines
all pivot corrections, and leaves a $485$-row central matrix of
rank exactly $485$.  Its projection onto the two square coordinates
has rank two.  Hence the remaining commutator dimension is
\[
 5151-(485-2)=4668,
\tag{SBC.6}
\]
and the group order is $2^{202+4668}=2^{4870}$.

Here is why this construction gives the full quotient in (SBC.1).
In that quotient the selected $102$ original generators form a basis
modulo the Frattini subgroup: their retained coefficient matrix is
invertible modulo two.  They therefore generate the finite two-group.
Their prescribed normal-coordinate values in (SBC.4) also form a
Frattini basis and hence define an automorphism of the free group
in the exponent-four class-two variety.  This changes coordinates
so that their central corrections are exactly zero.  Every other
original generator differs from its prescribed abelian word by an
actual central commutator.  The relator equations for these corrections
are precisely the simultaneous equations just reduced.  They force
the displayed pivot corrections and all $485$ residual central
relations.  Thus the constructed quotient and the universal quotient
of $N$ map to one another, fixing the selected generators.  These
maps are inverse.  This proves the target assertion, rather than
only a lower bound obtained from one representation.

The program \texttt{build\_signed\_binary\_class2.py} saves $P$, $R$,
$B$, every pivot, the central relation reduction, all $769$ corrected
generator images, and both square images.  It checks all $1152$
relators after correction.  Its further multiplication checks use
(SBC.4); no BCH denominator occurs.

For the common source, let $\tau:J\to T_2^8$ be the eight literal
insertions and let
\[
 \nu:J\hookrightarrow K\twoheadrightarrow
               K_2:=K/(\gamma_3(K)K^4).
\tag{SBC.7}
\]
Since $[K:J]=3$, the image of $J$ in the finite two-group $K_2$ has index dividing both three and a power of two; hence $\nu$ is onto.  The known abelian comparison gives the same actual map to $C_4^{97}$.
The program \texttt{build\_binary\_eight\_source.py} evaluates all
$8\cdot289=2312$ images of the recorded free Schreier generators
$g_1,\ldots,g_{289}$ of $J$ by (SBC.4).  Their abelian projection
is rechecked against the earlier matrix entry by entry.
Choose the recorded $97$ indices $b_1,\ldots,b_{97}$ whose rows form
a basis over $\mathbb Z/4$, and write $A_{ij}$ for the resulting
basis coordinates of each $g_i$.  These choices are actual source
words.  Define the actual central discrepancy
\[
 d_i=\tau(g_i)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{97}
                    \tau(g_{b_j})^{A_{ij}}\right)^{-1},
\tag{SBC.8}
\]
with the product in increasing $j$.
All $4656$ commutators of the selected generators have independent
images in the eight target central layers.  Each of the other
$192$ discrepancies has a unique coordinate vector in these
commutators.  Thus $\tau(J)$ is the free exponent-four class-two
group on these $97$ actual images: its order is $4^{97}2^{4656}$,
with no additional central factor.

It remains to identify the kernel by the actual marked map, not
merely by this order.  The program
\texttt{check\_binary\_modular\_factor.py} independently rewrites all
$289$ source words in the $97$ modular Schreier generators of $K$
and collects them in $K_2$.  The $97$ selected words give an
invertible matrix $S$ modulo two, so they generate $K_2$ by the
finite two-group Frattini criterion.  They are a free basis in the
same finite variety.  Their commutator coordinates are compared
using the exact exterior-square transport: for an alternating
coefficient matrix $D$ in the modular basis, its coordinate matrix
in the selected basis is
\[
                  (S^{-1})^{\mathsf T}DS^{-1}\pmod2.
\tag{SBC.9}
\]
All $289$ resulting discrepancy vectors agree with (SBC.8).
Consequently the isomorphism $\theta:K_2\to\tau(J)$ sending
$\nu(g_{b_j})$ to $\tau(g_{b_j})$ satisfies the literal equality
\[
                         \theta\circ\nu=\tau
                    \quad\hbox{on }J.
\tag{SBC.10}
\]
This proves equality of the kernels on $J$.  The joint eight-map
period quotient of $F_0$ has kernel exactly $J$ and order $288$,
so (SBC.10) proves (SBC.2) on the full source.  Both $J$ and $K$
are the already verified normal subgroups of $B_0$;
$\gamma_3(K)K^4$ is characteristic in $K$.  Hence no further
normal-core assertion is needed.  Finally $\nu$ is onto by the
selected Frattini basis, and its order gives (SBC.3).
\end{proof}

For the standard modular generators, the target map is also explicit.
The two quotient maps identify $K/\bigl(J\cap\gamma_3(K)K^4\bigr)$
with $C_3\times K_2$: they are onto and have coprime quotient orders.
The proved equality $\theta\circ\nu=\tau$ identifies the following
map with the extension $\theta:K_2\to\tau(J)$; its $i$th coordinate
has image $\tau_i(J)$.  The inclusion of the two-primary factor gives
\[
 K_2\longrightarrow T_2^8,\qquad
       \overline k\longmapsto
       \bigl(\iota_i(k^3)^{-1}\bigr)_{i=1}^8.
\tag{SBC.11}
\]
Indeed the original insertion on all of $K$ retains its $K/J$
period coordinate; it is not itself identified with this new map.
Cubing kills that $C_3$ coordinate and inversion after cubing is
the identity on the exponent-four factor.  On $J$ the formula
agrees with its literal insertion by (SBC.10) and exponent four.  The export
\texttt{export\_binary\_modular\_actions.py} records this map on
the $97$ standard modular Schreier words, their actual $s$- and
$r$-conjugation words, and the resulting linear and central matrices.
It independently verifies (SBC.11) on all $289$ original $J$ words.
