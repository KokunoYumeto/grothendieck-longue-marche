# Independent audit of the effective configuration-cover argument

Reviewed source: `tower_effective_smith_recognition_notes.md`, sections 1–4.
The scope is the literal configuration space X with nine labels H=C3^2,
one point anchored at zero, and its actual characteristic finite covers.
No conclusion about an unconstructed five-point source map is used below.

## 1. Cohomology killing and bounds

The two-row Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre argument is valid with nontrivial
base action. For a free kernel F, the actual restriction
H1(F,F3) -> H1(F1,F3) is zero once F1 is contained in F'F^3.
Thus the q=1 row map is zero after restriction of the base, regardless
of that base action. The positive q=0 row map factors through the
chosen cohomology-killing subgroup of the base. In each positive total
degree there are only two possible filtration pieces. Two successive
constructions therefore kill the actual cohomology map, not merely its
associated graded. The filtrations are compatible because all extension
maps are the literal inclusions in the induced free-group tower.

The graph quotient F/(N'N^3), N=F'F^3, has order
3^(s+1+3^s(s-1)). Its center maps trivially to F/N. One can see the
last assertion directly: for a nonzero deck translation v in C3^s,
choose a one-dimensional subgroup generated by w that does not contain
v. The cycle given by the cube of a lift of w has edge support on that
line; translation by v moves it to a different support. Graph H1 embeds
in edge chains. Thus the deck action on N/N'N^3 is faithful. This
applies here because every free rank is at least two.

The action kernel in the full extension consequently has intersection
with F inside N. Its index is bounded by |Aut(F/(N'N^3))|, in turn
bounded by the s-th power of the displayed group order because that
group is generated by s elements. The recursive index bound C(l,r) in
the reviewed source follows, including the second-stage Schreier rank
bound 1+a(r-1). Replacing the subgroup by kappa_m(K0) uses its actual
coset action, so containment, H-invariance and the inherited vanishing
maps all follow with the stated factorial bound.

## 2. Finite cell model

The rational periodic arrangement is invariant under the literal label
action `(a.z)_h=z_(h+a)-z_a`. The h-versus-zero hyperplanes include the
one-third coordinate grid, so the closed cells are small enough to
avoid internal identifications upon passing to the torus. The deleted
diagonals are a subcomplex. In the barycentric subdivision their
complement retracts onto the full subcomplex on vertices not in the
deleted subcomplex by deleting the forbidden coordinates and
renormalizing. The denominator is positive exactly on the complement.
This deformation is equivariant and lifts to every actual finite cover.
Further subdivision makes each cell stabilizer fix its cell pointwise.
The claimed dimension bound16 and the chosen fixed grid vertices are
retained by these operations.

## 3. Canonical fixed-basepoint argument

For the canonical H action fixing the chosen lifted grid vertex, the
reviewed relative-cochain argument is correct. The cellular relative
complex is supported in degrees0 through16. The complete C3 resolution
therefore gives a convergent Tate hypercohomology spectral sequence
whose filtration in each total degree has17 possible pieces. A map
zero on ordinary relative cohomology is zero on E2 and on E-infinity;
it moves the abutment by one filtration step. Seventeen consecutive
such maps are zero on the abutment.

For C3, all relative cells outside the fixed set have free orbits.
Their Tate hypercohomology vanishes because free modules have zero Tate
cohomology, with a bounded vertical complex. Restriction to fixed cells
is thus a natural isomorphism. On those fixed cells both complete-
resolution differentials are zero, so a zero Tate map makes every
ordinary relative-cohomology map zero. Applying the same construction
to H/A, after17 blocks for A, proves the289-map assertion.

The fixed sets are finite because they map into the48-point X^H with
finite fibers. Zero pullback on H0 relative to the chosen point means
every top fixed point maps to that chosen bottom point. Fiber points
over the original basepoint are deck cosets; their fixedness is exactly
centralization of H in `(K0/V) semidirect H`. This proves the precise
centralizer projection asserted by the reviewed source.

## 4. Reduced-cone extension: competing finite complements

Here is a complete version that avoids assuming a fixed point for a
competing complement. Retain289 of the actual cohomology-killing covers

`Y_289 -> ... -> Y_0 -> X`.

A complement in the top finite split extension acts on the top cover;
its images act compatibly on all lower covers and project isomorphically
onto the same named H. All maps below refer to this one action.

For every H-space Z use the natural complex

`D(Z) = Cone(F3 -> C*(Z,F3))`.

The map is the constant cochain. For connected Y_i, D(Y_i) is naturally
quasi-isomorphic to C*(Y_i)/constants, supported in degrees0 through16.
Its cohomology is exactly positive ordinary cohomology, so the actual
successive maps kill all its cohomology. This construction is
equivariant and needs no fixed point.

For an actual subgroup A=C3 of H, localization of the cellular pair
and the identical constant-cochain maps give a natural isomorphism

`Tate(A,D(Y_i)) = Tate(A,D(Y_i^A))`.

This remains valid when the fixed set is empty: its reduced cone then
has F3 in degree minus one. After17 original cover maps the left Tate
map is zero by the17-row filtration. A acts trivially on Y_i^A, so the
right complete-resolution differentials vanish; consequently the map
on every ordinary cohomology group of D(Y_i^A) is zero, including
degree minus one.

The actual A-fixed loci have real dimension four. To verify this with
the source coordinates, choose labels a,b generating H with A=<a>.
At a fixed configuration put t=z_a. Then 3t=0 and t is nonzero, while

```
z_(ka)=k t,
z_(b+ka)=u+k t,
z_(2b+ka)=v+k t.
```

There are eight choices of t and two free torus representatives u,v,
with only the literal collision loci removed. Thus each component is
an open subset of a real four-dimensional torus. A fixed lift projects
locally homeomorphically onto this locus, so the same dimension bound
holds in every finite cover. In the admissible cell structure every
fixed cell has dimension at most four.

Therefore D(Y_i^A) has a representative in degrees minus one through
four, with six possible filtration pieces. Now apply the complete
resolution for H/A to the17 block maps above. They are zero on all
ordinary cohomology of these complexes. Six such maps already kill
Tate hypercohomology; the retained17 blocks are a valid uniform bound.
Localization again identifies the resulting map with the one on
`D(Y_i^H)`. Since H/A acts trivially there, the induced map on ordinary
H^(-1) of these reduced cones is zero.

The bottom fixed set Y_0^H is nonempty. Indeed its emptiness would
force emptiness of every upper fixed set, by the equivariant covering
map. For two empty fixed sets D is the constant F3 in degree minus
one, and the induced map is the identity. This contradicts the zero
map just proved. This is a finite nonemptiness argument, not a limit
or an appeal to the existence of a fixed point in a profinite space.

Thus the image of every top complement fixes an actual point in the
bottom cover. That point lies over one of the48 actual fixed
configurations. A path from the reference configuration to that fixed
configuration defines its geometric section; deck translation carries
the chosen path lift to the fixed point, and conjugates that section
to the finite complement. This gives fusion to an actual geometric
section at the stated bottom quotient. Identifying that section with
the particular canonical marked section requires the retained
label/period comparison. It is not inferred from nonemptiness alone.

## 5. Ambient finite representation

The nonzero Euler characteristic verifies total rational-cohomology
faithfulness of the finite deck group: a nonidentity deck element has
zero trace on every cellular chain group and hence Lefschetz number
zero, whereas acting identically on all rational cohomology would give
the nonzero Euler characteristic. Equivariant universal coefficients
show that identity on cohomology modulo three gives identity modulo
three on every integral torsion-free cohomology lattice. Such a
finite-order integral matrix is identity, by the torsion-free principal
congruence subgroup at the odd prime three. This proves the asserted
finite modulo-three cohomology representation is faithful on the deck
quotient, without a claim about H1 alone.

The stated total dimension bound follows from the actual length-eight
free-group extension tower and the bound `(1+s)*dim(M)` on total
cohomology of a free rank-s group with any finite-dimensional module M.
Its compatibility with the ordinary mixed mapping-class group uses the
characteristic subgroup V of the normal point-pushing kernel K0; these
are the exact conjugation maps in the source.

No mathematical defect was found in the canonical-basepoint argument.
The reduced-cone extension above adds a genuine finite fusion result
to a finite family of actual geometric sections. The final marking and
finite pentagon source maps remain separate explicit comparisons.
