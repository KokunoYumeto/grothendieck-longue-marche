\paragraph{An explicit order-$144$ form of the fixed finite test.}
\begin{result}{B.2.11}{Proved}{The exact order-$144$ universal marked source quotient}
The quotients in B.2.10 are particularly small.  With the
actual source marking retained, one has
\[
\begin{gathered}
 B/L_0=B/L_1=\cdots=B/L_4\cong\mathcal T,
 \qquad |\mathcal T|=144,\\
 \mathcal T=
 \bigl(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\times
           \operatorname{Dic}_3\bigr)/\langle(-I,a^3)\rangle.
\end{gathered}                                                     \tag{B.R7}
\]
The canonical marked central kernel over $\mathcal T$ is zero.
The simultaneous six-word image has abelianization
$C_{12}\times C_{12}$, through its specified finite image below.
\end{result}
\begin{proof}
Here
\[
 \operatorname{Dic}_3=
 \langle a,b\mid a^6=1,\ b^2=a^3,\ bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle,
 \quad
 s\longmapsto[(S,b)],\quad r\longmapsto[(R,a^2)],
\]
where
$S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
and
$R=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
over $\mathbb F_3$.  This is an actual marked quotient of
$B$: the two displayed elements have orders two and three.
Projection to $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\times
(\operatorname{Dic}_3/\langle a^3\rangle)$ gives
$A_4\times S_3$ with the marking (B.R6).  Its central kernel
has order two and lies in the derived subgroup, since
$-I\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_3)'$.  The displayed
source pair generates $\mathcal T$: its image surjects onto
$A_4\times S_3$, and centrality plus the derived-kernel
condition then puts the kernel in that image.  The images
of $T_2,T_3$ have orders $24,12$ and commute, as is also
immediate from the two factors of the central product.
Thus $B/L_0$ maps onto the group in (B.R7).

For clarity, the finite upper bound and the equality of all
five quotients have the following complete central-cover
certificate.  The exact code
\path{check_universal_six_source.g} computes these four
cohomology spaces and restrictions to the specified source
torsion subgroup:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 \text{base}&p&\dim H^2(-,\mathbb F_p)&
 \operatorname{rank}(\text{source restriction})&
 \dim(\text{admissible kernel})\\\hline
 S_3\times A_4&2&2&1&1\\
 S_3\times A_4&3&1&1&0\\
 \mathcal T&2&1&1&0\\

\end{array}                                                     \tag{B.R8}
\]
The certificate file retains the complete polycyclic
presentations, both specified generator coordinates, the

The vectors in the four rows are respectively

The restriction criterion is the actual marked-source
criterion proved in (B.M9), with its identical three-primary
version.  Transfer excludes primes other than two and three.

It follows that the canonical central kernel over
$S_3\times A_4$ is a cyclic two-group.  The marked central
quotient $\mathcal T$ realizes its quotient of order two.
A larger cyclic kernel would give a further nontrivial
marked central extension of $\mathcal T$ by $C_2$.
The third row of (B.R8) excludes it.  A split extension
cannot supply such a marked quotient either: its
abelianization would have an additional $C_2$, whereas
every displayed source quotient has abelianization $C_6$.
Consequently $\mathcal T$ is the entire canonical central
cover of $S_3\times A_4$.  Since
$[B,T_2\cap T_3]\subseteq[T_2,T_3]$,
$B/L_0$ is a quotient of that cover.  The already proved
surjection $B/L_0\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal T$ proves
the first equality of (B.R7).  The last two rows of
(B.R8) show that the canonical central kernel over
$\mathcal T$ is zero.  Hence $[B,L_0]=L_0$, proving
all the remaining equalities in (B.R7).

\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{B.R10}
\]

\end{proof}
