\subsection{An explicit full signed-frame formula and its literal fixed-group restriction}

Retain $G=\widehat{\mathrm{GT}}_0$, $H=\ker\lambda$,
$D=D_P$, and the signed pure maps $\vartheta_p$ of
(B.AC1), for an odd prime $p$.  The full harmonic frame
$\beta_g\in F$ is characterized by
$\alpha_g(s)=\beta_gs\beta_g^{-1}$; it is unique, including
for multipliers other than one, by R1 E.65.14.
Let $\mathcal V_{p,g}$ be the reduction of the actual
intertwiner of R1 E.65.15.  Thus its matrix is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda_p(g)&t_{p,g}\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Define the following matrices and multiplier bits:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \mathcal W_{p,g}=\vartheta_p(\beta_g)^{-1}\mathcal V_{p,g},
 \qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\\
 e_4(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda_2-1}{2}\pmod2,\qquad
 a^{(p-1)/2}=(-1)^{\ell_p(a)}
       \quad(a\in\mathbb F_p^\times),\\
 \nu_p(\lambda)=\ell_p(\lambda_p)+e_4(\lambda)\ell_p(-1)
                              \quad\hbox{in }\mathbb F_2.
\end{gathered}                                                   \tag{B.AD1}
\]
Here $\lambda_2$ is the dyadic component of the full multiplier;
the first bit depends only on its residue modulo four.
The map $\nu_p:\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}\to\mathbb F_2$
is a continuous homomorphism: both the quadratic-residue
bit and the indicated modulo-four bit are homomorphisms.

\begin{result}{B.2.31}{Proved}{A full matrix formula and the prime-character values on the literal fixed group}
For every $g\in G$, the actual matrix
\[
 \mathscr C_p(g)=\mathcal W_{p,g}\mathcal W_{p,Dg}^{-1}
\]
belongs to $T_p=C_{\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)}(S)$.
The full character of B.2.30 has the choice-free formula
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \overline d_p(g)=\varepsilon_p\bigl(\mathscr C_p(g)\bigr)
                                      +\nu_p(\lambda(g)).
 \quad}                                                         \tag{B.AD2}
\]
In particular its restrictions to the two actual fixed
subgroups are exactly
\[
 \overline d_p|_{C_P}=\nu_p\circ\lambda|_{C_P},
 \qquad \overline d_p|_{C_L}=0.                                 \tag{B.AD3}
\]
For $p=3$, $\overline d_p$ is the earlier harmonic character
$\overline d_2$ by (B.C24) and B.2.30.
The first identity retains the full multiplier on $C_P$;
on $C_P\cap H$ its value is zero.
\end{result}
\begin{proof}
\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Exact signed covariance and the product law.}\quad
The standard braid action of R1 E.65.8 and its intertwiner
give an exact, signed identity on the actual pure subgroup:
\[
 \vartheta_p\alpha_g|_F
       =\operatorname{Ad}(\mathcal V_{p,g})\vartheta_p.
                                                               \tag{B.AD4}
\]
For completeness this is checked on the specified free
generators: $x=u^2$ maps to $u^{2\lambda}$, while $y=v^2$
maps to $f(u^2,v^2)^{-1}v^{2\lambda}f(u^2,v^2)$ in the
actual braid lift.  Their signed matrices are exactly
the two instances of E.65.15.  Equality on this free
basis proves (B.AD4) on all of $F$, without a central
sign ambiguity.

Use composition as $\alpha_{gg'}=\alpha_g\alpha_{g'}$.
Uniqueness of the pure harmonic frame and uniqueness
of the normalized intertwiner give
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \beta_{gg'}=\alpha_g(\beta_{g'})\beta_g,\qquad
 \mathcal V_{p,gg'}=\mathcal V_{p,g}\mathcal V_{p,g'},\\
 \mathcal W_{p,gg'}=\mathcal W_{p,g}\mathcal W_{p,g'},
 \qquad \det(\mathcal W_{p,g})=\lambda_p(g).
\end{gathered}                                                   \tag{B.AD5}
\]
The third identity follows by inserting (B.AD4) into
the inverse of the first one, and then cancelling
$\mathcal V_{p,g}^{-1}\mathcal V_{p,g}$.
In particular $\mathcal W_p$ is an actual continuous
matrix representation on the whole group $G$.

To specify its normalizer component, let
$\widetilde s=uvu$, $z=(uv)^3$ in $\widehat B_3$.
The central quotient map to $B$ has kernel
$\langle z\rangle\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, as in
the central-kernel argument preceding (A.J13).
Choose the lift of $\beta_g$ determined by the free
generators $x=u^2,y=v^2$.
The two lifts of the harmonic relation then differ
by $z^a$:
\[
 \alpha_g^{\mathrm{braid}}(\widetilde s)
        =\widetilde\beta_g\widetilde s
                         \widetilde\beta_g^{-1}z^a.
\]
Braid abelianization is multiplied by $\lambda(g)$,
so $3\lambda=3+6a$ and
$a=(\lambda-1)/2$ in $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.
The signed matrix of $z$ is $-I$.  Combining with
(B.AD4) and the braid covariance therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal W_{p,g}S\mathcal W_{p,g}^{-1}
                       =(-1)^{e_4(\lambda(g))}S.                \tag{B.AD6}
\]
The choice of $S$ or its simultaneous negative changes
neither this identity nor $T_p$.
Thus $\mathcal W_{p,g}$ lies in the actual signed
normalizer
$\mathscr N_p=\{M\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p):
MSM^{-1}\in\{S,-S\}\}$.
Since $D$ preserves the multiplier, its two matrices
in $\mathscr C_p(g)$ have equal determinant and equal
component in (B.AD6).  Their quotient has determinant
one and centralizes $S$.  This proves the stated
membership in $T_p$.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The crossed product and its quadratic character.}\quad
The literal product law (B.AD5) gives
\[
 \mathscr C_p(gg')
 =\mathcal W_{p,g}\mathscr C_p(g')
                      \mathcal W_{p,g}^{-1}\mathscr C_p(g).
                                                               \tag{B.AD7}
\]
Every member of $\mathscr N_p$ normalizes the cyclic
group $T_p$ and hence preserves its unique quadratic
character $\varepsilon_p$.
It follows from (B.AD7) that
$g\mapsto\varepsilon_p(\mathscr C_p(g))$ is a
continuous homomorphism to $\mathbb F_2$.
On $H$ this homomorphism is $w_p+w_pD=d_p$.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{The exact Cartan commutator and arithmetic correction.}\quad
The actual complex-conjugation element is
$c=(-1,1)$.  Its braid action takes $u,v$ to their
inverses, and $\widetilde s$ to $\widetilde s^{-1}$.
Consequently it fixes $s$ in $B$, so $\beta_c=1$
by uniqueness.  The normalized intertwiner is $J$.
Thus $\mathcal W_{p,c}=J$ literally.

For any $g\in G$ put $e=e_4(\lambda(g))$ and
$Z=\mathcal W_{p,g}J^e$.
Equation (B.AD6) says that $Z$ centralizes $S$, so
\[
 Z=aI+bS,\qquad d=\det Z=a^2+b^2
                        =(-1)^e\lambda_p(g)\ne0.
\]
Conjugation by $J$ sends $aI+bS$ to $aI-bS$.
Their product is $dI$, and therefore the actual
commutator is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [\mathcal W_{p,g},J]&=[Z,J]=Z^2/d\in T_p,\\
 \varepsilon_p(Z^2/d)&=\ell_p(d)=\nu_p(\lambda(g)).
 \end{aligned}
                                                               \tag{B.AD8}
\]
Here is the last equality with both types of Cartan
retained.  If $-1$ is a square modulo $p$, the two
nonzero eigenvalues of $Z$ belong to $\mathbb F_p$;
then $Z^{p-1}=I$ and
$(Z^2/d)^{(p-1)/2}=d^{-(p-1)/2}I$.
If $-1$ is a nonsquare, $Z$ is multiplication by
an element of $\mathbb F_{p^2}^{\times}$ with norm $d$;
then $Z^{p+1}=dI$ and
$(Z^2/d)^{(p+1)/2}=d^{-(p-1)/2}I$.
Both expressions are $(-1)^{\ell_p(d)}I$, proving
(B.AD8) in the exact convention (B.AC3).

For an actual arithmetic element $\gamma$, the proved
comparison is $D\gamma=c\gamma c$.
By (B.AD5) its difference matrix is consequently
$\mathscr C_p(\gamma)=[\mathcal W_{p,\gamma},J]$.
Equation (B.AD8) shows that the sum in (B.AD2) is zero
on the full arithmetic subgroup.
It is already a continuous character, and on $H$
its multiplier term is zero, so it restricts to $d_p$.
The uniqueness assertion in B.2.30 therefore identifies
it with $\overline d_p$, proving (B.AD2).

If $g\in C_P$, then $Dg=g$, so $\mathscr C_p(g)=I$;
this proves the first identity of (B.AD3).
If $g\in C_L$, then $Dg=cgc$, so the same commutator
calculation (B.AD8) makes (B.AD2) zero.
This proves the second identity on the full group,
including all multipliers.

\end{proof}
