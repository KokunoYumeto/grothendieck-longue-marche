# Universal modular comparison: B.16

## Outcome and scope

B.16 supplies an abstract profinite theorem for the precise group
`C = profinite completion of SL2(Z)`. It does not equate that completion
with `SL2(Zhat)`, and it does not identify the manuscript's universal
profinite group with the final affine matrix group scheme. The overall
status is **Open**, because three explicitly formulated source comparisons
remain. The central-twist, normalization, and quotient-normalizer assertions
inside B.16 are proved.

The source normalization matters: here `s = [[0,-1],[1,0]]`, so
`e = s r = [[1,-1],[0,1]]`. The final §48 matrix called sigma has the
opposite sign. The central involution is `omega = r^3 = s^2` throughout.

## Proved results

The abelianization is `C12`, with `r -> 2`, `s -> 3`, and `omega -> 6`.
An automorphism acting trivially on the central quotient has the form
`x -> x omega^chi(x)` where `chi(omega)=0`. Exactly two characters exist,
so the kernel is generated by the sign twist `Theta`. Its character is
the permutation sign after reduction modulo two. Every automorphism of
the central quotient lifts; the presentation and the inverse automorphism
give the proof of invertibility of a proposed lift.

The loop subgroup is a genuine copy of `Zhat`, because its image has
order `n` modulo every positive integer `n`. The sign twist fixes the
squared loop pointwise and does not preserve the conjugacy class of the
unsquared loop. Modulo three, the loop has order three while its negative
has order six. Therefore the subgroup of peripheral automorphisms of `C`
maps isomorphically to its counterpart in `C/omega`.

The subgroup `P0` is the completion of the rank-two free subgroup from
E.15. It is generated by `omega e^2` and `omega (r s)^2`, and the kernel
`P` of reduction modulo two is `P0 x <omega>`. The automorphism group `Z`
used by the formal construction preserves both. Its two signs are
determined by its loop multiplier: the relation in the abelianization is
`2 e_r + 3 e_s = 5 mu (mod 12)`. This proves that the linked and independent
numerical sign groups coincide for this particular completed modular group.

The formal group `G_Z` of B.13 has central quotient equal to the peripheral
automorphism group. The proof determines `N_C(L)=L <omega>` rather than
assuming that the antidiagonal quotient acts faithfully. It also specifies
the subgroup `Z^!`: it is the kernel of the action on `S3`, has index two
in `Z`, and fits into `1 -> L^2 -> Z^! -> gamma -> 1`.

For every actual automorphism in `Z^!`, the conjugators
`alpha in P0 tau^(nu3)` and `beta in P0` exist and are unique. Existence is
proved using the centralizers of a three-cycle and a transposition in
`S3`, followed by the product decomposition of `P`. Uniqueness is reduced
to the centralizers of the two finite factors in a free profinite product.
It is not inferred from computations in one congruence quotient.

The normalized formulas then define continuous homomorphisms
`a_bar: Z^! -> N_r^*/omega` and `b: Z^! -> N_s^*`. Their values on the
kernel are exact: `a_bar(e^(2t))=1`, while `b(e^(2t))=omega^(t mod 2)`.
Thus there are canonical sections of the two normalizers **after** quotient
by omega. The resulting groups are `C3 semidirect_epsilon3 gamma` and
`C2 x gamma`. The latter action is trivial because `Aut(C2)` is trivial.

## Executed computation and its limitation

`code/AB_modular_comparison.py` is a stand-alone exact computation. It
lists all matrices, propagates every proposed generator-image map along
the Cayley graph, checks every edge, and retains the bijections. The
machine-readable results preserve every automorphism's generator images.

| SL2(F3) | 24 | 24 | 24 | 1 |
| SL2(Z/4) | 48 | 48 | 24 | 2 |

For the modulus-four group, the quotient by omega is explicitly identified
with `S4` through conjugation on its four cyclic subgroups of order three.
The full permutation image has order 24 and kernel exactly the central
involution. The subgroup corresponding to `P0` has four elements.

All 48 automorphisms in the modulus-four group preserve the loop-subgroup
conjugacy class. The twist itself does so: `[[1,0],[2,1]]` conjugates `e`
to `-e^-1` modulo four. The finite model therefore does **not** replicate
the faithful peripheral lift of the full profinite group.

Using the four units modulo 12 gives 16 linked numerical pairs, of which
eight act trivially modulo two. Each of those eight pairs has one alpha
of the prescribed parity and two beta choices. The identity already has
beta equal to either `I` or `[[1,2],[2,1]]`. This is a concrete obstruction
to proving profinite normalized uniqueness from finite-stage centralizers.
The extra modulus-three calculation verifies the loop orders three and six;
it also confirms that the full profinite central twist does not descend to
an automorphism of `SL2(F3)`.

## Exact unfinished assertions

**R1: C3:573–576, 582, and 622.** Let the manuscript's earlier geometric
group be `Pscript = Mtilde_03`, with its source-defined action on `C/omega`.
The remaining comparison identifies that action, with its topology, with
the explicitly defined peripheral automorphism subgroup and identifies
`Ntilde_11` with the formal group `G_Z` over `C` and omega. B.16 proves the
abstract candidate and its unique peripheral lift. The earlier geometric
construction has not been reconstructed merely by assigning the same name
to that candidate. This is also distinct from identifying gamma with an
absolute Galois or GT group.

**R2: C3:615–618 and 623.** The first stronger lifting question is whether
the proved section `b_gamma: gamma -> N_s^*/omega` lifts to a continuous
homomorphism `gamma -> N_s^*`. Equivalently the explicitly defined central
pullback `E_s = gamma x_(N_s^*/omega) N_s^*` must split. The map `b` on
`Z^!` does not itself descend, since `b(e^2)=omega`. The source introduces
a chosen section `M(0)` and then `M_0^!=M(0)P0`, `M(1/2)`, and
`M'(-bar j)`. B.16 defines each of these conditional subgroup expressions
and records the exact required isomorphisms with gamma or `ker epsilon3`,
as well as their proposed geometric point-section identifications.

**R3: C3:619–621 and 625–626.** The other section defines
`E_r = gamma x_(N_r^*/omega) N_r^*`. The remaining question identifies this
particular profinite central extension with the manuscript's gamma-tilde
and question-mark normalizer, and identifies its class with the finite
dihedral cocycle analyzed in D.8. The finite cocycle calculation and the
arithmetic cup-product computation in D.12 do not themselves supply that
comparison. The later product-law and filtration questions are recorded in
B.17–B.18.

These remainders are additional to the endomorphism-surjectivity assertion
B.8.5 and the image/lifting problems of D.7. They are stated as unfinished
parts of this report, not as claims that every one of them is a new open
problem in the literature.

## Primary-source verification

Herfort and Ribes (1984), *On Free Pro-C-Products of Pro-C-Groups*,
*Anzeiger der Österreichischen Akademie der Wissenschaften,
Mathematisch-Naturwissenschaftliche Klasse*, **121**, 31–34, supplies
Theorems 1 and 2 on printed page 31. The whole archive PDF was downloaded,
the relevant pages were read directly, and the class hypotheses were
checked. The text is explicitly an announcement with a proof outline and
a reference to the subsequent full paper; B.16 accurately says so.

The only imported statements concern torsion conjugacy and centralizers in
a free profinite product. B.16 gives the reductions for `C3 amalg C2` and
`Zhat amalg Zhat`, proves the relevant kernel and finite-index completion
identifications, and proves the remaining normalizer, sign, and section
calculations. The bibliography fragment is
`B_modular_comparison_refs.bib`, with key `HerfortRibes1984` and an explicit
verified-primary-source marker.
