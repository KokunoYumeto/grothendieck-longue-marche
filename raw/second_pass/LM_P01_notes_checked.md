## First-pass notes

abbreviations, a legibility note and a source line, and `ALL_RESEARCH_NOTES.md` records "(no research notes found)" for
LM_P01. The doubts are settled row by row in `LM_P01_changes.md`. The abbreviation list and the legibility note make
claims about the text, so they are checked here.

## Second-pass notes (mine)

**S1. Scan 3, §§5, 7 and 8, conjecture (the plan of the first part).** The table announces the "Conjecture anabélienne"
fondamentale (§5, ms. p. 7′), its "bordélique" reformulation in groupoid terms (§7, "le purgatoire nécessaire") and a
"distinction des cas, où le purgatoire s'aménage un peu" (§8). With the corrected title, §8 sorts the cases in which the
groupoid formulation becomes manageable; the first pass's "définition du cas" made it a definition. Confidence: high for
the reading.

**S2. Scan 3, §12, claim without proof (scope restored).** The title claims that the groupoid "bordel" can be expressed
completely, "dans les cas anabéliens", by exterior groups with loops; the first pass's "dans le cas considéré" dropped
the hypothesis. (Second pass) The hypothesis is what makes the claim work: an anabelian π has trivial centre, and for a
group with trivial centre an extension of Γ by π is determined up to isomorphism by its outer action Γ → Autext(π), so a
groupoid with such vertex groups is described by exterior groups. Confidence: high for the reading, medium for the gloss.

**S3. Scan 3, §9, own idea (a lead for Conj. 5.5).** §9 is "Structure tangentielle en les s ∈ S (Sections d'extensions
'de deuxième type')", not "fonctorielle … de deuxième degré". In LM_P03 the margin of Conj. 5.5 reads "C'est un peu faux
cf §9"; with the corrected title, the qualification plausibly concerns sections attached to the points s ∈ S through
tangential data (what are now called cuspidal sections), which do not come from rational points of X∖S. This has to be
read in §9; it is a lead, not a finding (confidence: low).

**S4. Scans 3–4, §§14, 16, 27 and 32, implication not pursued.** The operations are *bouchage* (filling) and *forage*
(drilling) of holes, which pass between the types (g, ν) and (g, ν ± 1): §27 a) treats "bouchage de trous" as a change
of type, and §32 its arithmetic side, "relations entre Γ_{g,ν} et Γ_{g,ν−1}". The first pass's "bordage" suggested a
boundary construction and hid that §14's cohomological digression concerns the same operation. Confidence: high for the
reading.

**S5. Scan 3, §17, own idea (reading-dependent).** If the abbreviation is *op.* (opérations), §17 treats finite groups
acting on surfaces, which is what the application of §15 to the finite subgroups of Autext_lac(π) needs; §23 ("surfaces à
groupes (finis) d'opérateurs") returns to the theme. (Second pass) The passage from finite subgroups of the discrete
mapping class group to finite group actions on surfaces is Nielsen realisation (Kerckhoff, Ann. of Math. 117, 1983);
whether §§15 and 17 argue it has to be read there. Confidence: low.

**S6. Scan 4, §24, question.** §24 tries to determine the A°Γ and links them with the relations Π^Γ_{g,(ν,ν+n−1)} = (1),
ending in a "programme de travail" (the word is written above a cancelled one). What A°Γ denotes is not on these pages;
the CSG body of §24 defines A = Aut(U), so that Γ ⊂ A and A_U = A^Γ is the centraliser. Kept as an open point of reading
(confidence: medium for the reading A°Γ).

**S7. Scan 4, §§26, 28 and 29, conjecture.** A "Conjecture hâtive" is made in the second line of the repeated §25 (with
the discretisations and prediscretisations of the profinite Teichmüller groups and the link with the modular topos). It
"grince" in §28, on passing to a finite covering, and §29 "rajuste les notions et les conjectures". The first pass's
"regarde|reprend" lost that §29 changes the notions as well as the conjecture (confidence: high for the reading).

**S8. Scan 4, §32, claim without proof (hypothesis restored).** "On reconstitue, sans le dire, tout le topos étale d'une
courbe algébrique complète, à partir du π₁ d'un ouvert anabélien": the open must be anabelian; the first pass read
"auxiliaire". (Second pass) Part of the mechanism is standard: the inertia subgroups at the points of S are characterised
group-theoretically in the arithmetic π₁ of an anabelian U = X∖S, and π₁(X) is the quotient of π₁(U) by the closed
normal subgroup they generate. The whole étale topos also needs the closed points, through their decomposition groups,
and whether §32 goes that far has to be read there (confidence: medium).

**S9. Scan 4, §34, conjecture (heuristic).** "Description heuristique profinie de la cat. des courbes alg. définies sur
des sous-ext. finies de Q₀/Q": the first pass's "homotopique" announced a homotopy-theoretic description, where the ink
announces a heuristic, that is conjectural, description of curves over number fields in profinite terms. The kept
reading *sous-ext.* (not *sur-ext.*) is what makes these the finite subextensions of Q₀/Q, i.e. number fields.
Confidence: high for the reading.

**S10. Scan 4, §§35–37, claims (statements corrected).** §35 states the injectivity of Γ_Q → Autext_lac(Π̂_{0,3}); §36
the isomorphism Γ_Q ≃ Γ_{1,1} and the injectivity of Γ_Q → Autext(T̂⁺_{1,1}) = Autext(SL(2,Z)^), with the letter under
the hat unresolved (Γ or T); §37 the Legendre relation M_{1,1}[2]′ ≃ U_{0,3} ≠ M^!_{0,4}. The first pass's "Γ_Q ≃
Π̄_{1,1}" identified Γ_Q with a fundamental group, and GC's "=" in §37 reverses the relation the ink states. (Second
pass) If Γ_{g,ν} is the image of Γ_Q acting on the profinite group of type (g, ν), with kernel N_{g,ν}, as the titles of
§§30–33 suggest, then Γ_Q ≃ Γ_{1,1} is the faithfulness of that action for type (1,1), and §35 is what Belyi's theorem
(1979) gives for type (0,3). Confidence: medium; the definition of Γ_{g,ν} must be read in §30.

**S11. Scan 6, Prop. 1.1, Grothendieck's question, with an own idea.** Grothendieck doubts a) ⇒ b) and suggests assuming
E locally connected with enough fibre functors, "i.e. suffisamment de points". (Second pass) Both conditions hold in
every Ĉ with C a groupoid (each object of C gives a point), so a non-degenerate topos without points whose objects are
all locally constant would show that a) alone is not enough. My candidate is the topos of sheaves on an atomless
complete Boolean algebra B: it has no points (they would correspond to atoms of B), and since the axiom of choice holds
in the Boolean-valued model, each object has an ordinal cardinality that is constant on the pieces of a partition of 1,
so each object is locally isomorphic to a constant one. Confidence: medium; this is my sketch, not checked against the
literature on atomic and Galois toposes.

**S12. Scan 6, N.B. at Prop. 1.1 and Déf. 1.2, implication.** The margin says that the groupoid C of b) can be chosen
canonically, and Prop. 1.4 does so: C is the category of points, "souvent noté Π₁(E)". The first pass's *I|J* would have
pointed to the index set of b′), whose groups G_i depend on base points and are not canonical. In Déf. 1.2 the page
calls *C* "un topos multigaloisien (ou une catégorie multigaloisienne)", a slip for the topos E (confidence: medium).

**S13. Scan 7, Prop. 1.4, claim without proof, with a proof sketch.** "Ceci posé : (E étant multigaloisien)" states a)
and b) for every multigaloisian E; the first pass's "Cas part." made them a special case. (Second pass) Proof: E ≃ Ĝ for
a groupoid G (Déf. 1.2); the points of a presheaf topos Ĝ are its flat functors, i.e. ind-objects of G, and a filtered
diagram in a groupoid is essentially constant, so the category of points is equivalent to G (a groupoid absorbs the
passage to the opposite). This gives a) and b) without the doubtful direction a) ⇒ b) of Prop. 1.1 (confidence: medium).

**S14. Scan 8, Prop. 1.5 and its N.B., claims without proof.** The equivalent conditions (1-connected; 0-connected and
projective; Hom(S, −) a fibre functor, which, being already left exact, has only to commute with arbitrary colimits, and
by the N.B. with sums and passage to the quotient; E_{/S} punctual; S representable on C) are stated without proof, and
no reading on scan 8 changed. (Second pass) In Ĉ with C a groupoid they all say that S is isomorphic to a representable
h_c: a 0-connected object is a transitive Aut(c)-set G/H, and it is projective exactly when H = 1. Confidence: medium.

**S15. Scan 9, Prop. 1.7, claim without proof (reference corrected).** E_0 → Π₁(C) is said to be an equivalence "comme
de juste": every fibre functor is representable by an essentially unique 1-connected object. The inserted reference is
to Prop. 1.5 ("p. 1.5", read "par 1.5" by CSG and IUT), whose condition c) makes Hom(S, −) a fibre functor for a
1-connected S; the first pass's "p. 1.3" pointed to the margin proposition on induced topoi and sums, which does not give
the functor. (Second pass) Essential surjectivity follows from S13 and S14: a fibre functor is evaluation at an object
c, which h_c represents by Yoneda, and h_c is 1-connected (confidence: medium).

**S16. Scan 10, Cor. 1.8 to Scholie 1.10, claims.** A point P lifts to E_{/S} for a 1-connected S, uniquely up to unique
isomorphism; Déf. 1.9 names S the "rev. universel ponctué au dessus de P" (the first pass had "pointé"). Scholie 1.10
says that giving a point or a universal covering "revient essentiellement au même"; the first pass's sign ≂ left the
sentence without a statement. (Second pass) Both follow from S15: P corresponds to an object c, (S, α) to (h_c, id_c),
and an automorphism of h_c that fixes id_c is the identity. Confidence: high for the readings, medium for the sketch.
