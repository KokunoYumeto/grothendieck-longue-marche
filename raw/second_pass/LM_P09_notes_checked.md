## Second-pass notes (mine)

### C1 (mine) · scans 80–81 · unpursued implication · confidence high
The NB on scan 81 quotients ℰ′ by the invariant subgroup generated by the inertia groups L_{i′}=κ_{i′}(T(K̄)) of the
filled punctures. The ink primes only the index, not L or κ. The maps of these systems commute with the projections to
Γ, and the image of a decomposition group ℰ′_{i′} in Γ is open, so no such map can be trivial on ℰ′_{i′}. Scan 80's
« trivial sur ℰ′_{i′} » therefore means trivial on L′_{i′}, as the P08 note proposes; the transcription keeps the
author's ℰ′_{i′}.

### C2 (mine) · scan 82 · unpursued implication · confidence high
The arrow ℰ_i→ℰ is drawn with a hook and labelled ρ_i. So the local groups are subgroups (decomposition groups) of ℰ,
fixed only up to conjugation by π. That is why scan 83 speaks of « classes de conjugaison de sous-groupes » and of outer
homomorphisms κ_i.

### C3 (mine) · scans 86–87 · unpursued implication · confidence high
For K=Q the cyclotomic character is onto Ẑ*, so the χ(Γ)-torsor contained in the admissible η is all of T*: every
generator of T is admissible up to conjugation. Scan 87 uses exactly this statement (for every ρ∈Ẑ*, conjugates of the
ℓ_i^ρ fit again). That argument is therefore the case K=Q of the remark on scan 86, and it needs only "contiennent".

### C4 (mine) · scan 88 · unpursued implication · confidence high for necessity, medium for "VA"
"VA" reads as "variété abélienne". The quotient of (π′_ab)_(ℓ) by the submodule spanned by the inertia images is the
ℓ-adic Tate module of the Jacobian of the completed curve of the cover ℰ′. In the geometric case, Weil's theorems for
abelian varieties over finite fields give condition e) for every open ℰ′: purity, integral characteristic polynomials of
Frobenius at the good places, and independence of ℓ. So e) is a necessary condition, as its place in §11 requires.

### C5 (mine) · scan 90 · own idea · confidence medium
The 2-equivalence of scan 90 is a groupoid form of the Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem for punctured surfaces. Homeomorphisms
up to isotopy correspond to outer isomorphisms of π_1 that preserve the peripheral classes (the κ_i up to conjugation)
and the orientation (T). The first cases to test, together with R37, are those where π_1 is abelian or trivial: the
sphere with at most two points and the torus without points.
