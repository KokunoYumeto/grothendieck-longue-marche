## Own notes (second pass, mine)

**A — Scan 64, margin — implication (mine).**
- "Peut-être vrai si I = ∅" has a clear reason. Suppose some s ∈ S is K-rational. Its decomposition group in E maps onto Γ, and the extension of Γ by its inertia ℤ̂(1) splits (Kummer theory). This gives sections of E → Γ, the cuspidal sections, that come from no point of U(K) = (X∖S)(K).
- So the map Γ((X,S)/K) → {sections}/π-conjugacy is not surjective. That is what "faux tel quel … il faut des conditions supplémentaires" records. Bijectivity can hold only when S has no K-rational point, and I = ∅ is the simplest such condition.
- §9 treats these sections; CSG titles it "Les sections d'extensions 'de deuxième type'".
- Confidence: high.

**B — Scan 64 — implication and reading check (mine).**
- "g = 0 ⇒ n ≥ 2 i.e. qu'il n'y a pas de comp. irréd. … isom. à ℙ¹_K̄ ou 𝔼¹_K̄" is exactly the condition for U = X∖S to embed in its generalised Jacobian, an extension of the Jacobian by a torus:
  - for g ≥ 1 the Abel–Jacobi map is injective;
  - for g = 0 and n ≥ 2, ℙ¹ minus n points embeds in a torus of dimension n − 1;
  - ℙ¹ and the affine line 𝔼¹ (n ≤ 1) have trivial generalised Jacobian.
- Injectivity then follows from the Kummer maps G(K)/m → H¹(K, G[m]) once ⋂_m mG(K) = 0. That holds for K finitely generated over ℚ: A(K) is finitely generated, the torus has no non-trivial infinitely divisible points over such fields, and G(K)_tors is finite.
- A component of the proper X_K̄ cannot be an affine line, so the condition concerns U_K̄. This supports the witnesses' "U_K̄" in the unresolved X_K̄|U_K̄.
- Confidence: high for the mathematics; the reading stays open.

**C — Scans 69–70 — own idea: answer to the transporter question (mine).**
- Two facts hold for a hyperbolic curve X∖S in characteristic 0 (Nakamura; Tamagawa on the Grothendieck conjecture for affine curves):
  - the inertia groups of distinct cusps of the pro-universal covering meet trivially;
  - the normaliser in E of a cuspidal inertia group L_i is the decomposition group E_i.
- If g L_i^d g^{-1} ⊂ L_i, then g L_i g^{-1} and L_i share the open subgroup g L_i^d g^{-1}. So they are equal and g ∈ E_i. Hence Trans_E(L_i^d, L_i) = E_i for every d ≥ 1, and Transp_π(L_i^d, L_i) = E_i ∩ π = L_i, which is the "sans doute = L_i" of scan 71.
- Grothendieck's question therefore has an affirmative answer, and each α_{i′} is determined up to L_i.
- Confidence: medium-high. The facts are standard; the match with his definitions of E_i and Trans is inferred.
