- **S2.1** (scan 009, margin bracket attached to (12); unproved claim and reading question; reading confidence: "d'ailleurs … donc $\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}}$ est cpt" medium, "Somme finie" low). The bracket reads "[Somme finie d'ailleurs -- donc $\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}}$ est cpt…]": the topological sum in (12) is finite, so $\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}}$ is compact. This combines the NB on 008 ("cet ens d'orbites est fini", proved in N008.4) with compactness of each completed orbit (N009.1). - **S2.2** (scan 011, equation numbering; reading question, now resolved; confidence high). Only the hypothesis (*) is (16), so the NB "L'hypothèse (16) implique que dans $\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)$, les orbites sous $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ sont déjà ouvertes, tte orbite sous $\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(Y)$ étant réunion finie de telles orbites" refers to (*). The claim is correct: write $\operatorname{Aut}(Y)=\bigcup\alpha_iH_u$ (a finite union). For $g=\alpha_ih$ with $(\psi,h)\in G_u$ we have $guf^{-1}=\alpha_iu\psi f^{-1}$, so the two-sided orbit is the finite union of the $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$-orbits of the $\alpha_iu$.
- **S2.3** (scan 008, NB "Tout sous-groupe fini de $\mathcal T_X$ peut s'obtenir de cette façon comme stabilisateur !!"; own idea; confidence high). A stabilizer is a deck group transported to $X$, so it acts freely on $X$. For closed $X$ of genus 2, every finite étale $X\to Y$ with $Y$ anabelian has $Y$ closed with $\chi(Y)\le-2$, so its degree is 1 and every stabilizer is trivial. No nontrivial finite subgroup occurs at all, for example that of the hyperelliptic involution. The NB can therefore hold only for finite subgroups whose realization (presumably the "th. profond et récent", Nielsen realization as proved by Kerckhoff) acts freely. For punctured $X$ more subgroups qualify, since fixed points at punctures are not points of $X$. A version for all finite subgroups needs the ramified or hole-filling arrows that he introduces on 018. This affects how the category on 009–017 can be used to recover finite subgroups of $\mathcal T_X$.
- **S2.4** (scan 017, end of the programme sentence; reading question; confidence low). After "… qui sont des $K$-automs.…" comes a closed oval with an internal diagonal, the shape of his $\mathbb Q$ two lines above, then ")" and a full stop. If it is "(?)", he marks the finitely generated case ($k=\overline K$, $K$-automorphisms) as doubtful; if it is "ℚ)", there is no doubt mark and the sign closes a parenthesis that has no visible opening. The answer bears on whether the $\overline K$ part of the programme is stated as a goal or as a question.
- **S2.5** (scan 017, oblique margin note; unpursued implication; confidence low-medium, since it depends on reading "cartes").
