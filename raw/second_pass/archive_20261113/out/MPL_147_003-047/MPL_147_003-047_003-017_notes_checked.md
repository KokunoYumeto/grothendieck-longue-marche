**SP05** — scan 015, the NB after the definition of MD-graphs. *Unproved claim, checked.*
The NB criterion is (a) 0-connexe, (b) not a single genus-1 vertex of total weight 0, (c) genus-0 vertices of total weight ≥ 3. It
is equivalent to "0-connexes et stables" as defined just above. In a connected graph with at least two vertices, every vertex has
an incident arc, so every genus-1 vertex automatically has ν̂ ≥ 1; only the one-vertex graph needs (b). Vertices of genus ≥ 2 impose
nothing. The one-line proof is not written on the page. Confidence: high.

**SP06** — scan 016, proof of the Proposition. *Reading question and proof check.*
The inserted clause reads "arbre qui \unsure{n'est réduit} à un seul sommet", with no visible "pas". The argument needs "n'est pas
réduit": a tree with at least two vertices has at least two ends. The other insertion, "G n'est pas réduit à un sommet, puisqu'il
est stable de type (1,0)", supplies that hypothesis. The insertion "(donc aucun sommet n'est un bout)" completes the first case: a
connected graph with h₁ = 1 and no vertex of order ≤ 1 is a cycle, whose vertices have order 2 < 3. The cancelled "graphe top.
compact ou" shows that G first meant to treat both cases topologically. Confidence: high (elementary).

**SP07** — scan 017, first paragraph. *Reading question; no mathematical consequence.*
"dont les \unsure{poids|points} marqués doivent être ≥ 2": both readings give the same bound. An end of a genus-0 tree has order 1
and needs ν̂ ≥ 3, i.e. at least two marked points, so two ends give ν ≥ 4, against ν ≤ 2. The one-vertex case is excluded because
its total weight is ν ≤ 2 < 3 ("Il ne peut être réduit à un seul sommet"). Confidence: high.
