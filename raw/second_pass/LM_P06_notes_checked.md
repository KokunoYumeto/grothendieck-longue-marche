## Notes added in the second pass (mine, Claude Opus 5.5)

**C1 — scan 51 — own idea (author's slip).** In α′ = p(μ)α the factor p(μ) lies in Γ, so α, α′ ∈ Γ; in α′_{i′} =
ρ_i(μ_{i′})α_{i′} the factor lies in E, so α_{i′}, α′_{i′} ∈ E, as on scan 53 ("soit α ∈ Γ", "soit α_{i′} ∈ E"). The
annotations "α, α′ (∈ E)" and "α_{i′}, α′_{i′} (∈ Γ)" are therefore interchanged. The crossed membership sign under α
may be the author's own start of a correction; the transcription keeps the ink.

**C2 — scans 52–53 — conjecture ("sauf erreur … μ_0 = 1").** The step "μ_0 centralizes an open subgroup of π, hence
μ_0 = 1" is the slimness of π: it holds when π is the geometric fundamental group of a hyperbolic curve in
characteristic 0 (2g − 2 + n > 0), and fails for abelian π (an elliptic curve, or G_m), where the centralizer of any
subgroup is all of π. The page's actual condition is stronger: μ_0 must commute with all of f_E(E′), whose image in Γ
is open, so μ_0 is fixed by an open subgroup Gal(K̄/K_1) acting on π. For abelian π over a number field K this already
forces μ_0 = 1, since T(A)^{Gal(K̄/K_1)} = 0 for an elliptic curve A and Ẑ(1)^{Gal(K̄/K_1)} = 0; discreteness thus needs
either slimness of π or such a Galois argument.

**C3 — scan 54 (margin NB) — claim without proof.** "Le centralisateur dans Γ d'un ss-groupe ouvert est réduit à
l'unité" says that Γ = Gal(K̄/K) is slim. That is true for K a number field, false for K finite (Γ ≅ Ẑ is abelian) and
for K = ℝ (the open subgroup {1} has centralizer Γ ≅ Z/2). The uniqueness of α rests on it: two solutions of
pf = int(α)p′ differ by an element of the centralizer of p′(E′), which is open in Γ.

**C4 — scan 54 (margin; "évidemment uniquement déterminé") — claim without proof, checked.** Changing α_{i′} to
μ_{i′}α_{i′} with μ_{i′} ∈ E_i replaces f_{i′} by int(μ_{i′})∘f_{i′}; since k_i commutes with the action of E_i,
int(μ_{i′}) acts on L_i ≅ T(K̄) as multiplication by χ(p(μ_{i′})), so f^0_{i′} is multiplied by that unit. β_{i′} =
p(α_{i′})α becomes p(μ_{i′})β_{i′}, so χ(β_{i′}) is multiplied by the same unit, and the condition f^0_{i′} =
d_{i′}χ(β_{i′})·id is unchanged, as the margin says. d_{i′} is unique because χ(β_{i′}) is a unit: d_{i′} =
χ_{i′}χ(β_{i′})^{-1} in Ẑ, and N* → Ẑ is injective (Ẑ has zero divisors, so it is the unit, not a domain property, that
gives uniqueness).

**C5 — scan 55 (inserted "et donc I = ∅") — claim without proof, checked.** If p is injective, π = Ker p is trivial,
so L_i = E_i ∩ π is trivial for every i. Since k_i: T(K̄) → L_i is an isomorphism and T(K̄) ≅ Ẑ(1) is not zero, there
is no i, and I = ∅.

**C6 — scans 56 and 58 — own idea (transporter convention).** Put E = Gal(K̄/L) and E′ = Gal(K̄/L′). Then int(α)E′ ⊂ E
iff L ⊂ α(L′), i.e. α^{-1}(L) ⊂ L′, and the K-homomorphism L → L′ attached to α is α^{-1}|_L; it does not change when
α is replaced by μα with μ ∈ E, and two α give the same map only if α′α^{-1} ∈ Fix_Γ(L) = E, which is the condition
"α′ = μα" of scan 56. If Trans_Γ(L, L′) means {γ : γ(L) ⊂ L′}, the "=" of the scan 56 bracket is the inversion
α ↦ α^{-1}, and the switch from E\… to …/Fix_Γ(L) in the scan 58 display records exactly that inversion.

**C7 — scan 58 — own idea (author's slip in the display).** Taken literally, Transp_Γ(Γ′_0, Γ) = Γ, so the first line
gives E\Γ, the set Hom_K(L, K̄) of all K-embeddings, not Hom_K(L, L′). The condition α ∈ Transp_Γ(Γ′_0, E) two lines
above shows that E\Transp_Γ(Γ′_0, E) is meant. The transcription keeps the ink's Γ, which CSG and IUT also print.

**C8 — scan 60 — conjecture (a precise form of the proposed weakening).** "S′ ne disconnecte pas localement (ét) X′"
can be made precise as: for every s ∈ S′, Spec O^{sh}_{X′,s} minus the inverse image of S′ is nonempty and connected.
For X′ connected this implies the inserted "donc X′ − S′ 0-connexe": two disjoint nonempty open pieces of X′ − S′ would
have closures meeting at some s ∈ S′ and would disconnect the punctured strict henselization there. Where X′ has
dimension 1 at s and s is isolated in S′, the condition says that X′ is geometrically unibranch at s: normality is
enough, a node is not.

**C9 — scan 60 — claim without proof, proof sketch.** A K-morphism Y → Spec L is a K-homomorphism L → H^0(Y, O_Y), and
its image consists of elements algebraic over K. An element of H^0(X′ − S′, O) that is algebraic over K is integral
over the local ring O_{X′,s} at each s ∈ S′ and lies in its total ring of fractions (X′ − S′ being dense), so it lies
in O_{X′,s} when X′ is normal at s, and it extends to X′. Hence Hom_K(X′ − S′, X) = Hom_K(L, H^0(X′, O_{X′})) =
Hom_K(X′, X); the example H^0(A^1, O) = K[t] ⊋ K = H^0(P^1, O) shows that only the algebraic part of the global sections
agrees.
