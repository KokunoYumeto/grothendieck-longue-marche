## Checker's notes (mine)

- **C1**: scans 267–268; implication not pursued (checker's note, confidence medium). On scan 266 « essentiellement
  constant » is glossed as « il définit sur les π_1 l'hom. nul T_x → T_x ». A loop t ∈ π_1(Gr(𝒜_x)) = T_x acts on
  Isom(f(A), g(A)) by φ ↦ t′·t⁻¹·φ, where t′ is the image of t under the identification π_1(𝕊_x) ≅ π_1(D_x^*) given by
  F and G. So the lemma of scan 268 moves the whole weight onto t′ = t: the thickening A ↦ Ep(A) follows the radial map
  D_x^* → 𝕊_x, which induces the identity of T_x for the two orientation identifications. With the opposite sign, the
  loop would act by −2t and H would not be essentially constant.
- **C2**: scan 273, after (124); example (checker's note, confidence high). Let ρ_r be a diffeomorphism equal to the
  rotation by a fixed angle θ on the disc of radius r about x and to the identity outside radius 2r. As r → 0, ρ_r and
  ρ_r⁻¹ converge uniformly to the identity, but each ρ_r induces the rotation by θ on 𝕊_x. So for a group of
  diffeomorphisms with the topology of uniform convergence of u and u⁻¹, the action on directions is not continuous, and
  the extra hypothesis is needed. It also explains why the universal case G = Aut top(X,S) of scan 277 is treated
  without the differential data (« Oublions pour l'instant les données différentiables », scan 273).
- **C3**: scan 277, (138); checker's note (confidence high for (0,1), medium for the signs in (0,2)). For ν = 0 the set
  I is empty, T^I = 0 and G̃ = G, so the exclusions (0,0) and (1,0) play no role in this step. For (0,1) and (0,2), 𝒜^0
  contains the rotations about a marked point (about the axis through both points), and by R33 the extension restricted
  to that circle is the universal cover ℝ → SO(2) (embedded diagonally, up to sign, in the case of two points), which
  does not split. So « toute extension de 𝒜^0 par un groupe discret est canoniquement scindée » fails exactly in the
  excluded types with ν ≥ 1.
- **C4**: scans 279–280; implication not pursued (checker's note, confidence medium). Replacing each x ∈ I by its circle
  of directions 𝕊_x is the real oriented blow-up of X at I, whose boundary circles are the 𝕊_x. For orientation-preserving
  automorphisms fixing I pointwise, the extension 1 → ℤ^I → S𝒯 → 𝒯 → 1 should then be the capping sequence for the
  bordered surface, its kernel generated by the boundary Dehn twists. This agrees with the definition of § 25 through
  the automorphisms that are the identity near S (CSG draft, l. 5914–5925); I have not checked it against later pages.
- **C5**: scans 277–278, (136)–(141); checker's note (confidence medium). The kernel is T^I in (136), a Z and a T written
  over each other in (139) (left unresolved, scan 277), and ℤ^I on scan 278. With X oriented, T ≅ ℤ canonically
  and the two agree; without a chosen orientation, writing ℤ^I presupposes one, and orientation-reversing elements act
  on the kernel by −1 (R38). The overwriting in (139) records this choice.
