## F.4 | Refuted | sections/F.tex, F.4 | dep: F.2 | ms: Section 51, equations (371)-(373); folios 734 and 736; scans 173 and 178; explicit Selmer tasks in prompt Part F | doubtful: No
Abelian approximations do not separate every cusp — For the tripod the unrestricted abelian section sets are \[ H^1(G_\Q,\widehat\Z(1)^2) =\left(\varprojlim_m\Q^\times/(\Q^\times)^m\right)^2, \qquad H^1(G_\Q,\Q_p(1)^2) =\left(\widehat{\Q^\times}^{\,p}\otimes_{\Z_p}\Q_p\right)^2. \] They are infinite. The two tangential sections at $0$ and $1$, using respectively parameters $z$ and $1-z$ with unit tangent, have the same class in both sets. Thus the assertion that distinct cusps remain nonconjugate in every abelian approximation is false. This counterexample concerns an approximation, and does not identify the full profinite cuspidal sections.

## F.5 | Proved | sections/F.tex, F.5 | dep: B.12; B.14d | ms: Section 51, equations (371)-(373); folios 734 and 736; scans 173 and 178; explicit Selmer tasks in prompt Part F | doubtful: No
The effect of the degree-seven nonpentagonal element — Let $\ell$ be any prime and let $\beta$ be the pro-$\ell$ element supplied by B.12 for $\ell\ge5$ and by B.14d for $\ell=2,3$, with normalized conjugator $f$ having leading Lie term $q_7$. It acts identically on the integral lower-central quotients of class at most seven, and hence on the corresponding $\mathbb Q_\ell$-unipotent quotients $U_n$ for $n\le7$. In class eight, with the normalization $\beta(x)=x,\ \beta(y)=f^{-1}yf$, its first change is \[ \beta(\log x)=\log x,\qquad \beta(\log y)=\log y+[\log y,q_7]\pmod{\text{degree}>8}. \] It transports cocycles for $\rho$ bijectively to cocycles for $\rho^\beta=\beta\rho\beta^{-1}$. It need not act on the section set for the \emph{fixed} action $\rho$. For the primes $p\ge5$ and the local conditions specified in F.3, all tripod finiteness thresholds occur before this change can be detected.
