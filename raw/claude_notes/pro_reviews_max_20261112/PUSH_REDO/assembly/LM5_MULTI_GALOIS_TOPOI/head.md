# LM5_MULTI_GALOIS_TOPOI: the topoi of the Longue Marche. Multi-Galois topoi, the topos at the missing points, van Kampen and hole-filling, and the 2-isotopic description (§§1–4, 14, 16, 20)

## What is to be settled

The manuscript opens with topoi, not with groups: §1 "Topos multigaloisiens", §2 "Application aux revêtements des topos", §3 "Variantes pro-multigaloisiennes", §4 "Compléments, remords". The fundamental groupoid of a topos enters as the multi-Galois topos of its locally constant objects, with no base point chosen, and the systems of groupoids of §§5–11 are diagrams of such topoi. Three later sections return to topoi:

- §14, "Digression cohomologique (sur le bouchage de trous)" (cote 140-2, scans 119–137): for a curve `X` and the open `U = X − S` he introduces a topos `B_{X,U}` (he calls it "topos abracadabra") built from `U` and the local data at the points of `S`, and asks whether its cohomology is computed "galoisiennement" and whether `H^*(B_{X,U}, F) → H^*(X_ét, f^*F)` is an isomorphism for torsion `F` (scan 124); he rejects it in genus 0 for locally constant coefficients, "Non, à cause du genre 0 !!!" (129).
- §16, "Bouchage et forage de trous: préliminaires topologiques généraux" (scans 160–178): a van Kampen theorem for topoi; 2-cartesian squares of topoi (166); the expected acyclicity of `B_{D;S} → B_D` and `B_{X;S} → B_X`.
- §20, "Digression: description 2-isotopique de la catégorie des isomorphismes topologiques" (scans 233–250): Gr-categories describing spheres and sphere bundles up to 2-isotopy (232–239); neutrality of a gerbe, "brr !", to consult Giraud or Breen (240–241); bounds on fidelity in terms of `dim S`, which conflict (247–248), "Au concours !"; what a homotopy means arithmetically for forms of `P^1`: "sans doute un relèvement de u en une section du revêtement simplement connexe" (249); the 2-category of Azumaya algebras (250); "bien profond" for the simple connectedness of the Teichmüller space `M̃_{g,ν}` (scan 259, which is in §21, not in §20).

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1.** A multi-Galois topos in his sense is the classifying topos of a groupoid with (pro)finite or discrete automorphism groups, equivalently a topos generated by locally constant objects satisfying a finiteness or local-connectedness condition; §§1–3 are then the Galois theory of SGA 1, Exposé V, and SGA 4, Exposé IV, freed from base points and connectedness, and coincide with later treatments (Moerdijk's prodiscrete Galois theory; Bunge; the fundamental pro-groupoid of a locally connected topos; Leroy). Whatever he states beyond these is to be found and proved.
- **Lead 2.** `B_{X,U}` is the topos-theoretic form of "`U` together with its punctured formal neighbourhoods at `S`", glued along the local fundamental groups: in present terms the Kummer-étale or logarithmic topos of `(X, S)`, or the root-stack limit, or Deligne's tangential base point construction made into a space. With that identification his cohomological question has a known answer (Kummer-étale cohomology with torsion coefficients agrees with the étale cohomology of `U`, not of `X`), and the genus-0 failure he notes should be explained by it.
- **Lead 3.** His van Kampen theorem is the statement that the fundamental-groupoid functor (the multi-Galois reflection) sends suitable 2-pushouts of topoi to 2-pushouts; forms of it are in the literature for locally connected topoi and for étale fundamental groups (Stix's and others' van Kampen theorems), and the effective-descent hypotheses matter.
- **Lead 4.** §20 computes the homotopy 2-type of the group of homeomorphisms of the sphere and of sphere bundles (classically `O(3)`, by Kneser and Smale), and its arithmetic form asks for the étale or motivic homotopy type of forms of `P^1`; Azumaya algebras of degree 2 as a 2-category is the 2-gerbe-theoretic description of `B PGL_2`.

- **Level 1 is exactly what functors modulo natural transformations can carry** (CATHOT C.1–C.4, B.11; *checked*). For a groupoid `G`, `π0 Fun(X, G) = [BX, BG]` for every small category `X`; a category has this property only if it is isomorphic, modulo zigzags of natural transformations, to a groupoid; and for a homotopy type that is not a 1-type no category represents `X ↦ [BX, Y]`. The reflector is the fundamental groupoid. In Part E this is the precise sense in which the multi-Galois topos (the fundamental groupoid without base points) is the last level at which the 1-categorical language of §§1–4 loses nothing: use it to state which of his constructions extend to higher levels only after the language is changed, and cite it instead of re-proving the level-1 statement for presheaf topoi.

- **Level 1 among the levels.** C.4, C.15: for set-valued coefficients on a small category the constant-coefficient class is `W_0` (bijection on `π_0`), the class where restriction of locally constant coefficients is an equivalence is `W_1` (equivalence of fundamental groupoids), and the class where it is only fully faithful is "`π_0` bijective and `π_1` onto"; this middle class fails two-out-of-three (A.5: `e → BC_2 → e`). F.1: the fibre functors on all locally constant sets, with all natural transformations, form a groupoid equivalent to `Π_1 X`; with finite sets only the profinite invariant is recovered, and the two must not be identified. E.5: with `r`-truncated spaces as coefficients the classes are `W_r` and `W_{r+1}`, and the tower `W_0 ⊃ W_1 ⊃ W_2 ⊃ …` has intersection the weak equivalences; F.3: with space-valued coefficients the ∞-topos `Fun(X, 𝒮)` has shape `X`, and its homotopy category with the constant-diagram functor recovers `X`; F.8 (open there): what the bare ordinary categories with ordinary fibre functors recover above level 1. F.7: the coefficient theories for which locally constant coefficients see the whole homotopy type include spaces, `D(Z)` and spectra, and exclude sets, every finite truncation, `D(Q)` and `D(F_p)`. - **van Kampen at every level, in one direction only.** B.5: for every coefficient derivator the class of maps inducing equivalences on locally constant coefficients satisfies the direct Mayer–Vietoris axiom for a cover of a small category by two sieves (if the three maps on the pieces and the intersection are in the class, so is the map on the union) and commutes with filtered colimits; B.6: the reverse implications fail for every non-trivial coefficient theory. So for small categories the direct form of his van Kampen theorem (Lead 3) holds at all levels and for all coefficients, by one proof (B.3, the relative Theorem A for locally constant coefficients on a bare derivator), and no converse does. In Part C say whether his statement for topoi is of the direct kind, and in Part E whether B.3's proof (Kan extension along an opfibration preserves local constancy along the fibres) has a counterpart for morphisms of topoi.
- **For §3: the statement E.17 quoted above is claimed proved.** B.11–B.12: an ind-object of small categories modulo natural transformations, with small filtered index, whose associated functor is invariant under all weak equivalences is isomorphic as an ind-object to its stagewise fundamental groupoids. The proof rests on a cardinal-controlled Kan–Thurston theorem (B.11) that has not been checked by us yet.
- **A second route to the higher levels, for comparison only.** F.5–F.6: Kaledin (arXiv:2409.17489v3, Corollary 6.1.2.5, Theorem 7.3.1.3; preprint, Chapters 6–7 marked preliminary) recovers a homotopy type from the ordinary groupoids `Π_1 Map(BJ, X)` indexed by partially ordered sets `J`: the level of the values stays 1 and the shapes vary, instead of raising the coefficients. `Π_1 Map(BJ, X)` is not `Map(BJ, Π_1 X)` (F.6), so this is not a construction on the multi-Galois topos alone.

- **Gluing a topos from an open part, a closed part and a link (Parts B and C).** STRATA C.1, C.6: a category `X` with a sieve `A` (open) and the complementary cosieve `B` (closed) is the same thing as `A`, `B` and the bimodule `M(a, b) = Hom_X(a, b)` of crossing arrows; its presheaf topos is glued from those of `A` and `B` along the functor `i^*j_*`, as in SGA 4 IV 9.5.4 (the manuscript of *Les Dérivateurs* reproduces this at XII ms. 62–63), and `i^*j_* ≃ t_*s^*`, where `s, t` are the source and target functors of the category `E` of crossing arrows and commuting squares. C.2 (Known there; Douteau–Waas, arXiv:2112.02394v2, Theorem 6.1): `BE` is the homotopy link of the closed part, that is the homotopy type of a punctured neighbourhood of `BB` in `BX`, with its two maps to `BB` and to the open part. C.3: `BX` is the homotopy pushout of `BA ← BE → BB`. C.8: for locally constant coefficients in any complete and cocomplete ∞-category, in particular for sets, `Fun(BX, 𝒞)` is the pullback of `Fun(BA, 𝒞) → Fun(BE, 𝒞) ← Fun(BB, 𝒞)`; for sets this is a van Kampen theorem for the fundamental groupoid of the glued topos, with the link as the third term. C.4 (Refuted): the link cannot be replaced by the space of all paths between the two parts. So for presheaf topoi there are three different objects built from an open part and local data at the closed part: the open part alone; the glued topos (constructible objects); and the locally constant objects on the glued topos (the pushout of groupoids).
