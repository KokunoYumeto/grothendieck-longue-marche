### 0.3 Lemme 5 of §35 from slimness (so (2), (4) are not needed)

*Claim.* π a slim profinite group, π′ ⊆ π open, u ∈ Aut(π) with u|π′ = id. Then u = id. If u|π′ = int(a)|π′ for some a ∈ π, then u = int(a).
*Proof.* π″ := the intersection of the conjugates of π′ is open, normal in π, and u is the identity on it. For g ∈ π, h ∈ π″: ghg^{-1} ∈ π″, so ghg^{-1} = u(ghg^{-1}) = u(g) h u(g)^{-1}; hence u(g)^{-1}g ∈ Z_π(π″) = 1. For the second statement apply the first to int(a)^{-1}∘u. ∎
So Lemme 5 (2:sc498–501, "prouvé seulement modulo (2), (4)") and Corollaire 2 (2:sc499–500) are theorems by [Mzk04] Lemma 1.3.1.

### 0.4 F8: what follows for Θ_{g,ν} (§32 (f), 2:sc396–398) and for the Remarque of §35 (2:sc502–505)

With the §29 definition (N_{g,ν} := image of π_1(ℳ_{g,ν,Q}) in Out(π̂_{g,ν}); Γ_{g,ν} := N_{g,ν}/(image of the geometric π_1)), Θ_{g,ν}: Γ_Q ↠ Γ_{g,ν}.
*Proposition (from [HM11] Cor. 6.4).* Θ_{g,ν} is injective for every (g, ν) with 2g − 2 + ν > 0, for ordered and for unordered points.
*Proof.* Ordered points: let X be a curve of type (g, ν) with ordered rational cusps over a number field k, and σ ∈ G_k with Θ(σ) = 1, i.e. ρ_{X/k}(σ) ∈ ρ_{g,ν}(Δ_ℳ). By Cor. 6.4, σ ∈ Ker(G_k → G_Q) = 1. So Ker Θ ∩ G_k = 1, Ker Θ is a finite normal subgroup of G_Q, hence trivial (the elements of finite order of G_Q are the conjugates of complex conjugation, by Artin–Schreier, and they are infinitely many). Unordered points: if ρ(σ) lies in the image of the geometric group of the unordered stack, then ρ(σ^{ν!}) lies in ρ_{g,ν}(Δ_ℳ), so σ^{ν!} = 1 by the first case (apply it over k and then over Q as before), σ is 1 or a complex conjugation c; and ρ(c) is not geometric because c acts on the inertia groups through χ(c) = −1 while geometric classes act on them through +1. ∎
Consequences. (a) "(f)" in the form "the kernels of Θ_{g,ν} do not depend on ν" is true, every kernel being trivial; his doubt ("il ne serait pas impossible que (f) soit faux") is removed. (b) The stronger faithfulness of the Remarque of §35 (Γ → N(π)/𝒯̂⁺(π) injective), which he had only for the types (0, ν), holds for all types, (g, 0) included; his §36 theorem for (1, 1) is the case r ≥ 1 of Matsumoto–Tamagawa 2000, Theorem 1.1 (as described in [HM11]). (c) What is *not* given: the isomorphism (6) N^!_{g,ν} ≅ M^!_{g,ν−1} of 2:sc396 as groups. By [HM11] Cor. 6.5 the kernel of π_1(ℳ_{g,ν,Q}) → Out(π̂_{g,ν}) is the congruence kernel of type (g, ν) (it lies in the geometric part), so (6) holds if and only if the congruence kernels of types (g, ν) and (g, ν−1) correspond; this is F4, outside this fork's reading. His reduction "(6) bijective ⇔ Θ_{g,ν} and Θ_{g,ν−1} have the same kernel" silently uses the congruence subgroup property for both types.
(d) For H2 of HUNCHES.md: the thresholds of [HM11] Theorem B are: injective for n ≥ 1; bijective for n ≥ n_0 + 1, n_0 = 2 (affine), 3 (proper), i.e. n ≥ 3 resp. n ≥ 4 (as of the 2011 paper; later papers of Hoshi–Minamide–Mochizuki were not read here).
