## Parts

Every lettered ask is one or more rows of the results register, done only as "What the outcome must be" says: proved in his form or its strongest correct form; refuted by a complete counterexample with the strongest true statement proved in its place; or partly settled with the cases proved and the obstruction as an exact statement with the smallest undecided instance. SGA 1, SGA 4 and the later Galois theory of topoi are tools: when one of them gives a statement of his, cite the theorem and write the derivation of his statement in his generality and his form (no base points, no connectedness, groupoids and not groups).

**A. §§1–4: multi-Galois topoi (scans 6–18).**
(a) Proposition 1.1 (scan 6). Prove b ⇔ b' ⇒ a. For a ⇒ b he is "moins sûr, peut être faut il supposer que E est loc connexe, et qu'il a suffisamment de foncteurs fibres": decide it. Either prove a ⇒ b with no further hypothesis, or give a topos all of whose objects are locally constant that is not of the form `Ĉ` for a groupoid `C`, and then prove a ⇒ b under the weakest hypothesis that makes it true (test: locally connected; enough points; both; a Boolean topos without points). Give condition c) ("conditions d'exactitude ad hoc, du type de celles données dans SGA 1") as an explicit list of exactness axioms on a category and prove c ⇔ b.
(b) Prove 1.3, 1.4, 1.5 (all five conditions, with the N.B. on commuting with sums and quotients), 1.7, 1.8, 1.10 and 1.11 as stated, and write out 1.12, which is only a title (scan 11): the explicit description of `Hom_top(Ens(G), Ens(G'))` for pointed connected multi-Galois topoi, as a groupoid, with the proof.
(c) Theorem 2.1 and Definition 2.2 (scans 12–13): prove that `E_lc` is a multi-Galois topos and that the inclusion commutes with finite projective limits and arbitrary inductive limits, under his hypotheses (locally connected, locally simply connected), and decide his marginal doubt (is "enough points" needed?) by proof or counterexample. Construct the tower `E → ⋯ → E_[i] → E_[i-1] → ⋯ → E_[1] → E_[0]` he announces "moyennant des hypothèses convenables (du type locale contractibilité)": define `E_[i]` for every `i`, state the hypothesis exactly, prove the universal property of each stage, and prove that for the topos of a locally contractible space the tower is the Postnikov tower.
(d) 2.3 (scans 14–15): construct the two phenomena he asserts without example: a 1-connected topos `E` in which some `Hom` in `Pt(E)` has more than one element, and a locally connected, locally simply connected topos with a universal covering that is not defined by any point. Prove that `β` is an equivalence and determine exactly when `α` is faithful, full, essentially surjective.
(e) §3 is a title (scan 16: "Variantes pro-multigaloisiennes, resp. profinies"). Write the section: define pro-multi-Galois and profinite multi-Galois topoi and their fundamental pro-groupoids, and prove the analogues of 1.1, 1.4, 1.7, 1.11 and 2.1 for them, in the generality needed for the étale topos of a locally noetherian scheme and for the systems of groupoids of §7.
(f) §4 (scans 16–18): prove that a morphism of multi-Galois topoi is an induction morphism if and only if the functor of fundamental groupoids is faithful; prove the equivalence of `E` with the category of groupoids faithful over `C` with classes of pairs `(f, α)` as morphisms, and do what his margin asks ("Préciser les isomorphismes entre couples (f, α) et (g, β)"): describe the 2-category, prove that its Hom-categories are equivalence relations, and identify the quotient. Apply it to the étale schemes over a field as he does on scan 18.
Done for Part A: every numbered statement of §§1–2 and §4 is proved or corrected with a counterexample; 1.12, the tower of 2.2 and §3 exist as written sections with proofs.

**B. §14: the mixed topos and the cohomology of hole plugging (scans 119–137).**
(a) Define `B_{X,U}` (later `B_{X;S}`) exactly as on scans 123–127, prove that `B_{π_1,U} → B_{X,U}` is an open induction morphism with closed complement `B_{π_1,D}` and gluing functor `σ_* ρ^*`, and prove his formula `π_1(X) = π_1(U)/⟨⟨ρ_i(Π_i)⟩⟩` (scan 123).
(b) His question b) of scan 124: for which `(X, S)` and which torsion sheaves `F` on `B_{X,U}` is `H^*(B_{X,U}, F) → H^*(X_ét, f^*F)` an isomorphism? Prove his Proposition of scan 129 (it holds as soon as the cohomology of `U` is computed by `π_1(U)`), and then decide the hypothesis: prove that an affine curve over any field of characteristic 0, a proper curve of genus ≥ 1, and their open subschemes are `K(π,1)` for torsion locally constant coefficients (this answers his "quasi-projective ?" and his margin "il faut un argument spécial" for genus ≠ 0), and give the complete list of exceptions with the degree in which the comparison fails for each (genus 0, by his margin "Non, à cause du genre 0 !!!"). Treat positive characteristic with prime-to-`p` coefficients and say what changes for `p`-torsion.
(c) Prove the canonical isomorphisms of scans 130–131: `H²(π, T) ≅ Ẑ` for `S = ∅`; `H²_{s_i}(B_{U,X}, Ẑ) ≅ L_i^{⊗-1}`; `L_i ≅ H²(π, Ẑ)^{⊗-1}` for `g ≠ 0`, independent of `i`; and his marginal claim that in every case with `ν ≠ 0`, `H²_!(B_{π_1 U}, Ẑ) ≅ T_π^{⊗-1}`. Then carry out what he asks on scans 131–132: for a homomorphism of groups with loops `f: π' → π` with `τ: I'_0 → I` and degrees `d`, construct the morphisms of topoi `B_{X',U'} → B_{X,U}` and the trace map on cohomology with proper supports, and prove that it induces the isomorphism of orientation modules.
(d) Mixed morphisms (scans 132–137): construct the morphism `B_{X',U'} → B_{X,U}` for `S' = S'_0 ⊔ S'_1` from the data he lists (the torsors `P_{i'}` under `π`), prove his description with a group `Γ` of operators (a torsor `P` and a splitting of `E^P → Γ` over `Γ_{s'}`), and prove the rigidity statement of scan 136 (unique isomorphism when `π^{Γ_{s'}} = 1`).
Done for Part B: (a), (c), (d) proved; (b) is a theorem with a complete list of exceptions.
