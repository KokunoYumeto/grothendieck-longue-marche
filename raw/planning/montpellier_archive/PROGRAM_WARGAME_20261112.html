1. The shape of the mathematics

1.1 The books

Book I. Elements, continued (51 cotes, 3,158 pp.).

  • Objects. Schemes and their morphisms; representable functors (Hilbert, Hom, Picard); descent; group schemes and torsors; residues and duality; intersections; étale cohomology and the fundamental group.
  • Central questions. The chapters of the EGA after IV: which constructions exist and in what generality.
  • What he had, where he stopped. EGA I–IV in print (1960–67). The plan changed at least twice: thirteen chapters in the printed introduction of EGA I (1960) [B, from memory of that introduction], twelve in the plan transcribed as CSG U90d [C, SECTIONS_STATE.md §4]; cotes 26 and 48 are a third witness, unread. The prenotes of chapter V are typed (cote 25). The drafts for VI are short (102, 126–128, 131, 98, 91).
  • Later. Artin's algebraic spaces and representability criteria answered most of what an "EGA VI" had to settle [B]. The Stacks project is the continuation in fact: it has a chapter for nearly every chapter he planned [B]. So the map of his drafts has three columns (print by number, Stacks tags, the draft), and the residue to develop is expected to be small [G]: chapter V itself, non-flat descent (127, 128), Hom functors by non-projective methods (91), and what 26 lists as open.

Book II. Cohomology and motives (29 cotes, 2,397 pp.).

  • Objects. De Rham cohomology as a crystal; Barsotti–Tate groups; cycles, correspondences, Poincaré–Hodge algebras (16); motives over a field and over a base (12); motivic Galois groups (13, 15); ⊗-categories with fibre functors (10, 155); the divided-power de Rham complex (136, 137).
  • Central questions. One cohomology behind the ℓ-adic, de Rham and crystalline ones; which classes are algebraic; the Galois group seen through linear objects; in 1975–76, which multiplicative structure on cochains carries a homotopy type over Z.
  • What he had, where he stopped. Crystals were handed on in 1966–70 (letter to Tate; Pisa 1969). Motives were never written up ("Motifs", 24 typed pages from cote 12, is the only text [C, SECTIONS_STATE.md §3]). The standard conjectures are open in general. The 1976 lecture is lost; 460 pages of notes remain.
  • Later. Berthelot, Messing, Illusie; de Jong; Fontaine. At a prime p the common source he asked for is prismatic cohomology (Bhatt–Scholze), and crystals are quasi-coherent sheaves on a stack attached to the scheme (the de Rham stack; prismatization: Drinfeld, Bhatt–Lurie) [B]. Motives: Jannsen 1992; motivic Galois groups without the standard conjectures (Nori; Ayoub 2014) and motives over a base with the six operations (Ayoub 2007, built on derivators; Cisinski–Déglise) [B]. Standard conjectures: abelian varieties (Lieberman; Ancona for fourfolds in characteristic p [B]; all of them by 032 with Milne's theorems, claimed [C, RECENT_RESULTS.md L6]). Integral homotopy types by cochains with extra structure: Toën 2020 (the group scheme of integer-valued polynomials) and Horel 2024 (cosimplicial binomial rings) [C, lookups 2 and 5]. The plan's account of 136–137 names none of this line (section 2.3, stage 8).

Book III. The Long March (17 cotes, 2,922 pp.).

  • Objects. Profinite groups with loops (π̂ with its inertia classes); extensions E → Γ; Teichmüller groups and their two completions; the modular multiplicities M(g,ν) and their Deligne–Mumford compactifications; MD-graphs and "maquettes"; Teichmüller groupoids with their structure at infinity; sections and "paquets" at the cusps; the category of Galois–Teichmüller (finite étale maps between curves up to isotopy; 142 scans 3–22 [C]).
  • Central questions. Determine Γ_Q by its outer actions; say which outer homomorphisms are geometric; describe the whole by generators and relations.
  • What he had, where he stopped. 1978–80: the isotopy category, its "enveloppe profinie" without a universal property, and the conjecture that compatibility with all coverings and all morphisms defined over K is "suffisamment draconienne" to single out Γ (142 scans 12, 17, 21, 95–96 [C]); he calls it "de nature trop platonique" and turns to M(0,4), M(0,5) (scan 100 [C]). 1981: in today's terms Γ′(0,3) = GT₀ and Γ′(0,ν) = GT for ν ≥ 4 (LM2 B.m2; LM3 E.7, E.10: the returns' claims); he stops at the filling map ("corollaire hâtif", 2:sc349) and at centre and normalizer statements he could not prove profinitely (FINDINGS F5). Cote 147: thirteen sections; §12 ends with his "Remords" and the text ends inside the digression of §13. Cote 149 (1983): the conjectures in their last form.
  • Later. Belyi 1979; Drinfeld 1990, Ihara; Nakamura, Tamagawa, Mochizuki 1999 (the Hom-form); Harbater–Schneps 2000; Hoshi–Mochizuki 2011; Pop 2019 (I/OM), which is the 1978 conjecture in its tripod form [B for the identification]; Boggi 2026; Carlson–Haine–Wolf 2024 for the étale-topos form of the letter to Faltings (finitely generated fields of characteristic 0, after inverting universal homeomorphisms) [C, lookup 3]. The section conjecture over number fields is open; 019 claims the p-adic case.

Book IV. Maps and polyhedra (20 cotes, 1,954 pp.).

  • Objects. Maps as finite sets with an action of the cartographic group; cellular n-maps; regular polyhedra as the most symmetric maps, and their forms over rings and finite fields; polygons and cyclotomy; quadrics, quadratic forms, SO(3) ≃ GP(1), Azumaya algebras of rank 4.
  • Central questions. The Galois action on maps; the classification of regular polyhedra over a ring and their specialization.
  • What he had, where he stopped. Course texts of 1976–78; notes to 1986; in print only Esquisse §§3–4.
  • Later. SECTIONS_STATE.md and the recommendation say the programme has almost no successor. That is wrong. Abstract regular polytopes (McMullen–Schulte) are his cellular n-maps with a regularity condition [B]; Monson and Schulte (2004–08) construct regular polytopes over finite fields by reducing crystallographic Coxeter groups modulo p [C, lookup 6]; Γ_Q acts faithfully on regular dessins (González-Diez and Jaikin-Zapirain) [C, lookup 8, secondary sources]; GT-shadows compute the action of finite levels of GT on dessins (Guillot; Dolgushev and coauthors 2024) [C, lookup 14]; Ji 2023.
  • Prediction [G]: cote 79 ("2-polyèdres réguliers triangulés et modules de rang 2 sur Z/nZ et Z") is the combinatorial theory of the modular curves X(n); the Longue Marche §§36–37 has the cases n = 2, 3, 4, 5 (FINDINGS F14). Cote 83 (icosahedra, bi-icosahedra) is tied to M(0,5). The tie itself is in the returns [C]: 147 (near scan 68) reads the ten divisors at infinity of M̂(0,I), I of five elements, as the ten pairs of I, "d'après l'étude combinatoire de l'icosaèdre, choisissant une orientation de I — ce qui détermine un icosaèdre orienté", and asks for the full S₅-action (notes N068-02); cote 146 names the icosahedron five times; the Esquisse says the same [C, lookup 13].

Book V. Tame topology and the geometry of forms (18 cotes, 1,319 pp.).

  • Objects. Tame spaces; stratified spaces with conical neighbourhoods; unfolding ("déploiement"); stratified topoi; "stratifications locales"; in 1986 forms, networks, cuttings, workshops.
  • Central questions. A topology in which a stratified object has tubular neighbourhoods and can be taken apart and rebuilt; comparison of the tame theories.
  • What he had, where he stopped. 120–121 (1973–79), unread. 147 §§11–13 [C]: four elementary rank-one models (C*, the point, the germ of disc, the germ of punctured disc; scan 164), the expectation that products of these give all elementary topoi (scan 165) and express all canonical topoi of the stratification (scan 169), the reconstruction remark and the "Remords" (scans 179–180), axioms a)–d) of a stratification (scan 181). 150–151 (Dec 1981–Jan 1982), unread. 156 (1986): four versions of one chapter in six weeks, unread.
  • Later. O-minimal structures; the o-minimal Hauptvermutung of Shiota (2014): definably homeomorphic compact polyhedra are PL homeomorphic [C, lookup 10]. Exit paths and exodromy (MacPherson; Lurie; Barwick–Glasman–Haine; the unfolding is the "unzip" of Ayala–Francis–Tanaka [B]; the homotopy link, Douteau–Waas, in STRATA C.2).

Book VI. Stacks and derivators (34 cotes, 3,702 pp.).

  • Objects. Small categories as models of homotopy types; fundamental localizers; test categories; n-stacks; schematization; derivators; total asphericity; fibrations and cofibrations of categories.
  • Central questions. What a homotopy type is, algebraically; what a category of coefficients is; which axioms single out W∞.
  • What he had, where he stopped. The Tapis de Quillen (1968); Pursuing Stacks (1983); Les Dérivateurs (1990–91). The points where he stopped are now located [C, FINDINGS]: XII Théorème 2 was not verified by him and is false for W∞ (F-G1); the questions of XVI are answered (F3-3); the conjecture of II ms. 36ter is false (Δ_inj), and its true form is today's theorem (F4-6, unchecked).

His Remarque: when all strata D* are K(π,1) ("ce qui est le cas dans la situation de Teichmüller !"), the system of the fundamental groupoids of the elementary pieces with the canonical morphisms must rebuild the groupoid of X, the "topos quasi-galoisien" and "par là aussi les types d'homotopie … y compris X"; margin: this must hold "indépendamment de toute autre hypothèse (genre K(π,1)) de façon essentiellement triviale". S1. The tower with its structure at infinity is a system of profinite exit-path categories, and GT is its automorphism group

(a) E(g,n) is an ordinary category and its classifying space is the compactification. S6. The cyclotomic kernel sees the cusps (found in this pass; unchecked)

  • Statement. Let U be a hyperbolic curve over a number field K, π its geometric fundamental group, s any section of π₁(U) → Γ_K, and Γ° the kernel of the cyclotomic character. Then the centralizer of s(Γ°) in π is trivial if s is not cuspidal, and is the inertia group L_i if s is cuspidal at the cusp i. Hence s is cuspidal if and only if that centralizer is non-trivial, and the inertia groups are exactly the non-trivial centralizers of this kind for germs of sections.
  • Argument [B, mine]. Proper case. Let z ≠ 1 centralize s(Γ°); take a characteristic open N with z ∉ N and put H = N⟨z⟩. For a finite extension K′, s(Γ_K′) normalizes H, so H is the geometric group of a covering defined over K′, and the class of z in H^ab is fixed by Gal(K̄/K′(μ_∞)). H^ab is the Tate module of a Jacobian, and its invariants under that group vanish because the torsion of an abelian variety over K′(μ_∞) is finite (Ribet, 1981, appendix to Katz–Lang; cited, not opened today). But z generates H/N ≠ 1. Affine case. The invariants of H^ab are its part of weight −2, so z lies in an inertia group by Nakamura's characterization of inertia elements (1994, near (2.1.1)–(2.1.4); from memory); the centralizer lies in one L_i; the normalizer statements of FINDINGS F5 then put s(Γ) in the decomposition group of i.
  • Rests on. 149 scan 55 ("hautement plausible", to be put among the "conjectures-clefs") and the margin of scan 56; LM 2:sc433; FINDINGS F17 (2:sc483 is false as written) and hunch H5, whose note calls the point case conjectural. It agrees with LM3b D.5: a line with complex multiplication has an open normalizer and is not centralized by Γ°.
  • Plausibility. Proper case: I hold it proved, given Ribet's theorem. Affine case: it hangs on one lemma quoted from memory. Novelty: one search found nothing; none is claimed.
  • What would decide it. A check by GPT-6 Astra; then finitely generated fields (Katz–Lang), M(g,n), real places.
  • Bears on. III (149; LM §§32–34); LM6. V 4, T 5. Pursue (CYCLOTOMIC_KERNEL; then into LM6).
