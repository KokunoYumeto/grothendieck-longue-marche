# The open part of Grothendieck's mathematical archive at Montpellier: what each section contains, and how far it was developed

State of research: 10 November 2026. Scope: the 178 cotes of the open part listed at https://grothendieck.umontpellier.fr/archives-grothendieck/ (local copy `source/archives-grothendieck_listing_20261110.html`; machine-readable `MANIFEST.json`). Page counts are the site's scan counts, rectos and versos, 16,074 in all; the site warns that versos are often unrelated to the notes. The PDFs themselves were not opened for this report: the description of each folder rests on the archive's titles and footnotes and on the literature.

## Conventions

- **[V]**: I opened the source and checked the claim. **[S]**: known from a secondary mention, a catalogue record, or a link that resolves to the right title but whose content I did not read.
- **Edition / transcription**: a typed or printed text of this manuscript, complete or partial. **Published version**: a text Grothendieck (or a note-taker) published on the same subject; it is not an edition of the folder. **Later development**: mathematics developed by others, whether or not they knew the manuscript.
- **Under-described pages**: scan pages for which I found no edition, no transcription and no study of the manuscript itself. These are estimates: partial transcriptions are subtracted by their approximate extent, and versos inflate all counts. Each section keeps two questions apart: *was this manuscript read and published?* and *was the mathematics developed later by others?*

## How the site is organised

The archive's tree (verified from the indentation in the listing HTML) has topic folders and, between them, single top-level cotes. `MANIFEST.json` attaches each top-level cote to the preceding topic; the 22 topics below follow that grouping, as requested, and each section says which cotes are top-level. Top-level cotes: 1, 2, 19, 39–42, 55, 56, 60, 90–102, 122, 123, 125, 129–133, 135, 138, 139, 152–155, 158–160, 162-1, 162-5, 162-6.

Two consequences matter:
- Cotes **90–102** (592 pp.) are algebraic geometry of 1964–1981 and are not part of "Géométrie et topologie combinatoire". That group is **69–89** (2,063 pp.), nested inside "Autour de l'enseignement" (66–89) and dated 1976–c.1986 by the site. The table below gives 90–102 a row of its own.
- The site's footnotes [110] and [111] on the two "Topologie modérée" cotes (120, 121) repeat the Riemann–Roch footnotes of cote 2. They say nothing about these folders.

Facts about the fonds [V]: about 28,000 pages, about 18,000 accessible (https://grothendieck.umontpellier.fr/); online since 10 May 2017 (https://www.umontpellier.fr/en/articles/28-000-pages-dalexandre-grothendieck/); given by Grothendieck to Jean Malgoire, kept by him, and deposited at the university in 2010 (IHES note, https://www.ihes.fr/?p=8611).

Group footnotes worth keeping in view [V]: the abelian-varieties series (45–54) was assembled by Grothendieck and filed anti-chronologically (the site reverses it); the "Autour de Pursuing stacks" dossier (103–118) was assembled by Grothendieck in its present order; the Pursuing Stacks typescript was written 19 February–4 November 1983; folders 161-1 to 161-6 were numbered 1–6 by Grothendieck; Malgoire dates Les Dérivateurs October 1990–first half of 1991, while the Grothendieck Circle gives 1987.

## Section by section

### 1. Mathématiques / Recherche / Travaux (cotes 1–2, both top-level; 193 pp.)

**What.** Cote 1 (149 pp.): undated handwritten notes on functional analysis with a letter of 1953. Cote 2 (44 pp.): "Théorème de Riemann-Roch / RR Notes antiques (antérieures à 1958)". The archive's footnotes date the proof to 1957 (Récoltes et Semailles, note 3, p. 448) and recall the 1958 Collège de France course on Chern classes and Riemann–Roch [V].

**Grothendieck's state; published versions.** Working notes for mathematics he published himself. Functional analysis: the thesis *Produits tensoriels topologiques et espaces nucléaires* (Mem. (10; 1984).

**Grothendieck's state; published versions.** Notes. His summary is in Esquisse §§5–6 (dévissage of stratified structures, tame topology, the stratified structure of the modular multiplicities M̂_{g,ν}); the Esquisse dates the resumption of his reflection on stratified structures to December 1981–January 1982 [V].

**Read and published?**
- "Structures stratifiées" (1981), §§1–4, about five pages with many gaps, in Carmona's 2022 collection, https://web.archive.org/web/20221006223311/https://agrothendieck.github.io/divers/unpworks.pdf [V]; the CSG catalogue assigns it to cote 151 [V].
- Cote 154: pages reproduced, without analysis, by L. Le Bruyn, "Grothendieck's gallery No. 154" (25 July 2017), https://lievenlebruyn.github.io/neverendingbooks/grothendiecks-gallery-no-154/ [V].
- 150, 152, 153, 155: nothing found.

**Developed later.** Stratified spaces: Thom–Mather, Siebenmann, Quinn [S]; exit-path categories (MacPherson; Lurie, *Higher Algebra*, appendix A) [S]; Ayala–Francis–Tanaka, "Local structures on stratified spaces", https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0501; Barwick–Glasman–Haine, "Exodromy", https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03281 [titles checked]. None of these uses Grothendieck's notes. Pseudoline arrangements: Felsner–Goodman (§11) [S].

**Under-described.** ≈370 pp.

### 20. Vers une géométrie des formes (cotes 156-1–156-9; 691 pp.)

**What.** Nine chapters, each dated: I towards a geometry of (topological) forms (26 pp., 5.6.1986); II topological realisations of networks (18, 6.6); III networks via cuttings (40, 8.6); IV Analysis situs, first version (88, 10.6); V algebra of figures (48, 14.6); VI Analysis situs, second version (93, 18–20.6); VII third version (113, 23–26.6); VIII fourth version (126, 26.6–4.7; annotation 12.7.1986); IX and IX bis, "ateliers", with diagrams of the main types of workshops (139, 5–15.7).

**Grothendieck's state.** A sustained draft of six weeks, with four successive versions of the central chapter: work in progress, not a finished text. In letters of 1986 (to R. Brown, 17.6; to Yamashita, 9.6 and 16.9) he describes it as an approach to "topology" and "form" different from topological spaces, from topoi and from the tame spaces of the Esquisse, and links it to Riemann and to the Poincaré conjecture (as quoted by Cruz Morales) [V].

**Read and published?** Fragments only.
- M. Carmona, "Vers une géométrie des formes", cote 156-1 to 156-9, "déchiffré et transcrit", 12-page PDF: openings of chapters I–III and VI, many undeciphered words, chapters IV and V empty, https://web.archive.org/web/20210719150642/https://agrothendieck.github.io/divers/Formes.pdf [V]; reprinted in the 2022 collection [V]. The CSG catalogue lists the manuscript without a transcription [V].
- Study: J. A. Cruz Morales, "The notion of space in Grothendieck: from schemes to a geometry of forms" (2021; Handbook of the History and Philosophy of Mathematical Practice), https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08799 [V]. He gives the chapter titles, says the handwriting makes the manuscript hard to decipher, and states that his earlier discussions (a Spanish chapter, Bogotá 2017; a communication at ICM 2018) rested on Grothendieck's correspondence rather than on the manuscript [V].

**Developed later.** No later mathematics takes up this programme (searched: CSG catalogue, Circle, arXiv via web search, the Cruz Morales bibliography).

**Under-described.** ≈685 pp., practically the whole text.

### 21. Dérivateurs (cotes 157-1–157-5; 158–160 top-level; 2,275 pp.)

**What.** 157-1 chapters I–IV with a general summary (270 pp.; gap in pp. 33–40); 157-2 chapters V–VIII (441; date 14.11.1990); 157-3 chapters IX–XII with summary and appendices of XII (355); 157-4 chapters XIII–XVI (408); 157-5 chapters XVII–XIX with summaries (661). Top-level: 158 around derivators (85; c.1990–91); 159 projective connections and uniformization (33; 1970s–1980s per Malgoire; recalls the 1961 Cartan seminar); 160 group schemes (22; subject of SGA 3 III).

**Grothendieck's state.** A near-finished research text with chapter summaries, about 1,976 manuscript pages, October 1990 to the first half of 1991 [V, KMM page and archive note]. His letter to R. Thomason of 2.4.1991 introduces the theory [V].

**Read and published?** Fully, for 157.
- *Les Dérivateurs*, electronic edition by M. Künzer, J. Malgoire and G. - Letter to Thomason: https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~georges.maltsiniotis/groth/ps/lettreder.pdf [link checked]; English translation by T. Hosgood on the Circle, https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~leila.schneps/grothendieckcircle/Letters/LetterGrothendieckThomason.pdf [V listing].
- 158–160: nothing found.

**Developed later.** Fully, in parallel and afterwards: A. Heller, *Homotopy Theories*, Mem. AMS 383 (1988), https://doi.org/10.1090/memo/0383; J. Franke (1996) [S]; D.-C. **Under-described.** 140 pp. (158–160).

### 22. Dossiers rassemblés par Grothendieck (cotes 161-1–161-6; 162-1, 162-5, 162-6 top-level; 358 pp.)

**What.** Folders numbered 1–6 by Grothendieck [V]: 161-1 categories (19 pp.; linked to topoi per Malgoire); 161-2 universal algebra, or categories (111; c.1963–73); 161-3 topos, a popular presentation (54); 161-4 introduction to algebraic geometry, a popular presentation, perhaps for Vietnam, including flat descent (31); 161-5 Barsotti–Tate, annotated offprints 1967–69 (28); 161-6 graphs, icosahedron (33; c.1973–77; the icosahedron course of 1977). Top-level: 162-1 borrowed papers, a list of names and references (9); 162-5 Tapis de Quillen (68; typescript and copies 1968, notes 1968, offprint 1968; a bibliography up to 1970); 162-6 isolated documents (5).

**Grothendieck's state.** Notes and offprints. "Tapis de Quillen" is a typescript of 6 and 10 September 1968 with three parts: relations between categories and semi-simplicial sets (the nerve functor; when a functor induces a homotopy equivalence, via the Artin–Mazur cohomological criterion and the associated topoi); n-categories, n-fold categories and Gr-categories; a "motivic" point of view in cobordism theory [V, Carmona's contents and the CSG scan].

**Read and published?**
- 162-5: scan of the typescript (17 pp., 2018, with OCR), CSG U7u1, https://csg.igrothendieck.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/U7u1.pdf [V]; partial transcription by M. Carmona (part I, 3 pp., 2021), https://web.archive.org/web/20211118185830/https://agrothendieck.github.io/divers/tapisQ.pdf [V], extended with a passage of part 3 in the 2022 collection, https://web.archive.org/web/20221006223311/https://agrothendieck.github.io/divers/unpworks.pdf [V]. Copies of the same typescript are in cote 111.
- 161-1 to 161-4, 161-6, 162-1, 162-6: nothing found. (The CSG's "Sur la formalisation des catégories et foncteurs", 1958, comes from the Bourbaki archives, not from 161-1 or 161-2 [V catalogue].)

**Developed later.** The homotopy theory of small categories: Quillen's Theorems A and B (1973), Thomason's model structure on Cat (1980), test categories and Les Dérivateurs (§§15, 21) [S]. The 1968 date makes Tapis de Quillen the earliest text in the archive on this theme.

**Under-described.** ≈290 pp.
