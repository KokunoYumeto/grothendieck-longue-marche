You made the complete untranscribed archive, transcribed and developed in full by the most advanced current models, the main project for the coming weeks. You gave four reasons:

  • It is frontier mathematics.

6.2 Which parts need a full pass and which a light one (your question today)

Two kinds of pass:

  • Full pass, as for La Longue Marche, all by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max.
    • First, every line is read against its scan and the fixes are applied.
    • Then the global pass per chapter writes the page headers, the question-register entries and the concordance. It works in what we already have (the Longue Marche findings, Les Dérivateurs companion and NEW_RESULTS, the Pro returns), so each chapter's companion builds on the earlier ones.
    This is the part you called essential: most of this has never been edited. Fable takes no part in the second pass. After the pass, Fable reads the checked chapter and does the mathematics (section 7).
  • Light pass, where an edition or print already exists:
    • Against print: drafts of texts that were printed (most of Book I) get a map against the print. The full pass then covers only what print does not contain.
    • Against an existing edition, by script: typescripts and texts already written up are diffed against that edition, and Opus reads only the places that differ. This covers Pursuing Stacks (arXiv and SMF), the Esquisse (print), the Tapis de Quillen (partial transcription), part of cote 12 ("Motifs") and cotes 69 and 75 (the CSG course texts).
    • Les Dérivateurs: only the concordance and the doubtful places.
