# The Montpellier archive as one body of mathematics: organization, presentation and order

Recommendation of 10 November 2026, by Claude (Fable 5.2), asked for by the user: how to organize the 178 cotes
(16,074 site pages) so that the result is something people open and want to read, and the transcription order that
follows from it. Basis: `SECTIONS_STATE.md`, `PLAN_EVERYTHING.md`, `MANIFEST.csv`, and sample pages of 11 downloaded
cotes opened to judge legibility (nothing transcribed). Cotes not yet downloaded (83 and up, except 134 and 157-1) are
judged from the archive's titles and notes only.

## 1. The recommendation in short

- **Organize by programme, not by cote.** The archive's 22 sections are filing units. The mathematics forms six
  programmes, and Grothendieck left two tables of contents of his own that cover almost all of it: the **plan of the
  EGA** (chapters V to XII, 1960s) for the Paris years, and the **Esquisse d'un programme** (1984) for the Montpellier
  years. Use those two as the spines. Between them sits the work he never planned on paper: cohomology and motives.
- **Six books and a prelude**, each a programme with a beginning, an aim and an open end (section 3).
- **One front door: the Esquisse.** It is short, already loved, and each of its sections points at one book. A reader
  who clicks a paragraph of the Esquisse lands on the notes behind it.
- **Every page has three layers**: his text (scan, French, English); a commentary that says what it is and what became
  of it; a development with full proofs. The reader chooses the depth.
- **Transcription order**: follow the books that feed work in progress and hold the least-read mathematics first; the
  logical order of the books is different, and section 6 says where and why.

## 2. The mathematics underneath: six programmes

**The two periods.** Paris and the IHES, 1957 to 1970: foundations of algebraic geometry and the cohomology of
varieties. Montpellier, 1973 to 1991: geometry of surfaces and maps, Galois actions, new foundations for topology and
for homotopy. The archive holds both, and the second period is the one almost nobody has read.

**How they depend on each other.**

- **A carries B.** Motives and crystals are stated in the language of A (Picard, duality, étale cohomology,
  intersections). The planned chapters X to XII of the EGA are the foundations B needs. This is where "EGA V and
  after" sits: it is programme A, the common base of everything from the Paris years, and the first reference of every
  other book.
- **B and C are the same question asked twice.** B studies the Galois group through linear objects (cohomology,
  motivic Galois groups: cotes 12, 13, 15); C studies it through fundamental groups, where no linearization is made.
  The motivic Galois notes of 1965 and the Longue Marche of 1981 should be cross-linked page by page; the elliptic
  curve is the common first example (cotes 62-64, Longue Marche §37).
- **D is the elementary face of C.** A map on a surface is a curve over a number field (Esquisse §3; cote 138 is the
  earliest folder on it). D is where a reader without scheme theory can see the Galois-Teichmüller programme work.
- **C needs E.** The structure at infinity of M(g,ν) (cote 147) is a stratified structure; the notes on stratified
  spaces and tame topology (150, 151, 120, 121) were written for it. The geometry of forms (156) is the last and most
  radical step of E.
- **B leads into F.** The divided-power de Rham complex of 1975-76 (136, 137) is, by his own account, where the idea of
  schematizing homotopy types arose (*Pursuing Stacks*, chapter VI). It is the hinge between the two periods and
  between B and F.
- **F underlies all.** Topos and categories (19, 161-1 to 161-3), the Tapis de Quillen (1968), the Sính correspondence
  (1974), the notes around *Pursuing Stacks* (104-118) and around the derivators (158) form one line from 1968 to
  1991; the derivators are his final answer to the question of what a derived category of coefficients should be,
  which B had raised.

**What developing each programme would aim at.**

- A: a map of every draft against EGA I-IV, SGA and FGA, then the unwritten chapters, written out with proofs, each
  part's author named. His plan is the table of contents.
- B: his own route to motives set beside what Saavedra, Deligne, Jannsen, André and Voevodsky did; a clear statement of
  what each standard conjecture would give and what survives without it. The notes on cycles and Poincaré-Hodge
  algebras (14, 16) show what he tried; they bear on the hunch that the conjectures fail by showing which reductions
  he had and where he stopped, not by deciding it.
- C: the Longue Marche book, with the surrounding notes as its chapters on Teichmüller groupoids, the boundary, and
  the anabelian questions; each conjecture marked with its present status (Nakamura, Tamagawa, Mochizuki, Pop;
  Grothendieck-Teichmüller theory).
- D: a self-contained text from the cube and icosahedron to regular polyhedra over finite fields and dessins. Almost
  nothing of it has been developed by others; it is also the natural source of course material.
- E: the axioms of tame topology and the dévissage of stratified spaces, compared with o-minimality and exit paths;
  for the geometry of forms, first a faithful account of what the 1986 text does.
- F: the companion to *Les Dérivateurs*, extended backwards through the notes of 1982-84 to *Pursuing Stacks*.

## 3. The published shape

**Working title: _The Montpellier Manuscripts_**, subtitle "Grothendieck's unpublished mathematics: text, translation,
development". One site and one repository family, divided into a front door, six books and a workshop.

The archive's order stays available as a second index (cote, scan), so that every statement can be cited to the
manuscript; the books are the reading order.

**What a reader meets first.** One page: the map of the six books, the timeline, and three doors.

- **For the curious reader:** the annotated Esquisse; his two popular presentations, "Introduction à la géométrie
  algébrique" (161-4) and "Topos" (161-3); the icosahedron (Book IV). These need the least background and are in his
  own explaining voice.
- **For the student:** each book's opening text (the right-hand column above), then the development layer, which is
  written as lessons and linked from the Open Mathematics Courses.
- **For the researcher:** a **register of his questions**. Every question, conjecture and "à expliciter" in the notes
  becomes an entry: his words, cote and scan, and its status today (proved, by whom; refuted; open; not known to have
  been studied). Sources already visible: the list of questions (108), the thesis topics (66), the open problems for
  EGA VI (26), the questions on cycles in 14, the "conjecture hâtive" of the Longue Marche. This register is the part
  of the site most likely to be opened and cited, and it is produced as a by-product of the commentary.
- **For the historian:** the timeline and the concordance, with credit on each page to every earlier transcription
  (Malgoire, Künzer-Malgoire-Maltsiniotis, Elbaz-Vincent, Carmona and the CSG, the Circle).

**The arc.** Read in order, the books tell one story: he builds the foundations (I); asks what cohomology really is
and reaches motives (II); leaves, and comes back to the same Galois group through curves and their moduli (III), which
he first met through maps drawn on surfaces (IV); finds that topology itself is unfit for this and rebuilds it (V);
and rebuilds homotopy theory under all of it (VI). The Esquisse is that story told by him in fifty pages, which is why
it is the door.

**How each unit is presented.** The unit is a folder or a chapter of a long manuscript. Each has one page with:

1. **A header a reader can decide on**: what this is, in two sentences; date; how far he took it (finished text,
   sustained draft, working notes, calculation); what became of the mathematics (in print by him; developed by others,
   with names; not developed).
2. **His text**: reading text in French with the English beside it, each paragraph linked to its scan; the diplomatic
   transcription one click away. Versos that carry unrelated papers are listed in the concordance, not in the text.
3. **Commentary**, in blocks between his paragraphs, as in the *Dérivateurs* companion: the definition he is using,
   where it stands in EGA, SGA, the Stacks project or the later literature, and what is proved, claimed or asked.
4. **Development**, where the folder is research left open: statements and complete proofs in Stacks format with
   stable tags, the author and model of each part named. His text and ours never share a paragraph.

Rigour and invitation are kept apart by the layers: the header and commentary invite, the development proves, and his
text is left as he wrote it.

**Links.** Three kinds, all machine-readable: to print (EGA, SGA, FGA by number; *Pursuing Stacks* and *Les
Dérivateurs* by section); between books (the bridges of section 2, made at the level of paragraphs); to the question
register. Book I is the target of most links from the others, which is the practical meaning of "EGA V and after"
being the base.

**What changes in the six stages of `PLAN_EVERYTHING.md`.** Stage 4 publishes editions "per section": publish per
book and chapter instead, with the cote order as the second index. Stage 6 lists candidates for development one by
one: make the three layers the standard form of every page, so that development grows from the commentary. Add the
question register and the concordance as outputs of the second pass.

## 4. Splits inside the archive's sections

- **Théorie des motifs (10-19)**: cycles and conjectures first (16, 14), then his motivic Galois theory (13, 12, 15,
  18), then the tensor-category typescripts and correspondences (10, 11). Cote 19 (topos) belongs to Book VI.
- **Géométrie et topologie combinatoire (69-89)**, three parts: maps (76, 88, 70, 75, with 138 and 161-6), which feed
  Book III; regular polyhedra over rings (79, 83, 72, 86, 85, 69, 74), the core of Book IV; the rest (71, 73, 77, 78,
  80-82, 84, 87, 89), of which pseudolines, Jordan and immersions (80, 71, 81) go to Book V.
- **Top-level cotes 90-102**: they are Paris-period algebraic geometry. 93 and 100 go to Book II, the others to Book I.
- **Vers une géométrie des formes (156)**: the fourth and last version of "Analysis situs" (156-8) with chapters
  I-III, V and IX first; the three superseded versions (156-4, 156-6, 156-7) later.
- **Complexe de De Rham (136-139)**: 137 and 136 are Book II; 138 (arithmetic of maps) is Book III; 139 is a medal.
- **Jacobians to elliptic curves (43-64)**: 47 and 54 (Fourier transform and duality on abelian varieties) and 44 go
  to Book II; 64 (1980-82) to Book III; the rest to Book I under Picard and group schemes.
- **Dérivateurs**: 158 belongs with the notes around *Pursuing Stacks*; 159 (uniformization) with Book III.
- **Autour de la Longue Marche (141-149)**: the paginated texts with contents first (147, 149), then the 1978 origins
  (146, 142), then the dated notes (143, 145, 144, 141, 148).

## 5. Transcription order

Principles: (1) what feeds a book in progress comes first; (2) unread mathematics before drafts of printed texts;
(3) finish a chapter-sized unit before opening the next; (4) within a unit, by date, since his notation changes;
(5) test each new handwriting period on a small, checkable unit before committing a large one; (6) keep the scarce
Pro-mode runs for handwriting. Clean typescripts with a complete outside edition or needing no decipherment (24, 25,
119, 134, 157, 162-5) go by the route used for clean scans, outside this ranking; 25 ("Nouveau EGA V") early, because
Book I starts from it.

**If the Pro route ends early.** Ranks 0-11 (about 4,200 pages) are the first tranche: after it, Book III has all its
main sources, Book II has its core, Books V and VI have their unread notes, and Book I has its table of contents.
Ranks 12-20 (about 4,400 pages) complete Books II, IV and V. Ranks 21-25 (about 2,300 pages) are mostly drafts of
printed texts and lose least if they wait for another route.

**Legibility, from the samples.** The 1980s hand (79) is dark and close to the Longue Marche's. The 1960s notes (14,
15, 16, 57) are in a flattened hand, often faint blue; formulas are clear, connecting words are hard; pages are sparse.
Typescripts (10, 25) are clean. Unrelated versos are frequent: scan 40 of cote 64 is a 1982 administrative circular,
scan 60 of cote 33 a page of someone else's English typescript. An inventory of versos before packaging will shorten
64, 33 and 57 in particular.

## 6. Where the reading order and the transcription order pull apart

- **Book I is first logically and mostly last in transcription.** Its content is largely in print in final form. Only
  its plans and unpublished drafts come early (rank 3), because they fix where everything else is filed.
- **Book III depends on Books IV and V but is transcribed first.** It feeds the main book in progress and is the
  lowest-risk material. Its dependencies follow at ranks 5 and 10.
- **Cote 16 and the abelian-variety folders come before the rest of Book II**, out of their logical place, as the test
  of the 1960s hand.
- **Crystals before the 1976 notes** follows logic and date; both come after the motives core only because the motives
  folders are the larger unread body.
- **Book VI is last logically and almost wholly edited already**, but cote 158 and the *Pursuing Stacks* dossier come
  in the first tranche because the companion is nearly finished and needs them.
- **The geometry of forms is the end of the arc** (1986) and is transcribed in the middle: its final version early
  enough to be safe, its superseded versions late.

## 7. Differences from the order in `PLAN_EVERYTHING.md`

1. *Vers une géométrie des formes* moves from second place to ranks 11 and 17, split. It is four versions of one
   chapter, the hand is reported as hard (Cruz Morales; Carmona's transcription has many gaps), no project of ours
   depends on it, and the folder is not yet downloaded.
2. *Autour de la Longue Marche* moves to first place, split in two, with stratified spaces and tame topology directly
   behind it (the plan had them at 4 and 8, and tame topology in "the rest").
3. The combinatorial block (plan: fifth, as one unit of 2,063 pages) is split in three; only the part on maps is in
   the first tranche.
4. Motives move up and are split; cote 16 is pulled forward as the test; cote 19 leaves the section.
5. Crystals move from "the rest" to just before the De Rham notes, which they prepare.
6. The notes around *Pursuing Stacks* move from 11 to 9, joined by cote 158. Cotes 158-160 are in no row of the
   plan's table.
7. EGA: only the drafts and plans are ranked (188 pages instead of 425); the two typescripts 24 and 25 go by the
   clean-scan route, as do the CNRS dossier and the *Pursuing Stacks* typescript.
8. Sections 43-64 and 90-102 are no longer units: their folders are distributed to Books I, II and III.
