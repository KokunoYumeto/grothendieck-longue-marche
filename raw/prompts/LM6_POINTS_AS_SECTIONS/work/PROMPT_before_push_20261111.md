# LM6_POINTS_AS_SECTIONS: points as sections. The anabelian conjecture of 1981, real structures, and Mordell and Fermat through splittings (§§5–11, 13, 32–33, 51.16, 52.3)

## What is to be settled

The manuscript states, two years before the letter to Faltings, what became the anabelian conjectures, in a form of its own. §5 introduces "le contexte arithmétique" and the "conjecture anabélienne fondamentale": Conjecture 5.5 (scan 23) that the functor from anabelian curves over `K` (with a marked finite set `S`) to the associated systems of groupoids is fully faithful, with his later margin "C'est un peu faux cf §9", and the pointed form 5.5 bis; §6 the local analysis at `s ∈ S`; §7 a reformulation he calls "bordélique", with Kummer data ("comment en termes de groupes extérieurs exprimer les données de Kummer k_i ?", scans 47–48); §8 a taxonomy of cases; §9 the tangential structure at the points of `S` and "sections d'extensions de deuxième type"; §10 adjusted hypotheses; §11 conditions on the systems of groupoids that come from geometry; §13 the Galois formulation. Then:

1. Scan 65: "Ceci suggère une approche de la conjecture de Mordell" (finiteness of conjugacy classes of splittings for `g ≥ 2`), and "Idem pour une approche du th. de Fermat".
2. §32 and scans 404–436, 480–488: points as admissible lifts; his axioms ① to ⑥; "Je dis que cette application est injective"; pathological lifts (413); conditions "faisant intervenir les Frobenius" and an intrinsic description of admissible outer actions (480); admissible germs of splittings and the algebraic points of a moduli object (488); the distinctness criteria for `g > 0` and `g = 0` (487); the withdrawn "Conjecture provisoire profinie".
3. §33 and scans 136–148, 436–470: anti-involutions of compact oriented surfaces; `π_0(U^Γ) →` classes of sections for an anti-involution, "Cette application est-elle bijective ??" (139, 148); recover algebraically the position of the points at infinity on the components of `X^σ` (437, margin); classes in `𝒯_g`, "Nous montrerons (on l'espère !)" (440); anti-involutions of a cylinder (457); "Ce sont peut-être les sous-groupes abéliens-libres maximaux" (470), with the strips as rank-one subgroups.
4. §51.16 (cote 140-4, scans 172–178, "Réflexions sur le théorème de Fermat"): "si nous arrivions à prouver l'inexistence de scindages de (371) autres que ceux provenant des pts à l'infini, le th. de Fermat serait prouvé pour U_n, même sans avoir établi le yoga anabélien"; "il n'est nullement exclu qu'une analyse assez poussée de M_{0,3} et N_{1,1} … impliquant aussi des approximations de plus en plus fines de π_1(U_n)" would decide it; the richer extension (372) by `Γ_Q × 𝔖_3` and its quotient (373) in the Lie approximation of §51.

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1.** Conjecture 5.5 with its correction in §9 is the Hom- or Isom-form of the anabelian conjecture for hyperbolic curves, proved by Tamagawa (affine, finite fields and number fields) and Mochizuki (sub-`p`-adic fields); what §9 corrects is the role of tangential base points, that is, sections "at infinity". The exact relation between his system-of-groupoids formulation and the modern statements is to be written down.
- **Lead 2.** Item 2 is the section conjecture in a 1981 form that differs in detail from the 1983 one (admissibility conditions; Frobenius conditions). Known: injectivity (his own argument is the standard one, by Mordell–Weil and the Kummer sequence); the real case (Mochizuki; Wickelgren; Pál); the birational `p`-adic case (Koenigsmann; Pop); conditional and local results (Stix's monograph). Open in general.
- **Lead 3.** Item 3: the topological side is Weichold's and Harnack's classification of real curves; the bijection for anti-involutions is the real section conjecture, a theorem; the maximal free abelian subgroups of mapping class groups were determined by Birman–Lubotzky–McCarthy (1983).
- **Lead 4.** Item 4 anticipates the nilpotent approach to rational points: Kim's non-abelian Chabauty method on unipotent quotients of `π_1`, Ellenberg's and Wickelgren's 2-nilpotent section obstructions for `P^1 − {0, 1, ∞}`, Deligne's study of the unipotent fundamental group of the projective line minus three points. His first "approximation de Lie" in §51 is the metabelian quotient `π̂/[π̂', π̂']` restricted to the Fermat tower (compare Ihara 1986 and Anderson–Ihara).

## Parts

**A. The conjecture of §§5–13 as he states it.** The objects (systems of groupoids, exterior groups with loops, Kummer data, tangential structures), Conjecture 5.5 and 5.5 bis, the correction of §9, the "bordélique" form of §7, the conditions of §11: exact statements. For each, the modern theorem that proves it, with hypotheses on `K`, or the precise part that is open. Answer his question on expressing the Kummer data in terms of exterior groups.

**B. Sections and points (item 2).** His axioms and admissibility conditions stated exactly; injectivity proved; the relation between his admissible sections and the sections of the 1983 conjecture, including cuspidal (tangential) sections and the "packets" at a cusp; the pathological lifts of scan 413 explained; what the Frobenius conditions of scan 480 should be and what they imply (compare the birational and `p`-adic results); the statement of scan 488 made precise and proved or left open with its exact content.

**C. Real structures (item 3).** The topological classification he begins, completed; the real section statement with proof or exact citation, in his formulation with components; the position of the points at infinity recovered group-theoretically (prove); the classes in `𝒯_g` he hopes to show distinct; the maximal free abelian subgroups in the discrete and in the profinite Teichmüller groups, and what his "lanières" are.

**D. Mordell (item 1).** State the finiteness statement for splittings he has in mind, its relation to the section conjecture and to Faltings's theorem (which implications are proved: the section conjecture does not by itself give Mordell without injectivity and a finiteness input; say exactly what does), and what the nilpotent methods give.

**E. Fermat (item 4).** Write his proposal exactly: the extensions (371), (372), (373), the groups involved, what "scindages provenant des points à l'infini" are. Then carry it out as far as it goes: compute the sections of the metabelian (his first Lie) approximation for the Fermat curves `U_n` and decide whether that level can exclude non-cuspidal sections; determine the first nilpotent level at which an obstruction to a rational point on the affine Fermat curve appears, for small `n` by explicit computation (with code), and in general as far as it can be proved; compare with Kim's method for the same curves and say what a proof of Fermat along his lines would still require.
