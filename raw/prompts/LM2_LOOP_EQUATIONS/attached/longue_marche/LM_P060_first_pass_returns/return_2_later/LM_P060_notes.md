## 4. Legibility and limits

The principal displayed formulas are usually clearer than the continuous prose. Many French words have only their initial letters, an occasional ascender or descender, and a final horizontal stroke clearly visible; this makes plausible grammatical or mathematical completions especially hazardous. Readings were established from the supplied images and enlarged or rotated inspection crops, followed by rereading; no published transcription was used to recover wording. The later literature cited below was used only for editorial comparisons and was not used to supply missing manuscript text.

The least secure material is scan 12's dense inserted condition; the overlapping revisions at the foot of scan 16; the cancelled paragraph and small annotations accompanying scan 17's spheres; and the oblique algebra at the lower right of scan 18. Some factors in that last block touch the black page border, and its final lines cannot be reconstructed responsibly. The short categorical note in the left margin of scan 19 also remains partly illegible. These passages should be checked against a higher-resolution original if available.

The transcript retains the marked subsets `X^{!},Y^{!}` distinctly from the covering `X'`, the script group letter in “l'image de 𝒢 dans ℋ” on scan 13, the differing sigma subscripts on scan 16, and the visible `de rg 1` on scan 20. It also retains uncertain cancellation status where appropriate, including the possibility that dots beneath `provient` on scan 13 indicate restoration. The uncertainty marks are part of the deliverable, not claims that every unmarked stroke is beyond reconsideration.

## 5. Research notes — English, separate from the transcription

These are local reading and verification notes. “Unproved claim” means that a complete proof is not supplied in these ten pages; a proof may occur before or after them. “Own idea” labels an editorial proposal or explicitly checked calculation, with confidence and reason; it is neither attributed to Grothendieck nor claimed as new. No securely legible formal conjecture or explicit open-question formulation was identified in the supplied sequence. The announced wish to explain a group identification and the preference for one geometric choice are recorded in their appropriate items rather than recast as conjectures.

### Research item 1

**Scans 11–12, especially 12, lines 1–7 — unproved claim.** The sentence “Ce foncteur est une équivalence de catégories” asserts an equivalence for the quotient construction on the two covering categories. A proof would need to establish that the quotient has the prescribed local ramification, then prove full faithfulness and essential surjectivity with the stated group actions. The local multiplier `d_Y(y)=[G_x]d_X(x)` on scan 11 is the specific datum that such a proof must justify (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 11–12). Classical lifting theory (Hatcher, 2002, §1.3) and the covering theory of topological stacks with inertia (Noohi, 2005) are useful comparison frameworks, without themselves settling the manuscript's precise conventions.

### Research item 2

**Scan 12, lines 6–7 — unproved claim / proposed construction.** “Description d'un foncteur quasi-inverse” followed by “image inverse ‘normalisée’” announces a construction without carrying it out on this page. A rigorous version would need to define the normalization of the branched topological pullback, specify the lifted group action, and verify both composites of the proposed equivalence. The phrase should not be read as though those verifications had already been supplied here (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 12).

### Research item 3

**Scan 12, lines 8–10 — unpursued implication.** The instruction involving “X par X∖S” when `d_X` vanishes suggests a relation between zero signature values and punctures. The precise convention should be checked against preceding definitions, followed by a verification that the corresponding removal downstairs preserves the intended covering problem. This is an implication suggested by the replacement on this page, not a claim that the convention has been fully defined here (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 12).

### Research item 4

**Scan 12, lines 11–18 — unpursued implication.** The special case culminating in `Rev(X,d_X,𝒢)=Rev(X,𝒢)` and “sans donnée de ramification” relocates the nontrivial signature data to the quotient. Combined with scan 11's multiplier, it suggests studying how ordinary equivariant covering data upstairs encodes prescribed branching downstairs. An explicit family of examples would clarify the roles of the ambient group, its normal subgroup, and the stabilizers (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 11–12).

### Research item 5

**Scan 13, lines 1–3 — unproved claim.** The corollary calls its fundamental-group map “un iso. canonique.” To derive that statement formally from the preceding category equivalence, one needs compatibility with the fiber functors at the chosen points `x` and `y`, not merely an equivalence of the unpointed categories. The subsequent pair-and-path description is relevant evidence for the intended compatibility, but a complete justification must track the basepoints and action convention (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 13).

### Research item 6
