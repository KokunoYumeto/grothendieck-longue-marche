## Where this comes from

Cote 149, section 3, scans 99–104 (his pages 49–52). Having studied sections of `1 → π → 𝓔_{U_η} → 𝓔_η → 1` at places of good reduction, he turns to bad reduction: a henselian trait `S` with residue field `k`, a curve `U_η` of type `(g, ν)` over its generic point `η`, and

"(30) `1 → T → 𝓔_η → 𝓔_k → 1`", "(31) `1 → π → 𝓔_{U_η} → 𝓔_η → 1`", `T = T_∞` the inertia group, `π = π_1(U_η̄)`.

Scans 100–101: "Mais tout scindage de (30) définit une extension de `𝓔_k` par `π`, donc une action extérieure — est-elle « admissible » ?, i.e. définie par une courbe algébrique ? Sans doute pas — quelle courbe serait-ce, si ce n'est la courbe « réduite » de type `(g, ν)` (inexistant !) ?" Then (scans 101–102):

"*Conjecture-à-tout-hasard* : Les conditions suivantes sont équivalentes a) `U_η` a bonne réduction sur `S` b) L'action extérieure de `T` sur `π` est triviale … c) L'action de `T` sur `π_ab = H_1(U_η̄)` est triviale. d) Idem pour `H_1(U_η̄, Z_ℓ)`, où ℓ est premier à la car. résiduelle. (NB `ℓ = p > 0`, on doit pouvoir l'exprimer par un critère sur les groupes `p`-divisibles…) e) (En termes d'une section de (30), définissant une extension de `𝓔_k` par `π`, et supposant maintenant `k` de type fini sur `Q`) Ladite extension est admissible (quitte à passer à un sous-groupe ouvert, de `𝓔_k`, au besoin), i.e. peut être définie par une courbe alg. sur `k`, ou sur une extension finie de `k`. f) (En termes d'une section de (30) et d'une de (31), permettant de faire opérer `𝓔_k` sur `π`) : Cette action n'est pas trop loufoque (à définir convenablement !)"

His example (scan 99): `k = Q`, the function field of a modular curve of pointed elliptic curves, at a place at infinity; type `(1,1)`.

**Setting and definitions used below.** `S = Spec O`, `O` a henselian discrete valuation ring with fraction field `M` and residue field `k` finitely generated over `Q`; `X_η` a smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus `g` over `M`, `D_η ⊂ X_η` a reduced divisor of degree `ν`, finite étale over `M`, `U_η = X_η ∖ D_η`, `2g − 2 + ν > 0`. *Good reduction*: `(X_η, D_η)` extends to a smooth proper curve over `O` with a relative divisor finite étale over `O`. *Potentially good reduction*: good reduction after a finite extension of `M`. For a continuous splitting `σ` of (30), `E_σ` is the pull-back of (31) along `σ`, an extension of `𝓔_k` by `π`, and `ρ_σ : 𝓔_k → Out(π)` its outer action. `E_σ` is *admissible* (his e)) if there are a finite extension `k'` of `k`, a smooth geometrically connected curve `C` over `k'` and an isomorphism of profinite groups `π_1(C_k̄) ≅ π` carrying the outer action of `𝓔_{k'}` to `ρ_σ | 𝓔_{k'}`. A closed procyclic subgroup `A` of `π` is *essentially cyclotomically normalized* under `ρ_σ` if a subgroup of its normalizer in `E_σ` maps onto an open subgroup of `𝓔_k` and acts on `A` through the cyclotomic character.

## Results this builds on

None of these is to be proved again as if open; for each one you use, confirm the step you rely on or give the exact error.

- **R1** (CYCLOTOMIC_KERNEL, statement A3; Proved there; checked in full by the review). For a hyperbolic curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 with geometric fundamental group `π`: if `z ∈ π` and, for every open normal subgroup `N`, the class of `z` in `(N⟨z⟩)^ab` lies in the image of the inertia groups, then `z` lies in an inertia group. It is Nakamura 1994, Lemma (2.1.4), in another form.
- **R2** (the same return, A6 and AE1; Proved there; checked in full; the review finds it known in substance: Stix, Corollary 22 and Theorem 14, applied over `K(μ_∞)`, with Ribet's theorem). For a hyperbolic curve over a field `K` finitely generated over `Q`, a section `s` and `Γ°` the kernel of the cyclotomic character: the centralizer of `s(Γ°)` in `π` is trivial unless `s` normalizes an inertia group, and is then that inertia group, which is unique.
- **R3** (the same return, E4, proved half; spot-checked by the review; it is Stix's Theorem 20, attributed to Nakamura). H2 (a): for a smooth hyperbolic curve `C` over a field `k'` finitely generated over `Q`, a non-trivial closed subgroup of `π_1(C_k̄)` lies in an inertia group if and only if it is procyclic and essentially cyclotomically normalized. In particular a proper `C` has no such subgroup. Also in E4: at a node with local equation `xy = t`, exchanging the two branches acts on the local inertia group by inversion.
- **R4** (the same return, E4, the half it leaves open; "open as stated" in the review). H2 (b) is not proved there. The return names what is missing: an equivariant specialization for the full profinite group that carries the inertia group of each node injectively into `π`, for every splitting of the trait, with the permutations of nodes and of branches controlled; and it did not do the genus-2 test with two isomorphic CM components. This is item A1 below.
- **R5** (the review of CYCLOTOMIC_KERNEL, findings F1, F2: 56 statements, none wrong, 9 known, 4 open as stated; R1–R3 are among the correct or known ones). The review is not authority either.
- **R6** (war game of the programme, R4 (i) and (ii); Claude Fable 5.2, effort max, 12 November 2026; argued, checked by no one; the return did not use them). For stable bad reduction and any splitting: (i) if the toric rank `t` of the Jacobian is positive, `π^ab ⊗ Q_ℓ` under `ρ_σ` has Frobenius weights 0 and −2 with multiplicity `t`, which the `H_1` of a smooth proper curve has not; (ii) if `t = 0` and the components of the special fibre have pairwise non-isogenous Jacobians with endomorphism ring `Z`, a realizing curve would have a reducible theta divisor.
- **R7** (transcriber's notes RN103-03, RN103-04, GPT-6 Astra, first pass, unreviewed; re-derived by the reader; checked by no review). - **R8** (transcriber's notes RN100-03, RN103-01, RN103-02, RN103-05; unreviewed). c) and d) hold with bad reduction for a genus-2 curve degenerating to two elliptic curves joined at a point, and for `P¹ ∖ {0, t, 1, ∞}` over `Q((t))`, where inertia acts on the free group on `a, b, c` by conjugating `a` and `b` by `ab` and fixing `c`; for the Tate curve minus its origin, `ρ_σ` has a constituent of weight 0.
- **R9** (literature; not opened by the drafter). Oda's criterion, extended by Tamagawa (Compositio Math. 109 (1997), Theorem (5.3)): good reduction of the pair is equivalent to the triviality of the outer action of inertia on the pro-ℓ geometric fundamental group, for one, equivalently every, ℓ prime to the residue characteristic. Mochizuki 1999, Theorem A (dominant morphisms against open homomorphisms, over a sub-`p`-adic field), and from it the Isom-form: isomorphisms of hyperbolic curves over such a field correspond bijectively to outer isomorphisms of their geometric fundamental groups compatible with the Galois actions.

## Leads

Inferences by Claude Fable 5.2 (effort max), 12 November 2026, checked by no one. Each is to be proved or refuted.

- **Lead 1** (expected true). `E_σ` is admissible if and only if `(X_η, D_η)` has potentially good reduction; the answer does not depend on `σ`.
- **Lead 2** (expected true; three routes for item A1). After a finite extension, `(X_η, D_η)` has a stable pointed model whose special fibre has rational components, nodes, branches and marked points. Then `π` is the profinite fundamental group of a graph of groups (vertex groups: the components minus nodes and marked points; edge groups `Π_e ≅ Ẑ(1)`, one conjugacy class for each node `e`); `𝓔_η` preserves each class, a lift normalizing `Π_e` acts on it through the cyclotomic character, and `T` acts by a profinite Dehn multi-twist with exponents the thicknesses of the nodes. Routes: (i) the logarithmic fundamental group of the stable log curve and its specialization theorem; (ii) the description of the tame fundamental group of a degenerating curve by formal patching (Saïdi; Stix's thesis); (iii) Picard–Lefschetz over `C` for the action of `T`, then the statement that a profinite Dehn multi-twist determines its nodes (Hoshi–Mochizuki), applied to `g τ g⁻¹ = τ^{χ(g)}` for `g ∈ 𝓔_η`. From memory, unverified: the properties of edge-like subgroups are in Proposition 1.2 of Mochizuki's combinatorial paper.
- **Lead 3** (expected true). For split stable reduction with dual graph of first Betti number `t`, the module `π^ab ⊗ Q_ℓ` under `ρ_σ` has weights 0, −1, −2 with multiplicities `t`, `2(g − t)`, `t + ν − 1`, for every `σ`; for a smooth curve of type `(g', ν')` they are 0, `2g'`, `ν' − 1`. So a realizing curve forces `t = 0`, `g' = g`, `ν' = ν`, and then has `ν` classes of inertia groups, while `ρ_σ` has the `ν` cusp classes and one more class for each node, pairwise without common non-trivial element.
- **Lead 4** (expected true). If `ρ_σ | 𝓔_{k'}` is the action of a curve over `k'`, then `ρ_σ` is the action of a curve over `k` (Isom-form of R9 and Weil descent). So the clause "quitte à passer à un sous-groupe ouvert" changes nothing.
- **Lead 5** (expected true, for every henselian trait of residue characteristic 0). c) holds if and only if the Jacobian of `X_η` has good reduction and every geometric point of `D_η` is fixed by `T`; d) for one ℓ is equivalent to c).
- **Lead 6** (expected true). The image of `T` in `Out(π)` is finite if and only if the reduction is potentially good; then, with `n` its order and `ϖ` a uniformizer, good reduction holds over `M(ϖ^{1/n})`; and `ρ_σ` is the action of a twist of the special fibre `C'` of that good model: the splittings are a torsor under `H¹(k, Ẑ(1))`, and the twist is by the image, under `H¹(k, Ẑ(1)) → H¹(k, μ_n) → H¹(k, Aut(C'_k̄))` (the second map given by the monodromy action of `μ_n` on `C'`), of the class by which `σ` differs from the splitting that fixes a compatible system of roots of `ϖ`.
- **Lead 7** (expected true). No property of the action of `𝓔_k` on `π` that is invariant under isomorphism is equivalent to a): the twist of R7 and a constant family give isomorphic actions. The property "every essentially cyclotomically normalized procyclic subgroup lies in one of `ν` conjugacy classes of maximal ones" is equivalent to e).
- **Lead 8** (expected true). If `ρ_σ` is admissible, the action of `𝓔_k` on `π` obtained from `σ` and from the section of (31) of a point of `U_η(M)` or of a point of `D_η(M)` is, on an open subgroup, the action of a point or of a cusp of the realizing curve. This is his remark of scans 103–104.
