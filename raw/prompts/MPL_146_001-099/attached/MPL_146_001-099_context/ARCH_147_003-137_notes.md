### N007 — An explicit dual-number counterexample: failure of fullness

**Kind:** own idea.

**Scan/location:** scan 007, “pleinement fidèle” and “faux tel quel !”; scan 008, top margin.

**Source:** “faux p. ex. sur les … duaux d'un corps (si I≠∅)”.

**Note:** Put \(R=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), \(Y=\mathbf P^1_R\), \(I=\{\infty\}\), and \(\widetilde A=\varnothing\): the map \(x\mapsto x+\epsilon x^3\) is an automorphism of \(R[x]\) with inverse \(x\mapsto x-\epsilon x^3\), but near infinity it would give \(z=1/x\mapsto z-\epsilon/z\), which is not regular at \(z=0\). A cubic is chosen deliberately: a quadratic first-order perturbation can extend to a projective linear transformation if the infinity section is allowed to move.

## Appendix A — Calculations supporting the relative-curve notes

These calculations support N007, N008, and N010 and are supplied here explicitly; they are not claims of novelty.

### A.1 — The dual-number calculation (N007)

Let \(R=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) and define \(\phi^*(x)=x+\epsilon x^3\), \(\psi^*(x)=x-\epsilon x^3\). Substitution gives
\[
\psi^*(\phi^*(x))=x-\epsilon x^3+\epsilon(x-\epsilon x^3)^3=x,
\]
and the other composition is the same, because every omitted term contains \(\epsilon^2\). This calculation works in every characteristic.

The reduction is the identity. Thus any extension to \(\mathbf P^1_R\) would reduce to the identity on \(\mathbf P^1_k\), so its underlying map preserves the infinity chart. On its overlap with the affine chart, putting \(z=1/x\) gives
\[
\phi^*(z)=\frac{1}{x+\epsilon x^3}
=z(1+\epsilon z^{-2})^{-1}
=z-\epsilon z^{-1}.
\]
This Laurent polynomial is not in \(R[z]\), and is not regular in the local ring at \(z=0\), since its coefficient \(\epsilon\) is nonzero and is not divisible by \(z\). Hence there is no projective extension, even before requiring the infinity section to be fixed.

For the stable open curve use \(B=R[x,x^{-1},(x-1)^{-1}]\). Both
\[
\phi^*(x)=x(1+\epsilon x^2),\qquad
\phi^*(x-1)=(x-1)\left(1+\epsilon\frac{x^3}{x-1}\right)
\]
are units of \(B\), so \(\phi\) and its stated inverse extend to \(B\). This is a nontrivial infinitesimal automorphism of the constant three-punctured line, although the proper triple-pointed line has no such automorphism. The example concerns failure of surjectivity on isomorphism sets, that is, fullness.

For comparison, \(x+\epsilon x^2=x/(1-\epsilon x)\) comes from a matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\-\epsilon&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) in \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(R)\); it moves the infinity section. This is why the cubic gives the cleaner nonextension argument.

### A.2 — Recovering the relative normalization (N008)

For \(T=R[u,v]/(uv)\), the module of relative differentials has presentation with relation \(v\,du+u\,dv=0\), so \(\operatorname{Fitt}_1(\Omega_{T/R})=(u,v)=J\). As a \(T\)-module,
\[
J=(u)\oplus(v),\quad (u)\simeq T/(v),\quad(v)\simeq T/(u).
\]
There are no cross homomorphisms: a homomorphism from \((u)\) to \((v)\) would have image annihilated by \(v\), but multiplication by \(v\) on \(vR[v]\) is injective, including when \(R\) has nilpotents. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{End}_T(J)=R[u]\times R[v].
\]
Multiplication realizes this isomorphism canonically, and the finite presentation of the ideal makes its endomorphisms compatible with flat étale localization, so the local identities glue. This justifies the recovery argument for the constructed class. General nodal smoothings have a different singular ideal and are not being included in this assertion.

### A.3 — A proper stable family with constant geometric graph (N010)

The local obstruction can be made proper and stable without invoking algebraization. In \(\mathbf P^2_R\), let
\[
C:\quad XY=\epsilon Z^2
\]
and mark the four sections
\[
[1:0:0],\quad[1:\epsilon:1],\quad[0:1:0],\quad[\epsilon:1:1].
\]
The charts \(X\ne0\) and \(Y\ne0\) are affine lines over \(R\); on \(Z\ne0\), the ring \(R[x,y]/(xy-\epsilon)\) is free over \(R\) with basis \(1,x,x^2,\ldots,y,y^2,\ldots\), so the projective family is flat. The four sections are disjoint and lie in its smooth locus. Its sole geometric fibre is a union of two projective lines meeting at one node, with two markings on each component, hence a stable four-pointed curve of genus zero.

The relative node scheme lies in the last chart and is cut out by \((x,y)\), giving \(R[x,y]/(xy-\epsilon,x,y)\simeq k\). It is not flat or étale over \(R\), whereas scan 007's construction produces a finite étale node scheme. This gives a proper stable family with constant geometric graph that does not lie in the constructively defined standard class. The construction is a direct check of N010, not an additional separately numbered note.

## Appendix B — The two-node counterexample and its local boundary equation

This appendix gives our construction and proof, rather than a paraphrase of a published example. Standard local deformation and normalization facts are identified separately.

Let \(C\) be obtained from \(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}\) by identifying the pairs \(\{0,\infty\}\) and \(\{1,3\}\), and let \(p\) be the image of \(7\). The normalization exact sequence gives arithmetic genus two: there are two nodes, one rational component, and the dual graph has first Betti number two. The normalization has five special points including the marking, so \((C,p)\) is stable.

Every automorphism of \((C,p)\) lifts uniquely to a Möbius transformation preserving the branch set \(\{0,\infty,1,3\}\), its partition into the two identified pairs, and the point \(7\). The six cross-ratio values of this four-point set are

\[
3,\quad 1/3,\quad -2,\quad -1/2,\quad 3/2,\quad 2/3,
\]

which are distinct, so the four-point set has precisely the Klein-four Möbius symmetries; all preserve the partition into pairs. Besides the identity these are

\[
a(z)=\frac3z,\qquad
b(z)=\frac{3(z-1)}{z-3},\qquad
c(z)=\frac{z-3}{z-1}.
\]

Their values at \(7\) are respectively \(3/7,9/2,2/3\), none equal to \(7\). Hence \(\operatorname{Aut}(C,p)=1\). The marking is a substantive safeguard: without it, symmetries can exchange the two nodes and complicate the passage to coarse moduli.

Now let \(G\) have one vertex of genus one, one loop, and one marked leg. Its graph automorphism group is \(\mathfrak S_2\), exchanging the two ends of that loop. Partial normalization of \(C\) at either node gives a connected genus-one stable curve with three smooth marked points: the two preimages of the chosen node and \(p\). Swapping the first two marks is precisely the \(\mathfrak S_2\)-quotient, so both partial normalizations determine objects in

\[
Q=[\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,3}/\mathfrak S_2].
\]

If these two objects were isomorphic in \(Q\), gluing the corresponding marked pair would induce an automorphism of \((C,p)\) exchanging its two nodes. The calculated triviality of \(\operatorname{Aut}(C,p)\) excludes this. Thus the clutching morphism \(Q\to\overline{\mathcal M}_{2,1}\) has two distinct geometric points over \((C,p)\), even after the graph quotient. In particular it is not a monomorphism or a closed immersion, and its induced coarse map is not injective on geometric points.

For the smoothness assertion, take independent smoothing parameters \(u,v\) at the two nodes. Their independence is the standard node-deformation fact; the remaining two deformation parameters move the five special points of the rational normalization modulo \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\). A miniversal completed local ring for \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{2,1}\) is therefore \(R=\mathbb C[[u,v,z_1,z_2]]\). Both nodes are nonseparating, and smoothing either one leaves an irreducible one-node genus-two curve. Consequently the reduced closure \(\Delta_{\mathrm{irr}}\) of that one-node stratum has completed local ring

\[
R/(uv).
\]

It has dimension three and embedding dimension four, proving singularity; it is also nonnormal, with normalization

\[
\mathbb C[[v,z_1,z_2]]\ \times\ \mathbb C[[u,z_1,z_2]].
\]

These are the two locally smooth branches selected by the two partial normalizations. A trivial stabilizer makes the completed local ring of the coarse moduli space equal to the stack's versal local ring, so the singularity persists on the coarse boundary in this particular example. This proves failure of the general smooth-closure claim without inferring coarse singularity solely from stack singularity.

**External inputs, stated narrowly:** independent node-smoothing coordinates are established in Deligne and Mumford (1969), Theorem 1.6, with the boundary equation in Corollary 1.9, and extend to marked curves as in Knudsen (1983), Theorem 2.7. The completed coarse ring is the invariant ring of the stabilizer in characteristic zero; see Edidin and Fulghesu (2012), Proposition 2.14. The global identification of the compactified graph quotient as the normalization of the stratum closure is recorded in Abramovich et al. (2015), proof of Proposition 3.4.1, and Edidin and Fulghesu (2012), Theorem 4.7. These standard inputs are separate from the explicit coordinates, automorphism computation, and two-preimage argument above.
