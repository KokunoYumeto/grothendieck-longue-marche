## What is to be settled

The manuscript opens with topoi, not with groups: §1 "Topos multigaloisiens", §2 "Application aux revêtements des topos", §3 "Variantes pro-multigaloisiennes", §4 "Compléments, remords". The fundamental groupoid of a topos enters as the multi-Galois topos of its locally constant objects, with no base point chosen, and the systems of groupoids of §§5–11 are diagrams of such topoi. Three later sections return to topoi:

- §14, "Digression cohomologique (sur le bouchage de trous)" (cote 140-2, scans 119–137): for a curve `X` and the open `U = X − S` he introduces a topos `B_{X,U}` (he calls it "topos abracadabra") built from `U` and the local data at the points of `S`, and asks whether its cohomology is computed "galoisiennement" and whether `H^*(B_{X,U}, F) → H^*(X_ét, f^*F)` is an isomorphism for torsion `F` (scan 124); he rejects it in genus 0 for locally constant coefficients, "Non, à cause du genre 0 !!!" (129).
- §16, "Bouchage et forage de trous: préliminaires topologiques généraux" (scans 160–179): a van Kampen theorem for topoi; 2-cartesian squares of topoi (166); the expected acyclicity of `B_{D;S} → B_D` and `B_{X;S} → B_X`.
- §20, "Description 2-isotopique de la cat. des sphères topologiques" (heading on scan 233, with the margin "Une digression !"; scans 233–250): Gr-categories describing spheres and sphere bundles up to 2-isotopy (232–239), with the ∞-isotopic description left "Au concours !" (236); neutrality of a gerbe, "brr !", to consult Giraud or Breen (240–241); bounds on fidelity in terms of `dim S`, which conflict (247–248, his margin "??" on 247); what a homotopy means arithmetically for forms of `P^1`: "sans doute un relèvement de u en une section du revêtement simplement connexe" (249); the 2-category of Azumaya algebras (250); "bien profond" for the simple connectedness of the Teichmüller space `M̃_{g,ν}` (scan 259, which is in §21, not in §20).
- Two later manuscripts of the Montpellier archive, cotes 149 and 147, written after the Longue Marche, take these topoi further: the étale topos of a field as a pro-Galois topos over that of `Q`, described from six points of view (149, scan 19); the faithfulness of `X ↦ X_ét` (149, scan 52); and, for a divisor with normal crossings of any dimension, a gluing theorem for the topoi of punctured tubular neighbourhoods of the strata, with its consequences for fundamental groupoids and the "topos élémentaires" into which every topos attached to the stratification is to be taken apart (147, §§11–13, scans 106–190). The block "After the Montpellier cotes 149 and 147" types his statements; Part F settles them.

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1.** A multi-Galois topos in his sense is the classifying topos of a groupoid with (pro)finite or discrete automorphism groups, equivalently a topos generated by locally constant objects satisfying a finiteness or local-connectedness condition; §§1–3 are then the Galois theory of SGA 1, Exposé V, and SGA 4, Exposé IV, freed from base points and connectedness, and coincide with later treatments (Moerdijk's prodiscrete Galois theory; Bunge; the fundamental pro-groupoid of a locally connected topos; Leroy). Whatever he states beyond these is to be found and proved.
- **Lead 2.** `B_{X,U}` is the topos-theoretic form of "`U` together with its punctured formal neighbourhoods at `S`", glued along the local fundamental groups: in present terms the Kummer-étale or logarithmic topos of `(X, S)`, or the root-stack limit, or Deligne's tangential base point construction made into a space. With that identification his cohomological question has a known answer (Kummer-étale cohomology with torsion coefficients agrees with the étale cohomology of `U`, not of `X`), and the genus-0 failure he notes should be explained by it.
- **Lead 3.** His van Kampen theorem is the statement that the fundamental-groupoid functor (the multi-Galois reflection) sends suitable 2-pushouts of topoi to 2-pushouts; forms of it are in the literature for locally connected topoi and for étale fundamental groups (Stix's and others' van Kampen theorems), and the effective-descent hypotheses matter.
- **Lead 4.** §20 computes the homotopy 2-type of the group of homeomorphisms of the sphere and of sphere bundles (classically `O(3)`, by Kneser and Smale), and its arithmetic form asks for the étale or motivic homotopy type of forms of `P^1`; Azumaya algebras of degree 2 as a 2-category is the 2-gerbe-theoretic description of `B PGL_2`.

- **Gluing a topos from an open part, a closed part and a link (Parts B and C).** STRATA C.1, C.6: a category `X` with a sieve `A` (open) and the complementary cosieve `B` (closed) is the same thing as `A`, `B` and the bimodule `M(a, b) = Hom_X(a, b)` of crossing arrows; its presheaf topos is glued from those of `A` and `B` along the functor `i^*j_*`, as in SGA 4 IV 9.5.4 (the manuscript of *Les Dérivateurs* reproduces this at XII ms. 62–63), and `i^*j_* ≃ t_*s^*`, where `s, t` are the source and target functors of the category `E` of crossing arrows and commuting squares. C.2 (Known there; Douteau–Waas, arXiv:2112.02394v2, Theorem 6.1): `BE` is the homotopy link of the closed part, that is the homotopy type of a punctured neighbourhood of `BB` in `BX`, with its two maps to `BB` and to the open part. C.3: `BX` is the homotopy pushout of `BA ← BE → BB`. C.8: for locally constant coefficients in any complete and cocomplete ∞-category, in particular for sets, `Fun(BX, 𝒞)` is the pullback of `Fun(BA, 𝒞) → Fun(BE, 𝒞) ← Fun(BB, 𝒞)`; for sets this is a van Kampen theorem for the fundamental groupoid of the glued topos, with the link as the third term. C.4 (Refuted): the link cannot be replaced by the space of all paths between the two parts. So for presheaf topoi there are three different objects built from an open part and local data at the closed part: the open part alone; the glued topos (constructible objects); and the locally constant objects on the glued topos (the pushout of groupoids). Part C's van Kampen theorem and the 2-cartesian squares of scan 166 should be stated so that these are instances, or the difference said.
- **The example closest to §14.** STRATA F.1–F.2: for `X = P¹(C)` and `S = {0, 1, ∞}`, the exit-path category of `(X, S)` has one object `u` with automorphism group `G = π_1(X − S)`, free on `a, b`, three objects `p_0, p_1, p_∞` with trivial automorphism group, and `Hom(p_i, u) = G/⟨m_i⟩` for the three peripheral elements `m_i`; constructible sheaves of sets on `(X, S)` are the functors on it (F.8, with Jansen, arXiv:2308.09551, Theorem 6.7): a `G`-set `Y`, three sets `Y_i`, and maps from `Y_i` to the `m_i`-invariants of `Y`. The link of `p_i` is the groupoid of the infinite cyclic group `⟨m_i⟩`. An inference of the author of this prompt, unverified, to prove, correct or discard in Part B: his `B_{X,U}` "built from `U` and the local data at the points of `S`" can be compared with two topoi made from these pieces: (a) this constructible topos, in which the point `p_i` is a point with trivial group and the local group `⟨m_i⟩` enters only through the bimodule; (b) the topos in which each `p_i` is replaced by its link, with automorphism group `⟨m_i⟩` (profinitely, `Ẑ(1)`), which is the shape of Lead 2 (logarithmic or Kummer-étale). B.6: the ∞-category of presheaves of spaces determines the idempotent completion of the base category.
