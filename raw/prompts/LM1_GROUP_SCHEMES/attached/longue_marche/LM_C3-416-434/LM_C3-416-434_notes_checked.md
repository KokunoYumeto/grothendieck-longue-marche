## Claude (second pass)

- **C1 — scan 418, Remarque — unpursued implication.** Since M = M(0)·SG and M(0) normalizes L₀,
  Norm_M(L₀) = M(0)·Norm_SG(L₀), so the defect of the Remarque's inclusion is exactly Norm_SG(L₀)/L₀. By (30)–(31) an
  element of T₀ acts on L₀ by λ ↦ χ·λ; its product with a scalar c, c² = det⁻¹, lies in SG (fppf-locally), normalizes L₀
  and is not in L₀ when χ ≠ 1. Confidence medium-high: it assumes L₀ commutative, as the parametrization ε₀(λ)
  suggests.
- **C2 — scans 420 and 425, (47) and (75) — unpursued implication.** The subgroup 1 × ST_ρ × ST_σ × μ₂ of (47) has image
  μ₂ under the scalar map R° → G_m (ST_ρ × ST_σ = Ker Θ°, and the centre μ₂ of SG goes to ±1). That map is an
  epimorphism by (70), and (75) gives Centr(M°) = R°. So (47) is a strict inclusion, as its margin says. Confidence high.
- **C3 — scans 420, 422 and 431, R and the radical — own idea.** The struck "Rad(M°)" under (61), the margin "à propos des
  radicalités" (scan 420) and the heading "Groupes mixtes du radical (R, SR, R°, SR°…)" (scan 431) suggest that the
  letter R names radicals. (75) identifies R° with the centre of M°, i.e. the radical once R° is connected (88).
  Confidence medium: the naming is an inference.
- **C4 — scan 425, (72) — own idea.** The matrix as written, (μ 1; 0 1), has determinant μ, so it is not in SG despite
  "∈ ΓSG", and it is not multiplicative in μ. The author's struck S in "j_G : G_m → G" points the same way. The NB of
  (71) (det j_G(μ⁻²) = μ⁻²) needs a homomorphism G_m → T₀ ⊂ G with determinant the identity, e.g. diag(μ, 1); which torus
  T₀ is meant must be read from its definition earlier in §48. Confidence high on the arithmetic, low on the intended
  matrix.
- **C5 — scan 426, proof of (75) — unproved claim checked.** Take the section in the form (71),
  (j_{T₀}(μ²), a, b)·μ j_G(μ⁻²). The first factor acts on SG through T₀ → PG as int j_G(μ²) (31). The second has
  determinant μ²·μ⁻² = 1, so it lies in SG, and acts as int j_G(μ⁻²) because the scalar μ is central. Hence the product
  acts trivially, and the proof is complete once μ exists, i.e. fppf-locally on the base. Confidence high.
- **C6 — scans 430, 432 and 434, integral points in the modular case — own idea.** Take T_ρ, T_σ to be the centralizer
  tori with det = norm, as (56) and (81) presuppose. The norm forms of ℤ[ρ] = ℤ[ζ₆] and ℤ[σ] = ℤ[i] are positive definite,
  so every integral point of T_ρ, T_σ has determinant 1. The non-identity coset of N′_σ(ℤ) = 𝔻₄ consists of matrices
  (a b; b −a) of determinant −(a² + b²) = −1. Hence S′T_ρ(ℤ) = ST_ρ(ℤ), SN′_σ(ℤ) = ST_σ(ℤ), SR(ℤ) = SR°(ℤ) (by (59) and
  (98)), and SR → μ₂ × μ₂ (scan 432, NB) is trivial on ℤ-points; it is an epimorphism of fppf sheaves, the conics
  a² + b² = −1 and x² − xy + y² = −1 being flat and surjective over ℤ. Confidence high on the arithmetic, medium on the
  identification of the tori.
