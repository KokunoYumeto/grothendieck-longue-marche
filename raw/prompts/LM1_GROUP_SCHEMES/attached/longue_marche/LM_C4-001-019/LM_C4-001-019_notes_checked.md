## New notes — Claude (second pass)

- **C1 — scan 9, "Pour n=2 c'est trivial, Pour n=3 idem (dans (9) il y a un seul terme avec un dénominateur)" — own
  idea (checks a claim); confidence high.** (9) does not imply (10) for n = 3 over ℤ₍₂₎. The matrix g with ones on and
  above the diagonal has g and g⁻¹ = I − N + N² integral, but log g has the entry ½ in position (1,3). In this example
  g_s has order 4 in characteristic 2, so g is not unipotent in the sense of scan 5. With that hypothesis,
  (g − 1)² ≡ 0 mod 2, the implication holds over an unramified trait: writing X = Y/2, the conditions force
  Y² ∈ 8M₃(A) and then Y ∈ 2M₃(A). E03 shows it fails over a ramified trait.
- **C2 — scan 12, Lemme, "(néc^t. unipotente, d'après des résultats connus généraux de SGA 3)" — unproved claim
  (checked); confidence high.** In the sense defined on scan 5 the extended section need not be unipotent. Take
  S = Spec ℤ₍₂₎, U = Spec ℚ, G = GL₃, φ_U(t) = exp(t log g) with the g of C1: g_s has order 4 and is not φ(1) for any
  φ: G_a → G over a field of characteristic 2. It is unipotent in the usual (SGA 3) sense. The conclusion of the Lemme
  still holds: φ_U(2t) = exp(2t log g) = I + t(2 log g) + 2t²(log g)² has coefficients in ℤ₍₂₎.
- **C3 — scans 9, 10, 13, (11), (11 bis), (20) — own idea; confidence high.** In all three displays the linear term ±X
  of φ_η(±1) − 1 is not written. Read literally, (20) is false: any X with X² = 0 and X ∉ M₅(A) satisfies the
  hypothesis. (11) is not a consequence of (9) unless X ∈ M₄(A). With the linear term restored, the hypothesis is
  φ_η(±1) ∈ M_n(A), and for a ℚ-algebra (20) follows from the logarithm in the margin of scan 13.
- **C4 — scan 10, (12 bis) — own idea; confidence high.** The second condition is written with ½ where (12) gives 1/6.
  2 is assumed to be a uniformiser of A, so 3 is a unit and (1/6)Y ∈ M₄(A) ⇔ (1/2)Y ∈ M₄(A). The written condition is
  therefore equivalent to the intended one.
- **C5 — scan 16, Remarque — unproved claim; confidence medium.** The Remarque says the preceding arguments prove the
  "théorème" of 652′ when S is noetherian of dimension 1. The Proposition (scans 14–15) and its Corollary are proved for
  G affine (of t.f. or p.f.) and U = S∖V(n) schematically dense. The theorem concerns separated G locally of finite
  presentation. So the Remarque covers affine G unless a reduction to the affine case is supplied. One also needs the
  complement of U₁ to lie in V(n) for some n: it is a finite set of closed points when the generic point has
  characteristic 0.
- **C6 — scan 15, proof of the Proposition — own idea; confidence medium.** "mⁱaᵢ ∈ A" is tested inside A_n = A[1/n],
  which presumes A → A[1/n] injective, i.e. n a non-zero-divisor on A. For affine S that is exactly "U schématiquement
  dense dans S", the hypothesis inserted on scan 14. It also gives the uniqueness used in "Pour raisons de densité
  schématique et unicité".
- **C7 — scan 4, §53.5 — unpursued implication; confidence medium.** The corrected entry, "revêtements des germes
  d'espaces, et relations entre germes top. et germes différentiables de surfaces", announces a comparison of
  topological and smooth germs of surfaces. It presumably serves the isomorphism Gl(2,ℤ)^∼ ≃ S𝒯_{1,1} of §53.4. In
  dimension 2 the classical smoothing of surfaces makes the two kinds of germs agree, which is the input one expects
  there. The argument is on pp. 768′ ff., outside this package.
