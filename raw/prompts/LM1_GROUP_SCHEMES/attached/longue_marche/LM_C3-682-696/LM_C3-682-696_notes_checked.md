C. **Scan 688, implication not pursued (checker's note).** Before (124), the ink reads "M? ⊂ N_{ρ0} ×_{γ0} N_{σ0} ×_{Γ0} M". Either this is a slip for Γ₀ (as in (122) and (124)), or Grothendieck means that the ρ-component of M? already lies over γ₀; the latter would need proof.

D. **Scan 696, verification of a claim (checker's note).** Let I ≠ ∅ and G ⊂ 𝔤 = ∏_γ G_i satisfy (20) a) and b). Then each projection p_i: G → G_i is injective: an element of its kernel maps to 1 in γ, so it lies in G ∩ 𝒢^I = δ(𝒢), and a diagonal element with one trivial coordinate is 1. It is also surjective: lift the image in γ of h ∈ G_i to g ∈ G by b), then multiply by δ(p_i(g)⁻¹h) ∈ G. Hence θ_ij = p_j∘p_i⁻¹ is a transitive system that induces the identity on 𝒢 and is compatible with the identifications γ_i ≃ γ. Conversely, such a system gives G = {(θ_{i0 i}(g))_i}, which satisfies a) and b), so "équivaut" is correct for I ≠ ∅.

E. **Scan 693, implication not pursued (checker's note).** By (10), the situations (1) in which N really is a subgroup of G (the hook in (1), "sous-groupe N" in the prose) correspond exactly to the systems (2) with φ_SN injective. (9) measures the defect in general, so the equivalence of categories stated with arbitrary φ_N restricts to an equivalence between these two subcategories.
