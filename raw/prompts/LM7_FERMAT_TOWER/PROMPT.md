## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1.** The object he is computing is the Galois action on `π̂_{0,3}/[π̂', π̂']`, studied by Ihara (1986) for the pro-`ℓ` case: it is given by a universal power series `F_σ ∈ Z_ℓ[[u, v]]^×` whose special values are Jacobi sums, with Anderson's and Coleman's explicit formulas and the Anderson–Ihara theory of higher circular units. His extension of `Ẑ*` by `Ẑ` and his Kummer maps are the "Kummer characters" at the roots of `1 − ζ`; surjectivity and independence statements are then statements about circular units (known in the form of Ihara, Anderson, Coleman, Ihara–Kaneko–Yukinari; exact forms to be checked).
- **Lead 2.** The number 12 is the index of `Π_0` in `SL(2, Z)` (and the order of the abelianization of `SL(2, Z)`); the appearance of `GL(2, Z/12Z)` and the "order 32" should come out of an explicit computation with the finite quotient `GL(2, Z)/Π_0` of order 24 acting on `M ⊗ Z/N`.
- **Lead 3.** His "[mo]tivisation" (51.13) and "Jacobiennes généralisées" (51.14) describe `M̂ ⊗` as the Tate module of the generalized Jacobian of the Fermat curve with modulus the cusps, a 1-motive in Deligne's sense, with complex multiplication by `Z[μ_n]^2`; the splitting of 51.12 should be the statement that the weight filtration (toric part against abelian part) splits over the cyclotomic field up to bounded torsion.
