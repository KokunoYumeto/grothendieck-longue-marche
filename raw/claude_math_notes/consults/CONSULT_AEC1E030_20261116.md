## (a) The dictionary at a cusp

### What holds

**1. Goldstein's three cusp etas of Γ₀(4) are the three theta constants (exact).**
[W (11.7), (11.8)] attaches to the cusp ∞ of Γ₀(N) the function η_N(z) = ∏_{d|N} η(Nz/d)^{a_N μ(d)/d},
a_N = ∏_{p|N} p/(p−1). For N = 4: η_∞(z) = η(4z)²/η(2z). Transporting to the other two cusps:

| cusp | cusp eta | theta constant |
|---|---|---|
| ∞ | η(4z)²/η(2z) | θ₂(2z)/2 |
| 0 | η(z)²/η(2z) | θ₄(2z) |
| −1/2 | η(2z)⁵/(η(z)²η(4z)²) | θ₃(2z) |

Each has weight 1/2 and its 24th power vanishes at its own cusp only, to order 6 = the index of Γ₀(4). So (F2)
reads: **λ = 16(η_∞/η_{−1/2})⁴, 1 − λ = (η₀/η_{−1/2})⁴**, in the variable 2z, and Jacobi's identity is
16η_∞⁴ + η₀⁴ = η_{−1/2}⁴. The units of Y₀(4) are the monomials in the two ratios; their rank 2 is the number of
cusps minus one.

**2. Cusp inertia ↔ the parabolic stabilizer; Kummer class ↔ the difference of two generalized Dedekind sums
(mine; normalisation to be fixed, item C1).**
[W Thm 11.1]: η_c(γz) = e^{πi S_c(γ)}(cz + d)^{1/2} η_c(z) with S_c(γ) rational. For two cusps c, c′ the ratio
η_c/η_{c′} has weight 0, so γ ↦ S_c(γ) − S_{c′}(γ) is a homomorphism Γ₀(4) → ℚ: it is (1/πi) times the period of
d log(η_c/η_{c′}) along γ. Hence, for γ in Γ₀(4) (which is Γ(2) in the variable 2z):

- κ_B(λ)(γ) := (1/2πi)∮_γ d log λ = 2(S_∞ − S_{−1/2})(γ), and κ_B(1 − λ)(γ) = 2(S₀ − S_{−1/2})(γ). They are integers, and
  the pair is the abelianization F₂ → ℤ² of the tripod group: the winding numbers around λ = 0 and λ = 1.
- The profinite completion of κ_B(λ), extended to the arithmetic fundamental group, is the Kummer class
  κ(λ) ∈ H¹(π₁(Y₀(4)_ℚ), Ẑ(1)); pulled back along a section it is the κ(a) of our Longue Marche files, and κ(1 − λ)
  gives κ(1 − a). So **LM's pair (κ(a), κ(1 − a)) is the ℓ-adic form of the pair of Dedekind-sum differences
  (S_∞ − S_{−1/2}, S₀ − S_{−1/2})**, and a + (1 − a) = 1 is Jacobi's identity among the three cusp etas.
- The value of κ_B(unit) on the parabolic generator γ_c of a cusp is the order of the unit at c. That is the
  restriction of the Kummer class to the cusp inertia I_c ≅ Ẑ(1). So the dictionary is:
  cusp c ↔ I_c; γ_c ↔ a generator of I_c; ord_c ↔ restriction to I_c; S_c − S_{c′} ↔ the Kummer class of the unit
  with divisor a multiple of (c) − (c′).

### What does not hold

- **The Kronecker limit formula is not the archimedean side of A.1.** A.1 is a statement about the fundamental
  group (the fixed subgroup of the geometric π₁ is the cusp inertia). Goldstein's theory lives on H¹: Eisenstein
  series, Dedekind sums, units. Its counterpart is A.3's boundary term (point 3), and A.6 (point 5). A.1 follows
  on the ℓ-adic side from weights (Δ kills the cyclotomic character, so it fixes I_c ≅ Ẑ(1)) plus the non-abelian
  work of the run; nothing archimedean here reaches the non-abelian part.
- The single cusp eta has no Kummer class: (c) has degree 1. Only differences do. Its ℓ-adic counterpart is the
  class of the cusp in H² (Grothendieck's "c_i comme fibré conormal", cote 149, scans 80-85, as our anabelian
  handoff records it), not an element of H¹.

## (b) A.3's cusp-divisor term and the Banerjee-Merel criterion

**The mechanism of A.3 (mine; it is Lead 2 of the LM8_PUSH prompt, from the Longue Marche expert).** H₁ of the
open cover is an extension of H₁ of its compactification by the boundary module; it is the Tate module of the
generalized Jacobian, and the extension is classified by the cuspidal divisor classes in the Jacobian. A vector
fixed by an open subgroup of Δ lifts to a fixed vector exactly when its pairing with every cuspidal class has
trivial Kummer extension over K^ab; by Kummer theory for abelian varieties over K(E_tors) ⊂ K^ab (Ribet [memory])
that happens exactly on the orthogonal complement of the abelian subvariety generated by the non-torsion cuspidal
classes. So the term in A.3 is "the failure of Manin-Drinfeld for that cover".

**Does the criterion apply? Yes, after one step (exact for the step).** E minus O is not an unramified cover of
the tripod: (0,0) and (±1,0) are ramification points that stay in. E minus E[2] is (point 5). Each S₃, D₈, Q₈ cover
X of E minus O, restricted over E minus E[2], is a finite cover of the tripod, so its group is a subgroup of Γ(2)
of finite index and Banerjee-Merel's setting holds. The cusps of that group are the points over E[2]; the divisors
of A.3 are the ones supported over O, a subgroup of the cuspidal divisors. The criterion is stated for any
cuspidal divisor, so it applies to them.

**Does it decide? In principle, and it is an independent check, not a new result.** As described to me, the
criterion says a cuspidal divisor of degree 0 is torsion exactly when its Eisenstein cycle, whose imaginary part is
given by scattering constants, is real (rational). Non-torsion can be certified by a rigorous numerical bound
away from zero; torsion needs an exact argument. LM8 A.4 already claims the cusp divisor groups of all seven
covers by algebraic means. So the use is: compute the Eisenstein cycles of the divisors over O on the seven
covers and compare the rank of the non-torsion part with A.4. Agreement is an archimedean confirmation of A.4;
disagreement locates an error. I did not open the paper, so the exact form of the criterion and whether its
hypotheses include these groups must be read by whoever runs it (item C2).

## (c) Δ, eta, and the check on A.3

**1. Δ is the sign of the frame on η through the theta index: yes, given item 2(a) (exact).**
η = Σ_{r ∈ (ℤ/12)^×} χ₁₂(r) θ_{6,r}, up to the factor 2 from θ_{6,r} = θ_{6,−r} at z = 0 (F3; checked earlier on 60

**2. The eta multiplier does not give Δ, and no formula through Dedekind sums or Rademacher's function can
(exact, with proof).**
- The multiplier of η is an abelian character of the metaplectic group, of order 24. Any quantity computed from
  the frame through it, through Dedekind sums or through Rademacher's Φ, depends only on the image of the frame in
  the abelianization.
- For the element of LM7_PUSH E.58 the frame lies in H = F′F³ (our intake §9.2). The image of F = Γ(2)/±1 in
  B^ab = ℤ/6 is ℤ/3 (s ↦ 3, r ↦ 2, τ ↦ 5, and F is generated by conjugates of τ²), so F′ and F³ both go to 0: the
  frame is trivial in B^ab. Its Δ is 1. So Δ is not a function of the abelian image of the frame.
- Also, on the centre the multiplier of η² is not χ₁₂: η²(−1/z) = (z/i)η²(z) gives the value −1 at −I; the
  multiplier of η² is a character of SL₂(ℤ/4) × SL₂(ℤ/3) of order 4 × 3, the factor of order 3 is trivial on −I₃, so
  its values on 1, 5, 7, 11 are 1, 1, −1, −1. That is χ₋₄, the parity at 4 alone. It separates 5 from 7 and is not

- What is true: the scalar a·I acts in the Weil representation of index 6 by a phase times the permutation
  r ↦ ar. Δ is the permutation part read on η; the phase part is the multiplier. **Δ is the part of the frame's

- A formula on all of ĜT₀ therefore has to be read on the non-abelian quotient PSL₂(ℤ/12): the ghost note's

**3. The check on A.3: your reading is right, and it predicts "boundary only" (mine).**
- E has complex multiplication by ℤ[i] ⊂ K, so G_K acts on T_ℓE through an abelian quotient and Δ = G_{K^ab} acts
  trivially. A homomorphism E → Jac(cover) is defined over a finite extension, hence "an open subgroup of Δ".
- Conversely, let W be the part of V_ℓ(Jac) fixed by an open subgroup of Δ. That subgroup contains G_{K′K^ab} for a
  finite K′/K, and G_{K′} acts on W through Gal(K′K^ab/K′), which is abelian. An abelian semisimple ℓ-adic
  representation coming from an abelian variety is of complex-multiplication type, with a Hecke character of K′
  that factors through K^ab, i.e. is a base change from K (Faltings; Serre's abelian ℓ-adic representations
  [memory]). An algebraic Hecke character of K of weight −1 exists only if K contains a CM field.
- So: **over ℚ with Δ = G_{ℚ^ab}, and over any K without a CM subfield, the fixed homology is the boundary module
  alone.** For K = ℚ directly: G_{ℚ^ab} maps onto the anticyclotomic part Gal(K^ab/ℚ^ab) of G_K^ab, on which the
  character of E has infinite order, so no vector of V_ℓE is fixed by an open subgroup. This agrees with LM6 A.53
  ("none without a CM subfield") as our handoff records it.
- For K containing a CM field the extra term is the part of the Jacobian with complex multiplication whose Hecke
  character descends to K, minus what the non-torsion cuspidal classes remove; for K = ℚ(i) and these covers that
  is the E-isotypic part, as A.3 says.
