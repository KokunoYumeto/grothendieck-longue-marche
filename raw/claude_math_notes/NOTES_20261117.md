## 8. Geometric Langlands: the comparison of its forms, and where it bears on Book II (17 Nov ~16:0x UTC) [read]

Source: Gaitsgory-Raskin, "Proof of the geometric Langlands conjecture I: construction of the functor",
arXiv:2405.03599 (our library: F:/user/Documents/arxiv_latex/library/geometric_langlands/2405.03599*/functor.tex):
abstract; Introduction, "The Betti setting" and "Comparison of the different versions of GLC"; Section 5
"Restricted vs full GLC". Read at those places only.

- [read] Before the series proves anything, paper I shows that all forms of the conjecture are logically equivalent:
  - full de Rham <=> restricted de Rham, and full Betti <=> restricted Betti (Section 5, building on AGKRRV
    arXiv:2010.01906 Sect. 21.4; the route goes through the tempered version: Tempered => Full, and Tempered <=>
    Tempered restricted);
  - restricted de Rham <=> restricted Betti, by Riemann-Hilbert;
  - hence full de Rham <=> full Betti.
  Riemann-Hilbert applies because D-modules on Bun_G with nilpotent singular support are regular holonomic: AGKRRV,
  Cor. 16.5.6, which GLC I calls "a non-trivial result". The automorphic side in the Betti setting is the category of
  sheaves with nilpotent singular support (Ben-Zvi-Nadler). There is no Betti exponential D-module, so a torus action
  takes its place in the Whittaker coefficient. The series then works in de Rham, where Kac-Moody localization is
  available, and gets Betti for free.
- [read] Gaitsgory-Raskin, arXiv:2508.02237 (2025), abstract: for l-adic sheaves in characteristic p, automorphic
  sheaves with nilpotent singular support are equivalent to IndCoh on a union of connected components of the stack of
  Langlands parameters. This is part of the conjecture there, obtained from characteristic zero.
- [mine] Where it bears on Book II, and where it does not:
  - It is a model of "one statement across realizations". The AGKRRV framework of a "constructible sheaf theory"
    (Sect. 1.1.1: regular holonomic D-modules, Betti constructible sheaves, l-adic sheaves) is an axiomatization of
    the realizations, close to the shape of his motivic programme. A companion section that compares the de Rham and
    Betti forms of a categorical statement can cite GLC I for the method: the restricted form plus RH plus the passage
    restricted <=> full. It does not replace a comparison theorem.
  - It is an application of Riemann-Hilbert, not a new comparison theorem between cohomology theories of varieties. It
    says nothing about crystals, divided powers, p-adic or integral structures. So it is not an input for cote 136
    (the PD de Rham complex and his uniqueness conjecture for integral PD de Rham theories), for the crystals (4-9,
    100), or for the archive notes A06 (cote 15/18, "Archimedean places and integral Betti comparison") and F15
    (cote 60, "Betti symmetry needs the selfdual coefficient comparison"). Those concern integral or self-dual
    structure, which RH over a field of characteristic 0 does not see.
  - The regularity result (AGKRRV Cor. 16.5.6) is the place closest to his de Rham work. "Every D-module with this
    singular support is regular" is the kind of statement Deligne's 1970 regular-singularity theory was built for, and
    Grothendieck's 1966 comparison theorem started it. Worth one sentence in the Book II companion where it discusses
    Gauss-Manin and regularity (cote 93, "Connexions de Gauss-Manin et équations de Picard-Fuchs", still in the queue).
- [read] Two neighbours of Book II on the same shelf:
  - Maulik-Shen-Yin, arXiv:2407.05177 (abstract): the Corti-Hanamura motivic decomposition for the GL_n Hitchin
    system; the relative Lefschetz standard conjecture there (the inverse of relative hard Lefschetz is induced by an
    algebraic correspondence); chi-independence of the relative Chow motives. This sits beside cote 16 (the ring of
    correspondences over a universal Lefschetz ring) and the cycles cluster: a case of his standard conjecture B
    proved in a relative setting. Read the paper before the cycles-cluster prompt is written.
  - Faergeman, arXiv:2405.18268 (abstract): complex irreducible rigid G-local systems with quasi-unipotent monodromy
    and finite-order abelianization are motivic, generalizing Katz for GL_n, via motivic Hecke eigensheaves. This
    bears on "motives and their Galois groups" (cotes 12-15).
