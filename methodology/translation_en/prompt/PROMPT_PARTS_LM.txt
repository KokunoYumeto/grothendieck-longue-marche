@@ LM WORK
our diplomatic transcription of his manuscript La Longue Marche à travers la théorie de Galois (1981), <<COTE>> of the Grothendieck archive in Montpellier, his research notes: what he wrote, as he wrote it, with his deletions, insertions, marginal notes, underlining and abbreviations, and the transcriber's marks of doubt.

@@ LM STYLE
- These are notes written for himself: keep their sketchy, exploratory tone, the dashes, exclamation marks and unfinished sentences.
- His abbreviations are written out in English (the terms below give those on these pages; write out any other the same way). "ssi" is "iff".
- "soit" that restates the formula before it ("; soit", "soit (en passant à l'inverse)", \text{soit} between two formulas) is "i.e." or "that is", never "or"; "soit X ..." that introduces an object is "let X be".
- Latin stays Latin: loc. cit., op. cit., a priori, cf., i.e. ("loc." alone is local or locally).<<MORE_STYLE>>

@@ LM TEXT_MACROS
\deleted, \inserted, \margin, \unsure, \uline, \text<<MORE_TEXT_MACROS>>

@@ LM ANCHORS
\scanpage{n}, the scan; \mspage{n}, his page number

@@ LM APPARATUS
The marks of the transcription
- \deleted{...} is what he struck out, \inserted{...} what he added between the lines, \margin{...} a note in his margin. Translate their content, the deletions too, and keep each mark where it stands, also where it cuts a sentence; marks inside marks stay inside.
- \unsure{...} is a reading the transcriber is not sure of: translate it and keep the mark around the English. \unsure{A|B} gives two possible readings: translate each reading that is a word, in the same order, keeping the bar; readings that are symbols or formulas are copied.
- A mark holds exactly what it holds in the French: a word he broke off stays broken off (\deleted{repr} stays repr), and a bracket or other sign inside a mark stays inside it (\inserted{et]} becomes \inserted{and]}).
- \illegible{n} (n illegible words) is copied. \uline{...} is his underlining: keep it, with the words inside translated.<<MORE_APPARATUS>>

@@ SLOT MBAR
- His "même" is written m with a bar, which the transcription sets as a formula of its own ($\overline{\mathrm m}$, also \(\overline{\mathrm m}\) or $\unsure{\overline{\mathrm m}}$; in the plural $\overline{\mathrm m}^{s}$ or $\overline{\mathrm m}\mathrm{s}$; or as one of two readings, $\unsure{\overline{\mathrm m}|=}$): write it out as the English word the sentence needs (same, even, itself, likewise), without the formula. Never leave the formula beside the word, and never drop the word. Where the formula has \unsure, keep \unsure around the word; where the m with a bar is one of two readings, keep both readings in their order, the word for the m with a bar and the other reading as plain text: $\unsure{\overline{\mathrm m}|=}$ becomes \unsure{even|=} (or same, itself, likewise). "lui $\overline{\mathrm m}$" and "lui-$\overline{\mathrm m}$" are "itself"; "de $\overline{\mathrm m}$" is "likewise". Inside any other formula the shorthand is copied with the formula.

@@ SLOT FOOTER
- The archive's printed footer on a scan (its number, the address in \texttt{...} and the postal address of the Université de Montpellier) is copied as it is.

@@ SLOT DIAGRAMS
- \diagram{...} holds one of his diagrams and \figure{...} one of his drawings: copy them like formulas; only French words in \text{...} and in the labels of the drawing's nodes are translated, and so is a description in square brackets [...] that the transcription gives beside or in place of a drawing.

@@ SLOT QUOTES
- Quotation marks (“...”, «...», "...") are written ``...''.
