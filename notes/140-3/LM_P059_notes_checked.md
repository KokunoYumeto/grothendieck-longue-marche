**R4. Scan 4, §48 I. Own idea (first pass). Kept, with a check.**
If ρ acts by conjugation, ρ(α) = ραρ^{-1}, then αρ(α)^{-1} = αρα^{-1}ρ^{-1} = [α,ρ]. The heading's equality therefore fixes the convention [a,b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1}. Then [α,ρ] = [β,ρ] exactly when β^{-1}α centralises ρ, so the fibres of α ↦ [α,ρ] are the left cosets of the centraliser of ρ. In general the map is not a homomorphism. *Reason:* the reading is unchanged; the check is added.

## Second-pass notes (own)

**S1. Scan 10, (16) and the second (17). Claim checked: holds only up to ±1.**
Three involutions with (τ'_0τ'_∞)² = (τ'_1τ'_0)³ = 1 and no other relations give the Coxeter group of the (2,3,∞) triangle, the extended modular group PGL(2,Z) = Gl(2,Z)/±1. This group has trivial centre. Π_1(U^{an}_{0,3}, 𝔖_3 × Z/2) is the same group: Γ(2)/±1, free of rank 2, has quotient of order 12 ≅ 𝔖_3 × Z/2 in PGL(2,Z). So the annotation "≃ Gl(2,Z)" under 𝒯'_{1,1} is true only as Gl(2,Z)' = Gl(2,Z)/±1, matching "≃ Sl(2,Z)'" in (15). Taken literally with Gl(2,Z), whose centre is {±1}, it is false.

**S2. Scan 9 (14)–(16) with scan 10's first (17). Grothendieck's question "ou ρ^{-1} ?" — a check.**
The assignment ε'_0 ↦ l'_0, σ'_0 ↦ l'_1, ρ ↦ l'_∞ carries ρσ'_0ε'_0 = 1 to l'_∞l'_1l'_0 = 1, and ρ³ = σ'_0² = 1 to l'_∞³ = l'_1² = 1. So (17) is exactly the presentation (14) of Π'_{0,3} ≅ Z/3 * Z/2. Replacing ρ by ρ^{-1} alone breaks the product relation. The marginal question is therefore about the orientation of all three loops at once (invert all three and reverse the order of the product), not about ρ alone.

**S3. Scan 9, (12). Claim checked.**
In the local model z ↦ z^{d_a} near a ∈ S, a small positive loop around the puncture a maps to d_a times the positive loop around b = f̂(a). So the composite T_a → Π_1(X,G) ⥲ Π_1(Y,d) equals k_b ∘ (d_a·id) ∘ can, and diagram (12) commutes as drawn. This settles R24's test for orientation-preserving charts.

**S4. Scan 9, (13). Implication not pursued.**
f(z) = (27/4)·z²(z−1)²/(z²−z+1)³ = 1728/j(z), where j(λ) = 256(λ²−λ+1)³/(λ²(λ−1)²) is the j-invariant of the Legendre curve. So f maps the cusps {0,1,∞} to 0 (j = ∞), {2,−1,1/2} to 1 (j = 1728) and {−j̄,−j} to ∞ (j = 0), with ramification orders 2, 2 and 3. Over 1 and ∞ these are the orders in (14), l'_1² = l'_∞³ = 1. Over 0 the points are punctures, and the order 2 is the factor d_a = 2 of (12), recorded in (16) as "ε_i² = l_i".

**S5. Scan 7, Proposition. Implication not pursued: the Proposition inherits "si X ≠ S²".**
For X = S² and G = Z/n acting by rotations (n ≥ 2), Y^! has two points. But Π_1(X,G) = G is abelian with a single maximal lift (Γ = G), so the bijection fails. The exception stated two sentences earlier for the splitting classes is also needed in the Proposition, together with the finiteness of Γ from R19.

**S6. Scan 4, §46 IV–V. Reading consequence (conjecture about the structure).**
In IV, "0,1,∞" is struck and replaced by "−j̄, −j", next to 2, −1, 1/2. These are the points of U_{0,3} with nontrivial stabiliser in 𝔖_3: order 3 at {−j, −j̄}, order 2 at {2, −1, 1/2}. The cusps {0,1,∞} are thus moved out of IV. This favours the TOC's "q ∈ {0,1,∞}" for the subgroups listed under V ("Récapitulation en termes de M[0]"), although the ink of the sign there looks crossed (∉). The point stays open in the transcription.
