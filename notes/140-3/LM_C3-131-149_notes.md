## 4. Short note on legibility and trust

The surviving main displays are generally clearer than the rapid prose. The least secure regions are the faint quotient marks and cyclic-factor denominator on scan 131; the hooked addition and ornate group glyph on scan 133; the clipped expression on scan 136; the dense cancellations on scan 139; the diagonal and overwritten material on scan 142; and the superscripts, marginal additions, and cancellations on scans 145–149. The tiny sign in scan 149 (54) should not be settled from the algebra. The final scan was read and checked as fully as the earlier ones.

The long descending cocycle letter remains unresolved between zeta and xi. Faint hats, tildes, dots, word endings, accents, and punctuation are not equally secure across the pages. The literal matrix-group typography `Gl`/`Sl` follows the looped second letter rather than a modernization to capitals. The small lower-left handwritten numbers are reproduced without attributing their handwriting to the author.

Every page has been transcribed and reread, with additional independent checks of difficult passages. This does not make the readings infallible: the marked doubts and illegibilities are essential parts of the result. The fragment has also been checked structurally and compiled in a temporary wrapper supplying the requested editorial macros; that check concerns LaTeX syntax, not the correctness of a reading. No wrapper, inferred continuation, or editorial mathematical correction is included in the delivered fragment.
