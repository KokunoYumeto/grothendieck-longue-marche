## 4. Legibility and limits

The main prose, the large arrows and the skeletons of most formulas are substantially more legible than the small superscripts and revised passages. Enlargements and independent comparisons resolved some initially plausible readings, including *astucieux* on scan 39. They did not resolve every stroke. A repeated symbol or a mathematically attractive interpretation has not been used as sufficient evidence for an uncertain occurrence.

The least trustworthy passage is the parenthesis inserted on scan 31: the descenders of the line above obscure a word, the reading “non triviaux” remains tentative, and the double roof and dark superscript need further inspection. The overwritten symbols at the end of that scan and around (22), and the dense marginal note on scan 33, are also weak points. The first component above (28) is visibly a **dotted** u; the small downward arrow below the GP factor has been retained. The three-sided enclosure of (21) is represented without an invented lower border.

On scans 34–36 the hardest material consists of the revisions before (34), the small annotation under (36), the surviving `ext.` beside the cancelled line before (38), and the insertion between (39) and (40). The uncancelled “aussi/ainsi que (36)” remains in place even though the amended syntax is awkward. The precise chronological order of the alterations is not recoverable. Cancellations have been distinguished from surviving insertions where the ink permits; uncertainty about the state itself is marked and described in the table.

On scans 37–40 the overwritten group in (46), the hats and prime in the first Sl expression on scan 38, the section 40 heading, the superscript in (49), the mark after “où,” and the final matrix dimension in (54) remain insecure. The final word “opérateurs / opérations” is unresolved. The small “de” following the inserted “canonique” on scan 37 has been retained. No completion of an unreadable word, replacement of an unexpected dimension, or normalization of the Γ expression has been justified by what an argument would require.

## 5. Research notes (English)

These are observations and questions prompted by the supplied pages of Grothendieck (1981), not a literature review, verified mathematical conclusions, or claims of novelty. Each item gives its scan and, where possible, its equation or line locator. The **conjecture** category below includes explicit questions and expectations without turning them into assertions. **Unpursued implication** records a consequence or connection suggested by the displayed material; **own idea** identifies a proposed investigation and gives its confidence and reason. No research interpretation alters the transcription. Repeated questions are retained where their formulation changes across the extract.

1. **Scan 31, approximately lines 1–10 — conjecture (question).** The continuation asks whether the unnamed preceding conditions “constituent bel et bien une restriction” on the indicated outer automorphisms, and points to the difficulty of constructing such automorphisms other than by the arithmetic route. The preceding definition of those conditions is outside this extract, so a precise research question first requires recovering that definition rather than identifying it from later terminology.

2. **Scan 31, approximately lines 11–13 — conjecture (expectation).** The parenthesis says, “Il faudrait … refaire à l'occasion un effort en ce sens”. This is a stated intention to revisit effective constructions, not a claim that such a construction is known or that it must exist.

3. **Scan 31, approximately lines 14–18, continued in scan 32, items a) and b) — conjecture (question).** “Je me pose aussi la question” introduces reconstruction of the finer extension and the matrix homomorphism from the specified profinite groups with loop structure and their outer automorphisms. An eventual answer would have to specify exactly which group and peripheral data determine the extension, and whether the reconstruction is canonical; the wording here does not supply those details.

4. **Scan 32, (23)–(24) — unproved claim.** The action is said to be one “qui passe au quotient”. A proof would need to identify the kernel of the displayed quotient from the completion of Sl(2,Z) to Sl(2,Z-hat), then show that the relevant action preserves that kernel; this preservation is not derived on these pages.

5. **Scan 32, unnumbered homomorphism and (25) — unproved claim.** The homomorphism called “évident” is displayed as factoring through GP, with an injective final arrow. The missing verification includes the centralizer calculation that makes the scalar subgroup precisely the kernel, with attention to all local factors of the profinite coefficient ring.

6. **Scan 32, (26) — unproved claim.** “Et il se trouve que (24) se factorise en” introduces an additional factorization through GP. This does not follow merely from having the injection in (25); one must show that every automorphism arising from the extension lies in that injection's image.

7. **Scan 33, (28)–(29) — unproved claim.** The text identifies the kernel by the scalar condition “λ² = 1” and displays its product-of-signs description. A complete verification would separate the elementary scalar determinant calculation from the identification of the solutions of that equation in the profinite unit group, including the factor at the prime 2.

8. **Scan 33, (28), together with the small downward arrow — unpursued implication.** The arrow to the square-class quotient invites an explicit description of the image of the homomorphism (28). One can ask whether the image is exactly the pairs whose projective determinant class matches the class of their unit component; the transcription itself does not replace the drawn arrow with that verbal description.

9. **Scan 33, (30)–(31) and marginal note — unproved claim (additional condition).** The asserted factorization “par l'image de (28)” is qualified in the margin as “encore une condition”. A proof must therefore establish the compatibility required to land in that image, rather than assuming that an arbitrary projective action and an arbitrary character always combine to give the displayed quotient-valued homomorphism.

10. **Scan 33, (28)–(31) — own idea; confidence high as a verification strategy.** Write the proposed compatibility condition explicitly using determinant square classes, then verify it at finite quotients before comparing it with the author's marginal condition. The reason for this suggestion is directly visible in the extra arrow beneath the GP factor and in the determinant component of (28); it is a proposed way of organizing a proof, not a novelty or established assertion attributed to Grothendieck.

11. **Scan 33, (29) — own idea; confidence medium.** Finite sets of primes should make useful small test cases for separating a diagonal sign from independently chosen local signs in any later lifting problem. This is motivated by the displayed product indexed by P; it may help expose the source of ambiguity, but finite tests alone would not establish a canonical profinite lift.

12. **Scan 34, lines 4–8; kind: conjecture (expectation).** “L'hom … peut sûrement se construire directement” expresses an expectation that the map `\psi'` can be obtained from the indicated extension itself. The exact extension kernel and the inserted identification should be checked before formalizing this as a functorial reconstruction statement.

13. **Scan 34, equation (33); kind: unproved claim.** The stated requirement is that the canonical map be “encore injectif.” A proof would have to determine precisely the conjugation kernel after the indicated scalar quotient and the quotient by global `\pm1`, including any distinction between global signs and signs at individual profinite factors.

14. **Scan 34, lines 11–14, equation (34); kind: conjecture (question).** The corrected sentence asks “comment … expliciter le relèvement de” `\psi'` to the displayed primed linear group. It remains a construction problem on this page, and the word “canonique” makes naturality or a choice-free characterization part of what needs explaining.

15. **Scan 34, equations (34)–(35); kind: unproved claim.** “On en déduit l'ext. … par image inverse” asserts that the desired extension and the earlier homomorphism are recovered by pullback of the central extension (35). Establishing this would require identifying the pullback with the specified extension, with its maps and kernel, rather than merely finding an abstractly isomorphic group.

16. **Scan 34, equations (34)–(35); kind: own idea; confidence: medium.** One could organize the lifting problem by the obstruction class of the pulled-back central extension, then study the ambiguity among possible lifts. This is a proposed way of organizing the question, not a claim that the required obstruction vanishes; the reason for considering it is the explicit central extension and pullback language on the page.

17. **Scan 35, opening paragraph; kind: conjecture (question).** The author singles out “la détermination du relèvement (34) de (32)” as the essential question. The statement is a useful explicit marker that constructing the lift, rather than only the quotient-level action, remains unresolved at this point.

18. **Scan 35, equations (36)–(37); kind: unproved claim.** The extension is said to yield a homomorphism “induisant … l'opération int.,” and a second homomorphism is called “injectif.” A proof should spell out the conjugation action, verify its restriction to the kernel, and establish injectivity for the exact profinite quotient written on the page.

19. **Scan 35, paragraph after (37), continuing onto scan 36; kind: conjecture (question).** The parenthetical “si ce n'est déjà une conséquence de l'hyp. de commutation” leaves open whether the additional compatibility condition follows from commutation with the displayed group carrying subscript 3. This is a question about dependence among the hypotheses, not an assertion that the extra condition is redundant.

20. **Scans 35–36, equation (38) and its continuation; kind: own idea; confidence: low.** To test that possible redundancy, one could first seek a subgroup satisfying the commutation hypothesis and compare its induced action on finite quotients with the required cyclotomic action. The reason is that the text isolates these as separate conditions; this is only a proposed diagnostic, and neither existence of a counterexample nor sufficiency of finite tests is asserted.

21. **Scan 36, opening paragraph and equation (39); kind: unproved claim.** Compatibility is described using the character “cyclotomique” and the quotient `\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{*}/\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{*2}`. A proof must specify the action of this quotient on the primed special linear group and show that it agrees with the outer action induced by the stipulated character.

22. **Scan 36, between (39) and (40); kind: unproved claim.** The corrected “condition de congruence” passage asserts a factorization “par (37)” and an ensuing homomorphism described as “bien déterminé” (reading slightly uncertain). The factorization would require verifying that the relevant automorphisms lie in the image of (37), with uniqueness using its asserted injectivity; the heavy revisions make the exact domain worth checking against later pages.

23. **Scan 36, equations (41)–(42); kind: unproved claim.** The condition is said to ensure, “plus précisément,” that the pair consisting of the projective map and cyclotomic character factors through the quotient displayed in (42). One needs an exact description of the image of the injection mentioned at the page's end and a verification that the two components satisfy that image condition.

24. **Scan 36, equations (39)–(42); kind: unpursued implication.** The passage places a square-class quotient, a projective map, and the cyclotomic character in the same lifting problem. It suggests separating the projective-action information from the determinant or scalar information when comparing possible constructions; the exact strength of this separation is not proved here.

25. **Scan 36, equations (41)–(42); kind: own idea; confidence: medium.** A concrete first test is to write down the corresponding finite-quotient image condition for the pair `(projective class, character value)`, keeping the prime 2 separate. This is only a proposed calculation, motivated by the square-class quotient and the visibly written left subscript 2 on the scalar subgroup; no general lifting theorem or novelty is claimed.

26. **Scan 37, (44)–(46) — Unproved claim.** The kernel is identified in the sentence “dont le noyau est isom.” and the resulting inverse image is described as “une extension centrale.” A proof should verify the stated kernel, its diagonal quotient, and centrality for the precise source and target groups; it must keep the order-two scalar subgroup distinct from the diagonal pair of signs. The damaged name of the middle group in (46) has not been reconstructed from this interpretation.

27. **Scan 37, lower paragraph, continuing at scan 38, ll. 1–3 — Conjecture (explicit question).** “La question à élucider à présent” asks whether the lifting, or splitting, can be made explicit from the “seule donnée” of Γ satisfying the stated congruence conditions. The inserted word “canonique” makes the requested distinguished choice part of the question, beyond the bare existence of a lift. These pages do not by themselves resolve that question.

28. **Scan 38, upper paragraph — Unproved claim.** The requested restriction is said to be “déjà connu” on the indicated hatted subgroup, through the displayed sequence of Sl groups. A proof should check that this composite has exactly the required reduction and restriction properties, with the relevant continuity conditions. The uncertain hats and prime in the preceding identification prevent treating a normalized reconstruction as the text of the claim.

29. **Scan 38, (48) — Unproved claim.** The manuscript places the remaining “indétermination” in the kernel of a restriction map and identifies that kernel with Hom(Γ, ν), using ν here only as the provisional reading of the handwritten kernel symbol.
