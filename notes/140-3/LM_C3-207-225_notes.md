# LM_C3-207-225 — Transcription notes

## Scope and conventions

This apparatus accompanies the diplomatic transcription of all **19 supplied images, scans 207–225**, from Alexander Grothendieck's *La Longue Marche à travers la théorie de Galois* (1981), as identified in the supplied task description. The readings are based only on those images. No published transcription, external mathematical source, or continuation outside the supplied pages was used to complete an uncertain reading.

**Page references follow the image filenames.** The archive footers and the matching light lower-left numbers run from 206 to 224. They are treated as archive pagination and are not entered as the author's manuscript page numbers. The higher handwritten headers are recorded only where visible:

The LaTeX is a fragment, with no preamble or macro definitions. It uses the requested editorial macros and standard mathematical LaTeX, with tikz-cd for the readable diagrams. Deletions, additions, and marginal notes remain distinct. Doubt markers apply to the specific reading they enclose; alternatives separated by a vertical bar are unresolved alternatives, not simultaneous manuscript text. An illegibility count estimates words or short written units and is especially approximate inside cancelled formulas. Local bars, superscripts, parentheses, and repeated or overwritten equation numbers have not been regularized from the mathematics. Editorial braces needed to encode LaTeX are not supplied manuscript punctuation.

Each page was read as a whole, transcribed, and reread against the image; difficult regions were inspected in enlargements or rotated crops. Selected difficult passages also received an independent reading. Syntax was checked with a temporary wrapper defining the requested editorial macros; that wrapper is not part of the deliverable. A successful syntax check does not establish palaeographic certainty.

## Legibility and limitations

Most central prose and large matrix displays are more legible than the sloping marginalia and interlinear corrections. The least secure passages are the lower-left note on scan 210; the parenthesis about the hypothetical subgroup on scan 213; the dense margins on scans 208, 219, and 221; the cancelled factors in (170) and the end of the marginal correction to (172), both on scan 224. Ornate group initials and the order of hats, tildes, and exclamation marks remain uncertain in several places. These are marked locally, including uncertainty about the alphabet itself.

Crossing-out sometimes obscures the sequence of revisions. The transcription preserves recoverable layers without inventing a clean final formula. Some other short word endings and superscripts may still be misread despite the rereading; the fragment should be used with the scans and the local doubt marks, not as an error-free critical edition.
