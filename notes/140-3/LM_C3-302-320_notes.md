## 3. Legibility note

The main prose and large displays are often readable after enlargement, but the rapid handwriting leaves many endings, small signs, primes and alphabet distinctions insecure. The least trustworthy passages are the polynomial corrections on scans 305 and 307, the layered lemma revisions on scan 310, the corollary and locality insertions on scan 314, the prime states on scan 316, and the diagonal marginal notes and revised remark on scan 319. The final matrix calculation on scan 320 has also been checked closely; its overwritten coefficients and residual deleted w-like strokes remain marked where unresolved.

Dense overwriting can obscure both the original character and whether a stroke is a deletion, an underline or an insertion guide. The distinctions between Latin v and Greek nu, ordinary and script capitals, the A/T-like subgroup labels, and the raised stroke in E¹/E′ cannot always be settled from these scans. Some apparent algebraic inconsistencies and irregular condition labels remain because the visible reading was retained. The explicit uncertainty marks and doubt register should therefore accompany any quotation or mathematical use of the transcription; they do not rule out further unrecognized reading errors.
