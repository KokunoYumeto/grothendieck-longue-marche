## Legibility and limitations

Most of the main prose and the larger formulas are recoverable, but this is a difficult, faint, rapidly written manuscript with overtracing and several layers of correction. Small accents, primes, hats, terminal letters, and punctuation are substantially less secure than the broad argument. Mathematical expectations were not used to regularize unusual rings, group labels, formula references, or completion marks.

The least trustworthy regions are the opening expression before `réseau` and the superscripts in (30) on scan 21; the tiny raised mark in the scalar-centralizer calculation on scan 23; the diagonal NB on scan 24; the canceled drafts around (10) on scan 25; the crowded right edge of (13) on scan 27; and the corrections in (17), the overwritten determiner after (18), and the last canceled phrase on scans 29–30. The last phrase on scan 30 has deliberately been left unfinished. Apparent discrepancies such as `SL(2,2m)`, `Z/2m` without a final `Z`, the double completion marking, and `m` versus `2m` are retained or explicitly marked rather than repaired.

## Research notes — English

These notes are separate from the transcription and do not resolve any doubtful reading. All manuscript quotations and page-based claims refer to **Grothendieck (1981)**; each item supplies its scan and equation or approximate line locator. “Conjecture” includes explicitly labeled questions or proposed investigations, and does not imply that the author states a formal conjecture. Items labeled **own idea** are editorial proposals, with confidence and reasons; none is presented as an established result or as new. An item may be answered elsewhere in the manuscript or literature; that has not been assumed or checked here.

1. **Scan 21, eq. (29) and following lines — unproved claim.** The assertion “On a un isom. canonique (analytique)” and its normalization by the two ramification orbits are stated here without proof. A verification would require the definitions from preceding pages of the domain, quotient, and target coordinate, and would have to respect the exceptional-point labels actually written here.

2. **Scan 21, eq. (30) and final paragraph — conjecture / stated intention.** “que je voudrais expliciter, en identifiant” announces a desired explicit identification of the three primed tau elements with elements of the final written matrix group. This is a stated task, not an assertion that it has been completed on this page; the precise superscripts on that group and the fundamental group remain transcription uncertainties.

3. **Scan 21, final paragraph — own idea.** Once the preceding definitions and ambiguous superscripts have been resolved, one could choose matrix representatives for the three named elements and check their orders and relations against the branch normalization in (29). Confidence: medium that this would clarify the intended explicit identification; the current extract alone does not determine the representatives or all conventions.

4. **Scan 22, diagram (1) and first paragraph — unproved claim.** The assertion about the induced “action extérieure” is represented by a diagram whose middle vertical map is not constructed on this page. A justification would have to supply that map and its compatibility with the indicated geometric kernel and determinant; the subsequent discussion explains what the resulting exterior action would be once this compatibility is available.

5. **Scan 23, lower half, centralizer argument — unproved claim.** The step from commuting with SL(2,Zhat) to “c'est un scalaire” is asserted without the matrix calculation. A proof would compute the centralizer using both elementary upper and lower unipotent matrices; this is the step on which the asserted injectivity of (5) rests.

6. **Scan 23, (5) and its explanation — own idea.** As a verification project, repeat the scalar-centralizer and determinant argument over Z/nZ before taking inverse limits, keeping track of how the square-class quotients map between levels. **Confidence: high as a proposed check**, because all the groups and maps in the displayed argument arise from explicit two-by-two matrices; this is a suggested calculation, not a claim of novelty or a substitute for the manuscript text.

7. **Scan 24, (6) and the following paragraph — unproved claim.** The passage “par la théorie du corps de classes pour Q” identifies the exterior action's effective quotient as the “quotient multiquadratique” and says that the resulting action “est fidèle”. A full justification would have to define the subscript 2 notation, identify the cyclotomic square-class quotient with that Galois quotient, and combine this identification with the injectivity argument on scan 23.

8. **Scan 24, marginal NB — unproved claim.** The note says that the exterior action on SL(2,Z/nZ) “se fait par l'intermédiaire de” the indicated quotient of the units group. The finite-level factorization is stated without proof here; checking it would require following determinant conjugation through reduction modulo n and verifying which scalar matrices become trivial in exterior automorphisms.

9. **Scan 24, paragraph surrounding (7) — unpursued implication.** The phrase “conditions assez fortes” separates preservation of the congruence kernel from the restriction on the induced exterior action of its quotient. This suggests treating the two conditions as logically distinct tests on a candidate exterior automorphism; these pages do not assert that the tests characterize the Galois image.

10. **Scan 25, (8)–(9); kind: unproved claim.** The cusp-inertia image is identified, with the reservation «sauf erreur», as a subgroup of lower unitriangular matrices. A proof would have to relate the specified inertia homomorphism to a boundary monodromy calculation and account for the stated conjugacy ambiguity. The qualification is part of the author's statement and should remain visible in any later mathematical analysis.

11. **Scan 25, (10) — unproved claim.** The statement following (10) identifies the character on the procyclic inertia group as “cyclotomique”; the word transcribed as `sera` and the precise spelling of `caractère` remain doubtful in the fragment. Establishing this identification requires comparison with the action on roots of unity, including whatever choice of inertia convention fixes the exponent or its inverse.

12. **Scan 25, final paragraph, continuing into scan 26, (11)–(12); kind: unproved claim.** The inserted «on vérifie qu'» introduces the existence and uniqueness of χ(u), and the margin separately says «faire la vérification». The missing verification concerns both the choice of a representative of an exterior automorphism and the effect of conjugating the image of the inertia homomorphism.

13. **Scans 25–26, comparison of (11) and the second diagram — own idea.** One concrete check is to set $U(n)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\n&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $D(a)=\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1})$ for $a\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{*}$; direct multiplication gives $D(a)U(n)D(a)^{-1}=U(a^{-2}n)$, although conjugation by $D(a)$ is inner in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb Z})$. This tests whether passing from a chosen representative to an outer class loses the asserted uniqueness of $\chi(u)$, or whether the manuscript's conventions restore it. **Confidence: high for the matrix identity, medium for its relevance**, because the meaning of equality in the exterior diagrams and the chosen inertia data are not fully specified in this extract. This is a proposed verification, not an assertion of an error or a new result.

14. **Scan 26, paragraph beginning «Ceci dit»; kind: unpursued implication.** The requirements to preserve the kernel of (7) and to act on the quotient «suivant le composé» distinguish constraints on an outer action from constraints visible through the character alone. A useful continuation would separate the automorphisms satisfying the inertia requirement from those also preserving the congruence kernel, and identify where the second restriction adds information.

15. **Scans 26–27, final/initial paragraph; kind: unproved claim.** Since the center is nontrivial, the outer action «ne permet pas à priori de reconstituer (à iso unique près) l'ext.». The argument then passes to the quotient by {±1} and an extension obtained «par image inverse». To verify this passage, one would need to specify the extension equivalence relation and check the relevant center and automorphism sequence; the crowded notation in (13) requires independent decipherment before treating its displayed groups as settled.

16. **Scan 27, «Cette extension … a été aussi interprétée»; kind: unproved claim.** The text identifies the resulting extension with M₀,₃ ≃ N′₀,₄ and describes its kernel as an extension of the finite group displayed as isomorphic to SL(2,Z/2Z) by the hatted Π₀,₃. These identifications are recalled rather than proved on this page. A verification would require the actual homomorphisms, including their compatibility with the quotient by the central involution.

17. **Scan 27, final paragraph; kind: unproved claim.** «Nous savons déjà» recalls both faithfulness of the outer Galois action on the hatted Π₀,₃ and its commutation with the indicated finite quotient's outer action. The first statement needs a faithfulness argument or a precise reference to an earlier argument; the second needs a comparison of the two outer actions and their representatives. The unfinished last sentence signals that the commutation will be reinterpreted on the next scan, but that continuation does not supply a proof on this page.

18. **Scans 26–27, passage from the extension to its center quotient; kind: own idea.** One could keep separate records of the quotient extension and of the residual central lifting data, instead of assuming that reconstruction of the former determines the latter. **Confidence: medium**, because the explicitly noted center {±1} makes this a natural place to look for an obstruction or an ambiguity, but no classification of that data is carried out here. This is a suggested investigation, with no claim of novelty.

19. **Scan 28, lines 3–8 — Unproved claim.** The passage links triviality at modulus 2 to an “op. (fidèle)” of the unit quotient and states that the corresponding triviality is “fausse” for `n≥3`. A proof audit should identify the exact square-class notation, establish the factorization of the outer action, and then distinguish the special modulus 2 from the remaining moduli; none of those steps should be inferred merely from the visual transcription.
