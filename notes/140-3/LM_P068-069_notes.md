# LM_P068-069 — Notes accompanying the diplomatic transcription

## Scope and reference conventions

The transcription covers all **20 supplied images**, identified by their filenames as **scans 91–110**. All contain manuscript material. No missing page has been supplied from another source, and no published transcription or external mathematical text has been used to determine a reading.

**Numbering:** `\scanpage{N}` follows the supplied filename, as requested. The printed archive footer and the small number at the lower left run from **90 to 109**, one lower than those filenames. They are not treated as the author's manuscript pagination. The larger handwritten page numbers visible at the top are **335–344**, on scans 92, 94, 96, 98, 100, 102, 104, 106, 108 and 110 respectively; only those visible numbers are recorded with `\mspage`. No number is inferred for an unnumbered sheet face. The printed institutional footer is not manuscript text.

Locations in the doubt table use equation numbers, numbered items, margins and approximate body lines. An equation or marginal position is generally a more reliable locator than a line count on these pages. **High / medium / low** expresses confidence in a reading, not confidence in its mathematical validity; these are qualitative assessments, not measured probabilities. Repeated doubts about a letter may be grouped in one table entry, while the transcription marks the individual occurrences. Where cancellation makes even word boundaries uncertain, `\illegible{n}` gives an estimated count; an isolated mathematical expression may be counted as a single unreadable unit.

All discussion below is separate from the transcription. The English research notes distinguish the author's statements and questions from proposed checks and **own ideas**. Their quotations and scan references point only to the supplied manuscript (Grothendieck, 1981). They do not claim that an issue is unresolved elsewhere, that a suggested idea is new, or that a written formula is established. The cocycle letter ζ in the research discussion is shorthand for the explicitly uncertain ζ/ξ glyph; it is not a resolution of that textual doubt.

## 4. Legibility and limits of trust

The pages are unevenly legible. Most large formulas and much of the main prose can be read, but I trust the small diagonal margins, retraced letters and superscripts, and heavily canceled calculations least. The greatest difficulties are the annotations on scan 91, the revised catalogue on scan 95, the margins and small corrections on scans 97–100 and 103–104, and the overlapping canceled work on scans 107 and 109. The final page is clearer in its active text; its equation reference, canceled side formula and final word endings remain uncertain.

Each page was compared with its transcription after the initial reading, using enlarged or rotated views where necessary. This does not guarantee an error-free reading: the explicit doubt and illegibility marks identify real limits of trust. Visible repetitions, changes of notation and apparent inconsistencies were retained, including the repeated number (55), the inserted plus on the Pi group in scan 102, and the written condition c+d−b=1 alongside later expressions involving 1/δ. The notes propose checks of such points without using mathematical consistency to rewrite the ink.
