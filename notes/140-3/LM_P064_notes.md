## 3. Short note on legibility and trust

These pages are readable unevenly. Most of the main French argument, large equation labels, separated generator images in (2), and principal diagram objects are clearer than the rapid word endings and small additions. The least reliable places are the crossed-out representative and tiny matrix entries on scan 51; the cancelled words and page-spanning *conjec- / turale* on scans 52–53; the compressed §41 heading, clipped right endpoint of (1), and annotation beneath (3); and, above all, the dense margins and pale (10 bis) on scan 58, the small superscripts on scan 59, and the revised argument of (17) and cancelled margin on scan 60.

The transcription deliberately leaves some deletions illegible and some symbols unresolved. The ornate S/G-like capital has a continuous upper flourish: no independent tilde is supplied. Unclear links in (5) are described instead of reconstructed with guessed arrowheads. The apparently repeated `\ell_\infty^p` in (9), the named quotient source on scan 57, and incomplete visible expressions are retained. These choices favor a checkable record of the ink over a smooth or mathematically regularized text. The research notes must not be used as authority for removing a doubt marker.

### Scans 51–53

1. **Scan 51, approximately lines 1–7 — Unproved claim.** The ambiguity in choosing the point is attributed to the displayed kernel, said to be « lui-\(\overline{\mathrm m}\) isomorphe à \(\Pi_{0,3}\) ». Checking this identification would require the preceding definitions of the prime on SL and of the pointed covering, as well as a precise comparison of the two group actions; those definitions are outside these supplied pages. The first SL digit remains a transcription doubt, not a mathematical emendation. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 51.)

2. **Scans 51–52, the three matrices and continuation — Unproved claim.** The passage lists « les trois éléments » taking \(j\) to \(-\bar j\), then infers that the two points are not congruent under the kernel modulo 2. A full verification should check each matrix action, establish that the displayed list is exhaustive in the precise quotient group being used, and then perform the reductions; merely checking the three reductions would not establish exhaustivity. Two lower-left matrix entries remain explicitly doubtful in the transcription. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 51–52.)

3. **Scan 52, approximately lines 5–11 — Unproved claim.** Choosing the point above \(P\) to be a « racine de l'unité » is said to have « normalisé » the exterior homomorphism (73) into an effective one, and likewise (70). A proof should state exactly which inner-automorphism ambiguity the chosen lift removes and whether any residual stabilizer remains. The maps numbered (70) and (73) are referenced here but not defined within this batch. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 52, manuscript p. 314.)

4. **Scan 52, lower paragraph — Conjecture / expressed research intention.** « On aurait envie de reprendre les constructions précédentes dans le contexte des groupes profinis » is immediately qualified by « je manque des fondements nécessaires ». This identifies an intended profinite extension and a missing theory described by the tentatively read phrases « trouage » and « forage de trous »; it does not assert that such a theory has been constructed. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 52, manuscript p. 314.)

5. **Scan 53, approximately lines 1–10 — Conjecture / expectation.** « Pourtant on sent que c'est bien dans cette direction » introduces the hoped-for group-theoretic understanding of homomorphism (40), followed by « Ce devrait être une incitation ». A research task is to determine what data a purely group-theoretic description of that displayed homomorphism would require, especially which ingredients currently depend on the covering construction. The German adjective and the source-group alphabet are marked as uncertain in the transcription. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 53.)

6. **Scan 53, lower paragraph, continued on scan 54 — Conjecture / question.** The inserted « la question s'il y a une » reframes the extension to the profinite context as a question, explicitly distinguishing it from introducing elements of « structure “arithmétique” ». The text makes that question depend on the status of the congruence relations discussed in the continuation. It should therefore be studied as a conditional problem, rather than read as an assertion that arbitrary suitable profinite automorphism groups already carry the desired construction. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 53–54.)

7. **Scans 51–52 — Own idea. Confidence: high for the proposed check, low for any conclusion beyond it.** Once the uncertain entries and prime notation have been independently settled, a short table of the three fractional-linear actions and their reductions modulo 2 would provide an auditable local check of the orbit argument. The reason for confidence is that this is a finite calculation on the displayed matrices; it would still leave the completeness and covering-identification arguments to be proved. This is a verification proposal, not a claim of novelty or an established theorem.

8. **Scans 52–53 — Own idea. Confidence: low.** One possible way to formulate the missing profinite construction is to ask first for compatible constructions on every relevant finite quotient, then separately for preservation under the proposed arithmetic subgroup. The reason for low confidence is that compatibility, distinguished inertia data, and a universal property are not supplied here, so an inverse-limit formulation by itself would not establish existence. This separates two questions suggested by the passage without attributing this formulation to Grothendieck.


### Scans 54–56

These notes are separate from the transcription. Their source is the supplied manuscript image; interpretive proposals below are not claims of established results or novelty. Where a quoted word is doubtful, the note avoids depending on its exact recovery.

1. **Scan 54, ll. 1–7 — conjecture (explicit open alternative).** Grothendieck asks whether the congruence relations are “de nature arithmétique” and specific to outer automorphisms coming from `Γ_Q`, or instead consequences of a formalism of profinite groups described in the insertion as “purement ‘algébrique’.” The passage leaves the distinction open; identifying which hypotheses each relation actually uses would be the first step toward deciding between these possibilities.
