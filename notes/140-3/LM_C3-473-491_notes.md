The confidence labels should therefore guide further comparison with the originals, rather than be read as a guarantee of exact recovery. ## Research notes — English, separate from the transcription

These are manuscript-based prompts for further study. An **unproved claim** means that the cited passage states the claim without a proof there; it may have been proved elsewhere in the manuscript. **Conjecture / question / expectation** includes the author's proposed investigations and explicit qualifications. **Own ideas** are labelled, with a confidence level and reason, and carry no claim of novelty or established truth. These notes have not been checked against the remainder of the manuscript or the later literature and do not assert that any question remains open today. They do not alter any transcription reading. Citations below use the supplied primary source and scan numbers.

1. **Scan 473, (34)–(36) — unproved claim.** The phrase “elle définit tout de suite une loi de groupe” is followed by two extension structures. A proof would require checking that the stated maps to `G_m` are group homomorphisms, identifying each kernel scheme-theoretically, and specifying the sense in which each final arrow is surjective; the page does not supply those verifications (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 473).

2. **Scan 473, lower paragraph and (37) — unpursued implication.** The description “image isomorphique” identifies the subgroup cut out by `f=1` with the graph of the indicated morphism on `M[0]`. The formulation invites a comparison of this graph subgroup with the two projections in (35) and (36), including their intersections with the kernels, but these comparisons are not developed on this page (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 473).

3. **Scan 474, opening paragraph and (38) — unproved claim.** “il y en a un tout prêt” asserts that the displayed extension is split by the indicated subgroup, and the paragraph then describes the larger group as a semidirect product. One would need to check the section explicitly and verify that its conjugation action on `L_0` is the action through the written character `χ` (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 474).

4. **Scan 474, theorem (39) — unproved claim.** “On a un isom. can.” introduces the isomorphism to the scheme of systems satisfying the cited relations, with its coordinate map cancelled at the foot and continued on the next scan. A complete proof should establish a functorial inverse and compatibility with multiplication; those requirements should be kept distinct from a mere bijection on points (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 474).

5. **Scan 475, (40)–(41) — unproved claim.** The assertion that the displayed operation “correspond à l'opération de translation à gauche” identifies two actions before passing to a quotient. A verification would substitute the three coordinate formulas into the action, track the fourth coordinate, and prove that the proposed group quotient has the stated functorial meaning (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 475).

6. **Scan 475, proposition (42)–(43) — unproved claim.** The monomorphism and image description are called “une conséquence immédiate” of an earlier corollary and the fibre-product definition. These pages do not reproduce that corollary, so a self-contained proof would need the missing earlier hypotheses together with a reconstruction of the source element from the determinant-compatible coordinates (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 475).

7. **Scan 475, (43) — own idea.** Check the determinant conditions on arbitrary base rings, including rings in which 2 is not invertible, before treating the sign-valued condition as a two-case choice. **Confidence: medium.** The written condition uses `μ_2` rather than a constant two-element set; this makes a functorial calculation worth testing, without claiming that any difficulty actually occurs here.

8. **Scan 476, (43)–(44) and the following paragraph — unproved claim.** “Il est évident par prop p. 536'” introduces the claim that the indicated subgroup supplies a section, and the prose selects the lift with `U=b`. A proof would need the earlier proposition, a check of the determinant conditions for the chosen lift, and verification that the section respects the group law, rather than only giving representatives (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 476).

9. **Scan 476, (45)–(47) — unpursued implication.** The second construction is described as “une section partielle” over the subgroup defined by the equality of the two determinant characters, also written as a kernel. Comparing its domain with that of the first section would clarify precisely where the asymmetry between `ρ` and `σ` enters; that comparison is not developed here (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 476).

10. **Scan 476, (46) — own idea.** Compute the intersection of the two graph sections on their common domain, first as a functor of the base ring. **Confidence: medium.** The coordinate descriptions suggest that their coincidence is controlled by `a=b`, but any interpretation as a subgroup or fibre product still requires checking the ambient embeddings and hypotheses.

11. **Scan 477, remark after (48) — unproved claim.** “Ces sous-groupes peuvent se définir” extends the section constructions to a more general situation with two specified embeddings and compatible characters. A proof should identify exactly which assumptions from the earlier construction remain necessary, establish the morphisms over the stated base, and verify the section identities (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 477).

12. **Scan 477, (50)–(52) — unpursued implication.** The definition transports the subgroups by conjugation along the displayed homography, followed by the assertion that its third iterate is the identity. Checking the three-step consistency of this transport requires comparing the chosen group lift and its third power, not merely the action on the labels `q` (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 477).

13. **Scan 477, last paragraph — conjecture / question / expectation.** “Je voudrais : définir” introduces the desired subgroup at `−j`, and the sentence begins to discuss making a more specific choice within a prescribed conjugacy class. The criterion for that choice is not completed on this page; this is an explicit authorial aim, not a theorem established by the preceding formulas (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 477).

14. **Scan 477, (52) — own idea.** Calculate the label orbits `{0,1,∞}` and `{1/2,2,−1}` under the written homography, then use them as a consistency check for the conjugation convention in (51). **Confidence: high for the rational calculation, medium for its application to the group construction.** The displayed rational function determines the first calculation directly, whereas the group-level conclusion depends on the lift and the preceding definitions.

15. **Scan 478, first line and (53); kind: conjecture.** The conditional phrase « serait entre » leaves a proposed restriction of the choice to the three displayed conjugates, with indices 0, 1, and ∞. 16. **Scan 479, introductory paragraph and (1)–(4); kind: unproved claim.** The retrospective asserts that the construction gives « deux schémas en groupes sur Z ». To check the assertion directly from these displays, one should formulate the determinant conditions functorially over an arbitrary Z-algebra and verify closure under multiplication and inversion, while keeping the uncertain coordinate revisions distinct from the mathematical verification (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 479).

17. **Scan 479, (5)–(7); kind: unproved claim.** The quotient description and « produit 1/2 direct » are stated together with an embedding. 18. **Scan 479, (9)–(11); kind: unproved claim.** The sign condition defining N′_σ is followed by « d'où suite exacte ».
