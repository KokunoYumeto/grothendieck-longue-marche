## The first pass's notes

**N1. Scans 71–72. Claim without proof. Keep.** Formula (41) and the proof on scan 72 use N_{Π̂0,3}(L1) = L1 as known
(the symbol is overwritten on scan 71 and a clean 𝒩 on scan 72). It is the self-normalising property of the loop
subgroups that the manuscript records earlier as N_E(L_i) = E_i; it is not proved on these pages.

**N2. Scans 71–72, 75. Claim without proof. Keep, now checked.** "γ bien déterminé", "g ne dépend que de g0 mod L0" and
the invariance of (46) under g0 ↦ g0ℓ0^λ follow by substitution: σ∞(ℓ0^λ) = ρ(ℓ0^λ) = ℓ1^λ leaves
g = σ∞(g0)ℓ1^γρ(g0)^{-1} unchanged, and h0 ↦ ℓ1^{-λ}h0ℓ0^λ sends ξ0 = ℓ0^γh0^{-1} to ℓ0^{-λ}ξ0ρ(ℓ0^λ), which conjugates
ξ0ρ by ℓ0^{-λ} and so preserves (ξ0ρ)³ = 1. Reason: the first pass asked for exactly this substitution.

**N3. Scans 73–74. Model's own idea (first pass). Keep.** The corrected text confirms the inclusion ⊂ in (43); the surviving
computation and its NB give int(g0^{-1})u(ε0) = ε0^{2γ+1}, hence (44) with p = 2γ+1, and the NB of scan 74 proves the
converse, that every p ∈ Ẑ* has (p−1)/2 ∈ Ẑ. The remark that the power map fills the procyclic subgroup only for a unit
exponent matches the separate unit condition of scans 79–80.

**N4. Scan 74. Claim without proof. Correct.** The condition declared "superflue, i.e., automatiquement vérifiée" is the
boxed (9) of scan 58 (LM_P064), whose margin announces "on verra que (9) est superflu(e), et γ = (p−1)/2"; the digit here has
an open top like a 7, so the transcription keeps `\unsure{(9)|(7)}`. No proof is given ("on voit"): one must derive (9)
from (10) = (46) and the definitions of h0 and γ. Reason: the cross-reference identifies the condition the first pass left
open.

**N5. Scan 75, Théorème. Claim without proof. Keep, corrected.** Two lifts commuting with σ∞ differ by int(x) with
x ∈ Π̂0,3 and x^{-1}σ∞(x) central, so uniqueness follows from the triviality of the centre of Π̂0,3 and of Π̂0,3^{σ∞}, the
fact the margin of scan 79 invokes; existence rests on scans 71–74 and is not argued again here. The qualifier after "autom.
ext." is "à lacets" or its abbreviation "à lcts": the remaining doubt is of spelling, not of meaning.

**N6. Scan 75, condition b). Implication not pursued. Keep, completed.** By the NB, a) is equivalent to (gρ)³ = 1, so
σ∞ ↦ σ∞, ρ ↦ gρ defines an endomorphism of Ŝ+0,3; b) makes it surjective, and a surjective endomorphism of a topologically
finitely generated profinite group is bijective (such groups are Hopfian). So a) and b) together give an automorphism; the
text does not state this last step.

**N7. Scan 76, cancelled Corollaire. Claim without proof, cancelled. Correct.** The labels are [(24)] (not (21)) and
[(30)] or [(38)] (overwritten), and the qualifier is "extérieurement", now read without doubt; the two formulas are (24) of
scan 62 and (30) of scan 64, with α = β+γ as in the margin there, so the corollary recapitulates LM_P065. The margin "β resp.
α sont uniquement déterminés" is an unproved uniqueness claim, and the whole block is crossed out: it is not an accepted
result.

**N8. Scan 77, NB "λ ↦ ^λξ est injectif". Claim without proof. Keep, with a route.** Injectivity says that no λ ≠ 1 of
Π̂0,3 centralises the order-3 element ξρ. In Ŝ+0,3 = Ẑ/2 ⨿ Ẑ/3 the centraliser of a torsion element is the finite group it
generates (centraliser theorem for free profinite products, Herfort–Ribes 1985, to check), and that meets the torsion-free
Π̂0,3 trivially.

**N9. Scan 77, (51)–(54). Reclassified: Grothendieck's own formulation (the first pass called it a model idea). Keep.** The
text itself identifies the solutions of (51) with the splittings of the sub-extension of Ŝ+0,3 of Z/3Z by Π̂0,3 generated
by ρ̇, and with the order-3 elements above ρ̇, on which Π̂0,3 acts by (54); in modern terms this is the non-abelian
H¹(Z/3Z, Π̂0,3). The first pass's caution about conventions (cocycles versus lifts) still applies.

**N10. Scan 78. Grothendieck's explicit assumption ("Admettons (quitte à le vérifier par la suite)"). Keep, with a proof
route.** If every finite subgroup of the free profinite product Ŝ+0,3 is conjugate into a factor (Herfort–Ribes 1985,
to check), an order-3 element above ρ̇ is aρ^{±1}a^{-1}; since the centraliser of ρ̇ in 𝔖3 is ⟨ρ̇⟩ and ρ̇^{-1} is conjugate
to ρ̇ only by transpositions, this leaves exactly the Π̂0,3-classes of ρ and of σ∞ρ^{-1}σ∞. They are distinct because ρ and
ρ^{-1} have different images in Ŝ+0,3^{ab} = Z/6Z. (The word "deux" is now `\unsure` in the transcription, on ink grounds.)

**N11. Scan 78, bracketed remark after (56). Implication not pursued. Correct.** With ρ(ℓ0)^{-1} now settled, the line
ℓ1^{-1} = ℓ0(ℓ0^{-1})ρ(ℓ0)^{-1} is (56) with λ = ℓ0 (as ρ(ℓ0) = ℓ1): it checks that the representative ξ0 = ℓ1^{-1} of the
second class lies in family (56); the cancelled "i.e. ℓ0 = ρ(λ)ℓ1λ^{-1} … λ = ℓ∞ℓ0" is an abandoned attempt at the same
check. The families (55) and (56) are disjoint because they are the two classes of N10.

**N12. Scan 79, "au plus un seul g0". Claim without proof. Keep.** Uniqueness of g0 for fixed h0 follows from
Π̂0,3^{σ∞} = {1}, which the corrected margin invokes ("en utilisant que …"). That fixed-point statement is the centraliser
fact of N8 for the involution σ∞ and is not proved here.

**N13. Scan 79, "un tel g0 existe ssi (59)". Claim without proof. Keep, sharpened.** (59) σ∞(h0)h0 = 1 is equivalent to
(h0^{-1}σ∞)² = 1, so the "ssi" says that every involution of the coset Π̂0,3σ∞ is Π̂0,3-conjugate to σ∞, i.e.
H¹(⟨σ∞⟩, Π̂0,3) = 1. This follows from the conjugacy of finite subgroups into factors (as in N10), because the centraliser
of σ̇∞ in 𝔖3 is ⟨σ̇∞⟩; the page gives no argument.

**N14. Scan 79, "il existe au plus un γ". Claim without proof. Correct: it has a two-line proof.** Abelianising (60) in
Π̂0,3^{ab} = Ẑℓ0 ⊕ Ẑℓ1 gives ξ̄ + σ∞(ξ̄) = γ(ℓ0 + ℓ1), so γ is unique; S2 below computes it.

**N15. Scan 79, last parenthesis. Model's own idea (first pass). Correct.** The ink settles the star: "∃γ′ ∈ Ẑ*". The
identity (2γ+1)(2γ′+1) − 1 = 2(2γγ′ + γ + γ′) in the torsion-free Ẑ shows that the right range is Ẑ (γ = 0 forces γ′ = 0,
which is not a unit), so the star is the author's slip, not a transcription doubt.

## Second-pass notes (mine)

**S1. Scans 74–80. Own idea (an identification, to check in the literature).** The two conditions obtained here —
h0σ∞(h0) = 1 ((59)) and ξρ(ξ)ρ²(ξ) = 1 with ξ = ℓ0^γh0^{-1}, γ = (p−1)/2 ((46), (48), (51)) — have the shape of the
two-cycle and three-cycle relations f(x,y)f(y,x) = 1 and f(z,x)z^m f(y,z)y^m f(x,y)x^m = 1, m = (λ−1)/2, of Drinfeld's
ĜT (1990), without the pentagon, with p in the role of λ and h0 in that of f up to normalisation. If the match survives a
careful comparison of conventions, these pages describe in 1981 the outer automorphisms of Π̂0,3 that commute with 𝔖3 by
such pairs (p, h0); whether the literature (Ihara; Lochak–Schneps; Schneps's surveys) records this must be checked before
any claim.

**S2. Scans 78–80. Own idea (a computation).** In Π̂0,3^{ab} = Ẑℓ0 ⊕ Ẑℓ1 (ℓ∞ = −ℓ0 − ℓ1; ρ: ℓ0 ↦ ℓ1 ↦ ℓ∞; σ∞
exchanges ℓ0 and ℓ1) write λ̄ = aℓ0 + bℓ1. For ξ = λρ(λ)^{-1} (55) one gets ξ̄ = (a+b)ℓ0 + (2b−a)ℓ1, and the abelianised
(60) gives γ = 3b, p = 1 + 6b; for ξ = λℓ0^{-1}ρ(λ)^{-1} (56), γ = 3b − 1, p = 6b − 1. So γ is computable from λ, b is well
defined on L0\Π̂0,3, case A means p ≡ 1 mod 3 and case B p ≡ −1 mod 3 (matching the role of p mod 3 noted on scan 64),
λ = 1 gives p = 1 (identity) and p = −1, and the supplementary condition becomes 1 + 6b ∈ Ẑ* (resp. 6b − 1 ∈ Ẑ*), a real
restriction since no rational integer other than ±1 is a unit of Ẑ.

**S3. Scans 77–79. Own idea (a literature route).** Four statements used or assumed on these pages — Π̂0,3^{σ∞} = {1}
(margin of scan 79), the injectivity NB (scan 77), the two-class assumption (scan 78) and the existence half of (59) — all
follow from the theorems on torsion in free profinite products (finite subgroups conjugate into a factor; centralisers of
torsion elements finite), applied to Ŝ+0,3 = Ẑ/2 ⨿ Ẑ/3 with its quotient 𝔖3. Those theorems postdate 1981 (Herfort–Ribes
1985; the profinite Bass–Serre theory of Zalesskii–Melnikov, to check), so on these pages they are working assumptions; a
write-up should cite the exact statements.
