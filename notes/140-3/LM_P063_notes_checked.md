**R11. Scan 44. Kind: claim without proof.** Verdict: corrected (reading). Reason: π̃′ is called "le groupe fondamental cat." of E∖₂E (changes, row 14), where the first pass left "cat./ext." open.

**S02. Scan 43. Kind: own idea (proof sketch).** (Claude.) In the topological model the extension (58) is the orbifold fundamental group of (E∖{0})/±1, a sphere with one puncture and three cone points of order 2, i.e. the free product ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/2. Splittings are involutions mapping onto the generator of ℤ/2ℤ; by the Kurosh subgroup theorem every finite subgroup of a free product is conjugate into a factor, and the three factors are pairwise non-conjugate (they differ in the abelianisation), so there are exactly three classes. ℰ-conjugacy and π′-conjugacy of a splitting agree, since conjugating by the splitting involution fixes it. The profinite version would need the analogue for free profinite products (torsion is conjugate into a factor, Herfort–Ribes); to check.

**S03. Scans 41, 45–46. Kind: claim check.** (Claude.) The corrected "effectif" fits (55). An object of C^{ext′}_{1,1} has automorphism group Gl(2,ℤ)/±1 = PGL(2,ℤ), an extension of a group of order 12 by the free group Γ(2)/±1 of rank 2. An effective group with loops of type (0,3) has as automorphism group an extension of Out_lac (𝔖₃ × ℤ/2, order 12) by its inner automorphisms, again free of rank 2, while an exterior one has a finite automorphism group of order 12. So the target C₀,₃ of (55) is naturally the groupoid of effective groups of type (0,3), as π = L(π′) on scan 45 is "effectif"; this is a consistency check, not a proof, to compare with the definition of C₀,₃ on scan 40.

**S04. Scans 44–45. Kind: claim check.** (Claude.) The topological statements of scans 44–45 check out: x ↦ −x fixes exactly the four points of order 2 of E, so G acts freely on E∖₂E (Euler characteristic −4), and the quotient V of (61) has Euler characteristic −2 with four ends, a sphere with four holes, i.e. of type (0,4) as stated.

**S05. Scan 46. Kind: unpursued implication.** (Claude.) The kernel π̃′ of (66) is the fundamental group of the double cover of V branched at all four punctures, which is E∖₂E again: Legendre's correspondence is here the classical one between an elliptic curve with its four points of order 2 and P¹ with four marked points, through E → E/±1. The text uses this only through group theory; making the identification of π̃′ with the π̃′ of scan 44 explicit would show that the two constructions (forward on scans 43–45, reverse on scans 45–47) are inverse up to the gerbe of R19.
