## 2. Table of doubts

**Location convention.** Approximate manuscript lines count principal horizontal prose/formula rows from the top of each image, including canceled rows but excluding pagination, footer text, underbrace labels, and separate marginal notes. Interlinear and marginal material is located relative to its principal anchor; dense annotation stacks are identified separately. “TeX” gives the exact line in `LM_P066_transcription.tex` as an additional locator, not a claim about manuscript lineation. Different words on one TeX line can occupy several manuscript rows.

**Confidence convention.** `medium` means a plausible reading supported by visible strokes but with unresolved letters, endings, punctuation, or attachment; `low` means materially ambiguous or unrecoverable ink. `high` is used only for the separately listed readings resolved by reinspection. Counts inside `\illegible{n}` are approximate; in mathematical scratchwork one unit can be a compact symbol cluster rather than a prose word. Repeated alphabet doubts are grouped by scan below, while their individual locations remain explicitly marked in the transcription. No alternative is inferred merely because it would make an equation work.

This is a conservative provisional diplomatic reading, not a claim that every surviving stroke has been deciphered. The larger main formulas are usually clearer than the rapid prose, but small indices, primes, exponents, and overwritten equation numbers can remain decisive uncertainties. Scan 71 is particularly faint; the lower prose of scan 72 and the interlinear attachments on both pages are also insecure. In scan 73 the broad cancellation, nested calculation labels, small marginal reformulations, and clipped final formula make both reading and attachment difficult. The canceled corollary on scan 76 has further local cancellations inside it and visibly problematic parentheses; these were not algebraically repaired.

The recurrent ornate group capital and the Greek `\xi`/`\zeta` family remain the largest global notation uncertainties. The diagonal marginal calculations on scan 75 were inspected after rotation. The slanting marginal words on scan 79 and the compressed margin beside (C) on scan 80 are less secure than their neighboring main equations. The possible subscript in scan 72's last proof line, the first index in boxed (46), the repeated 56/57 numbering, and the possible star on the range of `\gamma'` have all been preserved as doubts even where a mathematical reconstruction might prefer one choice.

Each page was read and reread against its transcription, with independent visual review. The LaTeX fragment was checked for balanced structure and compiled inside a temporary wrapper providing the requested editorial macros; the wrapper and its PDF are not part of the deliverable. Such a syntax check validates the encoding, not the correctness of every paleographic reading. ## 5. Research notes — English, separate from the transcription

These notes identify questions for reconstruction and further study; they do not amend the diplomatic transcription. They use only the supplied extract, do not claim historical novelty, and do not establish whether a point is resolved elsewhere in the manuscript or literature. References below are to the supplied **scan numbers**, not the archive footer numbers. The provisional symbol $\xi$ in these notes does not resolve the transcription's $\xi/\zeta$ doubt, and no identification of the uncertain large group glyph is presupposed.

### 1. The normalizer statement behind the conjugator parameter

**Scan 71, (39)–(41), and scan 72, the calculation after (42). Kind: unproved claim.** The assertion that “$g_0$ est déterminé mod $L_0$” is accompanied by the use of a normalizer identity of the form $N_{\widehat{\Pi}_{0,3}}(L_1)=L_1$, which the following page invokes again without proving it in this extract. A reconstruction must identify the exact profinite subgroups and establish the required normalizer statement, keeping it distinct from a statement about the normalizer of one chosen element (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 71–72).

### 2. Independence from the representative of the right coset

**Scans 71–72, (40) and its following paragraph; scan 75, a), before (48). Kind: unproved claim.** The first passage calls $\gamma$ “bien déterminé” and says that $g$ “ne dépend en effet que de $g_0$ mod $L_0$”; the summary later describes the relation as “invariante par remplacement de $g_0$ par $g_0l_0^\lambda$.” These are related but distinct well-definedness checks: one concerns the reconstructed element and exponent, and the other concerns the equation imposed on a representative. They should be verified by substitution using the earlier action formulas, preserving the order of all factors and the side on which $l_0^\lambda$ acts (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 71–72, 75).

### 3. Inclusion, equality, and the exponent of a procyclic subgroup

**Scan 73, (43) and the surviving calculation below it; scans 74 and 79–80, the multiplier condition. Kind: own idea. Confidence: high as a conditional check; reason: the image of a procyclic generator under a power map is explicit.** The inclusion in (43), rather than an equality silently substituted for it, should be compared with the calculation giving the exponent $2\gamma+1$. If the indicated subgroup is isomorphic to $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, that power map has the entire subgroup as image precisely when the exponent is a unit, so the later requirement $p=2\gamma+1\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{*}$ should be tracked separately from the inclusion. This is a proposed consistency check on the formulation, not a correction to the canceled or surviving calculations (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 73–74, 79–80).

### 4. A condition declared redundant

**Scan 74, final sentence after (46). Kind: unproved claim.** The text says that an earlier condition is “superflue” and “automatiquement vérifiée,” but its handwritten reference remains uncertain between (9) and (7). Locating that exact earlier condition is therefore a prerequisite to proving the claimed redundancy. The proof must then derive it from the stated hypotheses without quietly adding a generation or automorphism assumption (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 74).

### 5. Uniqueness of the normalized lifting

**Scan 75, opening Théorème. Kind: unproved claim.** The theorem says that the exterior automorphism “se relève de façon unique” subject to the stated normalizations. A proof needs both existence of a representative with those normalizations and a determination of the inner automorphisms preserving them; the qualifier inserted after “autom. ext.” is itself a doubtful reading and must be settled before formalizing the theorem. This summary page does not supply that argument afresh (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 75).

### 6. The separate role of topological generation

**Scan 75, b), recalled (40), and the final NB. Kind: unpursued implication.** The requirement that $\sigma_\infty$ and $g\rho$ “engendrent top.” is stated separately from the three-factor relation. A reconstruction should accordingly distinguish preservation of a presentation, continuity of the resulting map, and the step that makes the map an automorphism. In particular, the exact structural hypothesis converting topological generation into the advertised automorphism conclusion should be made explicit (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 75).

### 7. The canceled corollary and its parameter relation

**Scan 76, canceled Corollaire, references (18), (21), and (38), and the adjacent lower margin. Kind: unproved claim in a canceled draft.** The corollary asserts an equivalence through “il f. et s.” and ends with “où nécessairement $\alpha=\beta+\gamma$”; the adjacent margin appears to assert that the parameters are uniquely determined. The whole corollary is crossed out, and its inserted commuting qualifier is uncertain between “extérieurement” and “strictement,” so it must be treated as an abandoned formulation, not cited as an accepted theorem. Verification would require resolving that qualifier and checking the equivalence, the exponent relation, and parameter uniqueness separately against the earlier definitions (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 76).

### 8. Freeness of the displayed twisted action

**Scan 77, (53)–(54) and the lower-right NB. Kind: unproved claim.** The note says that “$\lambda\mapsto{}^\lambda\xi$ est injectif” for the displayed action. A proof must show that a stabilizing element is trivial; under the displayed conjugation interpretation, this amounts to identifying the intersection of the kernel group with the centralizer of $\xi\rho$. The action formula is supplied, but that centralizer argument is not supplied on this page (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 77).

### 9. A nonabelian cocycle formulation

**Scan 77, (51)–(54). Kind: own idea. Confidence: high as a proposed formulation, not as a theorem about these groups; reason: both the norm equation and the twisted action are explicitly displayed.** The equation $\xi\rho(\xi)\rho^2(\xi)=1$ and the transformation $\xi\mapsto\lambda\xi\rho(\lambda)^{-1}$ suggest organizing the solutions as nonabelian cocycles modulo their usual equivalence. Before identifying the orbit set with a continuous $H^1$ set, one must verify the precise acting group, its action and topology, and the convention relating cocycles to lifts of the quotient generator (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 77).

### 10. The expressly provisional two-class classification

**Scan 78, opening paragraph. Kind: conjecture / explicit provisional assumption.** Grothendieck writes “Admettons (quitte à le vérifier par la suite)” before asserting exactly two conjugacy classes. The subsequent representatives and formulas therefore rest on an assumption explicitly left for later verification in this passage. A proof needs both exhaustion of the classes and their distinction in the stated profinite setting; a classification in a discrete group would not by itself settle that task (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 78).

### 11. Comparing the two proposed orbit formulas

**Scan 78, (55)–(56) and the bracketed calculation following (56). Kind: unpursued implication.** Conditional on the preceding classification, the page describes the solutions by two formulas “de la forme” involving a twisted conjugation. The calculation moves between representatives involving $l_1^{-1}$ and $l_0^{-1}$, making it useful to check the exact conjugating element and whether the resulting two families are disjoint. That comparison must retain the source's uncertain small indices and canceled calculation rather than using the proposed classification to resolve their transcription (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 78).

### 12. Uniqueness for the equation defining $g_0$

**Scan 79, paragraph after (58) and left margin. Kind: unproved claim.** For fixed $h_0$, the text states that there is “au plus un seul $g_0$,” and the margin records $\widehat{\Pi}_{0,3}^{\sigma_\infty}=\{1\}$. If two solutions of $\sigma_\infty(g_0)^{-1}g_0=h_0$ exist, their quotient is fixed by $\sigma_\infty$, so the marginal fixed-subgroup assertion gives the advertised uniqueness once that assertion is established. This concerns uniqueness only and does not establish existence (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 79).

### 13. Existence requires an additional norm-to-coboundary argument

**Scan 79, (58)–(59). Kind: unproved claim.** The same paragraph says that such a $g_0$ “existe ssi” the displayed norm-one condition holds. Necessity can be checked directly from the defining equation and the involution, whereas sufficiency requires every element satisfying the norm condition to arise as $\sigma_\infty(g_0)^{-1}g_0$. That is an additional assertion, not a consequence of the trivial fixed subgroup used for uniqueness, and no proof of it is given on this page (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 79).

### 14. Uniqueness of the profinite exponent

**Scan 79, Corollaire following (60). Kind: unproved claim.** For fixed $\lambda$, and hence fixed $\xi$, the text asserts “il existe au plus un $\gamma$” satisfying $\sigma_\infty(\xi)=l_1^\gamma\xi^{-1}l_0^\gamma$. Proving this requires an explicit property of the two indicated procyclic subgroups or a suitable quotient calculation in which their images are known. The assertion should be kept distinct from the existence question: the page does not say that every prescribed $\lambda$ admits such an exponent (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 79).

### 15. Testing the inverse-multiplier notation

**Scan 79, last parenthesis. Kind: own idea. Confidence: high for the algebra and low for the doubtful asterisk in the handwriting; reason: the proposed inverse relation can be expanded directly.** The identity $(2\gamma+1)(2\gamma'+1)-1=2(2\gamma\gamma'+\gamma+\gamma')$ provides a direct check on the last displayed relation. In particular, the case $\gamma=0$ forces $\gamma'=0$ in the displayed equation $2\gamma\gamma'+\gamma+\gamma'=0$, so a possible extra unit requirement on $\gamma'$ itself deserves checking separately from the unit requirement on $2\gamma+1$. This diagnostic does not alter the explicitly doubtful transcription of the superscript (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 79).

### 16. Descent to a left-coset parameter

**Scan 80, opening NB. Kind: unpursued implication.** The substitution “$\lambda'=l_0^\alpha\lambda$” is accompanied by formulas for the corresponding changes of $\xi$, $h_0$, and $g_0$, and a statement that (60) remains equivalent. These formulas provide the mechanism for the left-coset parameter appearing in the preceding corollary. Checking the descent means verifying all the transformations with their stated sides of multiplication, including the earlier right-coset freedom of $g_0$, rather than identifying the two quotient conventions by notation alone (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 79–80).

### 17. What remains in the final parametrization procedure

**Scan 80, steps 1°–2° and (A)–(C). Kind: unproved claim / proposed parametrization.** The text calls the procedure “la démarche la plus claire”: choose a solution of (A), then require an exponent satisfying (C) and the unit condition on $2\gamma+1$. The selection ends with restrictions on which candidates are retained, rather than a criterion deciding their existence for every initial candidate. Completing the argument would require identifying the admissible candidates and establishing that reconstruction has every automorphism property asserted earlier (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 80).

### 18. Finite-quotient obstruction tests

**Scan 80, (A)–(C). Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium as a research strategy; reason: the two displayed equations can be pushed to finite quotients while keeping factor order.** One could first test the order-three norm equation and the involution-compatibility equation in finite quotients on which the relevant actions descend, retaining the same candidate data under transition maps. A failed finite test would obstruct that proposed candidate, but successful finite tests alone would not prove a coherent profinite solution, uniqueness, or the final automorphism assertion. This is a suggested diagnostic construction, not a claimed result (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 80).
