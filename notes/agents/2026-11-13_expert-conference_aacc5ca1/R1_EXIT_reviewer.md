## 1. What E.10–E.13 mean for exit paths: five statements

**A. The limit of sheaf topoi sees only the intersection (proved here; contains E.11).** Let X be a space, K ⊂ X
closed, V_n ⊃ K open and decreasing with ⋂ cl(V_n) = K, and Z ⊂ X closed. Then the 2-limit of the topoi
Sh(V_n ∖ Z) along the inclusions is Sh(K ∖ Z). So lim Sh(V_n) = Sh(K), and lim Sh(V_n ∖ Z) is the empty topos as
soon as K ⊂ Z.
*Proof.* Localic topoi are closed under limits (the localic reflection is a left adjoint; Johnstone, not opened), so
the limit is the locale with frame O(V₁ ∖ Z)/θ, θ the frame congruence generated by U ~ U ∩ V_n. If U ∩ K = ∅,
every point of U has an open neighbourhood W ⊂ U missing some V_n, so W θ ∅, and U = ⋁W θ ∅. If U ∩ K = U′ ∩ K
then U ∪ U′ = U ∪ (U′ ∖ K) θ U, and symmetrically, so U θ U′. Conversely U ↦ U ∩ K is a frame map onto O(K ∖ Z)
that respects the generators. ∎
E.11 is X = C, K = Z = {0}; its proof is correct. Two consequences it does not state: the germ of the full
neighbourhoods is, as a limit topos, just Sh(K); and the statement is general, nothing is special to a disc.

**B. With compact stages the limit is right (proved here).** For closed N_n decreasing to K, lim Sh(N_n) = Sh(K)
(a meet of closed sublocales is the closed sublocale of the union of the complements). Take closed polydiscs N_n at
x adapted to the branches. Then lim Sh(τ⁻¹N_n) = Sh(τ⁻¹(x)), and τ⁻¹(x) = (S¹)^{I(x)}. At each stage
N_n ∖ D = ((0,ε]×S¹)^r × Δ̄^{d−r} ⊂ ([0,ε]×S¹)^r × Δ̄^{d−r} = τ⁻¹N_n is a homotopy equivalence and an equivalence on
locally constant sheaves. So the towers (Sh(N_n ∖ D)) and (Sh(τ⁻¹N_n)) agree stagewise in shape and in coverings;
the first has empty limit, the second has limit Sh of the torus of tangent rays. **The 2-limit of topoi is not
invariant under stagewise shape equivalence**; this is what E.11 exhibits.

**C. E.10 is the lattice of P1b1.** E.10 (Proved; its polar-coordinate proof is correct, and so is its normal
crossings form): 2-colim_n Cov(V_n ∖ D) = Λ_x-Set, and the fibre functors given by tangent rays form the fundamental
groupoid of the circle of rays. In my notation: the covering germ is Fun(Π₁(τ⁻¹x), Set); a tangent ray is a point
of τ⁻¹(x); the link of P1a1 from x to U is τ⁻¹(x); the mapping space Map(x, u) is the homotopy fibre of
τ⁻¹(x) → U; restriction of coverings of U to the germ is μ_x^*.
*Reformulation of criterion (ii) of P1b1 (proved here).* μ_x is injective ⟺ BΛ_x → BΠ₁(U) is a local
homeomorphism of topoi ⟺ every covering germ at x is a subobject of the germ of a covering of U. Profinitely:
T^{I(x)} → π̂₁(U) is injective ⟺ every finite covering germ at x is a sub-germ of a finite étale covering of U.
(If H ≤ G, an H-set E embeds in the restriction of G ×_H E; an open subgroup of a closed subgroup is the trace of
an open subgroup; if the kernel is not trivial it acts trivially on every restriction and not on a suitable
H-set.) This states his 1°) of 149, scan 130, in the language of coverings of germs.

**D. The category of sheaf germs is what enters the gluing, and it is not a topos (proved here).** Put
G_x = 2-colim_n Sh(V_n ∖ D). The Artin gluing functor is i_x^* j_* F = colim_n F(V_n ∖ D): global sections of the
germ. It factors through G_x; no limit topos is involved. For a punctured disc G_x is not a Grothendieck topos:
its subobjects of 1 are the germs of open sets, and this lattice is not complete. (Take disjoint open sets B_k,
each a union of small discs accumulating at 0, the whole family of discs locally finite in D*. If [W] were the
join of the [B_k], remove from W one closed disc of each B_k, at radii tending to 0: a strictly smaller upper
bound.) E.13's example on {0} ∪ {1/n} (germs of morphisms of infinite coverings are not uniform) is the same
phenomenon. The locally constant objects of G_x are the Λ_x-sets of C.

**E. In exodromy terms the germ is a comma category.** For F locally constant on U with values in sets or
complexes, (Rj_*F)_x = colim_n RΓ(V_n ∖ D, F) = RΓ((S¹)^{I(x)}, F) = RΓ(Λ_x, F_u), proved directly from the
polydiscs. This is the right Kan extension along Π_∞(U) → Exit(X, D): the limit over the comma category x ↓ U,
which is Π_∞(τ⁻¹x). For constructible sheaves, 2-colim_n Cons(V_n) = Fun(Exit(X)_{x/}, Set) (known for conical
stratifications, Lurie HA A.6–A.9, not opened; for a disc it reads F₀ → F_u^Z). So on the constructible side
"the germ at x" is the under-category of x, and its punctured part is the link.

## 2. My reading of the intake

- **I2: agree, and extend.** "Take the locally constant objects stage by stage, never the limit topos first" is
  right; A gives it for every K and Z, and B and D say what replaces the limit: a compact model, or the category
  of germs with its sections functor.
- **I2, coherence: agree for the étale case, and add the analytic counterpart.** Coherence is the reason on the
  étale side (SGA 4 VI 8.7, VII 5.7; not opened by me either). On the analytic side compactness does the same
  work (B). One precision for candidate P2: the limit topos is BẐ(1) only for a point of a curve, where the
  punctured strict henselization is the spectrum of a field. At a normal crossings point of dimension ≥ 2 the
  limit is the étale topos of the scheme X_(x̄) ×_X U, of positive dimension; its coverings are the finite
  continuous T^{I(x)}-sets in characteristic 0 (Abhyankar; SGA 1 XIII 5, not opened), and it is not a classifying
  topos: its terminal object has proper non-zero subobjects, the open subschemes.
- **A route I checked and that fails: the oriented product.** The intake asks whether the link could be the
  evanescent topos x ⃖×_X U. For a regular (for instance Hausdorff locally compact) space X it is empty. Proof:
  a 2-cell between two maps of a topos to Sh(X) gives p₂^*(W) → (const. stalk at x of W) or the reverse for every
  open W; with W ⊂ U of closure missing x the first forces p₂^*(W) = 0, and these W cover U; with W ∋ x the second
  forces p₂ to factor through every V_n ∖ x, and A applies. On the étale side the oriented product is the Milnor
  tube X_(x̄) ×_X U (known, not opened).
- **I5, "L E.11: no operator and no class, degree zero": I disagree on the chain level.** See §4 (d): on singular
  chains the germ is R lim = lim¹[−1], and nothing else; it is the same construction as F.15's Z₂/Z.
- **P3, "the two-level principle as a finality statement; neither return touches this": EXIT_ONE_TYPE has it.**
  P5b1: for every stable (g, n) the homotopy fibre of the inclusion of the thickened strata of dimension ≤ k into
  M_{g,n} is the realization of the poset P_k of multicurves with d − k or more components. P5c1: |P₁| is connected
  and |P₂| simply connected in every genus. P5b2: in genus 0, |P_k| is (k − 1)-connected for every k. P5b4: for
  (2, 1) and k = 3 it is a wedge of 2-spheres. I followed all four proofs (Harer's sphere theorem is cited, not
  opened). So the inclusion of the levels ≤ 2 has simply connected fibres, in every genus, and the level-3 inclusion
  for (2, 1) does not have 2-connected ones. This is the discrete statement; the profinite one is open (EXIT_TOWER
  Part D (c)). On the index category: the exit category of M̄_{g,n} has classifying space the stack M̄_{g,n} (P1c1),
  which is not weakly contractible; so it is not aspherical, hence not total, and constant coefficients already
  have T₂(Z) = H₂(M̄_{0,4}) = Z over the four-object exit category of (P¹, {0, 1, ∞}) (P1e2).

## 4. Floor question 4: the central statement, in the exit-path framework

(a) **Confirmed: the Remords.** Underived object: the homotopy category h𝓔, the glued 1-topos. Class:
ker μ_x = im(∂ : π₂(X^log, τ⁻¹x) → π₁(τ⁻¹x)) (exact sequence of the pair; X^log ≃ U). h𝓔 sees only the image of
μ_x: Hom(x, u) = π₁(U)/μ_x(Λ_x). The ∞-category 𝓔 absorbs it: for aspherical U the components of Map(x, u) are
K(ker μ_x, 1), and B𝓔 = X always (P1c1, P1d1). The class is the boundary value of a relative class one dimension up; for (P¹, ∞) the
disc is C and the class is [S²], the suspension of the dead meridian. P3c-Mori's affine lines in strata are
holomorphic instances.
(b) **Sharpened: the degree.** P1d3: B𝓔 → B(h𝓔) is 2-connected, and if the components of the mapping spaces are
(r − 1)-connected the first difference is π_{r+1} of the fibre, the cokernel of d₀ − d₁ + d₂ on H_r of the mapping
spaces; Q_m × C gives r + 1 = m for any m. So "first-derived" must read "lowest degree the mapping spaces allow".
This agrees with the intake's invariant form (the cone, in the lowest degree allowed).
(c) **Confirmed: the levels, and π₀ of flags.** Corollaire 2 (P5a1): truncating the flags at depth 2 leaves the
class of the hexagon in π₁ of the index nerve (Z⁴ against Z³); the triple flags, its 2-cells, absorb it. In general
depth r leaves [∂Δ^r] in degree r − 1 (P5a2). P4a1: π₀ does not commute with the fibre products of the flag
category; the fibre of π₀(A ×_B C) → π₀A ×_{π₀B} π₀C is π₁(A)∖π₁(B)/π₁(C), and the groupoid-valued flags absorb it
(P4a2).
(d) **E.11: two forms (proved here).** For topoi the failure is in degree zero: the limit is empty. For singular
chains it is a first derived limit and nothing else: lim_n C_•(V_n ∖ D) = 0 (no simplex lies in every V_n), so
1 − shift is injective on ∏_n C_•(V_n ∖ D) and
R lim_n C_•(V_n ∖ D) ≃ (lim¹_n C_•(V_n ∖ D))[−1], with H_{j+1}(lim¹ C_•) = H_j((S¹)^{I(x)}).
For a punctured disc H₁(lim¹ C_•) = Z is generated by a ray into the puncture (a locally finite 1-chain modulo
finite chains) and H₂(lim¹ C_•) = Z by the germ of the punctured disc, whose boundary is the meridian. So a point
of the germ is a ray, the tangential base point, and the meridian bounds a 2-chain: one dimension up, twice.
It is the cell "directed index, non-compact coefficients" of the shape map's table: a decreasing tower of
subgroups with zero intersection, exactly as 2ⁿZ ⊂ Z in F.15. On the cochain side i^*Rj_* is a filtered colimit,
which is exact: no class. With compact stages (B) the limit commutes.
(e) **Another form needed: discrete against profinite.** H^q(Ĝ, M) → H^q(G, M) is bijective for q ≤ 1 and injective
for q = 2 (Serre, CG I.2.6, not opened), so the first place where completion can fail is the cokernel in degree 2.
In my field this is row H.9 (goodness of the stabilizers of chains) and the profinite form of P1c1 (b).

## 5. For EXIT_TOWER and LM5

- **EXIT_TOWER, Part A.** His M*_{G,G′} ("the germ of M_G in M̂_{G′} with the divisors at infinity removed") and
  S_AΠM_G are fundamental groupoids of punctured germs. By A they cannot be defined through a limit topos. Three
  definitions work and should be proved equal, for stacks: covering germs (E.10), the open face over M_G of the
  real oriented blow-up (P1a2), the transporter Stab(σ, τ)/Δ_τ (Lead 1).
- **EXIT_TOWER, Part C, Lead 2.** E.12's exceptional cases compute the map of lattices of Lead 2 on the contracted
  component, and they are instances of P1b3. For the sphere with one marked point a full rotation winds the tangent circle once
  (Z → Z, the lattice element dies: a contracted tail goes to 0); with two marked points it winds by (1, −1)
  (kernel of (u, v) ↦ u + v: the two edges of a contracted bridge go to one edge). These are the transgressions
  π₂ → Z^I of P1b3, the Chern numbers of the tangent lines on the generator of π₂ of the non-aspherical stacks
  M_{0,1}, M_{0,2}. For hyperbolic types E.12's "weak contractibility kills the first term" is P1b3 with
  aspherical stratum.
- **EXIT_TOWER, Part E (b).** E.10: on finite coverings the transports between tangential fibre functors form
  Ẑ-torsors containing the dense Z-torsors of geometric paths; orientation reversal acts by −1. The list of
  decorations of the completed tower should say whether this integral structure on inertia is imposed.
- **LM5, Part F (d).** As limit topoi the germ 𝒱_{D′,Y} is Sh(D′) and the punctured germ is empty (A). The
  definitions that carry his theorems are in the table of §3.
- **LM5, Part F (f).** (243), Π₁^arcs P → Π₁^rev 𝒱*: E.10 is its proof at a point of a curve and in the local
  normal crossings model; E.13 gives the hypothesis for infinite coverings (a cofinal system of connected
  neighbourhoods).
- **LM5, Part F (g).** His four pieces for (E¹, 0): as limit topoi D = P and D* = ∅, so (266) C ≃ C* ⊔_{D*} D is
  a statement about shapes (C* ← S¹ → point), or about the X^log model, or the Artin gluing of Set and Sh(C*)
  along Γ_germ.
