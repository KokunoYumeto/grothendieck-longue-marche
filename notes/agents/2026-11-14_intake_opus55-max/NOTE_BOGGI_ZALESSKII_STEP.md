## The two papers

- Marco Boggi, *A congruence subgroup property for symmetric mapping class groups*, São Paulo Journal of Mathematical
  Sciences 20 (2026), Article 35, published 23 September 2026, doi:10.1007/s40863-026-00558-y; arXiv:2408.12486 (v2).
  The step is in Proposition 2.4 (`centgood` in the TeX). Proposition 2.4 is used in the general case of Theorem A,
  Lemma 3.2 and the proof of Theorem C; Theorem C(iii) states the centralizer equality below.
- Marco Boggi and Pavel Zalesskii, *Finite subgroups of the profinite completion of good groups*, Bulletin of the London
  Mathematical Society 57 (2025), 236–255, doi:10.1112/blms.13193; arXiv:2406.08639 (v2). The step is in the proof of
  Theorem 5.1(iii).

## The step

For a finite subgroup `G = ⟨x_1, …, x_k⟩` of a mapping class group `M`, both proofs end with the displayed equality

    ⋂_i closure(C_M(x_i)) = closure(⋂_i C_M(x_i)) = closure(C_M(G))   in the profinite completion M̂.

The results before it show that the left side is the centralizer of `G` in `M̂` (from the centralizers of elements of
prime order). The inclusion "⊇" always holds; the equality "⊆" is the step for which the run found no justification in
either text. It is the case of the statement for nonsolvable `G` that matters: the first open case is a geometric `A_5`
in the genus-0 mapping class group `Γ⁺_{0,ν}`, where the claimed equality is `C_{M̂}(A_5) = closure(C_M(A_5))`.

## The two proofs, read at the step (14 November 2026, ~11:3x UTC)

Read from the arXiv TeX sources the LM4 run was given (`literature/boggi-2408.12486/src_arXiv_2408.12486/CongSym.tex`
and `papers/boggi_zalesskii_finite_subgroups_arxiv/src_arXiv_2406.08639/main.tex` in the LM4 background zip).

- **Boggi, Proposition 2.4** (`centgood`): "Let G be a good, residually finite group of finite virtual cohomological
  type ... Then, for every finite subgroup H of G, Z_Ĝ(ι(H)) = closure(ι(Z_G(H)))." Proof, in full: by
  Boggi–Zalesskii, Theorem A(iii), Z_Ĝ(x) = closure(Z_G(x)) for every p-torsion element x; H is generated by
  p-torsion elements x_1, …, x_k (for possibly different p); "We then have:
  Z_Ĝ(H) = ⋂ Z_Ĝ(x_i) = ⋂ closure(Z_G(x_i)) = closure(Z_G(H))."
- **Boggi–Zalesskii, Theorem 5.1(iii)** (= Theorem C(iii), Corollary D(iii)): "For any C-subgroup H of G, there holds
  C_{G_C}(H) = closure(C_G(H))." Proof, in full: for each torsion element x, C(x) = closure(C_G(x)) (from their
  Lemma 2.2 and item (ii)); "For any C-subgroup H of G of order k, we then have:
  C(H) = ⋂_{x∈H} C(x) = ⋂_{x∈H} closure(C_G(x)) = closure(C_G(H))."
- **Boggi, Lemma 3.2** (`Gcentconf`, in the proof of Theorem A, general case) applies Proposition 2.4 to the group
  Π[r]·G and the finite group G.

## Analysis of the step (by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max, 14 November 2026; mine, checked by no one)

1. **The step is the conclusion.** In both chains the first two equalities are correct: a centralizer of a
   generating set is the intersection of the centralizers, and the per-element results give the second. So the left
   side of the third equality is already Z_Ĝ(H), and the third equality asserts exactly Z_Ĝ(H) = closure(Z_G(H)),
   the statement being proved. In general only "closure(⋂ A_i) ⊆ ⋂ closure(A_i)" holds. Neither paper gives an
   argument for the reverse inclusion. This is a leap in the proof, not a matter of exposition.
2. **What it amounts to.** For a cofinal family of torsion-free normal subgroups G_n of finite index:
   - z ∈ G/G_n centralizes the image of H if and only if g ↦ z̃ g z̃⁻¹ g⁻¹ is a 1-cocycle of H in G_n;
   - z lifts to Z_G(H) exactly when that class is trivial in H¹(H, G_n), and to Z_Ĝ(H) exactly when it is trivial in
     H¹(H, Ĝ_n).
   So Z_Ĝ(H) = closure(Z_G(H)) holds if and only if, for every n, a complement to G_n in G_n·H that is conjugate to H
   in the completion is already conjugate to H by an element of G_n. This is hereditary conjugacy separability of H
   as a homomorphism (simultaneous conjugacy of its generators), the analogue for H of the authors' own Lemma 2.2. It
   is what a proof must supply.
3. **For solvable H it is true, by the authors' own lemmas.** Where N_Ĝ(H) = closure(N_G(H)) is known (Boggi–Zalesskii,
   Theorem 5.1(iii), second sentence, proved via Lemma 2.3 without the step), Z_Ĝ(H) is the kernel of the continuous
   map N_Ĝ(H) → Aut(H). It is therefore open in closure(N_G(H)), and an open subgroup of the closure of a dense
   subgroup D is the closure of its intersection with D. So Z_Ĝ(H) = closure(Z_G(H)). LM4 gave its own solvable proof
   for genus ≤ 2. Two caveats: Proposition 2.4 is stated with only "good, residually finite, finite virtual
   cohomological type"; without Boggi–Zalesskii's extra hypothesis (ii) on normalizers, even solvable H that are not
   p-groups need an argument there.
4. **Where it matters: nonsolvable H.**
   - For mapping class groups of genus ≤ 2 (Boggi, Theorem C(iii)), the only nonsolvable finite subgroups are the
     icosahedral A_5 in genus 0. In genus 2 the groups have order ≤ 48 < 60; in genus 1 they are (Z/a × Z/b) ⋊ C_k;
     in genus 0 they are the finite rotation groups.
   - So Theorem C(iii) is in question exactly for A_5 ≤ Γ_{0,n} whose puncture set is a union of A_5-orbits (sizes 12,
     20, 30, 60), as LM4 F.7 says.
   - Smallest case: n = 12, the icosahedron's vertices. The quotient S°/A_5 is a thrice-punctured sphere (the
     (2,3,5) triangle orbifold with the order-5 point punctured), so Z_Γ(A_5) is finite. The claim there is that
     **Z_{Γ̂_{0,12}}(A_5) is finite**.
   - It does not follow from the solvable subgroups. Z(A_5) = Z(A_4) ∩ Z(D_10), and Z_Γ(A_4) and Z_Γ(D_10) are both
     virtually free (finite-index subgroups of the mapping class group of a four-punctured sphere). So the question
     is whether the closures of two virtually free subgroups of Γ̂_{0,12}, whose discrete intersection is finite,
     meet only in a finite group. That is the step again, now for two explicit subgroups.
   - Galois elements of the field of definition do commute with A_5 in Out(π̂_1). As far as I can see they are not in
     Γ̂_{0,12}, by the faithfulness of the arithmetic monodromy of the configuration space (not checked against the
     literature), so they give no counterexample.
5. **Further reach.** Boggi's Lemma 3.2 uses Proposition 2.4 for G itself, so Theorem A's general case (ramified
   actions), and Corollary B with it, rest on the step whenever G is nonsolvable. One example is the congruence
   subgroup property for the centralizer of PSL(2,7) acting on the Klein quartic (genus 3, quotient of genus 0); the
   free case of Theorem A is unaffected. This should be checked against the published text before it is said
   anywhere.

## The statements, as conjectures, stated as expected to fail (user, 14 November 2026)

The user: "turn the gap into conjecture"; then "the icosahedron has properties that might actually contradict the
theorem. We should state the conjecture as we think it's false. To be safe." So the open case is recorded in its
negative form. That is a hunch, not a result. My analysis shows only that the published proofs do not cover the case.
The mechanism below is why the icosahedral case is where a failure would come from. No counterexample is known. Our
own work never uses the positive statements.

**Why the icosahedron (by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max; reasoning, nothing computed):**
- S° = P¹ minus the 12 vertices and the 50 points over the cone points of orders 2 and 3. The A_5-cover S° → P¹ ∖ {0,1,∞}
  is the icosahedral dessin, and the quotient of the 12-punctured sphere is the rigid (2,3,∞) triangle orbifold.
- The discrete centralizer of A_5 is finite only because that quotient has no moduli. The solvable subgroups all have
  quotients with moduli; that is why their case goes through.
- The profinite symmetries of the triangle-orbifold group Ĉ_2 ⨿ Ĉ_3 that preserve each inertia class with exponent 1
  are enormous; they include every GT̂ element (1, f): x ↦ x, y ↦ f⁻¹yf.
- Each of them lifts to an outer automorphism of π̂_1 of the 12-punctured sphere that commutes with A_5:
  - the twisted generating triple is again of type (2,3,5);
  - by the rigidity of that class triple in A_5 it differs from the original by an inner automorphism, so the kernel
    is preserved;
  - the inertia of the 50 filled points is preserved.
- The published claim says that none of these lifts is a limit of mapping classes, apart from the finite discrete
  centralizer. For the Galois ones this seems to hold (item 4 above). For anything else, in particular a GT̂ element
  not coming from Galois if there is one, nothing in either paper or in our work decides it. So the claim is tangled up
  with questions of the GT̂-versus-Galois kind.

- **Conjecture 2⁻ (expected failure of Boggi, Theorem C(iii), in its smallest open case).** For the icosahedral A_5 in
  the mapping class group of the sphere punctured at the 12 vertices of the icosahedron, the centralizer of A_5 in
  the profinite completion Γ̂_{0,12} is infinite. Hence Z_{Γ̂_{0,12}}(A_5) ≠ closure(Z_{Γ_{0,12}}(A_5)), since the
  right side is finite.
- **Conjecture 1⁻.** Boggi, Proposition 2.4, and Boggi–Zalesskii, Theorem 5.1(iii) (Corollary D(iii)), fail for
  nonsolvable finite subgroups. This follows from 2⁻: Γ_{0,12} is good, residually finite and of finite virtual
  cohomological type.
- **Conjecture 3 (left neutral).** Boggi, Theorem A, in the ramified nonsolvable case: whether the congruence
  subgroup property holds for Γ(S)^G, e.g. for PSL(2,7) on the Klein quartic. Its published proof rests on the step.

What would settle 2⁻ either way: decide, for the congruence levels Γ_{0,12}[m], whether every element of the finite
quotient that centralizes the image of A_5 and comes from Z_{Γ̂}(A_5) already comes from Z_Γ(A_5). Equivalently,
decide whether a complement to Γ[m] in Γ[m]·A_5 can be conjugate to A_5 in the completion without being conjugate in
Γ[m]. A counterexample is an element of the profinite centralizer that is not in the finite discrete one; the lifts
above are where to look first.

## Conjecture 2⁻, refined: the user's hunch in precise terms (14 November 2026, ~12:2x UTC)

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max at the user's request ("frame it in those terms ... a more refined
formulation ... or a particular instance"). It is a conjecture, not a result. The Longue Marche expert leans about 2:1
the other way.

**The setting.** A finite subgroup G of a genus-0 mapping class group Γ_{0,n} has a *rigid* quotient when S/G, with the
punctures taken as cusps, is a triangle orbifold. Then the configuration is an isolated orbifold point of the moduli
space, with isotropy G, and the discrete centralizer of G is finite. The rigid cases are:
- C_m and D_m (quotients (m,m,∞), (2,2,∞), and so on);
- A_4, S_4 and A_5, over the spherical triangle orbifolds (2,3,3), (2,3,4) and (2,3,5), with the vertices of the
  tetrahedron, octahedron and icosahedron punctured.

**The dividing line.** Over the spherical triangle orbifold (2,3,k), the Seifert fibration with finite π_1 is S³/2T,
S³/2O or S³/2I. Only S³/2I, the Poincaré sphere, is a homology sphere:
- 2T has abelianization Z/3 and 2O has Z/2, while 2I is perfect;
- equivalently, A_5 is the only nonsolvable rigid isotropy group in genus 0.
So among the isolated orbifold points of genus-0 moduli, the icosahedral point is the only one whose "link" is a
homology sphere, the link of the E8 singularity. It is also the only point where the published proofs fail.

**Conjecture 2⁻ (refined).** Let G ≤ Γ_{0,n} be finite with rigid quotient. Then the centralizer of G in the
profinite completion Γ̂_{0,n} equals the closure of its discrete centralizer (and so is finite) **if and only if G is
solvable**. In particular, for the icosahedral A_5 (the case over (2,3,5), tied to the Poincaré sphere and the E8
singularity) the profinite centralizer is **infinite**. The smallest instance is X(5), the 12-punctured sphere, where
the candidates are the lifts of the elements F of GT̂_0 with λ(F) ≡ ±1 mod 5 (EXPERT_ON_BOGGI_A5.md).
- The "if" direction is known: solvable G, by Boggi–Zalesskii's normalizer result (item 3 above), and by LM4's own
  route in genus ≤ 2.
- The content is the A_5 case.

**Why it is interesting, and how it ties back to the earlier hunch:**
- The Poincaré-sphere hunch of 12 November is settled. PRETOT A.5's 2-complex is the spine of the punctured Poincaré
  sphere (POINCARE_SPINE_CHECK.md, by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max). The Longue Marche expert gave a second route:
  Dehn filling of the trefoil complement, which is the unit tangent bundle of the modular orbifold.
- Both sides are the modular group at level 5: SL_2(F_5) = 2I for the Poincaré sphere, PSL_2(F_5) = A_5 for X(5).
- In the family Σ(2,3,5,…,p_n) of Seifert homology spheres over consecutive primes, every member is the link of a
  singularity, but only (2,3,5) is rigid and has finite π_1. The conjecture places the failure of profinite finiteness
  exactly where rigidity and perfectness meet. In the user's words: a point that "points in a single direction",
  treated as a singularity, is "not finite" profinitely.

**What would decide it:** the item in PUSH_ITEMS_20261114.md 2.4 and EXPERT_ON_BOGGI_A5.md (c). Lift a non-Galois
element of GT̂_0 with λ ≡ ±1 mod 5 to X(5) and decide whether it lies in the profinite mapping class group. A
counterexample is such an element, written out; a proof of finiteness refutes the conjecture.
