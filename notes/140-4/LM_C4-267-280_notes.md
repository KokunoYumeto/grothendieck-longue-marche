## 4. Legibility and editorial limitations

### Scans 267–270



Scan 267 has generally clear page structure but highly compressed prose endings and several overlapping corrections in (110). The alphabet of the local circle and punctured-disc symbols is not certain: the straight central stroke of `S` and extended baseline of `D` suggest blackboard forms, but the scan does not establish a typographic alphabet conclusively. The overwritten left member of (110) is the least trusted reading in this page; no mathematical correction has been substituted for it.

Scan 268 is relatively legible except for the blotted word before (111), the cancelled symbol after `F_1` in (114), and the retraced operators in (116). The apparent `G` in the subscript `Tors(G)` in (113), and the visible use of `F_2` in (115)–(116) after `G_1` in (114), have not been normalized. The operator lettering in (116) is retained with doubt because its heavy horizontal strokes may be retracing rather than independent cancellations.

Scan 269 has a clear definition and paragraph structure, but the category-definition line and the lower-right object of (117) contain overlapping letters. The proposed cancelled reading `couples` beneath `triples` is particularly tentative. The star-like mark on the first cover in the torsor sentence has been retained as a doubt rather than silently changed to a tilde.

Scan 270 is readable in its main prose, but several interlinear corrections affect the labels of (119) and the last line. The additional glyph in the second chosen triple, the sign between the two rows of (119), and the deleted last word of the page are the least reliable readings. The first two rows of (118) are enclosed by the visible brace, while `u_T` is below that brace; this layout is retained.

### Scans 271–274



The main formulas (121)–(123) and the two outgoing arrows of (122) are substantially legible. The least reliable areas are the deleted phrase at the end of the opening paragraph, the word before “(120)” below the diagram, and the several overlapping revisions above the first line of (123). The distinction between blackboard and calligraphic letters, the placement of primes, and the tiny subscript of E require checking against the physical manuscript.

This page is more legible than scan 271. Its two prose paragraphs are complete; the principal residual doubts concern the short cancelled forms, two participial endings, and the equals-like shorthand for “même”. Literal dots at the ends of both arguments are manuscript punctuation, not editorial omissions.

The heading, principal prose and all three numbered displays are readable. The most uncertain items are the short alias-connectors in (124)–(125) and the obliterated word after G. Particular care was taken to retain the manuscript's switches between I and S, its subscripted regularity notation, and the apparently surprising output in the last assignment.

The main argument, formulas (127)–(130), and the final reduction to a singleton are legible. The crossed paragraph between (129) and (130) is the least trustworthy passage in this four-page portion: several layers of writing, straight cancellations, and looping cancellation marks overlap. Tentative words are marked in place; two completely blackened short passages across scans 273–274 are the only places in this portion where word-count illegibility marks were necessary.

### Scans 275–277



Scan 275 is legible through most of the central proof sketch. The opening is heavily reworked: the family notation, the deleted “suite décr.”, the singular/plural endings, and the cancelled filter sentence are the least trustworthy readings. The lift notation in and just before (133) visibly lacks some stars appearing elsewhere; those stars have not been supplied. Blackboard-style `D` is used provisionally for the two-stroke manuscript glyph; this typographic identification is less secure than the identity of the letter.

The first projection subscript, the doubled drawing of the second arrow, and the cancelled target formulas are uncertain. One cancelled word after the local topology has no defensible complete reading even under enlargement; an explicit illegible marker is retained there.

## 5. Research notes — English, separate from the transcription

Locations refer to the supplied scans and equation numbers (Grothendieck, 1981). “Unproved claim” means that the indicated verification is not supplied at that location, not that the claim is unknown or absent from the wider manuscript. Own ideas are labelled with their confidence and reason; none is presented as new or established.

1. **Scan 267, text around (108)–(109); kind: unproved claim.** The functors are said to “se factorisent en des équivalences.” A proof at this point would need to establish the asserted passage to the groupoid completion, full faithfulness, and essential surjectivity for both covering categories; these checks are not supplied on this page. The remark that the functors are independent of the chosen universal covers should also be distinguished from independence of any chosen natural identifications.

2. **Scan 267, final paragraph; kind: unpursued implication.** The words “par la propriété univ.” reduce the particular geometric construction to a comparison of functors on connected `T`-groupoids. This suggests that the geometric independence statement may be isolated as an abstract lemma about torsor-valued functors; the following pages need to be checked for the extent to which they complete that reduction.

3. **Scan 267, (110) and final paragraph; kind: own idea; confidence: medium.** One could audit the claimed naturality by evaluating every displayed isomorphism on a chosen object and then on its automorphism group, keeping the side of the `T`-action explicit. The reason for this suggestion is that a representable torsor comparison can conceal an inverse or an opposite-action convention; it is a proposed verification procedure, not an assertion that an error occurs here.

4. **Scan 268, (113)–(116); kind: unpursued implication.** The assertion that the comparison functor is “essᵗ. constant” is given an argument through quasi-inverses and the torsor isomorphism in (116), rather than left as an unsupported claim. What remains worth checking separately is the precise meaning of essential constancy and the dependence of the resulting identification on the selected quasi-inverses; the text only needs an essentially constant functor at this step.

5. **Scan 268, (114)–(116); kind: own idea; confidence: high for the proposed check.** A careful rederivation should give distinct names to the two quasi-inverses and track them through both representable functors. This is prompted by the visible `G_1` / `F_2` switch, and is an editorial verification proposal, not a correction to the transcription or a claim that the underlying result fails.

6. **Scan 269, paragraph after the definition; kind: unproved claim.** The statement “Cette identification est compatible” with inclusions of arcs invokes compatibility of the `H(A)` descriptions across `A ⊂ A'`. A complete verification would compare the restriction maps on both section torsors and check that the comparison isomorphism commutes with them; this page asserts the compatibility without displaying that square.

7. **Scan 269, torsor sentence; kind: unproved claim.** The correlations are said to “forment un torseur sous `T_x`.” To make the claim explicit one must specify the action and verify nonemptiness, freeness, and transitivity, including how the identification of the deck groups is used. The result may rely on the preceding construction of `H`, so it should not be interpreted as an independent conjecture.
