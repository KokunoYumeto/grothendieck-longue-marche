# LM_C4-210-228: transcription notes

## Source, scope, and conventions

This package transcribes the nineteen supplied manuscript images, using their filename scan numbers 210–228. The handwritten archival folios are 209–227; they are retained as marginal text, while the printed Montpellier footer is excluded. The visible original manuscript page numbers 753–761 are recorded separately with `\mspage`. Source identification and date follow the supplied task description (Grothendieck, 1981).

Readings are diplomatic: abbreviated words, cancellations, insertions, underlining, incomplete sentences, and surprising mathematical expressions are retained. Alternatives in `\unsure{reading|alternative}` record uncertainty, not an emendation. No external transcription was used. Approximate locations below refer to the manuscript's handwritten lines or numbered formulas, not to lines of the LaTeX file.

## 3. Legibility and verification limits

Most large formulas and much of the running prose are readable, but the script is frequently reduced to connected strokes. The least secure passages are the diagonal note overlapping (41)–(42) on scan 210; the stacked deletions and insertions above (57) on scan 217; the revised prose following “misleading” on scan 220; and the dense cancellations on scans 225, 226, and 228. In these places, fluent French or a mathematically expected statement was not treated as evidence for a reading. The detailed doubts table should remain attached to any use of the transcription.

The angular isolated mark on scan 213 is reproduced as TikZ strokes without assigning it a word. The partial display label on scan 214 is left incomplete. In (72), the abandoned six-object graph, its cancellation strokes, and its replacement triangle are all retained; (74) also preserves the abandoned fragment before the square. The unusual direction in the prose before (6), the right-hand side of (69), and the denominator in (10) are retained as visible rather than reconciled with mathematical expectations. The distinction between live “la” and the following cancelled glyph on scan 225 was checked independently.

The fragment has no preamble or macro definitions, as requested. It uses the specified editorial macros, standard LaTeX/AMS mathematical commands, and TikZ/tikz-cd; the diagram separators use `ampersand replacement=\&` so they can occur inside `\diagram{…}`. Page order, group and environment balance, inventory coverage, and ZIP contents were checked structurally. A full typesetting run could not be completed because the available TeX executables lack their format files and package tree; no claim of successful compilation or rendered-layout verification is made.

## 4. Research notes (English)

These are reading notes on what the supplied pages leave open or take as a dependency. “Unproved claim” means not proved in this excerpt; an earlier or later manuscript passage may provide the proof. No item is asserted to be new, currently open, or settled in the literature. The explicitly labelled own ideas are proposed checks, and none changes the diplomatic text.

### Scans 210–214

**A1.** **Scan 210, diagonal NB; kind: conjecture.** The note speaks of a “meilleur choix” from which the others are obtained by composition with `n id_{T₀}`. The words leading into this claim are partially overlapped, so the precise scope of the expectation must be checked against the original and the earlier definition of the local structure. A proof would need to identify which equivalence relation allows a preferred primitive choice and which choices are actually allowed to vary. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 210).

**A2.** **Scan 210, last paragraph and reference (21), p. 747′; kind: unproved claim.** “On voit … que cet … homomorphisme est quasi-unipotent” is used to pass from the discrete map in (42) to the group-scheme map (43). The assertion expressly points back to (21); a complete verification would require that earlier criterion, including its hypotheses on Π₀ and on the chosen local homomorphism. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 210).

**A3.** **Scan 211, paragraph preceding (44); kind: unproved claim.** The non-degeneracy condition requires the preceding homomorphisms to be “tous non triviaux, donc … des mono.” The step from nontriviality to a monomorphism should be checked for the exact additive group scheme and category of group schemes being used, including the treatment of kernels and any finite-type assumptions. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 211).

**A4.** **Scan 211, after (45); kind: conjecture.** Grothendieck writes, “il me semble qu’on peut tjrs trouver un tel M”. The desired model must support the map from M to its points over the profinite integers, not merely have the prescribed rational fibre; a proof therefore needs to address this compatibility along with existence of an integral affine model. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 211).

**A5.** **Scan 212, parenthesis after (46); kind: conjecture.** The qualifications “sous réserve de représentabilité des quotients” and “je crois que pour Raynaud il n’y a jamais de pb” leave representability as an explicit dependency. The hypotheses under which all three quotients are represented should be separated from the existence of their schematic closures and from any additional smoothness or finite-type requirements. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 212).

**A6.** **Scan 212, (48)–(49); kind: unproved claim.** After assuming that the rational character extends integrally, the text says it “évidemment se factorise” through Γ. The precise assertion to verify is that the extended character is trivial on the schematic subgroup being divided out, rather than only on its rational fibre. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 212).

**A7.** **Scan 213, first paragraph; kind: conjecture.** “Il serait intéressant de dégager un th. d’existence … d’unicité” explicitly asks for an existence theorem, and perhaps a uniqueness theorem under stronger conditions, for a smooth integral model. The two displayed compatibility conditions must be retained in formulating the problem; the line displays M → M(ℤ), without adding a completion not written there. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 213).

**A8.** **Scan 213, paragraph beginning “Ici”; kind: conjecture.** The qualification “je présume” accompanies the prediction that the exterior maps (43) generally do not extend as they stand over ℤ. This invites explicit examples distinguishing rational one-parameter subgroup data from compatible integral maps, with the permitted changes of local parameter stated carefully. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 213).

**A9.** **Scan 213, centralizer paragraph and (50); kind: unproved claim.** The centralizer is declared “un groupe métabélien”, and the image of T₀ a “sous-groupe distingué d’indice fini”. A proof needs the particular local or L-structure hypotheses on Π₀; neither assertion should be silently inferred from the abstract definition of a centralizer alone. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 213).

**A10.** **Scans 213–214, (51)–(52); kind: unproved claim.** The homomorphism is said to factor through Env(L̃_i,0) and then through one of its smooth quotient group schemes; the manuscript refers to “§50, 4°, p. 658′ ff”. Verification requires the earlier definition and factorization property of Env, and the claim that it is the projective limit of the displayed quotients. The doubtful abbreviation before “unipotent” on scan 213 must be resolved before treating it as the exact hypothesis of that factorization. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 213–214).

**A11.** **Scan 214, paragraph after the unnumbered displays; kind: unproved claim.** The text states that if the restriction is a monomorphism for a fixed n, it ceases to be one for a distinct multiple n′. The dependence on the lattice nT₀ should be worked out explicitly, including what happens in residue characteristics dividing the ratio n′/n. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 214).

**A12.** **Scan 214, (53) and marginal note; kind: conjecture.** “Un cas particulièrement favorable” is the existence of an n for which (53) is a monomorphism, and the margin adds “cet n est alors unique”. The manuscript gives a consequence—smoothness of the schematic closure of the rational one-parameter subgroup—but these lines do not supply a proof or a complete criterion for that favorable case. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 214).

**A13.** **Scans 211–214; kind: own idea.** One possible investigation is to test the local maps in a concrete rational representation and compare integral lattices stable under their images. A change in the lattice may reveal separately the denominators obstructing extension of the local map and those obstructing extension of the character. **Confidence: medium** as a method of producing examples, because it addresses the two different compatibility requirements visible in (45), (48), and (53); it is not asserted to solve the model-existence problem. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 211–214).

**A14.** **Scan 214, (52)–(53); kind: own idea.** In a simple additive model, multiplication by an integer can have a different kernel on a special fibre from the kernel on the rational fibre. Such models are candidates for testing the claimed loss of monomorphy under replacement of n by a proper multiple, while keeping the actual maps nT₀ → Π distinct from their abstractly isomorphic sources. **Confidence: medium** as a diagnostic example, because the manuscript’s transition from rational to integral subgroup data makes special fibres relevant; no general theorem is claimed here. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 214).

### Scans 215–219

**B1.** **Scan 215, opening paragraph — conjecture.** “Cette condition doit être aussi suffisante” proposes sufficiency of the preceding condition, qualified by the identity-component condition on the schematic closure. A proof would need to construct the integral one-parameter subgroup under precisely that qualification, rather than assume that the rational germ extends automatically. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 215).

**B2.** **Scan 215, around (54) — unproved claim.** “On trouve alors, en tout cas, un monomorphisme unique (à conjugaison près)” asserts an integral monomorphism whose rational fibre is a positive rational rescaling of the earlier germ. Existence, the allowed rescaling, and the conjugacy meaning need to be distinguished in a proof; no proof is supplied on this page. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 215).

**B3.** **Scan 215, before and after (55) — conjecture.** “Je ne suis pas sûr” explicitly questions whether the map on integral points factors through the discrete group, and “hypothèse suppl.” then adds this factorization as an assumption. The additional requirement that the discrete map belong to the stated germ must be retained as part of the problem. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 215).

**B4.** **Scan 216, following (56) — conjecture.** “On voudrait que” introduces integral generation of the Lie module by images of the available one-parameter maps. The manuscript emphasizes generation as a `Z`-module, so a proof must address integral saturation, not only rational linear span. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 216).

**B5.** **Scan 216, left diagonal margin — conjecture.** The note calls the hypothesis “raisonnable” in the displayed `SL(2, Z)` example, but adds “mais non si I = ∅” (the words “non si” remain uncertain in transcription). This is an explicit warning that the chosen family of local maps is part of the hypothesis and cannot simply be omitted. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 216).

**B6.** **Scan 216, lower paragraph — unproved claim.** “Il revient au même de dire” reformulates the generation condition by choosing one map for each index and adjoining all conjugates under the discrete group. Establishing the equivalence requires keeping track of the allowed integral maps and of the conjugation action, including the noncanonical generator choice indicated under (56). (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 216).

**B7.** **Scan 216, generation condition — own idea (confidence: medium).** For a concrete finite-rank example, compute the lattice generated by the indicated conjugate vectors and compare it with the full integral Lie module.
