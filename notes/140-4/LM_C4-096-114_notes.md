### Transcription conventions and limits

Only the supplied images were used to read the manuscript. No published transcription or mathematical reconstruction was used to fill the text. The ornate repeated symmetric-group glyph is represented by `\mathfrak S`; uncertain identifications of fonts, symbols, accents, underlining, and superscripts are marked locally. In particular, the circle with a vertical stroke and subscript 12 remains doubtful between a doubled D and Phi; the musical-looking superscript on G remains doubtful between a natural and sharp sign. The best reading can differ between occurrences because the local ink differs.

Revisions from X to U, surviving contradictory notation, repeated equation labels, apparent numbering jumps, and mismatched ordinary punctuation have not been normalized. A retraced glyph is marked doubtful when an earlier state cannot be recovered; no imaginary deleted symbol is supplied beneath it. Interwoven revision layers are retained in physical reading order where their intended integration is uncertain. The crossed-out arrow on scan 99 ends at an empty place in the source. Dots in the transcription, including the tentative dots after “eux-mêmes” in the scan 97 margin, represent visible punctuation rather than omitted content.

## Research notes — English, separate from the transcription

The following items distinguish the author's questions and expectations, claims needing fuller justification, implications worth pursuing, and explicitly labeled transcriber ideas. They are local observations on this selection, not claims of novelty or a literature priority survey. A point described as unproved here may be justified before or after these pages; that possibility is retained in each item's scope. Conditional mathematical checks never determine the diplomatic reading. Citations refer to the supplied manuscript images, using their filename scan numbers.

1. **Scan 96, discussion after (218); kind: unproved claim.** The text says “ces deux hom sont surjectifs,” referring to the two maps in (218). The cyclotomic character's surjectivity is mentioned in the same sentence, but the excerpt does not supply the additional argument controlling the other group factors. A proof would need the preceding definitions of the two targets and the relevant kernel images; surjectivity of the character alone should not be substituted for that argument (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 96).

2. **Scan 96, after (219); kind: conjecture / stated question.** Grothendieck writes “notamment voir s'il est surjectif” about (219). This explicitly leaves the image of the displayed arithmetic homomorphism to be examined. Determining that image would require recovering the precise target and comparing the cyclotomic and additive components (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 96).

3. **Scan 96, final paragraph; kind: conjecture / expectation.** The intended approach is “par ‘voie arithmétique’,” by proving that the homomorphism induced on `Γ_Q` is an epimorphism. This is an expectation about a method, not a proof on this page. It points toward arithmetic realizations of the relevant geometric quotient, subject to resolving the overwritten target notation (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 96).

4. **Scan 97, opening paragraph; kind: conjecture / research intention.** “J'ai envie de mettre ces homomorphismes en relation avec la ‘tour’” introduces the tower of Fermat curves as a possible geometric realization of the previously displayed homomorphisms. The following coordinate-power maps begin that construction, but an explicit identification with each earlier homomorphism remains a separate task at this point (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 97).

5. **Scan 97, (222); kind: unproved claim and textual issue.** The page writes `X_n/F_n° ≃ X_1 ≃ U_{0,3}`. The use of `X` here remains in tension with the newly inserted definition of the open subset `U_n`; it has been left unchanged in the transcription. Verification would have to distinguish the projective quotient from the quotient after removing the three coordinate divisors (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 97).

6. **Scan 97, paragraph after (223); kind: unproved claim.** The coordinate-power morphism is said to make the corrected object `U_n` a “revêtement principal (= torseur)” over `U_{n'}`. A proof should check the indicated diagonal quotient of roots of unity, the degree, the stabilizers, and the restriction to the open locus.
