# LM_C4-001-019 — Transcription notes

## Scope and editorial conventions

This edition follows the nineteen supplied scan files in filename order. Scan 1 is the institutional cover and uses the requested non-manuscript comment; scan 2 is the handwritten pagination notice; scans 3–4 are the contents; scans 5–19 contain the opening of §50. Original manuscript numbers, including the circled “bis”, the cancelled number, and the circled 654, are distinguished from the small lower-left archival folios and the printed archive footer.

The LaTeX file is a fragment: it has no preamble or document class. The host document must define the editorial macros specified in the task and load the usual mathematical and TikZ/tikz-cd facilities. No scan image, crop, silently completed continuation, or modern mathematical correction is embedded in the fragment.

An inventory value of `yes` means that the visible element has an encoded reading, including an explicit doubt or illegibility marker where necessary; it does not certify that an uncertain reading is correct. Faint reverse show-through is distinguished from writing on the recto. All `etc`, ellipses, and suspended phrases in the transcription reproduce visible manuscript wording or punctuation rather than editorial omissions.

### Scans 5–9

These observations use the supplied scans only. They distinguish questions and provisional statements in the manuscript from proposed follow-up work; no claim of novelty or literature priority is intended.

1. **Scan 5, ll. 14–20 — unproved claim.** The assertion beginning “On voit que si \(S\) est quasi-compact” passes from a fiberwise quasi-unipotence condition to a single positive integer exponent over the base. A proof should identify precisely which finite-presentation or constructibility input makes the exponent uniform, rather than using quasi-compactness alone without an open-cover argument.

2. **Scan 5, canceled paragraph near the foot — unproved claim, canceled formulation.** A preliminary “Théorème” involving a group scheme locally of finite presentation over a quasi-compact base is struck out before the surviving sufficiency statement. The cancellation and the uncertain end of its wording matter: this passage should be tracked as an abandoned formulation, not quoted as a proved theorem.

3. **Scan 6, ll. 3–5 — conjecture/question.** “Je me demande si c'est aussi nécessaire” asks whether a fiberwise unipotent section must come from a global homomorphism \(\mathbb G_a\to G\) taking \(1\) to that section, at least in suitable “bons” cases. The following pages begin to separate the characteristic-zero field case from extension over an arithmetic base.

4. **Scan 6, ll. 6–12 — unproved claim.** The “résultat d'unicité pour \(\varphi\)” in characteristic zero is used to invoke descent. A complete argument needs both uniqueness after base change and the resulting compatibility on the double overlap; it should also specify the class of group schemes for which the uniqueness statement holds.

5. **Scan 6, ll. 13–18 — unpursued implication.** The displayed example \(x\mapsto x^p-x\) shows why evaluation at \(1\) cannot by itself determine an additive-group homomorphism in positive characteristic. The proposed restriction to maps that are “mono” shifts the problem toward uniqueness of the embedded subgroup, a logically different question from uniqueness of its parameterization.

### Additional editorial research notes (English)

These items are the transcriber's mathematical observations, separate from the diplomatic text. They are not attributed to Grothendieck and make no claim of novelty. All of their source locations refer to the supplied scans; the two modern bibliographic leads below were consulted only for the notes.

#### E01 — Scan 5, definition before (1); scan 9, conditions (8)–(10) — own idea

The wording “est unipotent, i.e. s'il existe un hom de groupes” should be compared carefully with the usual linear-group definition: in characteristic $p$, an element in the image of $\mathbb G_a$ has order dividing $p$, whereas a unipotent Jordan block can have larger $p$-power order (compare Milne, 2022, §14.27). Thus the displayed parameterization is a substantive extra condition in positive characteristic, unless a special convention is intended here. In testing the subsequent matrix reductions, retain this fiberwise condition in addition to integrality of $\exp(X)$ and $\exp(-X)$. **Confidence: high**, because the additive group of a field of characteristic $p$ is killed by $p$; the interpretation of Grothendieck's terminology remains a question for the wider manuscript.

#### E02 — Scan 6, question about monomorphisms and a nontrivial section — own idea

For the question beginning “Un sous-groupe de $G$”, test $G=\mathbb G_a^2$ over a field of characteristic $p>0$: the two homomorphisms $t\mapsto(t,0)$ and $t\mapsto(t,t^p-t)$ are distinct closed immersions and both take $1$ to the nonzero point $(1,0)$. Their images are different copies of $\mathbb G_a$, so specifying that point does not determine the subgroup, even over a field. **Confidence: high**, since $t^p-t$ is additive and the first coordinate gives an inverse from each image to $\mathbb G_a$; this is an explicit test of the proposed uniqueness route, not a claim about what Grothendieck proves later.

#### E03 — Scans 6–10, necessity without taking a power — own idea

For “Je me demande si c'est aussi nécessaire”, try the ramified discrete valuation ring $A=\mathbb Z_2[\pi]$, $\pi^2=2$, and $g=I+N\in\mathrm{Gl}_3(A)$ with $N=E_{12}+\pi E_{23}$: its generic fiber has the usual exponential parameterization, and its special fiber is $I+E_{12}$, hence also is the value at $1$ of a $\mathbb G_a$-homomorphism. However, $\log(g)=N-(\pi/2)E_{13}$ has a nonintegral entry, so the uniqueness of the generic-fiber parameterization $t\mapsto\exp(t\log g)$ prevents it from extending over $A$ with value $g$ at $1$ (Milne, 2022, §14.29). This does not obstruct the power statement: $\exp(2t\log g)=I+t(2N-\pi E_{13})+2\pi t^2E_{13}$ has integral coefficients and takes $1$ to $g^2$. **Confidence: high for this explicit calculation**, because $N^2=\pi E_{13}$, $N^3=0$, and $\pi/2=1/\pi$; the example is offered for checking against the rest of the manuscript, with no novelty claim.

#### E04 — Scan 13, (18)–(19), and scans 14–16, scaling argument — own idea

An explicit bound may supplement “il existe un entier $d>0$”: if $X^r=0$ and an integer $D>0$ satisfies $DX\in M_n(A)$, then $d=D(r-1)!$ gives $d^iX^i/i!\in M_n(A)$ for $0\le i<r$. If $X=\log(g)$ for an integral unipotent matrix $g$ in characteristic zero with $(g-I)^r=0$, one may take $D=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,r-1)$. **Confidence: high under these stated matrix hypotheses**, because the logarithm is a finite polynomial and $i!$ divides $(r-1)!$; this does not supply the manuscript's deferred reduction for arbitrary group schemes or nonreduced bases.
