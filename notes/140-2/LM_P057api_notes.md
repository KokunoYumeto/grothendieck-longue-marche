## 3. Legibility and reliability

The main horizontal prose is moderately legible, and most numbered formulas can be followed. The most serious limitations are the sloping calculations on scans 567–568, the extensive bottom-of-page reworking on scan 567, and the interlinear terminology on scan 570. Those passages are not complete reliable readings: substantial portions are explicitly left unread.

Small primes, superscripts, repeated tildes, and cancellation boundaries need particular caution. The exponent in (62), the condition in the sentence introducing (70), and the names of the cartes on scan 570 should not be quoted as settled readings. Some connecting strokes may be insertion leaders rather than underlining; emphasis has been marked only where reasonably identifiable. No claim is made that the unresolved marginal material has been recovered from the surrounding mathematics.

## 4. Research notes — English

These are separate from the transcription. “Unproved” means not proved in these supplied pages, not that no proof exists elsewhere. No literature or subsequent manuscript pages have been checked here. Uncertain readings are not used to establish mathematical claims.

### R1 — Scan 561, paragraph after (53)
**Kind: unproved claim**

Grothendieck writes “facile de vérifier a priori” and invokes the Deligne–Mumford compactification to obtain the integral description of the object denoted `\widetilde M_{1,1}`. A proof would need to identify precisely the compactified object being used, establish the asserted smooth proper relative genus-zero curve with section, and justify the final trivialization over `Spec Z`. In particular, the passage from a moduli object to an ordinary relative scheme should not be taken for granted from the notation alone.

### R2 — Scans 561 and 563, normalization of the distinguished sections
**Kind: unpursued implication**

The choice to “désigner par ∞” the section coming from the punctures is explicitly contrasted with the convention used for cartes. This explains why the modular coordinate and the carte coordinate exchange the roles of zero and infinity. Tracking this choice through all later ramification and group-action formulas would prevent normalization changes from being mistaken for substantive discrepancies.

### R3 — Scan 562, first half
**Kind: unproved claim**

“Ces deux sections ne sont pas disjointes” is followed by the assertion that they meet in characteristics 2 and 3. The orbit calculation explains part of the phenomenon away from 2, but an integral proof still needs the characteristic-2 moduli description. Scheme-theoretic intersection multiplicities, rather than just coincidence of reduced points, are not calculated here.

### R4 — Scans 562–563, normalization up to sign and (54)
**Kind: unproved claim**

The text says that the chosen zero section determines the isomorphism “au signe près,” followed by a positive-sign normalization and the theorem “pour mémoire - c'est bien connu.” One should separate the existence of an integral affine-line coordinate from the elementary uniqueness statement about its remaining automorphisms. The latter alone does not establish the former.

### R5 — Scan 563, paragraph following (54), and scan 565, margin
**Kind: unpursued implication**

The integers in `2^a3^b` are described as “par moi oubliés,” and the later marginal instruction is to “déterminer a.” The calculation on scan 564 addresses `b`, but the value of `a` is not established in these pages. A useful continuation would track the chosen normalization through an integral elliptic-curve equation at 2 rather than importing a familiar value of the classical invariant.

### R6 — Scan 564, (58)
**Kind: unproved claim**

“On trouve immédiatement” introduces the rational function obtained from its zeros, poles, multiplicities, and normalization. A direct verification should record the divisor on the projective line, including the point at infinity, and evaluate at a member of `(2,-1,1/2)` to recover the scalar. This would also make explicit where characteristic restrictions enter the calculation.

### R7 — Scan 564, deduction of `b=3`
**Kind: unproved claim**

The argument is that `j` must “garder un sens en car. 3 et ne pas être constante.” To turn this into a proof of `b=3`, one must check regularity of the chosen integral moduli coordinate and the reduction of the remaining rational factor. Merely observing the power of 3 in (59) does not replace those checks.

### R8 — Scan 565, first paragraph
**Kind: conjecture**

As a work programme, he proposes studying the situation near 2 without the Legendre presentation, “par exemple” by rigidification of order 3. This suggests constructing a suitable level-3 model over a base where 3 is invertible and comparing its quotient coordinate with (60). The comparison must retain the normalization fixed in (54).

### R9 — Scans 565–566, “Remarque”
**Kind: unproved claim**

“On voit tout de suite” introduces the integral identification of `P^1_Z/S_3` with `P^1_Z` and its normalized sections. A proof would require a quotient construction over `Z`, not just the invariant function over `Q`, together with a check of the asserted disjointness and coincidences in every residue characteristic. The distinction between this quotient and the integral elliptic-moduli model is essential.

### R10 — Scan 566, (61)–(63)
**Kind: unproved claim**

“Le calcul explicite est d'ailleurs évident” precedes the normalization giving `2^2/3^3`. The calculation should be redone independently, because the denominator exponent in (62) appears to be 2 but is not securely read, whereas the surrounding formulas suggest a consistency check. That textual uncertainty must be resolved before treating the displayed formula as evidence of a mathematical error.

### R11 — Scan 566, last paragraph
**Kind: own idea**

The warning that these calculations “ne se rapportent plus” to the indicated moduli objects suggests comparing two integral models of the same generic quotient curve. One could study their relation locally over 2 by comparing the three distinguished sections and the valuations of their differences. **Confidence: medium** — the differing coincidence patterns motivate this comparison, but the exact integral models and their relation are not established in the excerpt.

### R12 — Scan 567, opening and (64)–(65)
**Kind: conjecture**

“Il serait naturel de regarder de plus près le schéma” introduces the level-4 investigation. The work programme is not just to count the cover: it also requires a precise moduli interpretation of the component determined by a primitive fourth root of unity. The complicated cancelled ending of this page should not be used to settle that interpretation without further inspection.

### R13 — Scan 567, cardinalities, (66), and following paragraph
**Kind: unproved claim**

The “calcul immédiat” gives degree 8, and the text then asserts failure of geometric connectedness over `Q`. A proof should combine the reduction-kernel calculation with the determinant or Weil-pairing map to the quadratic base `μ_4^*`. This would account separately for the quadratic base extension and the degree-4 geometric cover appearing on the next page.

### R14 — Scan 568, proposition (68)
**Kind: unproved claim**

The proposition identifies the unprimed reduction kernel with trace-zero matrices over `F_2`, with `±1` corresponding to scalar matrices. A proof can be organized around `I+2A ↦ A`: multiplication becomes addition modulo 2, and the determinant condition becomes the trace-zero condition. The scalar quotient and conjugation action then need to be checked explicitly; this also explains why the distinction between primed and unprimed groups matters.

### R15 — Scan 568, final paragraph
**Kind: unpursued implication**

The phrase “revêtement abélien universel … de groupe annulé par 2” points to the maximal elementary-abelian 2-cover of the three-punctured line. Its three boundary generators have one relation, giving the displayed quotient of `F_2^{\{0,1,∞\}}` by the diagonal. A fuller treatment should specify the base-point or unpointed-cover convention and explain the claimed canonicity over `S'`.

### R16 — Scan 569, first paragraph
**Kind: unproved claim**

Grothendieck explicitly leaves “un calcul amusant à faire”: identify the conjugation action on trace-zero matrices modulo scalars with the permutation action on the three-coordinate quotient. An abstract isomorphism of two-dimensional vector spaces is insufficient. What is needed is an equivariant isomorphism for the indicated action of `Sl(2,F_2)`.

### R17 — Scan 569, same calculation
**Kind: own idea**

For a trace-zero matrix `A`, try evaluating `v ↦ det(v,Av)` on the three nonzero vectors of `F_2^2`. Scalar matrices give zero, and conjugation should act by permuting those three evaluation positions; one can then compare the resulting module with the quotient by constant functions. **Confidence: medium-high** — this is a small explicit matrix calculation, but the exact identification and all action conventions have not been verified here; no novelty is claimed.

### R18 — Scan 569, (70)
**Kind: conjecture**

The proposed identification with the octahedral automorphism group should be proved at the level of the particular covering action, not only as an abstract group isomorphism. This requires matching the inertia data and the permutation of the distinguished punctures. The wording of the sentence immediately before (70) is too uncertain to quote as a secure affirmative or negative conjecture.

### R19 — Scan 569, (71)
**Kind: conjecture**

“On devrait avoir … un isom. canonique” proposes a Fermat-conic description. Establishing canonicity requires tracking the chosen fourth root of unity, the ordering of the three coordinates, and the level structure. The notation `M_{1,1}[4]_{S'}` should also be checked against (67): a selected component and the entire base change must not be silently identified.

### R20 — Scans 569–570, conic equation and unit conditions
**Kind: unpursued implication**

Equation (71) names the conic, whereas scan 570 requires all three coordinates to be units. These conditions distinguish the open curve from its projective compactification. Over the stated base, the coordinate-zero loci suggest the six punctures of the compactified level-4 picture; a scheme-theoretic verification would clarify exactly what (71) is intended to identify.

### R21 — Scan 570, (72)–(73)
**Kind: own idea**

Test the Kummer model obtained by adjoining square roots of `λ` and `1-λ` on the three-punctured line. With `X=x/z`, `Y=y/z`, and `i^2=-1`, setting `λ=-X^2` gives `1-λ=-Y^2`, so multiplication by `i` relates the conic equation to that model. **Confidence: high for this algebraic calculation, medium for its exact moduli identification** — the remaining issue is matching Grothendieck's ordering and level-structure conventions.

### R22 — Scan 570, opening paragraph
**Kind: conjecture**

“Il faudrait expliciter la relation” asks for a correspondence between elliptic curves with the specified level-4 Jacobi rigidification and the nontrivial solutions of (72), modulo common unit scaling. A proof should be functorial in `S`, give constructions in both directions, and verify descent. The written convention `e_1∧e_2=[i]^{-1}` is part of the problem and should not be replaced by its inverse convention unnoticed.

### R23 — Scan 570, last paragraph
**Kind: unproved claim**

The geometric interpretations are explicitly qualified by “modulo des vérifications et une étude un peu plus poussée.” To complete them one needs an explicit map of the two cartes, its order-4 covering group, and its ramification at the three vertices. The uncertain adjective transcribed as “octogonale” should be checked in the original before deciding whether it is a slip, a different intended description, or simply a misreading.
