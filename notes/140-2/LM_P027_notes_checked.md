The text says that *any* ("tout") ∞-connected space E on which A_g acts, with A_{g,ν}^0 acting freely, lets one interpret the canonical extension Γ'_{g,ν+1} of Γ_{g,ν} by Π_{g,ν} as a mixed fundamental group. ## Second-pass notes (mine)

**S01. Scan 261. Claim without proof (second pass).** "A_0^0 est simplement formé des autom. de X_0 ≃ S² qui conservent l'orientation" says that the group of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of S² is connected. This is a classical theorem (Kneser: it deformation-retracts onto SO(3)), used here without reference. The isotopy of any two complex structures on S², and hence the conclusion that the M_{0,i} (i ≤ 3) are points, depends on it.

**S02. Scan 264. Claim attributed, not proved (second pass).** The words "comme des|ds Ladegaillerie" make Y. Ladegaillerie's work the source of the 2-category 𝒮, and probably of the 2-fidelity "on sait" on scan 268. In the witnesses' later text (CSG l. 5499) the "résultat déjà connu (Ladegaillerie)" is stated for Γ = 1. The equivariant statements of scans 268–270 therefore go beyond the cited result and should be marked as the author's extension.

**S03. Scan 264. Check of a claim (second pass).** "Donc ce sont des K(π,1)" needs each A_{g,ν}^0 with (g,ν) ≠ (0,0) to be aspherical. This holds in the anabelian cases (contractible identity components) and in the cases (0,1), (0,2), (1,0) (a circle, a circle, a torus), by classical results (Kneser, Hamstrom, Earle–Eells) to be checked against the literature. The exclusion (0,0) is necessary, since A_0^0 ≃ SO(3) is not a K(π,1). Admissibility is what removes (0,0).

**S04. Scan 265. Type check (second pass).** In α: ρ f_{D*} ≃ f_U ρ', we have f_{D*}: B_{D'*} → B_{D*} and f_U: B_{U'} → B_U, so both composites run B_{D'*} → B_U. The formula type-checks, and it fixes the direction of 1-morphisms (from the primed object to the unprimed one). The ∼ on the curved arrow makes α invertible, as a "donnée de commutativité" requires. What "définis ad hoc" leaves open is the 2-morphisms between pairs (f, α).

**S05. Scan 266. Type check (second pass).** The cross arrows D̃* → Ũ of the cofibred groupoid are isomorphisms ρ_!(D̃*) ⥲ Ũ, so they must live in Π_U. This decides the overwritten "Π_{B_U}" on mathematical grounds, although not on the ink. All arrows are cocartesian because the fibres are groupoids, as the text states.

**S06. Scan 267. Implication not pursued (second pass).** "Fibres topoi; change-of-base functors left exact and commuting with arbitrary colimits" characterises inverse-image functors of geometric morphisms, since such a functor between topoi has a right adjoint. The further left adjoint ρ_! in "ρ_! ρ^* ρ_*" is extra: it expresses that morphisms of multigaloisian topoi are essential. The fibred-topos description of 267 does not encode this by itself, so ℳ as "morphismes de topos multigaloisiens" needs it added.

**S07. Scan 268, margin. Implication not pursued (second pass).** With B_{D*} the empty topos, there are no punctures and each U_i is a closed surface with H²(U_i, Z) ≅ Z, so an iso T ≃ H²(U_i, Z) is an orientation. T is presumably the coefficient module of the earlier sections. In an arithmetic setting the corresponding H² carries a Tate twist (cyclotomic character), so the datum would no longer be a free choice; this is plausibly the meaning of "il n'en sera|est plus de m̄". The author does not pursue it.

**S08. Scans 269–270. Content of the conjecture (second pass).** For Π = [Γ] with Γ finite, essential surjectivity of R_fS → R_fM̃_lac on objects says that every admissible diagram B_{D*,Γ} → B_{U,Γ} → B_Γ comes from an action of Γ on an admissible surface. This is a group extension of Γ by a surface group with loop structure being realised geometrically: the Nielsen realisation problem for finite groups. It was solved in general by Kerckhoff (Ann. of Math. 1983), after this manuscript; Nielsen had treated the cyclic case and Fenchel the solvable case. The conjecture adds the 2-categorical fidelity on top of this, and that part is not covered by those results.
