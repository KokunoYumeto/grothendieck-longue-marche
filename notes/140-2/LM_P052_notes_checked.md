## Second-pass notes (own)

**S01, scans 513–516, claim checked (own).** SL(2,Z) = ⟨u4, u6 | u4⁴ = u6⁶ = 1, u4² = u6³⟩, i.e. Z/4 *_{Z/2} Z/6. For example u4 = [[0,−1],[1,0]] and u6 = [[0,−1],[1,1]] give u4² = u6³ = −I. Hence (22) is surjective, with kernel the normal subgroup generated by ℓ1⁴, ℓ∞⁶ and ℓ1²ℓ∞⁻³, as the ink says with "invariant". This also settles the "sans doute" on 514: −1 = u4² lies in ⟨u4, u6⟩, so generation mod ±1 gives generation of SL(2,Z).

**S02, scan 514, claim checked (own).** Every element of finite order in SL(2,Z) has order 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6 and is conjugate to a power of u4 or u6. The elements of order 4, and those of order 6, each form two conjugacy classes interchanged by inversion. This is exactly the qualification "à conjugaison et passage à l'inverse près".

**S03, scan 512, implication (own).** The inserted "(ni m̄ sur (Π'03)ab)" makes the argument purely abelian. Π'03,ab ≃ Z/2 × Z/3 is generated by the classes of the inertia generators ℓ'1 (order 2) and ℓ'∞ (order 3), and Γ acts on inertia classes through the cyclotomic character. So τ, with χ(τ) = −1, inverts the Z/3 factor and acts nontrivially even on the abelianization.

**S04, scan 517, claim without proof (own).** The canonical isomorphism (26) M^!_{0,4} ≃ U_{0,3} is the cross-ratio: move three numbered points to 0, 1, ∞ by the unique projective transformation, and the fourth point becomes λ ∈ P¹ ∖ {0,1,∞}. "Canonical" presupposes a fixed convention for which numbered points go to 0, 1 and ∞. The S3 of (29)–(30) acts by changing it.

**S05, scan 516, implication (own).** If (24) extends (23), elements of T^!_{0,4} that reverse orientation (the coset of τ in the extension of {1,τ} by Π0,3) should map to Gl(2,Z) ∖ Sl(2,Z). Otherwise the restriction to the "+" parts would not match (12). This is a necessary condition to test any candidate image of τ, not a statement in the text.

**S06, scan 519, slip in the ink (own).** As written, y² = √(x(x−1)(x−λ)) gives y⁴ = x(x−1)(x−λ), which is not the intended elliptic curve. The context ("la courbe elliptique … sur U0,3, pour avoir U0,3 → M1,1") requires the Legendre family y² = x(x−1)(x−λ), which all witnesses print. The diplomatic text keeps the radical; an edition should flag it.

**S07, scan 518, claim checked (own).** The margin "distinctes et partant ≠ 1" is a valid inference. If α, β, γ = α+β are pairwise distinct at every point, none is the origin (α = 0 would give γ = β). Conversely, α, β form an F2-basis of ₂E exactly when α, β, α+β are the three nonzero points. The margin therefore restates the basis condition pointwise.

**S08, scan 520, implication (own).** At level 2, −1 lies in the kernel of SL(2,Z) → SL(2,Z/2Z), so it acts trivially on level structures. M₁,₁[2] is then a μ2-gerbe over the λ-line, and removing the symmetry ("à symétrie près") gives M'₁,₁[2] ≃ P¹ ∖ {0,1,∞} = U0,3, as (30) says. The Galois group Sl(2,F2) ≃ S3 then permutes α, β, γ, i.e. the branch points 1, ∞, λ. Identifying this S3 with the S3 = Π̂'03/Π̂03 of (9 bis) is not done in this package; it is the open point behind (31).
