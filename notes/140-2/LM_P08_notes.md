## 3. Legibility and reliability

The continuous prose is substantially more legible than the revisions and compact formula annotations. The least reliable passages are the nested cancellations spanning scans 74–75, the overwritten fraction-field letters and finite/limit indices of the paired formulas on scans 75–76, the projection-functor label and added base-object identification on scan 72, and the final subgroup/commutation notation on scan 80. Small primes, bars, subscripts, and abbreviated superscripts remain a particular risk. The resulting reading is provisional, not a certified transcription.

The source base was the ten supplied JPEGs, inspected directly and in enlarged crops; no OCR, published transcription, or external mathematical source was used to supply words. The cancelled passage continuing from scan 74 into scan 75 has separate `\deleted{…}` spans on the two pages; nested deletions record visible local cancellations without claiming a secure chronology. The visible `t/s`, `K/L`, singular/plural, and coefficient/subscript discrepancies have not been harmonized. In particular, the unit-change formula on scan 76 has been retained as read, not repaired by algebraic expectation.

The LaTeX file is a fragment, not a standalone document. A temporary wrapper defining the requested editorial macros and loading standard mathematics, underlining, and `tikz-cd` support was used to check its syntax; that wrapper and its PDF are not part of the archive.

## 4. Research notes — separate from the transcription

These notes concern only what this batch supports or leaves to check. The definitions of the earlier numbered cases and the full hypotheses preceding scan 71 are not included. Short quotations below retain the relevant wording; uncertain readings are identified rather than treated as established statements.

1. **Scan 71, approximately lines 6–10 — conjecture.** “Je présume qu’un peu de sueur permettrait de prouver que cela marche encore dans le cas général” explicitly proposes removing the assumption that the images `Γ_i` equal `Γ`. A proof would have to identify exactly which boundary data can still be recovered from the stated outer-conjugacy class when these images are proper subgroups; the preceding definitions are outside this batch.

2. **Scans 72–73, the section/groupoid comparison — unproved claim.** The assertion that “les classes d’isomorphismes des couples … correspondent aux classes de … conjugaison” identifies categorical section data with conjugacy classes of splittings. The added choice of an isomorphism involving the lifted object is part of the argument, not disposable notation. A complete verification should track natural isomorphisms, the compatibility with the projection functor, and dependence on the chosen universal-cover object.

3. **Scan 73, approximately lines 8–11 — unproved claim.** The parenthesis “s’il y en a, et on sait qu’il y en a…” asserts nonemptiness of the splitting torsor at this point without giving the construction there. The local construction on scans 75–76 subsequently addresses existence, so this should be cross-checked against that construction rather than treated as an entirely abandoned question. Its precise hypotheses still need to be matched with those of the extension on scan 73.

4. **Scan 73, the cohomology/inverse-limit displays — unproved claim.** The displayed identification with `lim H¹(K_i, μ_n)` needs a justification for passing from the inverse system of finite coefficients to the cohomology group on the left. The Kummer sequence identifies the finite-level terms but does not by itself specify all topology, transition-map, or inverse-limit hypotheses. The small subscript on the earlier coefficient is itself uncertain, and must be resolved before formulating the exact statement.

5. **Scan 74, approximately lines 3–7 — unproved claim.** “Comme K_i est de t.f. sur Q, cet hom est injectif” asserts that the kernel described by membership in every power subgroup is trivial. A proof should isolate the role of finite generation and address both valuation data and elements in the constant field, including torsion. No corresponding assertion for arbitrary fields should be inferred from this wording.

6. **Scan 74, approximately lines 8–11 — unpursued implication.** The proposed “sous-K_i*-torseur” is also described as “un élément de Σ_i/K_i*”. This suggests that the intrinsic datum is an orbit, not a preferred splitting. It is worth tracing how that orbit behaves under changing the universal-cover object and the identification of its automorphism group, choices used on the preceding pages.

7. **Scans 74–75, the large cancelled paragraph — unproved claim.** The deleted argument says that the completed local ring “contient canoniquement le sous-corps K_i” and relates a uniformizer to a power-series isomorphism. The intended canonicity, its hypotheses, and its compatibility with the chosen algebraic closures merit checking. Because this passage is cancelled, it must not be quoted as an unqualified assertion adopted by the surviving argument.

8. **Scan 76, approximately lines 3–9 — unproved claim.** The phrase “des iso can.” introduces base-change identifications for the root-adjoining rings and their fraction fields. A proof needs to establish compatibility of the finite-level maps, passage to the direct limit, and the claim that the indicated fraction-field tensor products have the required field interpretation for the allowed extensions. The overwritten first indices should be resolved before deciding whether the neighbouring displays distinguish finite and limit levels or are revised versions of one formula.

9. **Scan 76, approximately lines 10–15 — unproved claim.** “On sait que L′(∞,t) est alors alg. clos” is the crucial assertion supporting the construction of a section. Its proof would need to show that, under the stated characteristic-zero henselian conditions and after making the residue field algebraically closed, the indicated root tower accounts for the relevant finite extensions. The following identification of the algebraic closure of the residue field inside that tower also needs to be made functorial to obtain the claimed section of groupoids.

10. **Scan 76, approximately lines 16–20 — own idea.** Check the unit-change display literally before using it: with the readings `s′ = s u^n` and `T̄_n ↦ u T̄_n` for an arrow from the quotient with relation `T_n^n − s` to the one with relation `T_n^n − s′`, the image of the source relation is `u^n s′ − s`, which does not vanish formally from that displayed equality alone. The exponent, direction, and interpretation of the chosen unit therefore need a separate check; this is not a licence to reverse the arrow or change the exponent in the transcription. **Confidence: medium**, because the polynomial calculation is direct but the manuscript’s compact exponent and conventions could resolve the apparent discrepancy.

11. **Scan 76, approximately lines 21–23 — unproved claim.** “D’où par passage à la limite” requires more than the existence of an isolated finite-level isomorphism. One should write down the compatibility equations between the unit changes at different levels and the transition maps of scan 75. In particular, determine whether the notation `u` represents one unit or a compatible system of roots, without inserting that interpretation into the diplomatic text.

12. **Scan 77, approximately lines 3–6 — unproved claim.** The principal-unit group is described as “un s.-groupe divisible de O*”, and sections arising from the corresponding changes of parameter are said to be isomorphic. A proof should verify root existence in `1 + m` under the actual henselian and characteristic assumptions and establish the compatibility needed for the entire tower. It should also distinguish isomorphism of sections from literal equality of chosen representatives.

13. **Scan 77, the paragraph beginning “On constate aussitôt” — unproved claim.** “Cette application est compatible avec l’hom” Θ asserts equivariance of the map from bases of `m/m²` to section classes. An explicit check should compare the cocycle measuring a change of parameter with the image under Θ of its residue-field scaling factor. The action conventions and any inverse/sign introduced by the root-tower maps are part of that verification.

14. **Scan 77, the source `(m/m²)*` of the displayed map — own idea.** Compare the torsor of bases of `m/m²` with the torsor of nonzero vectors in its dual, without renaming either one in the transcription. The two scalar actions may help diagnose a reciprocal factor in a change-of-parameter convention. **Confidence: medium**, because the comparison is elementary once the actions are fixed, but these pages do not explicitly make that duality convention.

15. **Scan 78, approximately lines 10–13 — unpursued implication.** “Il faudrait expliciter le comportement de cette structure suppl. relativement à des morphismes” is an explicit request for a functorial description. A useful next step is to compute the induced map on the distinguished sub-torsors for a local morphism, keeping the residue-field map and the leading term of the parameter change visible. The sentence limits attention to morphisms “provenant de situations géométriques”, a qualification that should be retained.

16. **Scan 78, approximately lines 14–16 — unpursued implication.** The requested “relation avec la norme des 1-formes différentielles” is not worked out on these pages. One should determine the precise operation called the norm, its source and target, and how its effect on the relevant one-dimensional spaces compares with the cohomological torsor map. The author explicitly postpones the exposition in “j’ai la flemme de l’expliciter en long et en large !”.

17. **Scans 78–79, the “transcendantes” / “géométriques” distinction — unpursued implication.** The text describes a large family parametrized by a cohomological torsor and a smaller family described as “géométriques”, parametrized by a multiplicative-group torsor. A careful formulation should keep the repeated qualification “plus précisément” and avoid choosing an origin implicitly. The terminology alone does not settle whether the final proposed exhaustion concerns the entire larger family, only the smaller family, or another precisely defined union.

18. **Scan 79, approximately lines 7–13 — unproved claim.** “On vérifie que” asserts separation of the classes obtained from distinct indices, even after passing to an abelianized extension, while “je ne fais pas le détail des vérifications, via Mordell-Weil” explicitly withholds the argument. A proof should identify the exact abelianized extension and the invariant distinguishing the indices, then state the hypotheses under which the indicated arithmetic input applies. The mere mention of Mordell–Weil is not a proof of the separation statement.

19. **Scan 79, approximately lines 13–14 — unproved claim.** The boundary-related section classes are also said to be “distincts des scindages associés aux pts. rat/K de X\S”. This is a separate separation assertion from the comparison between two boundary indices. It would be useful to identify an invariant that distinguishes these two sources of sections and to check whether the same abelianized quotient suffices.

20. **Scan 79, final paragraph — conjecture.** The proposed exhaustion is expressly hedged by “sous toutes réserves, encore !” and “peut-être”, before “pas d’autres scindages … que ceux-ci”. Both the case numeral, provisionally read as III with IV as an alternative, and the antecedent of “ceux-ci” must be settled from the wider manuscript before restating a formal conjecture. This is an expectation to investigate, not an established classification on these pages.

21. **Scan 80, approximately lines 2–8 — unproved claim.** In broadening the morphisms of open pairs, the text says they are “se prolongeant bien sûr” to the compactified objects. The exact geometric hypotheses making that extension assertion valid are not all restated here. Any reconstruction of the revised category should recover those hypotheses from the preceding manuscript rather than assume the phrase holds for arbitrary varieties and compactifications.

22. **Scan 80, the subset `I′_f` and partial boundary map — own idea.** Work out composition for the revised morphism data by tracking which source boundary components remain in the boundary through each successive map, and restrict the commutation data accordingly. This tests whether the proposed enlargement really defines a category and whether the additional torsor structure survives the enlargement. **Confidence: medium**, because the partial indexing is explicit but the complete commutation and triviality conditions continue beyond the supplied page.

23. **Scan 80, approximately lines 20–22 — own idea.** Resolve the subgroup letter in the clause “est trivial sur” before interpreting its mathematics: it may not be the same object as the earlier extension group. A literal requirement that a homomorphism over a fixed nontrivial Γ kill an entire subgroup that projects nontrivially to Γ would be incompatible with that projection, which gives a conditional diagnostic for the reading rather than an emendation. **Confidence: medium**, because that algebraic implication is straightforward but the subgroup glyph and the full surrounding hypotheses remain uncertain.
