# LM_P036 — research notes checked against the corrected transcription (scans 351–360)

Source: Grothendieck, *La Longue Marche à travers la théorie de Galois* (1981), Montpellier archive, cote 140-2, scans 351–360 (authorial folios 178–182). Quotations follow `LM_P036_checked.tex`; π stands for the doubtful Π/π group letter, and 𝔊 for the looped C/G letter.

Each first-pass note is given a verdict (keep, correct or drop) with the reason. Notes S1–S9 are new; they are labelled [2nd pass] and are the checking model's own.

## First-pass notes, checked

### Scan 351

1. **351 — implication not pursued (formula (3)).** Keep. The relation 2g′−2+card I′ = n(2g−2+card I) and the NB, which identifies 2g−2+card I as minus the compactly supported Euler–Poincaré characteristic, say that this invariant is multiplied by the index n on passing to π′. *Reason:* agrees with the corrected text; the hypotheses (n the index, I′ over I) are on the preceding scan.

2. **351 — claim without proof (orientations).** Correct. „De façon évidente, tte discrétification de π en définit une de π′” (the first pass read „en fournit”). For oriented discrétifications Grothendieck needs „un peu de cohomologie”: that H²_!(U,ℤ) → H²_!(U′,ℤ) has the form nθ⁻¹, with θ: T′ ⥲ T the trace isomorphism. This is stated „dans le cas discret p.ex.”, without proof. *Reason:* the verb has been corrected, and the insertion is not cancelled.

3. **351 — question.** Correct. „Il n'est pas clair pour moi à première vue” whether Discret(π) → Discret(π′) is surjective, or injective. The inserted quantifier „(pour tt π′ d'indice fini de π)” qualifies „L'injectivité”: injectivity for every finite-index π′ would mean that two commensurable discrétifications of π are equal. *Reason:* the first pass placed the insertion after the display, so that the quantifier applied to the map itself.

### Scan 352

4. **352 — conjecture.** Keep, with an addition. Under „Conjecture”, restriction Discret(π) → Discret(π′) for an invariant π′ is injective. Its image consists of the π′₀ for which G → Autext_lac(π′) ≃ 𝒯̂̂(π̂′₀) factors through 𝒯(π′₀) = Autext_lac(π′₀), „et en fait, m̄ par 𝒯(π′₀)⁺”. The ink defines „G = π′/π”, a slip for π/π′. *Reason:* the note agrees with the text; the slip is recorded (see S3).

5. **352 — claim without proof (right-hand note).** Keep. „En d'autres termes”: ∀g ∈ π ∃h ∈ π′ with int(hg)(π′₀) ⊂ π′₀, i.e. π′·π₀ = π, for π₀ the normaliser of π′₀ in π. The equivalence is stated without argument. *Reason:* unchanged. The first factor (π′ or π′₀) is still palaeographically doubtful, but only π′ makes the product meaningful, since π′₀ ⊂ π₀.

6. **352 — claim without proof (reconstruction).** Correct. π is recovered from the outer action of G on π̂′₀ because Centre(π̂′) = 1. Grothendieck identifies it as „le complété profini de l'extension π₀ de G par π′₀”; a first wording, „l'adhérence dans …”, is struck. Compatibility with the „structure à lacets de π” is explicitly left to check („mais il faut voir”). *Reason:* the cancelled words are now read; the substance is unchanged.

7. **352 — the first-pass model's own idea.** Keep. The suggestion is to separate the descent into an extension problem, a completion problem and a lacet-compatibility problem. *Reason:* still a sound plan, grounded in the manuscript's own distinction between profinite structure and lacet structure.

Doing the same for π₀ ⊃ π′₀, „on devrait trouver” a lacet structure on π₀ „donnant lieu aux m̄s dᵢ”. *Reason:* only the reading of „m̄s dᵢ” changed.

12. **353 — the first-pass model's own idea.** Keep. The test is whether failure to preserve an embedded discrete subgroup distinguishes outer actions with isomorphic profinite extensions. *Reason:* this is the construction Grothendieck attempts on scan 354.

## Second-pass notes (new, the checking model's own)

**S1. 351 — implication not pursued [2nd pass].** By (3), −EP_!(U′) = n·(−EP_!(U)), so the anabelian condition −EP_! ≥ 1 passes to every finite covering, as the margin says. The integer that „mesure … le degré d'anabélianité” is itself multiplied by the degree. Along a tower of coverings, only its positivity, or its ratio to the index, is invariant.

**S2. 351 — claim without proof, argument supplied [2nd pass].** The „peu de cohomologie” is the degree formula. For a degree-n covering U′ → U of oriented surfaces, pull-back H²_!(U,ℤ) → H²_!(U′,ℤ), read through the trace isomorphisms, is multiplication by n. Since n > 0, a generator of T (an orientation) goes to a positive multiple of a generator of T′, so oriented discrétifications restrict to oriented ones.

**S3. 352 — note on the text [2nd pass].** The ink writes „posons G = π′/π”. π′ is the invariant subgroup, so the intended group is π/π′, as the later uses show („G = π₀/π′₀ ≃ π/π′”, scan 360). Both witnesses silently correct the slip.

**S4. 354 — claim without proof [2nd pass].** The equivalence „int(γ).G ⊄ 𝒯, i.e. G ne stabilise pas γ⁻¹(π′₀)” presupposes that 𝒯(π′₀) is the stabiliser, in 𝒯̂̂(π′₀), of the π̂′₀-conjugacy class of the discrete subgroup π′₀ ⊂ π̂′₀. Given that identification, int(γ)G ⊂ 𝒯(π′₀) holds exactly when G stabilises γ⁻¹(π′₀) up to conjugacy. The existence claim „Un tel γ existe tjrs” then needs G ≠ 1 and a γ that moves π′₀ off the G-stable discrétifications. Neither is argued on this page.

**S5. 357 — the checking model's own idea [2nd pass].** π = π′·π₀ holds whenever π′ is open in π and π₀ is dense: every coset gπ′ is open and therefore meets π₀. This settles the step the first pass called unjustified, and it is the only property of π′ that the conjugacy argument on scan 357 uses.

**S6. 359 — check of a claim [2nd pass].** The margin „plus faible que (b)” beside (e) is correct. M(π′₀) is the normaliser of 𝔊̂⁺(π′₀) and therefore contains it, so congruence modulo 𝔊̂⁺(π′₀) implies congruence modulo M(π′₀). Hence (b) implies (e), provided both refer to the image of the same group 𝔊̂̂(π₀).

**S7. 359 — check of a claim [2nd pass].** Write Γ = M/𝔊̂⁺; the denominators in the display are written 𝔊̂(π₀), 𝔊̂(π′₀). Given m′ ∈ M(π′₀), (b) gives m′ = y·k with y in the image of 𝔊̂̂(π₀) and k ∈ 𝔊̂⁺(π′₀) ⊂ M(π′₀). Then y ∈ M(π′₀), and by (d) its preimage lies in M(π₀): so (b) and (d) give surjectivity. (a) gives injectivity once (c) defines the map, so the word expected in „(pour sa …ectivité” is „surjectivité”, the witnesses' reading, even though the overwritten ink may show „bijectivité”.

**S8. 355, 357 — note on the text [2nd pass].** A revision in darker ink adds the + superscripts in (4). The same ink blots what look like subscripts 0 in all four arguments of (4) and (5), and under π′ in „si π′ est un ss-groupe” on scan 357. If the blots are cancelled subscripts, the comparisons were first written for π₀, π′₀ and then restated for π, π′ and oriented objects. Confidence: low, because the blots cannot be read.

**S9. 360 — question [2nd pass].** Grothendieck calls (b) „beaucoup plus problématique, voir douteuse”: doubtful, not false. He finds (a) plausible and leaves (b) open, which is why the page goes on to compare the groups in diagram (8). The witnesses' „voir fausse” would make the doubt about the surjectivity of P(π) → P(π′) stronger than the manuscript states.
