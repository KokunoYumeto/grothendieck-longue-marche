## 3. Legibility and reliability

The middle scans, especially the main prose of 172–176 and the large diagrams, are appreciably more legible than the beginning and end. Scan 171 contains a broadly cancelled paragraph with additional local cancellations; scan 177 has a crowded factorization; scan 179 has oblique and nearly vertical marginal writing plus a severely overwritten foot. Scan 180 is readable in outline, but the insertion before “un scindage” and the qualification in the last paragraph remain uncertain.

The least reliable passages are scan 171’s parenthesis and proposed replacement notation, scan 179’s final condition and the ends of its marginal notes, and scan 180’s final parenthetical phrase. Some readable words in those regions are secure while their placement or cancellation status is not. Conversely, uncertainty marks also remain on some probably correct readings: a mathematically plausible completion was not treated as evidence for missing letters. No percentage accuracy is claimed. The LaTeX fragment intentionally retains incomplete sentences, mismatched punctuation, and surprising notation where the ink supports them; it is not a normalized exposition and should not be cited as an independently established transcription without comparison to the scans.

## 4. Research notes — separate from the transcription

These are source-based research prompts, not a literature review. The labels “conjecture” and “unproved claim” distinguish an expressed expectation or reservation from a claim whose proof is absent, only sketched, or dependent on material outside these ten scans. “Own idea” identifies a proposed check or construction by the transcriber, not an assertion of originality or an alteration of the manuscript. Quotations are deliberately short; no argument below is used to repair an uncertain reading.

### R01 — Scan 171, cancelled critique, approximately L02–17
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The contrast between the two uses of `B`, including “nettement plus gros”, raises a substantive distinction between a classifying-type topos and the larger topos used for the gluing construction. A useful reconstruction would state exactly which categories these symbols denote and which comparison functors survive the proposed change of notation; the cancelled passage should not be treated as a settled naming convention.

### R02 — Scan 171, approximately L18–21
**Kind: unproved claim.** “Toutes ces opérations” are described as “triviales et sans mystère”, with an independence assertion whose precise wording is partly uncertain. A proof would need the definitions of those operations from earlier pages and a statement of which hypotheses on the local groups are actually unnecessary, rather than an inference from the reassuring phrase alone.

### R03 — Scans 172–173, action/torseur correspondence
**Kind: unproved claim.** The passage beginning “la donnée d’une action” identifies an action on a topos with a covering/torseur presentation over a quotient topos. To turn this into a precise equivalence, specify the morphisms and 2-morphisms, the coherence required of an action, and the two reconstruction maps; the page’s object-level description does not by itself establish all of that structure.

### R04 — Scan 173, D01 and following paragraph
**Kind: unproved claim.** The square is asserted to be “2-cartésien” with vertical “Γ-galoisiennes” arrows. A proof should identify the relevant 2-fibre product, its descent data, and the comparison with the displayed upper-left topos; the profinite possibility mentioned on scan 172 also requires an explicit continuity convention.

### R05 — Scan 174, approximately L01–07
**Kind: unproved claim.** “On voit que” introduces preservation of the multigaloisian property by the quotient construction. Establishing this requires a definition of “multigaloisien” in use here and a construction or criterion showing that the quotient remains in that class, including any restrictions on the acting group and on its action.

### R06 — Scan 174, connectivity paragraph
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The criterion involving `card π₀(B_U)/Γ = 1` is followed by the explicit nonemptiness and transitivity conditions. It suggests isolating the induced action on connected components as the obstruction to connectedness of the quotient; the empty-component-set case must not be absorbed into an unqualified “transitive” formulation.

### R07 — Scan 175, arithmetic/geometric comparison
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The interpretation over an algebraic closure with Galois action is said to be “la plus commode”. One could compare the arithmetic gluing construction directly with equivariant descent of the geometric construction, recording which parts are invariant under choices of points and which need continuous descent data rather than merely an abstract group action.

### R08 — Scans 175–176, orbit and stabilizer discussion
**Kind: unproved claim.** Connected components are related to “orbites”, and a transitive component is identified with the classifying topos of a stabilizer. A direct proof would compare the fibre of a Γ-set over the chosen point with induction from its stabilizer, then check that the resulting equivalences assemble over all orbits and respect the topological conventions in a profinite case.

### R09 — Scan 176, “NB” paragraph
**Kind: unproved claim.** The condition “est injectif” on groupoid arrows is used to recognize the discrete fibre situation, followed by a reconstruction from cosets. The recognition argument needs both directions: passage from a Γ-set to faithful groupoid data, and recovery of that Γ-set from the indicated family of subgroup maps, with the equivalence relation between different presentations stated explicitly.

### R10 — Scan 177, kernel quotient and final factorization
**Kind: unproved claim.** The phrase “factorisation canonique” is clearer than some of the surrounding revised wording. To make the assertion precise, define the kernel congruence on each relevant Hom-set, verify that composition passes to the quotient, and state in what sense the factorization is invariant under equivalence of groupoid presentations; the uncertain “bi/bij” insertion should be checked before fixing its object-level property.

### R11 — Scan 178, approximately L04–12
**Kind: unproved claim.** The quotient construction is said to “commute aux opérations” involved in passing to the glued topoi. This calls for an explicit comparison morphism and a proof that it is an equivalence, using the actual universal property of the gluing operation and its equivariant compatibility; the ambiguous `B_S` annotation must not be silently made into a particular arrow to force such a proof.

### R12 — Scans 178–179, page transition
**Kind: conjecture (expectation).** “Le temps” is declared ripe to explain an “opération inverse hypothétique” described as “forage”. This is an announced programme rather than a proved inverse construction in these scans; a formulation would have to specify the data to be recovered, the admissible outputs, and whether an inverse is unique or depends on choices.

### R13 — Scan 179, surface-action conditions
**Kind: unproved claim.** “Conditions équivalentes” introduces a list involving isotopy, homotopy, outer actions, extensions, and the revised final condition.
