## 3. Legibility and limits

The main prose is moderately legible, and many displays can be followed, but this is a conservative working transcription rather than a definitive edition. The weakest areas are the opening cancellation on scan 121, small or overwritten diagram labels, the local subscripts on scan 128, the clause preceding `BL_i` on scan 130, and especially that scan's long oblique marginal note. Some cancellation boundaries and the attachment of interlinear additions are also uncertain. The doubt table is part of the transcription's evidential status, not an optional supplement.

The scans were read without filling gaps from mathematical expectations. The coefficient change `E`/`F`, the reversal `B_{X,U}`/`B_{U,X}`, incomplete parentheses, abbreviated prose, and cancelled hypotheses are retained rather than corrected. The last marginal note is only partially recovered and should not be used as a reliable quotation of a mathematical assertion. Unmarked text is not a guarantee against error; an independent collation would still be appropriate before publication or mathematical reliance.

## 4. Research notes — English; separate from transcription

The locators below refer to the supplied scans and the approximate `Lnn` anchors. Quoted fragments are from the supplied handwriting as read here; any inference is identified as a task for further work. “Unproved claim” includes compressed arguments whose required details are not supplied on these pages; it does not mean that no argument is suggested, or that the claim is false. The record of a question is retained even when a subsequent supplied page begins to address it.

### R01 — The amalgamated fundamental topos
**Scan 121, L10–16. Kind: unproved claim.** The text says that the fundamental topos appears as a “somme amalgamée” and identifies the locally constant case with the gluing map being an isomorphism. A detailed argument should specify the category or 2-category in which this universal property is meant and show how the isomorphism condition on the triples gives that property. This is stronger than merely recognizing a commutative diamond of maps.

### R02 — From the group-action description to the quotient fundamental group
**Scans 122–123; scan 123, L07–08. Kind: unproved claim.** After the description by group actions, the phrase “d'où résulte” introduces a quotient of the group attached to `U` by a subgroup generated by local inertia images. The local-to-global passage is compressed; it should be checked using exactly the indicated homomorphisms and the relevant profinite convention for a generated invariant subgroup. The handwritten formula does not license inserting a closure sign into the transcription.

### R03 — The stated programme
**Scan 123, L09–11. Kind: conjecture (research objective).** “Un calcul galoisien (si possible) de la cohomologie de X” is an explicitly qualified programme, not a theorem asserted for arbitrary coefficients and arbitrary `X`. It is worth separating which parts concern locally constant sheaves, which concern the glued topos, and which concern comparison with the full étale topos.

### R04 — An explicit profinite-group calculation
**Scan 124, L03–09. Kind: conjecture (question).** The first question asks for a “Calcul ‘explicite’” of the cohomology of the glued topos in terms of cohomology of profinite groups. The note distinguishes this task from the cohomology of the ordinary fundamental topos. A useful completion would give the complexes and maps, not just identify the groups indexing the pieces.

### R05 — The torsion-cohomology comparison
**Scan 124, L10–13. Kind: conjecture (question).** “Vérifier si” introduces the question whether the canonical map from the cohomology of `B_{X,U}` to the cohomology of `X_ét` is an isomorphism for a torsion sheaf. The precise exponent in the displayed question is pale, but the direction of the comparison and the torsion qualification are readable. Any proposed answer must retain the coefficient and geometric restrictions rather than turn this into an unrestricted equivalence of topoi.

### R06 — Which hypotheses are actually used?
**Scan 124, L14–17. Kind: unproved claim.** The author says that the hypothesis `dim X=1` has not yet been used, nor the noetherian hypothesis, and points instead to a closed discrete `S` and connected `X`. An audit should separate the purely categorical construction already made from later local assertions for which dimension becomes important. These lines do not justify dropping all geometric assumptions from the conclusions on later scans.

### R07 — Canonical inertia versus a noncanonical copy of the profinite integers
**Scan 125, L03–10. Kind: unproved claim.** The page contrasts `Π_i ≃ Ẑ` “non canoniquement” with an “iso. can. Kummérien” from a local Tate system to inertia. A full verification would recover the exact local hypotheses from the preceding setup and check equivariance and compatibility of the Tate systems. The damaged opening sentence should not be replaced by whichever local hypotheses would make the statement familiar.

### R08 — A localization sequence for the glued topos
**Scan 125, L11–18. Kind: conjecture (expectation).** “On aimerait pouvoir paraphraser” introduces the wish to reproduce the localization long exact sequence on `B_{X,U}`. Scans 126–127 begin to realize this wish by identifying open and closed pieces, but the expectation should still be recorded as such at this point. The relevant comparison is between localization sequences, not merely between their individual terms.

### R09 — Objectwise limits and colimits
**Scan 126, L03–05. Kind: unproved claim.** Finite inverse limits and arbitrary direct limits of triples are said to be calculated “terme à terme.” A proof should check that the comparison maps of a limit or colimit triple exist and satisfy the universal property, using the actual variance of the functors in the gluing data. This check is also relevant to the subsequent assertions about the resulting topos.

### R10 — Explicit functors for the open and closed pieces
**Scans 126–127; scan 126, L06–16. Kind: unpursued implication.** The text describes the open object by a final `U`-component and an initial `D`-component and then describes the residual piece. A natural continuation is to write the associated restriction and extension functors explicitly on triples, including their adjunctions. The given objectwise descriptions alone do not record every derived functor needed later.

### R11 — The gluing functor
**Scan 127, L01–08. Kind: unproved claim.** The description “typique d'Artin” identifies the relevant functor with `σ_*ρ^*`. Its direction matters: a proof should identify the inverse image to the closed piece of the direct image from the open piece, rather than replace it by a similarly named inverse-image composite. This is a useful place to check all maps against the opening boxed formula.

### R12 — Reduction by the five lemma
**Scan 127, L14–20. Kind: unproved claim.** The author says that “par le lemme des cinq” it is enough to prove comparison on the open piece and on cohomology with support. To complete that reduction, one should construct a natural morphism of the two long exact sequences and check the degree ranges used by the argument. The reduction does not establish the open-piece comparison by itself.

### R13 — The degree-zero support functor
**Scan 128, L02–10. Kind: unproved claim / formula to check.** The prose explicitly requires elements “invariants par les Γ_i,” whereas the final product-of-kernels display has no clearly visible invariant decoration. It remains to decide whether the displayed kernel notation implicitly includes invariants or is merely an abbreviated calculation. The transcription preserves the visible formula instead of choosing an interpretation.

### R14 — The forgotten local calculation
**Scan 128, L11–20 and left margin. Kind: unproved claim.** The reduction is to the “calcul habituel” for a homomorphism of local groups, with the candid marginal qualification “que j'ai un peu oublié !”. Reconstructing that calculation would require specifying the supported-sections functor and its derived functors for the local glued category. The overwritten diagrams and local subscripts must first be collated sufficiently to identify the intended source and target.

### R15 — Dimension one and local fundamental topoi
**Scan 128, L21–24, continuing on scan 129, L02–04. Kind: unproved claim.** The local situation is said to be “déjà entièrement définie en termes des topos fondamentaux,” followed by an asserted equivalence with a local étale topos. The exact definitions of `D`, `D*`, and the localized points are not supplied in these ten scans. A proof must recover those definitions and the applicable hypotheses before treating the assertion as a general statement about every one-dimensional local scheme.

### R16 — The conditional comparison proposition
**Scan 129, L05–11. Kind: unproved claim.** The proposition is expressly conditional: the isomorphism of localization sequences holds “pourvu que l'on sache” the comparison between group cohomology and the cohomology of `U_ét`. The condition is quantified over the cohomological degrees in the text. A careful formulation should preserve both this condition and the coefficient data used to define `F_U`.

### R17 — The affine-curve example and coefficient restrictions
**Scan 129, L12–16. Kind: unproved claim.** In the affine-curve example over an algebraically closed field, the author writes “OK pour i=0,1” and discusses the vanishing of the two members for `i=2`, with interlinear prime-to-characteristic qualifications. A full proof must verify the coefficient restriction, the claimed group-theoretic freeness in the intended sense, and all degrees required by the preceding proposition. The visible `i=2` has not been silently changed to `i≥2`.

### R18 — Passage to a non-algebraically-closed ground field
**Scan 129, L16–18. Kind: unproved claim.** “D'où on déduit le cas analogue” announces a further case over a general ground field without supplying the descent argument here. One should identify the arithmetic comparison and the exact compatibility needed with the geometric calculation. The letter naming the affine object in this sentence is itself uncertain in the transcription.

### R19 — The quasi-projective question
**Scan 129, L18–19. Kind: conjecture (question).** The phrase “simplement quasi-projective ?” is explicitly interrogative. It should be studied with the surrounding dimension and coefficient hypotheses still attached; these lines do not establish a result for arbitrary higher-dimensional quasi-projective schemes. The particular passage from the affine case is not explained here.

### R20 — A proposed corollary is rejected or qualified in the margin
**Scan 129, left margin beside L12–19. Kind: conjecture (proposal withdrawn/qualified).** A proposed Galois-cohomological description is followed by “Non, à cause du genre 0 !!!”. The note then says that for smooth projective curves of nonzero genus “il faut un argument spécial.” This is evidence against reading the surrounding discussion as an unconditional assertion that the fundamental group always computes the full cohomology.

### R21 — The unpunctured case and positive characteristic
**Scan 130, L02–05 and right-side note. Kind: unproved claim.** For `S=∅`, the page writes a canonical degree-two comparison and an identification with `Ẑ`. The nearby note says, with a small wording uncertainty, “avec grain[s] de sel en car. p>0.” A precise theorem would need to resolve this coefficient/characteristic caveat and the preceding genus-zero warning, not omit either.

### R22 — Reconstruction with a discrete boundary
**Scan 130, L07–15. Kind: unproved claim.** The diagram identifies the boundary side as a “topos discret défini par S=I” and introduces components written `BL_i`. The clause linking these components is partly unreadable, so the exact assertion cannot safely be quoted in full. A reconstruction should first determine that clause and then distinguish the stated input local groups from any structure claimed to be recovered from them.
