## Second-pass notes

S1. **Scans 290–292 — implication not pursued (second pass).** The conjectural status of the functor (note 1) and programme item I (note 4) are the same open point. Scan 290 says the bijectivity of (∗) is not proved, "sauf si la situation de départ est réalisable, ou si I'! réduit à un seul élément, mais alors Γ cyclique et la situation est réalisable". The cyclic case, where the programme is to start (note 6), is therefore the case Grothendieck regards as accessible.

S2. **Scan 293 — question (second pass).** If the second 2-category is "opérations isotopiques" (actions up to isotopy, i.e. homomorphisms to a Teichmüller group), the classification theorem is a 2-categorical form of the realization problem studied here. For finite Γ, Kerckhoff's theorem on the Nielsen realization problem (1983) is the evident point of comparison. It was not checked against the corpus in this pass, and it does not by itself give the stronger 2-categorical statement or items II–III.

S3. **Scan 296 — check of a computation (second pass).** From u(ℓ) = int(g_i^{-1})ℓ^α and v(ℓ) = int(h_i^{-1})ℓ^β one gets (vu)(ℓ) = int(v(g_i)^{-1}h_i^{-1})ℓ^{αβ} = int((h_i v(g_i))^{-1})ℓ^{αβ}, as written. The rule (v,(h_i))(u,(g_i)) = (vu, h_i v(g_i)) is associative, with inverse (u^{-1}, (u^{-1}(g_i^{-1}))). It is the semidirect-product law on Aut(π) ⋉ π^I. The witnesses' "(uv)" and "h_i u(g_i)" are errors.

S4. **Scan 297 — question (second pass).** ℰ^! is written "Autext_{lac}(π, I)", but the pairs (u,(g_i)) need u to be an automorphism of π, since u(ℓ) = int g_i^{-1}(ℓ^α) is an equation in π. The kernel ∏ L_i of Sℰ^! → ℰ^! also presupposes this. On scan 300 Grothendieck writes ℰ_{g,ν} = Aut_{lac} with "ext" cancelled and puts π as the kernel of ℰ → Γ. So the "ext" on scan 297 should be read against scan 300, either as a slip or as a convention that changed.

S5. **Scan 294 — implication (second pass).** An element of SA_{g,ν} is the identity near every point of S_{g,ν}. On a connected surface it therefore fixes S_{g,ν} pointwise and preserves orientation, so SA_{g,ν} ⊂ A^{!+}_{g,ν}. This explains why the canonical map on scan 295 lands in Γ^{!+}_{g,ν}. It may also explain the cancelled "!" on SA: the superscript was redundant (a hypothesis).

S6. **Scan 300 — question (second pass).** The exclusions (g,ν) ≠ (0,0),(0,1) are exactly the cases π_{g,ν} = 1, as the cancelled "i.e. π_{g,ν} ≠ 0" says. For (g,ν) = (0,2), π ≅ ℤ and Aut_{lac}(π) is very small. Whether ℰ_{0,2} ≃ Γ_{0,3} needs a further exclusion or a specific convention (orientation character, stabilizer of the new point) should be checked against the definitions of §19.

S7. **Scans 295 and 297 — implication not pursued (second pass).** The cancelled lines on scan 297 identify the kernel of SΓ^!_{g,ν} → Γ^!_{g,ν} with (SA ∩ A°)/(SA)°. Grothendieck then drops this topological description in favour of the pairs (u,(g_i)) and the classifying-object description of scans 298–299. Comparing (SA ∩ A°)/(SA)° with T^I would show that the topological SΓ^! of scan 295 and the algebraic one coincide. The surviving text does not do this.
