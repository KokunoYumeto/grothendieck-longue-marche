## 4. Legibility and reliability

This is a provisional manual diplomatic reading, not an authoritative edition. The broad prose and most unaltered displayed equations are substantially more legible than the corrections. The weakest areas are the first scan's opening and membership annotations; the crowded margins and uniqueness clause of scan 54; the first paragraph and interlinear chain of scan 55; the oval addition and cancelled square on scan 56; and the first transporter quotient on scan 58. The last scan's proposed weakening and the exact scope of its final insertion also remain insecure.

The images were read visually, with enlarged crops and rotated views of the densest marginalia. Enlargement does not recover strokes that have been overwritten or lost in the scan. `\illegible{n}` therefore estimates unreadable visual word-units; it does not claim that a crossed mathematical symbol was literally a word. A proposed reading inside `\unsure` should not be cited without its uncertainty marker.

Paragraphs were not completed across missing context. Scan 51 begins in continuation; scan 60 ends in continuation. Large cancellations, including the affine-envelope draft on scan 59, remain in the fragment rather than being silently suppressed. The possible extent of that cancellation is documented above. No added mathematical argument in the research notes below changes a transcription reading.

## 5. Research notes — separate from the transcription

These notes are in English and are based on this extract alone. “Conjecture” below includes an explicitly qualified expectation, not necessarily a formally stated conjecture. A claim described as unproved here may have been proved earlier or later in the manuscript. Proposed proof tasks and examples are research suggestions, not claims of novelty or established results.

### R1 — Scan 51, approximately lines 5–19
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The equations `f'_{\mathcal E}=int(\mu)f_{\mathcal E}` and `\alpha'=p(\mu)\alpha`, together with their indexed analogues, suggest organizing the choices as objects and arrows of a groupoid rather than immediately taking a quotient set. The point worth checking is which simultaneous transformations preserve all local conditions, since the page displays both global and indexed conjugating elements. The doubtful ambient-group annotations must be settled before specifying that groupoid precisely.

### R2 — Scan 52, approximately lines 1–5
**Kind: unproved claim.** Grothendieck writes that injectivity of `\rho_i` determines the `\mu_{i'}` “de façon unique,” and gives the image-membership criterion `\alpha'_{i'}\alpha_{i'}^{-1}\in\rho_i(\mathcal E_i)`. A complete verification should distinguish uniqueness of a possible lift from compatibility of that lift with the local conjugation equation: the latter may use definitions or hypotheses outside these ten scans. The elementary uniqueness observation alone does not recover those omitted hypotheses.

### R3 — Scan 52, approximately lines 6–16
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The page identifies the residual choice of `\mu` through a centralizer condition and calls it “l'indétermination exacte.” It is worth separating existence of a compatible `\mu` from the stabilizer of an existing choice, and then checking the side on which the residual centralizer acts. This would make the subsequent passage from 0-morphisms to uniqueness transparent without assuming that the centralizer vanishes.

### R4 — Scan 53, approximately lines 1–4; continuation of scan 52
**Kind: conjecture.** The explicit qualification is “sauf erreur cela aussi implique `\mu_0=1`,” after the preceding page has required centralization of an open subgroup of `\pi`. The research question is the precise centralizer-triviality statement and the exact hypotheses on `\pi` under which it holds. The extract does not give a proof or a complete list of excluded cases, so it should not be quoted as an unconditional statement about profinite groups.

### R5 — Scan 53, approximately lines 2–7
**Kind: conjecture.** Grothendieck continues “il semble bien” and concludes that the relevant Hom-category is “discrète,” in the cases associated with dominant homomorphisms of algebraic curves. One should distinguish uniqueness of a 0-morphism when it exists from literal discreteness of a chosen presentation, as opposed to equivalence to a discrete category. The manuscript's preceding conventions on objects, 0-morphisms, and identification of conjugate representatives are needed to settle that distinction.

### R6 — Scan 54, approximately lines 1–2
**Kind: unproved claim.** “Le `i\in I` correspondant à `i'\in I'` est unique” produces the function `\tau`. A proof would need the relevant uniqueness property for the target peripheral subgroups and sufficient nontriviality of the source image. Those properties are not proved on this page and should not be inferred merely from the displayed containment in a conjugate.

### R7 — Scan 54, approximately lines 7–13, including the upper-left margin
**Kind: conjecture.** The main text contains “sans doute déterminé,” while the marginal note states that the centralizer in `\Gamma` of an open subgroup is “réduit à l'unité.” These concern different groups from the earlier geometric-kernel centralizer and should be checked separately. The exact uniqueness claim for `\alpha`, the normalizer/transporteur ambiguity for `\alpha_{i'}`, and the underlying assumptions on `\Gamma` all require verification; part of the connecting prose is itself uncertain.

### R8 — Scan 54, approximately lines 14–19 and lower-left margin
**Kind: unproved claim.** The compatibility formula `f^0_{i'}=(d_{i'}\chi(\beta_{i'}))id` is followed by the assertion that the positive integer is “évidemment uniquement déterminé.” The margin also claims invariance under a change of the indexed conjugating element, although its verb phrase is partly unreadable. Verification should track the change in both `f^0_{i'}` and `\beta_{i'}` and identify what makes the integer coefficient unique, without assuming the unreadable marginal scalar belongs to the unit group.

### R9 — Scans 51–54
**Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium. Reason:** the displayed transformations supply concrete equations, but several domain/codomain annotations remain doubtful. Test simultaneous conjugation and residual stabilizers in explicitly chosen finite-group analogues, keeping global and local subgroups distinct. Such tests could detect a convention or handedness error in a proposed interpretation; they would not prove a statement about the profinite objects of the manuscript.

### R10 — Scan 55, approximately lines 1–15
**Kind: unproved claim.** The “Réflexion taxonomique” identifies the dimension-zero case by injectivity of `p` and the dimension-one case by its failure. The scope of this criterion must be checked against the earlier definition of admissible objects, especially any restrictions incorporated into the curve data. It should not be detached from that setting and asserted for every scheme or every curve.

### R11 — Scan 56, approximately lines 9–21
**Kind: unproved claim.** After describing pairs `(f,\alpha)` and their equivalence, Grothendieck says the resulting “corr. 1-1” with the field/scheme Hom-set “est clair.” A detailed proof should fix the transporter convention, verify the effect of replacing `\alpha` by `\mu\alpha`, and show that this is exactly equality of the corresponding field homomorphisms. The final letter of the preceding compatibility formula is a textual doubt, not a license to rewrite it.

### R12 — Scan 57, approximately lines 1–9
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The condition `I'=\varnothing` in case II is explained using `f^{-1}(S)=S'`, alongside the nonexistence of a map from a nonempty `I'` to the empty `I`. This is an obstruction attached to the chosen definition of morphisms of marked pairs, not merely to the dimensions of the underlying schemes. The final page's proposed modification should therefore be checked against this earlier obstruction.

### R13 — Scan 57, approximately lines 10–20
**Kind: unproved claim.** The text associates the open image `\Gamma'_0` with `L'` and then uses the fixed isomorphism `L'\simeq H^0(X',\mathcal O_{X'})`. To justify the reduction to `L→L'`, one must identify the assumptions making the global-section object the stated field and explain the compatibility with the chosen Galois data. These hypotheses cannot be supplied just from the phrase “Les choix faits.”

### R14 — Scan 58, approximately lines 10–19
**Kind: unproved claim.** The transporter quotient is used to conclude “la pleine fidélité.” A proof should verify both the quotient's side of action and the passage from subgroup transporters to field transporters. The first quotient line is overwritten and partly doubtful, so a mathematical reconstruction must remain separate from the diplomatic reading.

### R15 — Scans 56 and 58, transporter calculations
**Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium. Reason:** these pages explicitly give representatives, an equivalence relation, and a field interpretation, but not all conventions. Write two independently defined maps, one from conjugating elements to field homomorphisms and one back to equivalence classes, and test their compositions before simplifying either quotient. This is a way of checking the intended formula without using the expected answer to decide what the pen strokes say.

### R16 — Scan 59, approximately lines 5–12
**Kind: unproved claim — cancelled draft.** The diagonally cancelled passage begins “Il suffit que `X'` soit un schéma connexe quasi-compact quasi-séparé” and reduces morphisms to homomorphisms `L→A'`. It is worth determining whether the cancellation rejects a hypothesis, an argument, or only the choice of presentation. The cancelled wording must not be represented as an unqualified final theorem of this extract.

### R17 — Scan 59, approximately lines 13–20; scan 60, lines 1–4
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The live replacement uses “Sections” of the finite étale pullback and a category of finite local systems, then returns to the common description of cases I and II. This suggests that the relevant data for maps to the particular dimension-zero target can be read at the level of finite étale objects rather than all geometric morphisms. The visible reversal of arguments in the displayed Hom-expression is retained in the transcript and requires separate interpretation before a proof is formalized.

### R18 — Scan 60, approximately lines 1–4
**Kind: unproved claim.** “Sans aucune hyp. autre … que … la 0-connexité” is a substantial relaxation of the earlier assumptions, but it belongs to the continuing discussion of cases I and II. A proof should state exactly what 0-connectedness means in the author's conventions and why the groupoid over the base can be described by the single homomorphism displayed here. It does not by itself establish a full-faithfulness claim for arbitrary targets.

### R19 — Scan 60, approximately lines 6–10
**Kind: conjecture.** The parenthetical “il suffirait `=` que …” appears to propose a weaker condition involving failure of local disconnection, with an inserted étale qualification. Because the wording is partly doubtful, the first research task is textual verification, followed by a precise formulation of the local condition and its connectedness consequence. No standard named condition is substituted for the manuscript's words.

### R20 — Scan 60, approximately lines 13–16
**Kind: unproved claim.** The equality `Hom_K(X'-S',X)=Hom_K(X',X)` is asserted under normality of `X'` at the points of `S'`. A proof should establish both extension and uniqueness in the current dimension-zero-target setting and specify the role of the local hypotheses near the removed subset. The equality should not be generalized here to unspecified targets.

### R21 — Scan 60, approximately lines 17–20
**Kind: conjecture / proposed change of definition.** “Ceci encourage à modifier la définition” explicitly proposes changing where the inverse-image condition on the marked set is imposed. The sentence ends after “la réunion des comp. irréd. de `X'`,” before the relevant components are characterized. The next scan is indispensable for stating the proposal completely; this extract does not authorize a completed definition.

### R22 — Scans 57 and 60, old obstruction versus proposed definition
**Kind: own idea. Confidence: low. Reason:** the motivation is visible, but the proposed selection of components is outside the supplied extract. Once the continuation is available, test the revised condition on sources with several components, including components whose maps have different image dimensions. Compare precisely which morphisms are excluded by `f^{-1}(S)_{réd}=S'` and which the proposed componentwise condition would admit; this is a proposed test, not a claim about what the completed manuscript says.
