## B. Second-pass notes [2nd pass]

**B1 — scans 485–489 — [2nd pass] unpursued implication.** The recurring datum is the arithmétisation a of (π,a) (ten
occurrences, all misread "authentisation"). On 489 each prédiscrétification has a "corresponding" arithmétisation; the
expectation of 486 (a unique Γ-invariant one) and of 489 (a canonical prédiscrétification whose arithmétisation is
Γ-invariant) fit together.

**B2 — scan 482 — [2nd pass] proof sketch. Confidence high.** For each g, arbitrarily large ν occur: take X over Q of
genus g and a Belyi map f: X → P¹ over Q; U = X − f⁻¹{0,1,∞} is of type (g,ν) and étale over U_{0,3}. Composing with
h(z) = z² (branched only over 0, ∞, with h{0,1,∞} ⊂ {0,1,∞}) gives a Belyi map with punctures f⁻¹{0,1,∞,−1}, so ν strictly
increases.

**B3 — scan 482 — [2nd pass] question. Confidence medium.** The curve U over Q is a Q-point of M_{g,ν}, and the arithmetic
fundamental group of M_{g,ν,Q} is its geometric part extended by the section of that point. So if the outer action of
ℾ_{0,3} on π_{g,ν} is the one coming from U (with ℾ_{0,3} ≃ G_Q), the decreed subgroup is the image of π_1(M_{g,ν,Q}) in
𝒯̂̂_{g,ν}, independent of the cover; whether every compatible outer action is of this kind is the open point.

**B4 — scan 483 — [2nd pass] own idea. Confidence high.** The literal margin statement fails for the closure of [π,π]: it
is characteristic in π, hence normal in ℰ (normaliser ℰ, open image), it is ≠ 1 and non-abelian, so it lies in no
procyclic L_i. The criterion must be read for a restricted class (abelian or procyclic closed subgroups), the form of
later group-theoretic characterisations of cuspidal inertia groups (H. Nakamura).

**B5 — scans 483–485 — [2nd pass] later results.** The full faithfulness for open homomorphisms and dominant morphisms is
the Hom form of the anabelian conjecture for hyperbolic curves, proved by S. Mochizuki (1999) over sub-p-adic fields,
number fields included; the isomorphism form used on 485 was proved by A. Tamagawa (1997) for affine hyperbolic curves
over number fields.

**B6 — scan 485 — [2nd pass] unpursued implication. Confidence medium.** With "isomorphismes" the deduction is direct: an
admissible lift comes from a pointed curve (U′,x′) whose Γ-outer group is isomorphic to that of U; full faithfulness for
isomorphisms gives U′ ≅ U, carrying x′ to a K-point of U whose lift is conjugate to the given one.

**B7 — scan 485 — [2nd pass] later result.** The commutant statement for U_{0,3} is the genus-0 Galois rigidity of
H. Nakamura (Galois rigidity of the étale fundamental groups of punctured projective lines, 1990); injectivity of
G_Q → 𝒯̂̂_{0,3} is Belyi's. The exact ambient (all outer automorphisms or lacet-preserving ones) should be checked
against Nakamura's statement before citing.

**B8 — scan 486 — [2nd pass] unproved claim. Confidence medium.** The inserted argument needs: in ℾ_π every open subgroup
has trivial centraliser. This is a standard property of absolute Galois groups of number fields, so it holds once ℾ_π is
identified with G_Q, the identification the page assumes (ℾ_{03} ≃ ℾ).

**B9 — scan 486 — [2nd pass] check. Confidence high.** 𝒯̂_{0,3} = 𝔖_3 (mapping classes of the thrice-punctured sphere
permute the punctures, the pure part is trivial), and 𝔖_3 = Aut(U_{0,3}) is defined over Q, so it commutes with ℾ_{0,3}.
The centraliser of ℾ_{0,3} in 𝔖_3 × ℾ_{0,3} is 𝔖_3 × Z(ℾ_{0,3}) = 𝔖_3, as the page says.

**B10 — scan 488 — [2nd pass] conjecture.** Admissible germs of splittings ↔ algebraic points of M_{g,ν,Q} is the section
conjecture for the moduli multiplicité (germs = sections over open subgroups = points over finite extensions). Grothendieck
formulated the section conjecture for hyperbolic curves in his 1983 letter to Faltings; for M_{g,ν} it is not known.

**B11 — scan 489 — [2nd pass] unpursued implication. Confidence medium.** Γ′ acts trivially on I(π′), so filling the hole
i ∈ I′ produces a point of the partially compactified curve rational over the field of Γ′; its decomposition group is a
splitting, which is how the outer action "est relevée en une action effective grâce à i". Hence "en I′", not its
complement.

**B12 — scan 490 — [2nd pass] proof. Confidence high.** If a path class φ: π(x) → π(y) sends π_0(x) into π_0(y), then
φ(π_0(x)) is a finitely generated subgroup of π_0(y), dense in π(y). π_0(y) is free or a surface group, hence LERF
(M. Hall 1949; P. Scott 1978), so that subgroup is closed in the profinite topology of π_0(y) and equals π_0(y): "dans"
is automatically "sur".

**B13 — scan 487 — [2nd pass] question.** The symbol of "pas de condition pour α|ν = 3" has a closed loop and differs from
the ν of "ν > 3" on the same line; the case meant is type (0,3), listed among the limit cases two lines below.

**B14 — scan 481 — [2nd pass] unpursued implication.** With "qui invarie π′" the construction starts: the effective action
of Γ″ on π̂_{03} normalises π′, so it restricts to an effective action on π′, from which filling holes and forgetting the
lift gives the outer action on π. The first pass's "laisse π′" left the condition incomplete.
