# LM_P050 — Transcription notes

## 1. Scope and editorial conventions

The accompanying `LM_P050_transcription.tex` is a UTF-8 LaTeX fragment, without a preamble or macro definitions. It transcribes the ten supplied image files, in filename order, from `LM_P050_scan491.jpg` through `LM_P050_scan500.jpg`. The French text is based on the supplied images; no published transcription has been used to supply or correct a reading. External literature is confined to the separately labelled research notes below.

The filename scan numbers are used in `\scanpage{…}`. They are one greater than the visible lower page labels. Original foliation visible at the top of a sheet is represented by `\mspage{…}`; the separate handwritten numerals at the lower left are retained with `\margin{…}`. The printed Montpellier page label, website address, and institutional footer beneath the black scan border are reproduction metadata and are not transcribed as manuscript text. No original top foliation is invented for pages on which none is visible.

Paragraphs follow the manuscript, while ordinary line-wrap hyphenation within a page has been joined. The split `géo-` / `métrique` at the first scan boundary is retained; page-spanning sentences otherwise remain incomplete at their boundaries. The fragment ends with the visible final comma, without supplying a continuation. Incomplete or awkward punctuation and grammatical forms have not been repaired.

Interlinear and marginal additions are attached where their position or connector indicates. Overwriting that permits a reading of both stages is represented linearly using the permitted deletion/insertion notation, or a deleted original followed by its replacement; this does not claim to reconstruct an exact writing chronology. A faint stroke that could be punctuation or a correction mark is marked as uncertain. `\illegible{n}` counts are estimates; for an isolated mathematical sign, one unreadable sign occupies one such unit. Unheaded line segments in the marginal diagram on scan 492 are preserved as unheaded segments, rather than being completed as arrows. The name `Белый` is retained in Cyrillic.

The confidence labels describe the readings, not the validity of the mathematics: **high** means the visible strokes are well supported; **medium** means a plausible reading has a locally doubtful letter, ending, or correction; **low** means alternatives remain materially unresolved. A reading may be intelligible mathematically and still have low visual confidence. Raw Cyrillic in the fragment requires a suitable Unicode-capable LaTeX setup and font when the fragment is incorporated into a document.

## 4. Legibility and limits of confidence

The continuous prose is substantially more legible than the revisions. Every page was read as a whole, transcribed, and checked again against the image; an independent visual reading checked the first seven scans, and the last three were also checked against the initial transcription. This is a conservative reading, not a claim of an error-free or complete recovery of cancelled ink.

The least secure passages are the basepoint symbol and overwritten/deleted text on scan 491; several compressed endings and the centre glyph on scan 492; the corrected curve notation and paragraph opening on scans 493–494; the identity and subgroup abbreviation on scan 495; the multiply revised puncture formula on scan 496; the overwritten curve letters and marginal τ on scan 497; the diagonal note beside Lemma 5 on scan 498; and the generator letters, inserted word provisionally read `disque`, and centralizer formula on scan 500. The repeated uncertain generator is not silently identified with a preferred mathematical notation. The explicit phrases about being stuck, presuming a result, admitting a lemma, and not having proved a claim remain in the transcription.

## 5. Research notes — English, separate from the transcription

These are reading and research leads, not a completion of the proofs. Manuscript statements below are attributed to Grothendieck (1981), with the supplied **scan numbers** as locators; bibliographic dating and the archive designation are taken from the supplied task. Notes marked **own idea** state a confidence and reason and make no claim of novelty. A point is retained even when another page in this batch returns to it. Any literature comparison is explicitly identified and does not change the French transcription.

1. **Scan 491, left margin; kind: unproved claim.** The margin qualifies the proof by referring to «le lemme 2 plus bas». The announced injectivity theorem therefore has an explicitly recorded dependency; it should not be catalogued as an unconditional completed proof solely from its opening statement. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 491).

2. **Scan 491, Lemma 1 proof; kind: unproved claim.** The interlinear reduction says «Il suffit de voir que l'opération extérieure» on the punctured curve is trivial. A separate proof audit should make explicit how that outer action recovers the action based at the removed rational point, and which peripheral or decomposition data are being used. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 491).

3. **Scan 491, lower third; kind: unproved claim.** The assertion attributed to `Белый` requires a map «défini sur Q» and sending the specified rational x to 0. The strengthened pointed version, its field of definition, and the precise inclusion of open curves needed for the following quotient map deserve verification separately from the transcription. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 491).

4. **Scan 492, normalizer paragraph; kind: unpursued implication.** The text controls the image in outer automorphisms by the finite quotient `N/π′`. One could isolate the exact group-extension lemma behind this step, including the dependence on the centre Z and the compatibility of the two extensions in the margin diagram. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 492).

5. **Scan 493, Lemme 2 and following paragraph; kind: unproved claim.** The two assertions are triviality of the centre and absence of nonidentity torsion in the free profinite group on two generators; Grothendieck says «je reporte pour plus tard». Both are used to pass from a finite group of inner automorphisms to the identity, and the later return to these assertions should be tracked separately. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 493).

6. **Scan 494, first paragraph; kind: unproved claim.** Grothendieck warns that a finite outer-action quotient «n'est pas suffisant» to prove the action trivial. This is a useful checkpoint in auditing the argument: finiteness alone does not complete Lemma 3, and the later based action is a separate input. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 494).

7. **Scan 494, middle and lower paragraphs; kind: unpursued implication.** He says one could replace U by «n'importe quelle courbe algébrique qu.proj. lisse géométriquement connexe» for the finiteness stage, but then uses the existence of a rational point on the open subset of the projective line. The number-field corollary later in this batch should be compared with this precise point at which rational points enter. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 494).

8. **Scan 494, rational-point passage; kind: own idea.** Compare the argument with a version based on a closed point after finite scalar extension, keeping track of the residual finite kernel before trying to remove it. **Confidence: medium:** the page itself identifies a rational basepoint as the extra ingredient, and the later pages visibly introduce a finite extension; this is a proposed proof comparison, not a claim of novelty or a completed argument. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 494).

9. **Scan 495, approximately lines 10–15 — Unproved claim.** The assertion that the action on `I=S(Q̄₀)` is “déduite de l'action extérieure” through the conjugacy classes of “ss-groupes lacets” (abbreviation uncertain) is invoked without a proof on this page. A detailed argument would have to identify the peripheral subgroup conjugacy classes, explain their Galois equivariance, and account for the inserted condition `card I≥2`. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 495).

10. **Scan 495, approximately lines 17–21 — Unpursued implication.** The passage “Ceci étant vrai pour tt V” converts statements about finite complements of open subsets of the projective line into triviality of the action on all algebraic points. It would be useful to specify a cofinal family of such complements and spell out how each algebraic number occurs in one of them. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 495).

11. **Scan 495, approximately lines 10–15 — Own idea (confidence: medium).** Test the peripheral-conjugacy-class argument first when the complement has exactly two points, since this is the boundary included by the inserted inequality. The reason for the test is that a rank-one geometric fundamental group can make two oppositely oriented inertia generators generate the same closed subgroup; the manuscript's precise meaning of “ss-groupes lacets” (abbreviation uncertain) therefore matters. This is a proposed check, not a correction to the transcription or a claim of novelty. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 495).

12. **Scan 496, approximately lines 10–14 — Conjecture / explicit unresolved question.** “Finalement je cale sur a)” expressly leaves the asserted triviality of the centre of the free profinite group unproved at this point. The further instruction “Consulter Deligne à ce sujet !” records a proposed consultation rather than a proof; any later resolution must be distinguished from the state of these pages. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 496).

13. **Scan 496, approximately lines 11–14 — Conjecture / explicit unresolved question.** Grothendieck says that he also does not see “pourquoi le centre de Γ” and the centres of its open subgroups must be trivial; the abbreviated or compressed word before “sous-groupe ouvert” is uncertain (`tt` or `tout`). This is a separate question about the absolute Galois group and its open subgroups, and should not be conflated with the free-profinite-group assertion immediately above it. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 496).

14. **Scan 496, approximately lines 17–24 — Unproved claim.** The torsion argument uses the assertion “Comme π est libre” to obtain a section of `E_L→π`. An expanded proof would specify the profinite universal property being used, the continuity of the section, and why the chosen punctured projective line realizes the relevant finite free rank; the overlapping corrections to the number of removed points make this last detail particularly worth checking. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 496).

15. **Scan 496, approximately lines 17–24 — Own idea (confidence: high as a proof-checking strategy).** Track the number of punctures and the free rank separately throughout this construction, keeping `ν=card I` distinct from `ν+1 points`. The reason is the manuscript's explicit revision at exactly this step; this is an audit of the written argument, not an assertion that the corrected construction is new or already fully proved here. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 496).

16. **Scan 497, approximately lines 2–6 — Unproved claim / cited external input.** “Mais d'après Artin” introduces the field-theoretic statement on nontrivial finite-order automorphisms of an algebraically closed field. A full account would identify the precise Artin result, explain the order-two conclusion and the description of the fixed field as an ordered maximal field, and distinguish these external inputs from the subsequent argument written here. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 497).

### Additional literature comparisons and proof checks

31. **Scans 493 and 496, centre assertion — own idea; confidence: high as a textual comparison.** Compare the admitted difficulty with Belyi's printed corollary `c(F,F)=1`, where F is the profinite completion of the free group on two generators (Belyi, 1980, §1, pp. 248–249). **Reason:** the primary paper states that corollary explicitly; comparing its proof with the present dependency may clarify the rank-two step, without inferring what Grothendieck had read or resolving his separate question about the centre of Γ. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 493, 496).

32. **Scans 491 and 498, use of Белый — own idea; confidence: medium.** Audit the marked-point and field-of-definition requirements against Belyi's polynomial construction in Theorem 4, rather than treating the bare three-branch-point criterion as all that is needed (Belyi, 1980, §4, pp. 255–256). **Reason:** the manuscript asks for the specified rational point to go to 0 and later for an open covering over k; checking those extra features is a concrete proof obligation. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 491, 498).

33. **Scans 497 and 499, appeal to Artin — unproved claim / bibliographic follow-up.** The phrase “d'après Artin” should be matched carefully to the finite-algebraic-closure and ordered-fixed-field results; Artin and Schreier (1927) is an original-paper lead. Its publication metadata have been checked against the journal record, but its full proof has not been audited for this transcription; the precise match remains a research task. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 497, 499).
