## 4. Honest assessment of legibility

This is a provisional diplomatic reading with substantial local uncertainty. Most of the main French prose is more legible than the small mathematical subscripts. Scans 418–420 have comparatively clear principal text; the isolated formulas in 417 and the surviving condition (3) on 413 are also reasonably secure. That does not resolve their unclear primes, overwritten signs, or compressed endings.

The least trustworthy regions are the oblique marginal notes and cancelled replacement layers on 412–414, the crowded opening and centralizer/transport passage on 416, and the upper middle node and small internal annotation of diagram (26). The first inclusion in the right column of (30) reaches the black border and remains incomplete. The small diagram and overwritten vertical relation on 420 are described or left as a lacuna. The limits of cancellation strokes and the attachment of a few insertions are sometimes as uncertain as the words themselves.

Independent rereading corrected a spurious extra closing parenthesis on 411, rejected an apparent page reference in its margin in favor of a doubtful dotted-i expression, and recovered brackets around the diagonal on 416. Bare or ambiguous line endings in diagram (26) remain unheaded and are described explicitly. None of those checks makes the unresolved passages secure.

## 5. Research notes — English, separate from the transcription

All source-attributed entries below refer to **Grothendieck (1981)**, with the supplied scan and approximate location specified in each entry. These are observations about what is stated, required, questioned, or left unproved **within this supplied passage**; a point may be settled elsewhere in the manuscript. No claim of novelty or of agreement with later literature is made. Items labelled **own idea** are editorial research suggestions, not statements attributed to Grothendieck. Their confidence and reasons are stated separately from paleographic confidence.

1. **Scan 412, condition (1), lines 14–15 — conjecture / question.** After admissibility under passage to an open subgroup, the author adds « et inversement ? ». This explicitly raises a converse; a proof would have to address whether admissibility of a restricted action descends to the original acting group under the proposed definition.

2. **Scan 412, condition (2), lines 16–18 — unproved claim / proposed axiom.** The numbered requirement says that after « bouchant I′ », « l'opération de Γ sur π′ est admissible ». Here this belongs to a list introduced by « On suppose »; it should be tested as a requirement on the proposed admissibility class, not cited as a theorem proved on this page.

3. **Scan 412, lower-left margin beside (2) — unproved claim, provisional reading.** The note contains « Il suffit » and apparently « card I′=1 »; the verb between them remains doubtful (prouver / poser). If that reading is confirmed, the reduction needs a justification for iterating one-hole filling, including the way the chosen holes and actions change at each step; the damaged ending must not be treated as a recovered proof.

4. **Scan 412, conditions (1)–(2) — own idea.** Test separately the one-hole reduction for a hole fixed by Γ and for a finite Γ-orbit of holes. Confidence: medium as a diagnostic, because the main text requires an invariant part I′ while the marginal reduction appears to use cardinality one; whether subgroup restriction bridges this difference is a question, not a conclusion.

5. **Scan 413, lines 2–9 — unproved claim.** « On trouve ainsi une équivalence » relates a system with a Γ-stable i ∈ I(π) and an exterior operation to one with a « vraie action ». The equivalence is asserted without an accompanying proof here; a proof would need the two functors, their effects on morphisms, and the natural identifications of their composites.

6. **Scan 413, revised middle paragraph — conjecture / expectation.** The author writes that « l'inverse ne sera pas vrai » and predicts « des relèvements “pathologiques” » of an admissible exterior action. These pages give no example at this point; it is worth identifying exactly which extra property of a lift fails and whether the failure persists after passage to an open subgroup.

7. **Scan 413, surviving condition (3) — unproved claim / proposed axiom.** The condition requires π^Γ=(1) when Γ acts in the specified admissible way; the interlinear « effect. » is part of the statement. This is introduced by « On suppose de plus », so the page offers it as a requirement, not a proved fixed-point theorem.

8. **Scan 413, final paragraph — unproved implication.** The author concludes « Cela implique que la catégorie des Γ-Pts … est rigide ». A direct verification would identify the automorphisms of a section with the appropriate fixed subgroup of π and check that the stated admissibility hypothesis applies to every relevant section; that proof is not written on this page.

9. **Scan 413, condition (3) and final paragraph — own idea.** Separate the existence of section objects from the vanishing of their automorphism groups when testing the proposed category. Confidence: high as a logical distinction, since the displayed condition controls a fixed subgroup while the conclusion concerns rigidity; no existence claim follows from that distinction alone.

10. **Scan 411, lines 1–9 — conjecture / explicit doubt.** The page first says « sauf erreur » that a functor is an « équivalence de catégories », then immediately retracts confidence: « ce n'est pas du tout évident ». This is an explicit hesitation, not an established equivalence on this page; its preceding definition is outside the supplied passage.

11. **Scan 411, lines 10–17 — unproved claim / proposed framework.** The author proposes to « élucider la situation axiomatiquement », using closed subgroups and the « condition essentielle » that the displayed maps induce isomorphisms.
