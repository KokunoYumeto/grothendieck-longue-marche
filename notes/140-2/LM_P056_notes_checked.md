## B. Notes added in the second pass

**B1. Scan 552, claim verified (second pass).** The cancelled "ne … pas" and "égales [à l'identité]" show that Grothendieck first wrote that the isomorphisms between the four stabilisers are not induced by the bijections equal to the identity on I∖{i,j}, and then that they are induced by the canonical bijections (37), equal to the transposition there. Check for i = 4, j = 1: through Ĩ, 1 ↦ {1,4}|{2,3} ↦ 4, 2 ↦ {2,4}|{1,3} ↦ 3, 3 ↦ {3,4}|{1,2} ↦ 2, the bijection written on 555, which is the transposition of {2,3} = I∖{1,4}.

**B2. Scans 553 and 555, claim verified (second pass).** Identifying [1,2,3] and [2,3,4] with (0,1,∞) in increasing order turns 1 ↦ 4, 2 ↦ 3, 3 ↦ 2 into 0 ↦ ∞, 1 ↦ 1, ∞ ↦ 0, the transposition of the elements ≠ 1, that is σ_1. This confirms int(σ_1) in (39) and answers the question of 553: the two actions of 𝔖_3 are conjugate by σ_1 rather than equal.

**B3. Scan 554, unproved claim supported (second pass).** The affine group AGL(2,F_2) = F_2² ⋊ GL(2,F_2) has order 4 · 6 = 24 and acts faithfully on the four points, so it is all of 𝔖_I: every permutation is affine. GL(2,F_2) acts faithfully on the three non-zero vectors, which gives 𝔖_Ĩ ≅ Aut(V(I)) through (33).

**B4. Scan 556, implication (second pass).** "équivaut bien sûr": since M!_{0,I} represents the functor (rigid for card I ≥ 3), an element σ of 𝔖_I acts trivially if and only if every family (X, (s_i)) is isomorphic to its relabelling (X, (s_{σi})), i.e. there is an automorphism u of X with u ∘ s_i = s_{σi}. The uniqueness of u, noted as "évidemment unique", is what lets the local isomorphisms glue, so the corollary on 556 for the three even involutions is the Proposition of 552.

**B5. Scan 556, slip (second pass).** The proof writes "s_1 = 0, s_{i_2} = ∞", then "… , s_{i_3} s'identifient à des sections de O_S^*", then "pour échanger s_2, s_3" followed by λ/s_3 = s_4, λ/s_4 = s_3. The prose indices should be those of the two remaining sections (s_3, s_4, or s_{i_3}, s_{i_4}); the transcription keeps the ink and the witnesses normalise it.

**B6. Scan 557, own idea (second pass, medium).** In (43) the left vertical arrow is an isomorphism, so a diagram chase shows that the upper extension is the pullback of the lower one along Γ_n → Γ'_n = Γ_n/±1; the kernel of π_1(M_{1,1}) → π_1(M'_{1,1}) is then {±1}, central because −1 is central in GL(2, Z/n). This is the Proposition "extension centrale … par {±1}". For n = 2 one has −1 = 1 in GL(2, F_2), so Γ_2 = Γ'_2 and the sign cannot be seen at level 2: the reconstruction must use n ≥ 3 (as in the first line of (41)), and level 2 needs the replacement M_{1,1}[2]' ("m̄ grain de sel pour n = 2").

**B7. Scan 557, unproved claim supported (second pass).** The Weil pairing of a basis of E[n] is a primitive n-th root of unity, so the components of M_{1,1}[n] over Q̄ are indexed by the φ(n) primitive n-th roots of unity, and Gal(Q̄/Q) permutes them transitively exactly because the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_n is irreducible over Q. Hence M_{1,1}[n] is connected but, for n ≥ 3, not geometrically connected, which is the content of the margin note.

**B8. Scan 559, slip (second pass).** The annotation over π_1(M̄'_{1,1}) in (49) reads "≃ Sl(2,Z)^" without the prime, while (45) and (50) identify this group with Sl(2,Z)'^ = Sl(2,Z)^/±1. Since M'_{1,1} is the quotient by −1, the primed group is the right one; the transcription keeps the ink.

**B9. Scan 560, own idea (second pass, high).** If the coordinate on the coarse scheme is the j-invariant, the two marked sections are j = 0 and j = 1728 (the curves with automorphism groups of orders 6 and 4), and since 1728 = 2⁶ · 3³ they meet exactly over the primes 2 and 3. This agrees with the next manuscript page as given by the CSG witness ("elles se rencontrent en caractéristique 2 et en caractéristique 3"), and the integer "±2^a 3^b" that the author says he does not know by heart there would be 1728, i.e. a = 6, b = 3, once j = 0 is taken as the zero section and the sign is fixed as plus.
