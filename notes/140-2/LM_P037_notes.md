## 4. Legibility and limits

The running prose is moderately legible, and the larger displayed formulas are often clearer than the surrounding corrections. The weakest areas are the revised normalizer sentence on scan 362; the overwritten common-group, puncture-set, and arithmetic-class symbols on scan 364; the equivalence definition and angled marginal note on scan 365; the short word before the discretified group on scan 367; and the small added adjective and parameter on scan 370. Word endings, primes, and small accents remain a recurrent source of uncertainty. No numerical accuracy estimate is claimed.

The transcription preserves the apparent repeated `(1)` label, the absent stabilizer indices in displays (3)–(4), the unprimed arguments in the image identification on scan 368, and the diagram's double-headed arrow and double prime. These have not been corrected by inference. Stacked hats and tildes are distinguished; uncertain overwritten alphabets and insertion readings remain marked. The fragment was checked for LaTeX syntax in a temporary wrapper, which is not part of the requested deliverable.

## 5. Research notes — separate from the transcription

These are observations and proposed investigations prompted by this extract, not a literature survey or a verification of the assertions. They remain subject to uncertain readings, omitted earlier definitions, and possible treatment later in the manuscript. **Own idea** identifies an editorial proposal, with confidence and reason; no proposal is presented as established or novel. Each source location below refers to Grothendieck (1981).


### R01. Scan 361, approximately ll. 5–9 — Unproved claim

The revised wording `on doit pouvoir montrer sans trop de mal` introduces the proposed description of the two groups in (8) as normalizers of `G` in the corresponding larger groups. A proof would need the definitions and hypotheses preceding this extract and would have to establish both containments; the manuscript here only says that this would imply condition a).

### R02. Scan 361, approximately ll. 10–16 — Conjecture / doubt

Grothendieck writes `je ne vois aucune raison plausible que (b) soit vrai` and interprets it as an equality of the arithmetic information defined by a larger group and a smaller normalizer group. The relevant noun is tentatively read as `transcendance`; the uncertainty belongs to the transcription and should not be resolved by the mathematical interpretation.

### R03. Scan 361, approximately ll. 17–19 — Unpursued implication

The statement that conditions a) and b) `ne sont pas impératives` separates these auxiliary conditions from the fundamental conjecture relating the groups of type `(g,ν)` to `Γ₀`. Studying the precise logical dependencies would require recovering the omitted definitions of a), b), and (8); this extract alone cannot justify replacing those conditions by weaker ones.

### R04. Scan 362, approximately ll. 1–7 — Unproved claim

The words `il faudrait absolument avoir c)` link the definition of the displayed maps to condition c), while condition a) is said to give `les injectivités qu'il faut`. A proof audit should distinguish existence of those maps from injectivity and should use the actual hypotheses of c), which are not stated afresh here.

### R05. Scan 362, approximately ll. 8–13 — Conjecture / question

The question beginning `on se demande bien pourquoi` asks why belonging to a normalizer and normalizing its intersection with the positively marked group should force normalization of the whole positively marked group. Because the sentence has been extensively revised, its exact hypotheses must be checked against the source before formulating a formal assertion.

### R06. Scan 362, approximately ll. 14–19 — Conjecture / expectation

The restrictive hypotheses concerning a `ss-groupe car.` are expected to `se refléter par des propriétés particulières de G`. Identifying those properties is an open task on this page; the final sentence begins to question whether passing to characteristic subgroups has simplified the problem at all.

### R07. Scan 362, approximately ll. 8–16 — Own idea

As an abstract diagnostic, one could search for finite triples `(E,K,G)` in which an element of `N_E(G)` normalizes `N_E(G)∩K` but does not normalize `K`, then ask which extra hypotheses from the manuscript would exclude such triples. **Confidence: medium** that this is a useful logical test, since it isolates the normalizer implication, but **low** that an arbitrary finite example would model the profinite geometric groups at issue; this is a proposed test, not a counterexample to Grothendieck's setting.

### R08. Scan 363, approximately ll. 2–8 — Conjecture / proposed investigation

`Pour y voir plus clair` introduces two concrete cases: an index-two subgroup with `G ≃ Z/2`, and the kernel of the canonical map to the mod-two abelianized quotient. The handwritten condition explicitly distinguishes `ν=0` from `ν≥1`; those cases should remain separate when testing the preceding normalizer questions.

### R09. Scan 363, approximately ll. 9–11 — Unproved claim

The example `x²+y²+z²=0` is called a `revêtement octaédral` of the punctured projective line. The covering map, the precise open subcurve, and the action intended by `octaédral` are not given on this page; verifying the example requires specifying them before computing its automorphisms or inertia.

### R10. Scan 363, approximately ll. 12–20 — Conjecture / provisional assumption

The wording `admettons provisoirement` first assumes injectivity hypotheses and c), and then `en admettant même, provisoirement (pour voir)` supposes that (9) is an isomorphism. This is an exploratory assumption, not a proof that the injection is onto; a dependency analysis should track exactly which later constructions use this stronger hypothesis.

### R11. Scan 363, approximately ll. 6–11 — Own idea

One could write explicit generators for the kernel of the mod-two abelianization map in the `(0,3)` case and compare its peripheral classes with those of the written conic example. **Confidence: medium**, because the page itself singles out both constructions, but their identification and the interpretation of `octaédral` still require verification; no equivalence is asserted here.

### R12. Unproved claim — scan 364, approximately lines 10–12

Grothendieck writes “s'il le faut” and then “sinon on pourrait factoriser” when arranging that the sets of holes filled by the two legs have empty intersection. A proof would need to construct the quotient that fills the common holes and check that both induced legs still have the stated form: a hole-filling morphism followed by an isomorphism onto a finite-index subgroup. The heavily revised names of the common group and its puncture set should be resolved before using this as a formally stated reduction.

### R13. Own idea — scan 364, approximately lines 10–23

It would be useful to test whether eliminating holes filled by both legs also preserves the arithmetic class required by the pullback equality at the end of the page, rather than only preserving the underlying correspondence of groups. **Confidence: medium.** The reason is that the later definition depends on a compatible class on the common group, and the present reduction is stated before that compatibility is imposed; this is a question to check, not a claimed obstruction or a new result.

### R14. Unproved claim — scan 365, approximately lines 2–6

The two triangles are introduced as a “diagramme d'isom. de torseurs,” and their composition produces a map between the displayed torsor–group pairs. What needs verification is that each leg really induces the asserted isomorphism under the hypotheses then in force, including the corresponding group map. The arrowheads and prime marks have deliberately not been regularized, so the exact diagram should be established before any proof is reconstructed from it.

### R15. Unproved claim — scan 365, approximately lines 13–16
