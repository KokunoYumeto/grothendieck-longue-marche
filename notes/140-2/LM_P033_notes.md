## 4. Legibility and limits

The ordinary horizontal prose is generally more legible than the marginalia and revisions. The least trustworthy portions are the diagonal note on scan 321, the marginal challenge to the normalizer conjecture on scan 322, the overwritten symbols below (15), the altered first equation in the conjugacy proof on scan 325, the tiny barred references and operator subscripts on scans 327–328, and the lower row of (25) and final interlined qualification on scan 329. The number of hats, the natural-like superscript, the capital naming the normalizer quotient, and some word endings remain real uncertainties.

The normalizer passage deserves particular caution: a nearby annotation calls the earlier thought “ultra-faux” and “un moment d'égarement !”, but several words linking the annotation to its replacement question are difficult. The first paragraph of scan 330 is canceled as a whole; it has been retained with its internal corrections rather than treated as a current assertion. The unclosed parenthesis near the end of scan 329 and the unfinished final “Ainsi π” are preserved.

Each page was read and compared with its draft; especially difficult passages received separate readings. Disagreement led to alternatives or an illegibility mark, rather than a mathematically convenient decision. This remains a provisional diplomatic transcription, not a certified critical edition. The LaTeX fragment was checked for balanced structure and parsed with a temporary wrapper defining the requested editorial macros; the wrapper is not part of the deliverable.

## 5. Research notes — separate from the transcription

All source-based entries below refer to **Grothendieck (1981)**, with the supplied scan number and a local anchor as the locator. “Unproved claim” means that the supplied passage does not give a full proof, not that the statement has never been proved elsewhere. Own ideas are explicitly identified, carry a confidence and reason, and assert neither established results nor novelty. No entry changes the transcription.

1. **Scan 321, main lines 4–8 — Unproved claim.** The manuscript says the classes of discretifications form an “espace hom.” and identifies the stabilizer with “l'adhérence” of the discrete automorphism group. A proof would need the exact definition of the class equivalence, the topology on the ambient automorphism group, and a verification that the stabilizer is precisely that closure; these ingredients are not supplied on this page.

2. **Scan 321, main lines 9–18 and (10) — Unpursued implication.** “Celle-ci contient toujours π” and the inclusion of extensions suggest studying how much of a discretification class is remembered after passage to outer automorphisms. This page alone does not establish that the outer subgroup determines the class.

3. **Scan 321, left marginal quotient diagram — Own idea.** Compare the fibers of the quotient by the group marked + with the quotient by the group without +, once the marginal notation has been verified. Confidence: medium as a research direction, because the two displayed quotients and the final reference to “orientées” are visible, while the intervening explanatory words are not securely readable.

4. **Scan 322, first paragraph — Unproved claim.** “On montre par voie arithmético-géométrique” precedes the assertion that the inclusion of the one-hat group into the two-hat group “n'est jamais un iso.” The promised arithmetic-geometric argument is absent here; its range of types and any exceptional cases would need to be checked against the surrounding manuscript.

5. **Scan 322, (11) and following paragraph — Unproved claim.** The map to closed subgroups is said to be “évidemment compatible” with the indicated action, and its values form a “classe de conjugaison, bien déterminée”. A proof would need to distinguish the action on discretification data from conjugation on the associated outer subgroup and verify independence of representatives.

6. **Scan 322, lower main paragraph — Conjecture, with a marginal challenge.** The text proposes that the geometric group be “son propre normalisateur” and associates this with a property of map (11), whose final word is transcribed with doubt. The adjacent margin calls the preceding thought “ultra-faux” and “un moment d'égarement !”; this must not be reported as an unqualified conjecture maintained by the author.

7. **Scan 322, lower left margin — Conjecture / question, uncertain reading.** The annotation appears to ask whether the one-hat group is “invariant” in the two-hat group, rather than self-normalizing. Its connective words and precise relation to the main conjecture are partly obscured, so any stronger interpretation should await a more secure reading.

8. **Scan 323, (12)–(13) — Unpursued implication.** The character χ and the homomorphism involving I(π) are followed by the variants marked !, +, and !+. Comparing the corresponding kernels could clarify what information about multiplicators and the permutation of the indicated conjugacy classes survives under the passage to a geometric subgroup; the handwritten target alphabet of (13) remains uncertain.

9. **Scan 323, after (13) — Unproved claim.** “χ ne prend sur” the one-hat group “que les valeurs ±1”. A verification would have to connect the character's definition with the geometric source of this subgroup; that derivation is not given on the page.

10. **Scan 323, lower paragraph — Conjecture.** “On supposerait plutôt” that the subgroup marked + is “invariant” in the two-hat group. This expectation is stated separately from the assertion that it has index 2 in its associated one-hat group, and the two assertions should not be conflated.

11. **Scans 323–324, page break — Conjecture.** The parenthesis beginning “peut-être” continues “n'y en a-t-il qu'un seul et unique!” in the definition of strict geometric Teichmüller subgroups. The suggested uniqueness is stronger than the ability to construct these subgroups from discretification classes and should be investigated separately from uniqueness of those classes.
