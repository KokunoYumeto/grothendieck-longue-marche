## 4. Legibility and limits

The supplied contrast-enhanced images make most running prose readable, but the pen strokes remain very thin in places. Scan 182 is substantially harder than the others because several layers of revision, small mathematical subscripts, and interlinear additions overlap. The top conditions on scans 182 and 187, the first coefficient field on scan 181, the objects and maps in the middle of scan 184, the ornate group letter and lower homotopy display on scan 188, and the final kernel annotation on scan 190 should not be quoted without returning to the images.

This is a provisional diplomatic reading, not a clean critical edition. Explicit uncertainty marks are intentionally frequent. They do not guarantee that every remaining unmarked reading is correct: punctuation, whether a short stroke is a prime or part of a letter, the distinction between handwritten `\pi` and `\Pi`, and whether a faint line is a cancellation or an insertion leader remain potential sources of error. The fonts selected for ordinary handwritten letters are typographical readings of the ink, not an independent identification of a mathematical convention.

No mathematical inconsistency has been repaired, no missing argument supplied inside the fragment, and no continuation after the last scan invented. The research notes below are not alternative readings. A temporary wrapper with definitions of the requested editorial macros was used to check that the fragment compiles with standard mathematical LaTeX and `tikz-cd`; that syntax check is not evidence for the correctness of the paleographic readings.

## 5. Research notes — English, separate from the transcription

These observations are limited to the supplied pages. A claim may be proved elsewhere in the manuscript. No securely readable occurrence of an explicit “je conjecture” or equivalent conjectural announcement has been identified here; the clearest explicit deferral concerns compatibility checks. The items labelled “own idea” are proposals for investigation, not established conclusions or claims of novelty.

### R01. The explicitly unfinished abelian case

**Scan:** 181, approximately lines 12–15. **Kind:** unproved claim.

Grothendieck writes that the abelian case “n'est pas tout à fait démontré” and points to the topological classification of finite-group operations; the complex one-dimensional expression has an uncertain exponent, whereas the displayed product `S^1 × S^1` is clearer. A proof would have to provide the missing classification or a sufficiently precise substitute for the action statement actually used. The remark should not be converted into a blanket claim that the entire surrounding argument is already complete.

### R02. The fixed-point reduction in the same remark

**Scan:** 181, approximately lines 16–25. **Kind:** unproved claim.

Under the written hypothesis `U^\Gamma ≠ ∅`, the text reduces the assertion to a faithful action and asks for triviality of the group when its operation on `\pi` is trivial. The closing reference, “on fait comme plus haut”, does not reproduce the needed earlier argument on these supplied pages. Verification should keep the common-fixed-point hypothesis and the faithful-action reduction explicit.

### R03. Realizations and chosen groupoid objects

**Scan:** 182, approximately lines 11–25. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The realization discussion repeatedly uses “à isom. unique près” in relating group-theoretic choices to objects of the displayed fundamental groupoids. A careful proof should specify the categories of choices and their morphisms, rather than replacing the stated correspondence by a correspondence of isomorphism classes alone. Several indices and additions in this passage are unreadable, so a precise reconstruction of its functors must await a firmer reading of those particular symbols.

### R04. Paths, peripheral subgroups, and equivariance

**Scans:** 183, approximately lines 20–23; 184, lines 1–3. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The text asserts an “application surjective” from the indicated path-isomorphism set to the family of subgroups and then states compatibility with the natural action. A proof should track the chosen point on the small boundary, the two boundary loops, and the action by composition versus the action by inner automorphisms. Surjectivity should not be strengthened to a bijection without computing the fibers.

### R05. Constancy as the descent mechanism for filling

**Scans:** 184, approximately lines 16–21; 185, lines 1–11. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The assertion that “ce foncteur est constant” is accompanied here by the argument that an element of the chosen peripheral subgroup becomes `1` in the quotient, so its induced inner action is trivial. This is not simply an omitted proof: the relevant argument is present. A useful further formulation would isolate exactly how triviality of these automorphism actions gives the asserted independence of the chosen groupoid object.

### R06. The remaining peripheral structure after filling

**Scan:** 185, approximately lines 12–22. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The passage from “On trouve ainsi un foncteur” to the homomorphism of automorphism groups treats the filled quotient as a group with its own lacet structure. A complete verification should track every remaining peripheral class and every allowed morphism, not just the underlying quotient group. The written source and target type indices should first be reconciled by examining the manuscript's conventions, not by altering this transcription.

### R07. The partial amalgamated construction

**Scan:** 186, approximately lines 7–18. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The expression “somme amalgamée partielle” and the four-arrow diagram are used to obtain a distinguished object after filling. The construction warrants a precise account of the categorical setting, its universal property, and how the displayed map from the filled point determines the claimed object. The uncertain noun in the subsequent sentence should not be taken as a securely established technical term.

### R08. The equivalence theorem and its range

**Scans:** 186, final line; 187, approximately lines 1–11. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The theorem asserts an “équivalence de catégories” and states the corresponding automorphism-group map to be an “isomorphisme”. The later pages give an inverse-construction discussion and a moduli-based argument, but the supplied extract does not carry out every compatibility check. A full verification needs the precise categories, the written nonabelian hypotheses, essential surjectivity, and full faithfulness; the overwritten numerical inequality must be read independently rather than reconstructed from those requirements.

### R09. The moduli-topos identification

**Scan:** 188, approximately lines 3–16. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The phrase “on sait que” introduces the identification of the written Teichmüller-type group with the fundamental group of the complex moduli topos. The interlinear restriction concerning the identity on `I(\Pi_{g,\nu})` and `T(\Pi_{g,\nu})` is important data for checking that identification. A verification should preserve the stated topos and these rigidifications rather than silently substitute a claim about a coarse moduli space.

### R10. The marginal contractibility assertion

**Scan:** 188, left margin beside approximately lines 9–12. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The margin says that the universal covering of `M_{g,\nu}` “est contractible” and names Teichmüller, without giving a proof here. Establishing the assertion in precisely the manuscript's moduli-topos setting is needed before using it to eliminate the leftmost homotopy term. The margin is an assertion by the author, not a new conjecture formulated by the transcriber.

### R11. The two ends of the homotopy sequence

**Scan:** 188, approximately lines 17–24. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The explanation of the extra marked point leads, with “d'où”, to the displayed homotopy sequence and its second row. As a matter of exactness, the left-hand injectivity and the right-hand surjectivity use different endpoint information, so a proof should record those uses separately. It should also make explicit the basepoints and identifications, especially because the small annotation in the first top-row term is unreadable.

### R12. The explicitly deferred compatibility checks

**Scan:** 189, approximately lines 3–6. **Kind:** unproved claim.

This is the clearest explicit deferral: “les vérifications de compatibilité sont laissées ...”. The argument mentions passing to a subgroup with the written index expression `2\nu!`; the proposed verification must identify the subgroup and compare the moduli extension with the previously constructed automorphism extension, including the kernel identifications. Merely obtaining two exact sequences of the same displayed shape would not establish that comparison.

### R13. The quasi-inverse construction

**Scan:** 189, approximately lines 7–20. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The “foncteur quasi inverse” is described through a chosen point of the topos, a connected `T`-groupoid, and a new displayed system with one additional distinguished class. A proof should construct its action on morphisms and the two comparison isomorphisms, not just describe its effect on objects. The placement of the inserted word “connexe” matters for the data being asserted.

### R14. Strict actions versus outer actions

**Scan:** 190, approximately lines 4–12. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The parenthesis “pas seulement extérieurement” specifies a genuine action on the filled group as the input, while the constructed action on the punctured object is explicitly “extérieure”. That distinction should be retained in any equivariant formulation of the quasi-inverse. These lines do not, by themselves, state that an arbitrary outer action can be upgraded to a strict one.

### R15. Exactness of the normalizer extension

**Scan:** 190, approximately lines 13–19. **Kind:** unproved claim.

After selecting a peripheral subgroup, the text introduces “le normalisateur” and says “on trouve” the final extension. Verification has two separate tasks: prove that this normalizer maps onto `\Gamma`, and identify its intersection with the kernel of the ambient extension with the subgroup appearing at the left. The latter requires checking the relevant normalizer property in `\pi'`; the faint annotation identifying the kernel with `T` should not be used as a substitute for that check.

### R16. A small group-theoretic test of the path construction

**Scans:** 183–185. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** medium.

Try a free group on two generators with an explicitly chosen cyclic peripheral subgroup, and compute which conjugating elements give the same conjugate subgroup. This would test the distinction between the path-torsor description and the family of subgroup choices, and indicate whether a centralizer or a normalizer enters at each step. The reason for the confidence level is that this is an elementary test suggested by the visible surjectivity statement, but its match to all the manuscript's categorical conventions has not been checked.

### R17. Boundary cases outside the theorem's hypotheses

**Scans:** 181 and 187. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** medium.

Work out the low-complexity cases excluded by the clearly written genus/puncture cases before treating the equivalence as uniform. Compare the center, the automorphisms of a chosen realization, and the uniqueness asserted in the filling construction. This is motivated by the explicit use of a trivial center and the separate unfinished abelian discussion, but the exact exceptional list should not be inferred from the obscured inequality alone.

### R18. Splitting is a further question, not part of the final display

**Scan:** 190, final paragraph. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** medium.

Investigate whether the final normalizer extension splits in explicit examples, beginning with small finite acting groups and a specified action on the orientation module. The displayed exact sequence alone does not furnish a section, so splitting and functorial construction must be treated as distinct questions. This proposal is motivated directly by the last construction; no splitting result is established here.

### R19. Scope of the specialization `T = \mathbb Z`

**Scans:** 182 and 188–190. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** low.

Separate the arguments formulated for the orientation module `T` from the step explicitly introduced under `T = \mathbb Z`, and ask which parts might extend to a completed or profinite setting. The confidence is low because these pages do not supply comparison theorems or the needed topology on such an extension of the categories. This is a proposed direction of study, not a claim that the complex-moduli argument transfers unchanged.
