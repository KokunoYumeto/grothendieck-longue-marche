## First-pass notes

**R01 — scan 211 — claim without proof — keep, corrected.** The symbol is 𝒫_{ν'} (as in (25)–(26) on scans 207–208), so the claim is that G°_ν acts transitively on U_ν and also on the set 𝒫_{ν'}(U_ν) of ν'-element subsets, for every ν' ≥ 1. No proof is given; it is the multiple transitivity of the identity component of the homeomorphism group of a connected surface on finite sets of points.

**R02 — scans 211–212 — claim without proof — keep, corrected.** "Les deux carrés sont cartésiens" and the invariance of the horizontal inclusions remain unproved. The bracketed arrow of the second row of (31) ends in an inclusion hook against A_{g,(ν,ν+1)}: it is the inclusion of A_{g,(ν,ν+1)} into A_{g,ν+1}, the one inclusion the text exempts from invariance.

**R03 — scan 212 — implication not pursued — keep.** The equality B°_{g,ν} = (A^!_{g,ν+1})° = A°_{g,ν+1} is derived from (A^!_{g,ν+1})° ⊂ (A^!_{g,ν})° = A°_{g,ν}. An interlinear "= A°_{g,ν+1}" (doubtful sign) stands over the first inclusion of that display; see C02 for the missing justification of the identity-component equalities.

**R04 — scan 213 — claim without proof — correct.** The structure group is B_{g,ν} (no circle in the ink) and the torsor is called "can." (canonical); the first-pass doubt about a circle on B is settled and that part of the note is dropped. The fibre {u ∈ A°_{g,ν} : u(a_{g,ν}) = x} is a B_{g,ν}-torsor; its local triviality needs local sections of A°_{g,ν} → U_{g,ν}, which the text does not supply.

**R05 — scans 213–215 — claim without proof — correct.** The proof runs through (35)–(36) and the "Théorème bien connu" of scan 215, and the last line of scan 215 reads "et ils ont comme conséquence que Ũ_{g,ν} est simplement connexe dans les cas anabéliens" (not "il est connu classiquement"): simple connectivity is derived from the relations (37)–(38), and the unproved input is the "bien connu" theorem itself. **R06 — scan 214 — own idea (first pass) — keep.** Checked on the image: the quotient before (35) is written A°_{g,ν+1}/A°_{g,ν}, and the map after (35) π_1(A°_{g,ν}) → π_1(A°_{g,ν+1}); both are reversed relative to (33) and to (35) itself. **R08 — scan 215 — claim without proof — keep, corrected.** The "Théorème bien connu" has no proof or reference. **R09 — scans 216–217 — claim without proof — correct.** The ink reads "l'inclusion G ⊂ Aut°(U) ≃ A°_{g,ν} est une équivalence d'homotopie": G = Aut°_ℂ(U) is the finite-dimensional group of complex automorphisms, a subgroup of the topological group Aut°(U). The first-pass reading "G = Aut°(U)" would have made the claim empty; the claim is supported only by the diagonal note of scan 217 (R10, C06).

**R10 — scan 217 — claim without proof — correct.** The note says G is "simplement transitif" (not "k-plement transitif") on k-tuples of distinct points, which is exactly what the unique factorisation u = g·u′ needs; the first-pass objection rested on the misreading and is dropped. Still unproved in the note: dim G = k, the simple transitivity itself, and the contractibility ("∞-connexe" in the witnesses) of A°_{g,ν+k} in the anabelian case. **R11 — scan 217 — implication not pursued — correct.** The ink reads "(donc à son sous-groupe cpt maximal SO(3,ℝ)/{±1})", a consequence and not an alternative ("ou"), and GP(1,ℂ) is legible. **R12 — scan 218 — implication not pursued — keep.** The comparison of the fibrations G_0 → G → U and B_{g,ν} → A°_{g,ν} → U_{g,ν} gives the equivalence of the two homotopy-equivalence statements; the text asserts it ("équivaut à celle que …") without the five-lemma argument on homotopy groups.

**R13 — scans 218–219 — own idea (first pass) — keep, corrected.** The corollary reads "(et dans ceux là seulement, bien sûr)": the converse is asserted as evident, not tentatively ("dirons nous"). It holds because in the anabelian cases B_{g,ν}/B°_{g,ν} ≅ Π_{g,ν} ≠ 1 by (34).

## Checker's own notes

**C01 — scan 212 — implication not pursued (checker's note).** The margin of scan 212 proposes a simpler notation A•_{g,ν} for the stabiliser A_{g,(ν,ν+1)} of the marked point a_{g,ν} in A_{g,ν} = Aut(U_{g,ν}), the dot standing for the pointed object; it is distinct from the identity component A°_{g,ν}. The main text of scans 211–220 still writes A_{g,(ν,ν+1)}; the CSG reading edition substitutes A• throughout.

**C02 — scan 212 — own idea (checker's note).** The equality (A^!_{g,ν})° = A°_{g,ν} used in the display holds because A^!_{g,ν}, the kernel of A_{g,ν} → 𝔖_ν, is an open subgroup, and an open subgroup of a topological group contains its identity component. The same argument gives (A^!_{g,ν+1})° = A°_{g,ν+1}, the interlinear equality of the display.

**C03 — scan 213 — claim without proof, scope (checker's note).** The corollary (34), B_{g,ν}/B°_{g,ν} ≅ π_1(U_{g,ν}; a_{g,ν}) =: Π_{g,ν}, inherits the exclusions (1,0), (0,2) inserted in the theorem, although they are not repeated. In those two cases B_{g,ν} is connected (corollary of scan 219) while π_1(U_{1,0}) ≅ ℤ² and π_1(U_{0,2}) ≅ ℤ, so (34) fails there; in the cases (0,1) and (0,0) both sides are trivial.

**C04 — scan 215 — claim without proof (checker's note).** The hard parts of the theorem, the contractibility of A°_{g,ν} in the anabelian cases and the vanishing π_i(A°_{g,ν}) = 0 for i ≥ 2, (g,ν) ≠ (0,0), are classical results on homeomorphism groups of surfaces (Hamstrom, 1960s); the manuscript calls the whole theorem "bien connu" and gives no reference. The corrected reading of the last line of scan 215 makes the logical order explicit: from these relations follows the simple connectivity of Ũ_{g,ν}.

**C05 — scan 215 — implication not pursued (checker's note).** The inserted "(g,ν) ≠ (0,1)" in c) and d) is redundant: by (39), π_1(A°_{0,1}) ≅ ℤ, so c) and d) fail for (0,1) anyway, as a) and b) do. A possible explanation, offered as a guess only: the cancelled bracket under c) ("… A°_{g,ν} contract.") and the struck word before K(ℤ,1) in the (0,1) row of (39) suggest that A°_{0,1} was first taken to be contractible.

**C06 — scan 217 — own idea, check of the diagonal note (checker's note).** In the four abelian cases: (1,0), k = 1, G = U acting on itself by translations; (0,2), k = 1, G = ℂ*; (0,1), k = 2, G = Aff(1,ℂ), simply transitive on ordered pairs of distinct points of ℂ; (0,0), k = 3, G = PGL(2,ℂ) = GP(1,ℂ), simply transitive on ordered triples of distinct points of ℙ¹. In each case dim_ℂ G = k, so "dim G = k" is the complex dimension, and the simple transitivity claimed in the note holds.

**C07 — scan 220 — claim corrected by computation (checker's note).** The map (42) Γ'_{g,ν+1} → Γ_{g,ν} has kernel Π_{g,ν} exactly in the anabelian cases (the point-pushing sequence). In the abelian cases it is an isomorphism, as the margin NB says: (1,0) gives SL(2,ℤ) → SL(2,ℤ), kernel 1 ≠ Π_{1,0} ≅ ℤ²; (0,2) gives ℤ/2 → ℤ/2, kernel 1 ≠ Π_{0,2} ≅ ℤ; (0,1) and (0,0) give 1 → 1 with Π trivial. So the exclusion "(g,ν) ≠ (1,0), (0,1)" before (43) should read (1,0), (0,2), matching the hypothesis of the theorem on scan 219.

**C08 — scan 220 — claim corrected (checker's note).** The inclusion from which (42) is "déduit" is written A_{g,(ν,ν+1)} ↪ A_{g,ν+1} in the ink (both witnesses also have the target A_{g,ν+1}). That inclusion induces the inclusion Γ'_{g,ν+1} ⊂ Γ_{g,ν+1}; the homomorphism (42) comes from A_{g,(ν,ν+1)} ↪ A_{g,ν} together with A°_{g,ν+1} ⊂ A°_{g,ν}, so the target index is a slip.

**C09 — scan 216 — claim without proof (checker's note).** The second alternative of the corollary is "espace homogène sous un groupe topologique … par un ss-groupe connexe" (the condition had been lost in an illegible stretch of the first pass), with SO(3,ℝ) or probably GP(1,ℂ) for S². The connectedness of the isotropy group is what makes π_1(U) abelian through the homotopy sequence, as the NB at the top of scan 217 explains; the converse ("La réciproque … remarquable !") is stated without proof.
