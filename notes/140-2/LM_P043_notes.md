The fragment begins with `\scanpage{421}`. The authorial numbers visible at the tops of alternate pages are recorded with `\mspage`; the separate lower-left/archive numbering and institutional digitization footers are not treated as manuscript prose. No missing authorial numbers have been supplied.

The transcription retains the manuscript's French, mathematical lettering, abbreviations, cancellations, and additions. Clear word divisions caused only by the end of a handwritten line are joined where the line break is omitted; lexical hyphens remain. An unfinished sentence, parenthesis, or canceled draft can continue across a scan marker. The fragment ends where scan 430 ends, without supplying a continuation.

The requested state and doubt macros remain undefined in the delivered fragment, as required by the instruction to provide no preamble. Source comments give approximate line locators; they are not part of the transcribed wording. Ordinary arrows displayed as mathematical expressions are not converted into additional diagrams. The source includes no separately identified drawing requiring a figure description.

Line locators below refer approximately to handwritten main-text baselines, with displayed formulas counted as lines. An insertion is located by its host line; oblique marginal writing is identified separately. The boundaries and attachment of crowded revisions are sometimes uncertain, and the relevant entries say so. Alphabet, prime placement, an abbreviated ending, and deletion status are treated as textual questions rather than resolved from what would make the mathematics work.

`\unsure{reading}` records a tentative reading; `\unsure{reading|alternative}` records competing readings, with the first being the preferred reading only where a preference is possible. `\illegible{n}` gives an estimated number of unread words, not an exact count. Confidence labels are qualitative: **high** means a substantially secure reading with a residual local doubt; **medium** means a plausible reading with unresolved strokes; **low** means that competing readings or substantial obscuration remain. These are not calibrated probabilities. Several occurrences of the same local difficulty may be grouped in one row of the table.

## 4. Legibility and limits

The ordinary main prose is substantially more readable than the crowded revisions. The transcription is a cautious reading of these images, not a claim that every word or symbol has been recovered. Doubt also attaches to small endings and punctuation in otherwise readable sentences; mathematical plausibility has not been used to remove those doubts.

The least secure portions are the oblique marginal note on scan 423; the upper-left margin and finite-set exception on scan 424; the renumbered, extensively canceled draft across scans 425–426; scan 427's canceled lines and rewritten reconstruction sentence; scan 428's point-map target and cramped right-hand explanation; and both marginal notes on scan 430. ## 5. Research notes — English

These are source-grounded questions and proposed checks, not a literature survey, completed proofs, or claims of novelty. Each scan/line locator refers to Grothendieck (1981), the sole primary source cited here. A tentative reading is not promoted to an attributed mathematical assertion. A point may be resolved elsewhere in the manuscript; no claim is made that it remains open in the literature. Entries labelled “own idea” are the transcriber's suggestions, with their confidence and reason stated separately from the author's words.

1. **Scan 421, ll. 1–8 — conjecture / question.** The opening asks, “L'unicité est-elle de … façons claire ?”, and subsequently describes a partial splitting expected to extend “de façon unique”. The question is whether the partial section over the indicated subgroup determines an extension over the whole group; the unclear opening adjective does not affect that explicit question.

2. **Scan 421, ll. 14–17 — conjecture / expectation.** After (31), he writes “je [présume] qu'on doit avoir” and displays `L^{Γ′}=1`; the verb is a tentative reading. This isolates the vanishing of the subgroup-fixed part of the kernel as a desired condition, said to generalize the earlier circled condition 3, whose content is not supplied in this extract.

3. **Scan 421, ll. 14–21 — unproved claim.** The normalizer identity (31) and “cette condition implique l'unicité” are stated without a full proof on this page. A proof should first specify the embedded image of the partial section, then identify the kernel of the normalizer's projection and justify the asserted relation between surjectivity, existence, and uniqueness in the relevant category.

4. **Scan 421, ll. 18–21 — own idea.** Check the argument separately for abstract groups and for the intended topological groups: a bijective homomorphism need not by itself justify all desired continuity properties of an inverse. **Confidence: medium**, because the manuscript's “épi (donc iso…)” invites this distinction, while the exact hypotheses on the groups belong to earlier pages and are not recovered here.

5. **Scans 421–422, 421 ll. 22–23 and 422 ll. 1–9 — conjecture / proposed investigation.** “Il faudra essayer de préciser” introduces the notion of an “action … admissible” in specified arithmetic contexts, followed by a proposed return to the relation between “courbes (potentielles)” and finite coverings.
