## 4. Legibility and limits

The principal prose is moderately legible overall, with clearer passages in scans 436 and 440, but many word endings are reduced to short strokes. The least trustworthy material is the diagonal marginal writing in scans 432 and 437, the densely corrected margins and inverted cancelled block in scan 439, the brief phrase following “modulo” in scan 436, and the compressed qualification added to the theorem in scan 440. Enlarging or rotating the images separates some strokes without reliably restoring every letter.

Several mathematical distinctions remain unresolved: the angular superscript on the early group symbols; whether Γ belongs to the arguments of `Pt` or to the subscript of `B`; the hollow or double-stroked Γ-like letters; and some overwritten coefficients, signs, and letter alphabets. These are explicitly marked rather than reconstructed from the expected mathematics. The doubt table also records ambiguities of punctuation, abbreviation, emphasis, and insertion placement where the text alone would conceal them.

The repeated “effective” in scan 432, the feminine “égale” following the revised phrase in scan 433, the main assertions later qualified in the margins of scans 438–439, and the clear `+1` in scan 440, display (4), are retained. The long cancellation spanning scans 432–433 and the inverted cancelled writing on scan 439 remain part of the record. Some cancelled or overwritten words cannot be recovered and are marked with estimated word counts. Ordinary typesetting should not be taken as a guarantee that every unmarked reading is infallible; the marked passages identify the principal remaining doubts after rereading.

### Scans 431–433


- **Scan 431, approximately lines 11–13 — Conjecture.** Grothendieck writes, with the verb provisionally read, “Mais je présume que la première condition … implique les autres.” The proposed implication concerns the first non-normalization condition on the subgroups `L_i` and the other conditions associated with the derived actions. A proof would have to track normalizers through the specific filling operations and through the extension/action denoted by the uncertain angular superscript; the exact superscript should be settled before formalizing a theorem.

- **Scan 431, approximately lines 14–16 — Conjecture / expectation.** The added qualification “dans la définition d'admissibilité” accompanies the need to take account of a preceding condition, provisionally numbered `(3°)`, “qui implique l'existence de suffisamment de relèvements.” This records an additional existence requirement rather than a consequence established here. Its quantitative meaning should be compared with the infinitude of germs of splittings in 432(c), without assuming that the two formulations are already equivalent.

- **Scans 431–432, heading and conditions a)–c) — Own idea.** The provisional definition combines an open image condition for `Γ→Γ_a`, non-normalization conditions after filling punctures, and an infinitude condition on classes of germs of splittings. **Confidence: medium as a research question, not as a claim.** One useful test is to separate these three requirements and ask which implications, if any, follow under additional restrictions on `Γ`; the author's own margin warning suggests that their independence and scope cannot be taken for granted.

- **Scan 432, upper left diagonal margin — Conjecture / explicit doubt.** The note says that “Cette condition c) exclut sans doute des cas comme” the displayed `Γ=N_{g,ν}` example with `(g,ν)≠(0,3)`, and connects the issue with the probable absence of “multisection étale.” The group alphabets and part of the intervening text are uncertain, so the observation should be treated as a provisional warning about the breadth of condition c), not as a fully recovered theorem. Testing it would require identifying the precise extension and the meaning of a germ of splitting, including which base changes are permitted.

- **Scan 432, a) and following parenthesis — Conjecture.** The displayed conclusion is `π^Γ=(1)` for an action described as effective and admissible, followed by “peut-être” and a stronger-looking fixed-point assertion whose acting-group superscript is uncertain. A proof would need to exclude every nonidentity invariant element, with careful attention to how the non-normalization hypotheses control its centralizer or generated subgroup. The suggested strengthening must not be interpreted before the `+`/dagger-like mark and the group's definition have been resolved.

- **Scan 432, cancelled b), continuing into cancelled c) on 433 — Conjecture, withdrawn formulation.** A cancelled passage asserts, provisionally read, “alors itou sur `π(I')`” when a subset of punctures is stable. This broad inheritance claim must be distinguished from the replacements in 433(b–c), which explicitly refer to a fixed puncture `i`. Comparing the removed and surviving hypotheses may identify the obstruction that motivated the revision, but these pages do not supply that explanation.

- **Scan 433, b)–c) — Unproved claim.** Both clauses conclude that a derived action “ne normalise aucun `L'_j`,” under hypotheses involving a fixed puncture, and c) explicitly inserts “laissant fixe `i∈I`.” A proof would need to compare peripheral subgroup normalizers before and after the quotient or filling operation, and to distinguish the exterior action in b) from the effective action in c). The revised formula in c) remains textually uncertain, so the precise target group is not yet secure.

- **Scan 433, d), first assertion — Conjecture.** The map on the displayed admissible-point constructions is said to be “injective.” Its arguments versus subscripts remain uncertain in the transcription, but the rightward direction of the map and the injectivity assertion are visible. A proof would have to show that two admissible point or splitting classes with the same image already agree, using the actual equivalence relation in the `Pt_ad` construction rather than assuming equality of representatives.
