## Second-pass notes

**N401.1 — scan 401 — implication not pursued (second pass).**
The "pas extérieurs !" of the equivalence fits a Birman-type picture: once a hole is filled, its point can serve as a base point, so the result is a genuine group of type (g,ν−1) rather than an exterior one. On that reading the functor needs a chosen hole on the source side, which the sentence leaves implicit. Confidence medium; the page does not state the mechanism.

**N403.1 — scan 403 — claim without proof (second pass).**
The parenthesis reads "(ce qui trivialt. (or moralt.) revient à se donner une clôture alg. Q̄ de Q …)". For G = Gal(Q̄/Q), G-torsors correspond to algebraic closures Ω of Q, via P ≅ Isom(Q̄, Ω). For a torsor under 𝕀Γ_{g,ν} this presupposes 𝕀Γ_{g,ν} ≅ Gal(Q̄/Q), the identification the whole construction is aiming at. So whichever adverb the ink holds, the equivalence is heuristic; the witnesses' "moralement" says so outright.

**N403.2 — scan 403 — implication not pursued (second pass).**
A plausible reason for the (0,3) exception: M_{0,3} is a point over Q, so the π_1 of M_{0,3,Q̄} is trivial and compactification conditions have nothing to act on. For (1,1) the geometric π_1 is the profinite completion of SL_2(Z), so such conditions can bite. This is my gloss, not a reason stated by the author.

**N404.1 — scan 404, margin — claim without proof (second pass).**
"Le cas où P est le torseur trivial, est celui des π ext (?) munis d'une prédiscrétification orientée α (d'où une suite exacte 1 → T̂_α → 𝒩_α → 𝕀Γ_α → 1)". Neither the identification of a trivialised torsor with an oriented prediscretification nor the exactness is argued. Exactness in the middle is the analogue for α of 𝒩_{g,ν}/T̂_{g,ν} = 𝕀Γ_{g,ν}. 𝒩_α must first be defined from α; I assume it is the subgroup of 𝒩_a compatible with α, which the note does not say.

**N405.1 — scan 405 — claim without proof (second pass).**
"Or ceci revient exactement à ⓖ". This assumes, as the surrounding text suggests, that compatibility with the arithmetisations means differing by an element of 𝒩_{g,ν}.
- *ⓖ suffices.* Let u_0 be compatible and u another isomorphism of the stated kind. Then g = u u_0^{−1} normalises T̂_{g,ν} and transports the open subgroup u_0(Γ′·Σ) of 𝒩_{g,ν} into 𝒩_{g,ν}, so ⓖ gives g ∈ 𝒩_{g,ν}.
- *The converse needs a splitting.* Since such g normalise T̂_{g,ν}, ⓖ need only be checked for open 𝒩′ ⊇ T̂_{g,ν}. Recovering it from the stated condition needs each such 𝒩′ to be of the form u_0(Γ′·Σ) with Γ′ ∩ Σ = (1), i.e. a splitting of 𝒩′ → 𝒩′/T̂_{g,ν}. So "exactement" holds only given such quasi-sections.

**N407.1 — scan 407 — textual and mathematical point (second pass).**
The ink reads "on aura un hom canonique T̂(π) → T̂(π′)" with one hat on each T. The double-hatted T̂̂ of 401, 404 (19) and 405 shows two clearly stacked hats. Since 𝒩_a ⊂ T̂̂(π) (404 (19), 405) and 𝒩_a is larger than T̂_a, a map defined only on T̂(π) cannot induce the top row 𝒩_a → 𝒩_{a′} of (22). So T̂̂(π) → T̂̂(π′) is meant, as CSG and IUT print it; the single hats are probably a slip of the pen, and the transcription keeps the ink.

**N408.1 — scan 408 — own idea: proof sketch (second pass).**
Read "quasi-section of 𝒩_{a′} over 𝕀Γ_{a′}" as a closed subgroup that meets T̂_{a′} trivially and maps onto an open subgroup of 𝕀Γ_{a′}, and take Γ ⊂ 𝒩_a to be one over 𝕀Γ_a. Then (23) follows formally from (21)–(22).
1. Since (21) gives 𝕀Γ_a ⥲ 𝕀Γ_{a′} and (22) is compatible with the quotient maps, an element of Γ whose image lies in T̂_{a′} (in particular is 1) has trivial image in 𝕀Γ_a.
2. Such an element therefore lies in Γ ∩ T̂_a = 1, so the composite is injective and its image meets T̂_{a′} trivially; its image in 𝕀Γ_{a′} is open.

Confidence high for that reading of "quasi-section", which these pages do not define.

**N409.1 — scan 409 — claim without proof / notation (second pass).**
(24) is written Γ → π′(i′), yet it is said to "relever son action extérieure". A lift of the outer action Γ → Out(π′) is naturally a homomorphism to Aut(π′(i′)), the automorphisms of the vertex group at the Γ-fixed object i′, or a section of the corresponding extension; it is not a map into π′(i′) itself. The display probably abbreviates one of these. As written, its target does not match the sentence.

**N410.1 — scan 410 — implication not pursued (second pass).**
The margin "il faut pour ceci les restrictions anabéliennes habituelles" presumably requires both types, (g,ν) and (g, ν − Card I′), to stay anabelian, i.e. 2g − 2 + ν > 0 before and after filling. Otherwise π′ is abelian or trivial, and the condition π′^Γ = 1 and the sets Pts(π′, α′, Γ) change character. The exact restrictions are not stated on the page.

**N410.2 — scans 409–410 — own idea: proof sketch (second pass).**
For a fixed π′, the reduction to two points is formal on the groupoid side of 409. Whether i′ and j′ give the same π′-conjugacy class of liftings depends only on the full subgroupoid on {i′, j′}. That subgroupoid is Γ-stable, since Γ fixes both objects, and still describes π′, since any nonempty set of objects does. If "Card I′ = 2" instead means filling only two holes, the reduction also needs the point maps to be compatible with filling the remaining holes, a passage that can merge points; so the groupoid reading is the one under which the reduction holds as stated. Confidence medium.
