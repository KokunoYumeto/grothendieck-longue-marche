## Notes added in the second pass (mine)

**A. Scan 501 · claim without proof (proof supplied).** Relation (3) dies in the abelianisation, so in $\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}\simeq\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{2g}$ the images of $x_i,y_i$ form a basis. The composite $\widehat{\mathbb Z}\to\Lambda_i\to\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}$, $1\mapsto\bar x_i$, is therefore injective, so $\widehat{\mathbb Z}\to\Lambda_i$ is an isomorphism, and likewise for $\Lambda'_i$. This is the "trivial en passant à π_ab" of the insertion.

**B. Scan 502 · conjecture (later status).** The first expectation was later proved: faithfulness of the outer Galois action on $\pi_1$ of a hyperbolic curve over a number field. Belyi (1979) proved it for $\mathbb P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$, M. Matsumoto (J. reine angew. Math. 474, 1996) for affine hyperbolic curves, and Hoshi–Mochizuki (Hiroshima Math. J. 41, 2011) for proper ones. The stronger injectivity into $\Gamma_\pi=\mathcal N(\pi)/\widehat{\mathcal T}^{+}(\pi)$ is a separate question.

**C. Scan 501 · claim without proof (dependency).** The free case cites "(∗)" (CSG/IUT print "(2)") for $u(l_i)\in L_i$, that is, the centraliser hypothesis $L_i=\mathrm{Centr}_\pi(L_i^n)$, marked "pas prouvé !" on scan 500. The non-free case rests on the equally admitted (4). Lemme 5, and through it Corollaire 2, therefore remain conditional on centraliser identities in profinite surface groups.

**D. Scan 505 · claim without proof (proof supplied).** Let V → U be a finite étale cover of degree d of a curve U of type (0,ν). Every puncture of U has at least one puncture of V above it, so ν′ ≥ ν ≥ 3, and Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2 − 2g′ − ν′ = d(2 − ν). So no type with ν′ ≤ 2 arises from (0,ν) by finite covering: in particular no (g,0) and no (g,ν) with g ≥ 1, ν ∈ {0,1,2}. This is the stated exclusion.

**E. Scan 506 · own idea.** $\Pi_{0,3}$ is free on $l_1,l_\infty$ (with $l_0l_1l_\infty=1$), so (1) gives $\Pi'_{0,3}=\langle l_1,l_\infty\mid l_1^2=l_\infty^3=1\rangle\simeq\mathbb Z/2*\mathbb Z/3\simeq\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. In the map picture, $l_\infty^3=1$ makes every face a triangle or a monogon, and the fixed points of $l_1$ are folded edges. This matches "boucles aplaties" and the "arête (repliée)" of the universal map on scan 508.

**F. Scan 506 · claim without proof (proof sketch).** σ ∈ $\Gamma_{\mathbb Q}$ sends each loop to a conjugate of $l^{\chi(\sigma)}$ (χ the cyclotomic character). So it sends $l_1^2$ and $l_\infty^3$ into the closed normal subgroup they generate, and the outer action descends. The $\mathfrak S_3$ of automorphisms of $\mathbb P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$ permutes the three loop classes, while the relations treat 1 and ∞ differently and leave 0 free. Hence "mais non celle de $\mathfrak S_3$ !".

**G. Scan 507 · claim without proof (verified).** In the anharmonic group, the stabiliser of 0 is $\{x,\ x/(x-1)\}$, that of −1 is $\{x,\ 1/x\}$, and that of −j is generated by $x\mapsto 1/(1-x)$, whose fixed points are the roots of $x^2-x+1$, namely $-j$ and $-\bar j$. The orbits $\{0,1,\infty\}$, $\{-1,\tfrac12,2\}$, $\{-j,-\bar j\}$ thus have ramification indices 2, 2, 3 over $a_0,a_1,a_\infty$, as the text says. This also confirms the minus signs (the witnesses' $\{j,\bar j\}$ is wrong). The normalised quotient is $f(x)=27x^2(x-1)^2/\bigl(4(x^2-x+1)^3\bigr)=1728/j(x)$, with $j(x)=256(x^2-x+1)^3/(x^2(x-1)^2)$ the Legendre j-invariant. It has double zeros at 0, 1, ∞, triple poles at $-j,-\bar j$, and $f-1=-(x+1)^2(2x-1)^2(x-2)^2/\bigl(4(x^2-x+1)^3\bigr)$.

**H. Scan 507 · own idea (the cancelled claim checked).** For the f pinned by $a_0,a_1,a_\infty\mapsto 0,1,\infty$, the cancelled remark is false. The primitive pair $(27x^2y^2(x-y)^2,\ 4(x^2-xy+y^2)^3)$ has the common zero (0:1) mod 2 and (1:−1) mod 3, so f is a morphism only over $\mathbb Z[1/6]$. In the quotient, $a_0$ and $a_1$ meet mod 2 and $a_1$, $a_\infty$ meet mod 3. The quotient map does extend over $\mathbb Z$ with the target coordinate $u=(x^2-xy+y^2)^3/(x^2y^2(x-y)^2)$, which has no common zero in any characteristic. So the remark holds only after the pinning is given up, which may be why it was struck out.

**I. Scans 508–509 · own idea (consistency check).** Classically, $\pi_1(\mathbb P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\})\simeq\Gamma(2)/\{\pm1\}$ is normal of index 6 in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)\simeq\Pi'_{0,3}$, with quotient $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\simeq\mathfrak S_3$, and $f=1728/j$ is the map $X(2)\to X(1)$. This is the embedding (2) that §36 builds through (4)–(6), with $\Pi'^{\circ}_{03}$ the kernel of (6). The Euler characteristics agree: $\chi(\mathbb Z/2*\mathbb Z/3)=-1/6$, and a torsion-free subgroup of index 6 has χ = −1, so it is free of rank 2.

**J. Scan 509, (6) · claim without proof (calculation completed).** Send $l_1$ to a transposition τ and $l_\infty$ to a 3-cycle ρ. Then $l_0=(l_1l_\infty)^{-1}\mapsto(\tau\rho)^{-1}$, again a transposition, as (6) requires. The six admissible pairs (τ, ρ) form a single free orbit of $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak S_3)=\mathrm{Inn}(\mathfrak S_3)$. So the homomorphism is unique up to inner automorphism, and its kernel $\Pi'^{\circ}_{03}$ does not depend on the choice.

**K. Scan 510, (7) · claim without proof (proof sketch).** Let Z → Y be étale over $Y\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$, with indices dividing 2 over 1 and 3 over ∞. By Abhyankar's lemma (in characteristic 0 the local extensions are Kummer), the normalisation of $Z\times_Y X$ is étale over $X\setminus f^{-1}(0)=X\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$. Together with note 36, this gives the inverse functor and hence the equivalence (4).

**L. Scan 506 · implication not pursued (proof sketch).** Given (2), the Proposition follows from Belyi's faithfulness on $\widehat\Pi_{0,3}$. If σ acts on $\widehat\Pi'_{0,3}$ as int(g), it acts on the normal open subgroup $\widehat\Pi_{0,3}$ as int(g), whose outer class is the deck symmetry $s\in\mathfrak S_3=\widehat\Pi'_{0,3}/\widehat\Pi_{0,3}$ of X. σ preserves each of the three conjugacy classes of loop subgroups at 0, 1, ∞ (they are already distinct in $\pi_{\mathrm{ab}}$), while s ≠ 1 permutes them; so s = 1, σ is inner on $\widehat\Pi_{0,3}$, and σ = 1. What still needs writing out is the identification of the outer action of $\widehat\Pi'/\widehat\Pi$ with the deck group of X → Y.
