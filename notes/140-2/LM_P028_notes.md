## 4. Legibility and reliability

The main, uncancelled prose on scans 274–278 and the final summary on scan 280 is substantially easier to read than the opening material and the revisions. The least trustworthy passages are the crowded diagram and superscripts on scan 271, the parenthesis after “déjà connu” on scan 272, the correction beneath the displayed map on scan 273, the overlapping cancelled lines on scan 279, and the opening of the slanted marginal note on scan 280. Individual word endings, the small raised circle on A, and the exact scope of some insertions remain unresolved.

The images were examined directly, using enlarged crops and a rotated view of the final marginal note. The fragment was checked again against these passages. The uncertainty marks are substantive: they should not be stripped out in a subsequent edition without consulting the scans. In particular, the cancelled argument on scan 279 is only partially recoverable; its marked gaps are not a reconstructed earlier proof. Scan 273 ends with an unfinished sentence, and scan 280 ends with a comma; neither ending has been completed. The supplied ten pages do not establish hypotheses that may have appeared earlier in the manuscript.

## 5. Research notes — English; separate from the transcription

These notes distinguish what the pages state or request from proposed work. Quotations reproduce short, reasonably legible passages unless explicitly identified as doubtful. The items called “unproved claim” mean that no full proof is supplied in these ten images; they do not assert that a proof is absent elsewhere. The examples in the final items are my own diagnostic constructions, not claims of novelty and not additions to Grothendieck's text.

### R01 — Equivariant homotopies from the induced data
**Scan:** 271, approximately lines 14–21. **Kind:** unproved claim / proof task.
The paragraph ends “A prouver que α et β sont homotopes” after imposing equality on the induced data in a diagram. A proof would have to identify the spaces in which the two homotopies live and show that the indicated equalities detect their homotopy classes while preserving the Γ-action. Several diagram labels are uncertain, so the precise formulation should be settled from the ink before attempting the proof.

### R02 — Injectivity for the fixed subgroup
**Scan:** 272, approximately lines 15–20. **Kind:** unproved claim.
The boxed conclusion is “π₁(A^Γ) → π₁(A) est injectif,” followed by a reduction to π₁(A^Γ) = 0 when π₁(A) = 0. The preceding argument invokes a result “déjà connu” for Γ = 1, but its parenthetical identification is unreadable here. A complete proof needs the base-point convention and a justification that the induced-isomorphism condition is precisely the condition used in this injectivity statement.

### R03 — A provisional 1-faithfulness condition left unfinished
**Scan:** 273, approximately lines 1–7. **Kind:** conjecture / provisional expectation.
The page describes 1-faithfulness through an isomorphism arising from a path and ends “sauf erreur cette condition”. That reservation and the unfinished ending should remain visible in any account of the argument. The supplied next scan begins a new discussion rather than completing this sentence, so no completed criterion can safely be attributed to this paragraph alone.

### R04 — The Gr-category associated with a topological group
**Scan:** 274, approximately lines 1–12. **Kind:** unproved claim.
The text constructs a Gr-category with underlying “groupoïde fondamental (naïf)” and multiplication induced by A × A → A; it adds that the associativity is strict. A detailed construction would specify the fundamental-groupoid convention, the product comparison, and how the stated π₀-action on π₁ is recovered. This is a proof-development task suggested by the page, not a replacement of its terminology by a later formalism.

### R05 — From torsors to a champ of torsors
**Scan:** 274, approximately lines 13–19. **Kind:** unproved claim.
The statement “tout torseur sur A définit un 1-torseur” is extended to a base S and to a champ associated with the Gr-category. What is not supplied here is a complete relative construction, including restriction to open sets and descent compatibility. The inserted and cancelled occurrences of A_S must be resolved before assigning a precise type to every intermediate object.

### R06 — The higher-categorical equivalence assertion
**Scans:** 274, last line; 275, approximately lines 1–5. **Kind:** unproved claim.
After the condition πᵢ(A) = 0 for i ≥ 2, the text asserts “une équivalence” between categories of torsors, with a higher-category level discussed in an insertion. The n-like signs and part of that insertion are not fully secure, so this is not yet an unambiguous theorem statement. A proof would need the exact level, the intended notion of equivalence, and the hypotheses on the base/topos that make the construction effective.

### R07 — Conjugacy and transporter spaces
**Scan:** 275, approximately lines 6–20. **Kind:** unproved claim.
The discussion identifies connected components with “classes de conjugaison d'homomorphismes” and displays the transporter condition v = int g.u. To establish the assertion precisely, one should track a chosen trivialization of the torsor and verify both the conjugation convention and the topology assigned to the transporter. The displayed set is an anchor for the proof; the transcription does not insert missing parentheses or change the composition notation.

### R08 — Isotopy categories and categorical automorphisms
**Scan:** 276, approximately lines 1–14. **Kind:** unproved claim.
The 0-isotopic and 1-isotopic constructions use π₀ and Π₁ of the transporter, respectively; the paragraph then identifies the automorphism category using the transporter from u to itself, annotated by A^u. It also describes the isomorphism category as empty or a torsor under that automorphism category. A proof needs compatibility of transporter composition with paths and homotopies, together with the relevant torsor action and its coherence.

### R09 — Conditions for the comparison to be a 2-equivalence
**Scan:** 277, approximately lines 7–11. **Kind:** conjecture / explicit expectation.
The author writes “On aimerait avoir … des conditions” for the displayed 2-functor to be a “2-équivalence”. This is an explicit request for a criterion, not a proof that the functor always is an equivalence. The subsequent faithfulness calculations should be read as an attempt to analyse that request.

### R10 — An algebraic classification still to be supplied
**Scans:** 277, approximately lines 12–18; 278, approximately lines 1–9. **Kind:** unpursued implication / proposed programme.
The text asks for “une compréhension de la classification” of the objects in the target and calls the relevant data “de nature purement algébrique”. It then relates an action on a trivial torsor to a morphism of Gr-categories from Γ to the underlined A. What remains to be written out is the passage between actions, these morphisms, and equivalence classes of the corresponding objects; these pages do not supply that classification in full.

### R11 — The transporter category is not yet defined
**Scan:** 278, approximately lines 10–15. **Kind:** unproved claim / unfinished definition.
The text calls for a category Hom(u,v), not merely a set, through a transporter subcategory “à définir ad-hoc”. Defining its objects and arrows and proving that it models the intended morphisms are therefore explicit remaining tasks. In particular, its notation should not by itself be treated as a completed construction.

### R12 — The reduction when π₁ vanishes
**Scan:** 278, approximately lines 16–19. **Kind:** unproved claim.
Under “le cas où π₁ = 0,” the text says that the underlined A “se réduit à π₀”. To turn this into a usable reduction, one needs to say whether “se réduit” denotes a chosen model or an equivalence, and to carry that choice through the relevant morphism categories. The pages immediately following work in this simplified setting rather than establishing the unrestricted comparison.

### R13 — The 0-faithfulness test and the neutral component
**Scan:** 279, approximately lines 5–6 and the interlinear NB. **Kind:** unproved claim.
The 0-faithfulness statement is expressed by π₁(A^u) = 0, and the added NB rewrites it using the subgroup written provisionally as (A^0)^u. A proof needs to identify the neutral components involved and justify why their fundamental groups give the same condition. The superscript circle and the initial overwritten exponent are marked as reading doubts, so the exact notation is not independently settled here.

### R14 — The 1-faithfulness test
**Scan:** 279, approximately lines 7–22. **Kind:** unproved claim.
After the heavily revised transporter argument, the text says that the map “π₀(A^u) → π₀ est injective” and concludes with the boxed condition π₀((A^0)^u) = 0. A proof should show explicitly how equality of the projected transporter data becomes a statement about connected components, and how the kernel is related to the intersection appearing in the insertion. The crossed-out intermediate wording is too uncertain to use as a complete proof.

### R15 — Uniqueness of lifts and the stronger marginal formulation

### R18 — Separate the local conditions from conjugacy uniqueness
**Scans:** 279–280. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the proposed unrestricted example; its relevance to hypotheses on earlier pages is unknown.
Take A = (R,+) and Γ = Z, with homomorphisms n ↦ nt for real t. The fixed subgroup for conjugation is all of R, hence connected and simply connected, while the homomorphisms have identical projection to π₀(A) and are not conjugate when t differs, because A is abelian. This separates the visible connectedness tests from conjugacy uniqueness and warns against deriving the latter from the former alone; it is not a counterexample to the combined criterion actually written.
