## 5. Research notes — separate from the transcription

These English notes distinguish manuscript assertions from proposed checks. “Unproved claim” means that a proof is not written out at the cited locus; it does not mean that Grothendieck lacked a proof or that the claim remains open. Some points are developed later within this ten-page extract, as indicated, and others may be addressed elsewhere in the manuscript or literature. These notes make no claim of novelty and have not been used to repair the transcription. Parenthetical APA references identify the supplied primary source; no survey of later literature is implied.

1. **Scan 541, (13)–(14) — unproved claim.** “On récupère les” introduces the level-change formula and its expression as a quotient by a kernel. A proof would need to identify the level-change functor, the action conventions in the contracted product, and the base over which the quotient has the asserted meaning; equations (9)–(10), cited later, are outside this extract. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 541.)

2. **Scan 541, margin beside (14) — unproved claim.** The note says the kernel “opère librement” under the stated restriction on the lower level, followed by “mais pas bien sûr” at levels 1 or 2. A proof should distinguish the universal automorphisms ±id from the extra automorphisms at exceptional elliptic curves and check which survive the lower-level structure. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 541.)

3. **Scan 541, final paragraph — unproved claim.** “On peut expliciter” proposes descriptions over two principal opens and gluing over their overlap. The omitted verification is that the identifications furnished by (9)–(10) agree after passage to the common level and satisfy the relevant descent compatibility, separately for the moduli object and its coarse object. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 541.)

4. **Scan 542, theorem — unproved claim.** The assertion “M̃_{1,1} ≃ 𝔼¹_ℤ” is recorded “pour mémoire” without a construction on these pages. An integral proof would have to construct a coordinate and verify the universal property of the coarse object over the whole base, including the small residue characteristics. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 542.)

5. **Scan 542, discussion of two sections — unpursued implication.** “Deux sections privilégiées” provides a possible way to remove some of the affine-coordinate ambiguity mentioned immediately before it. These pages do not give their coordinates or state whether their reductions remain distinct at every prime. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 542.)

6. **Scan 542, two sections — own idea.** Compute the difference of the two sections after fixing an integral coordinate and use its prime divisors to locate fibres in which the sections meet. **Confidence: medium**; distinct generic values need not give disjoint sections over ℤ, but neither the coordinate nor the difference is written here. This is a proposed check, not a claim of novelty or a completed calculation. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 542.)

7. **Scan 542, automorphism groups — unproved claim.** The groups of orders 4 and 6 are followed by “très facile p.ex. par voie” and a doubtful reading of “transcendante”. A proof along that proposed route would classify the origin-preserving automorphisms of the relevant complex elliptic curves, then explain the passage to the characteristic-zero assertion used on the next page. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 542.)

8. **Scan 542, terminology — conjecture / expressed doubt.** The qualification “sauf erreur” concerns the name “anharmoniques”. This is a terminological hesitation, not an explicit conjecture about the automorphism groups. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 542.)

9. **Scans 542–543, (15) and its continuation — unproved claim.** “S'identifie canoniquement au stabilisateur” is extended to a general base scheme at the start of scan 543. The functorial construction should compare automorphisms of an elliptic curve with changes of its level trivialization, retaining the group or group-sheaf structure and the base hypotheses. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 542–543.)

10. **Scan 543, Proposition — unproved claim.** “Il y a exactement deux orbites” is asserted over Spec ℚ. A careful formulation should say whether the orbits are geometric orbits, orbit subschemes, or orbits of rational points, and then justify exhaustiveness from the automorphism classification; the transcription does not supply a missing qualification. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 543.)

11. **Scan 544, lines 1–11, (17) — Unproved claim.** The statement “qu'il ne dépend pas … du choix de n” is followed by the identification of the quotient presentation with `M′_{1,1}`. A proof would need compatible descent comparisons between levels after restricting to the base where the relevant integers are invertible; the diagonal marginal note makes that base restriction explicit. The wording refers back to the preceding section, so the claim may already be established elsewhere in the manuscript. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 544.)

12. **Scan 544, lines 12–17 — Unproved claim.** The remark says that validity “pour un n” implies validity “pour tous,” invoking the free action of the relative level kernels. To justify this transfer one would need the comparison maps to identify the relevant orbit and stabilizer data, and to verify the stated divisibility and exclusions on the intermediate level. The doubtful verb ending in this remark does not affect what must be checked, but it must not be silently regularized in the transcription. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 544.)

13. **Scan 544, lines 19–22 — Unproved claim.** The corollary gives stabilizers “Z/2Z et Z/3Z” in characteristic zero for the effective group action. A proof would have to identify the passage from the stabilizers of the earlier proposition to their images after removing the universal involution, and show that no further kernel remains. Scan 545 explicitly extends the conclusion to the modified level-two object; the marginal qualification is retained as written, independently of this mathematical comparison. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 544–545.)

14. **Scan 545, lines 6–18, (19) — Unproved claim.** Grothendieck invokes “le résultat général de rigidité rappelé au début” to conclude that the effective relative group acts “librement.” The missing argument here is the identification of the relevant stabilizer kernel with the universal involution, together with the rigidity statement for an automorphism preserving the higher-level structure. The prime visibly present in the numerator of (19) is a separate textual issue, not a reason to repair the equation during transcription. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 545.)

15. **Scan 545, lines 15–18 — Unpursued implication.** The sentence “le cor. de la prop. est valable encore” extends the preceding orbit-stabilizer conclusion to the modified level-2 object. This suggests examining precisely which moduli information survives the effective quotient and which information about automorphisms is removed. Scans 547–548 take up this distinction through the gerbe and quotient morphisms, so this is a pointer to their comparison rather than a claim that the excerpt leaves the distinction unaddressed. (Grothendieck, 1981, scans 545, 547–548.)

16. **Scan 546, lines 11–17 — Unproved claim.** The phrase “donc par construction … une équivalence” asserts a categorical representing property for the elliptic-curve moduli problem in terms of homomorphisms of locally ringed topoi. A complete proof would require a construction of the equivalence on objects and morphisms, compatibility with pullback in the base scheme, and descent. The exact class of morphisms intended by the displayed subscript of `Hom` is part of what would need to be fixed. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 546.)

17. **Scan 544, corollary — Own idea; confidence: medium.** It would be useful to test the displayed stabilizer description in characteristics 2 and 3, with particular attention to the distinction between the original group action and its effective quotient. No extension or counterexample is asserted here. (Grothendieck, 1981, scan 544.)
