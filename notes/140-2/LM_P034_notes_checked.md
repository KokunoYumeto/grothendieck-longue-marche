The lower middle group is N_Σ = Norm_{T̂̂(π)}(Σ), and Σ is by definition the image of π₁(M_{g,ν,Q̄}), so the left arrow is surjective. ## Second-pass notes (mine)

**S01 — scan 331 — conjecture or question (mine).**
The unfinished insertion "isomorphe à" after "l'image de π₁(M_{g,ν,Q̄})", together with the ≀ or 2 beside the left arrow of (26), asks whether π₁(M_{g,ν,Q̄}) → T̂̂(π) is injective, i.e. whether Σ ≅ π₁(M_{g,ν,Q̄}). Since π₁(M_{g,ν,Q̄}) is the profinite completion of the mapping class group, this is the congruence subgroup problem for mapping class groups. It is known in low genus (Asada for genus 0 and 1, Boggi for genus 2); I have not checked the present state for higher genus. The identifications in (26)–(28) and on 335 depend on it (R03, R05).

**S02 — scan 333 — implication not pursued (mine).**
With Ŝ⁺ read correctly, there is an extension 1 → π̂_{g,ν} → Ŝ⁺ → T̂⁺ → 1, the orientation-preserving part of CSG §26 (2′). The indeterminacy in π(U) ≅ π acts through inner automorphisms (from π̂) and through T̂⁺. Hence Σ = T̂⁺ is canonical, while the oriented quasi-discretification needs the choice Q̄ ≃ Q̄₀; this is the content of R11.

**S03 — scans 335, 338 — implication not pursued (mine).**
Two correspondences of the same shape occur. On 335, curves over Q̄ (and, over finite subextensions, curves over number fields) correspond to germs of splittings of π₁(M_{g,ν}) → Gal(Q̄/Q). On 338, rational points of U correspond to π-conjugacy classes of lifts of an outer action. The second is an early form of what Grothendieck later proposed as the section conjecture (letter to Faltings, 1983); the first is its analogue for the moduli stack. The "conditions sur un scindage" of 336 are the question which splittings are geometric. These notes do not assess the present status of that conjecture.

**S04 — scan 336 — conjecture or question (mine).**
"Pleinement fidèle, … pas 2-fidèle, i.e. il faut des conditions sur un scindage": here "2-fidèle" seems to name the property that every splitting germ comes from a curve, which would usually be called essential surjectivity. Before normalising, the term should be compared with Grothendieck's other uses of "n-fidèle" in the manuscript.

**S05 — scan 338 — conjecture or question (mine).**
"Qui fait opérer ext. le noyau de groupe défini par Γ_Σ sur π": the ink has "noyau", and CSG/IUT read "le noyau du groupe". What acts is the germ of open subgroups of Γ_Σ on which the splitting is defined. "Noyau" in the sense of a kernel does not fit, so Grothendieck may use "noyau de groupe" here for a group germ. Other occurrences should be checked before this passage is paraphrased.

**S06 — scans 339–340 — implication not pursued (mine).**
With "doit" inserted, the last paragraph of 339 states the same expectation that opens §27 on 340: "on prévoit qu'une pré-discrétification … donne naissance … à une vraie discrétification". The strict prediscretification is expected from (π, Σ) plus a lifting germ, and the discretification from an admissible action germ. The two passages should be cited together as one expectation, not as a theorem and a conjecture.

**S07 — scan 338 — own idea (mine).**
The vanishing π^{Γ'} = (1) needed for uniqueness could be checked through abelianisations of open subgroups of π. For a curve over a number field, these are extensions of Tate modules of Jacobians by cuspidal parts, and open Galois subgroups have no invariants on them, by weights. This would make the uniqueness in R31 unconditional over number fields. I have not traced a published reference for this exact statement.

**S08 — scan 340 — implication not pursued (mine).**
The heading "27. Changement de type (g,ν) : a) bouchage de trous" places the change of terminology, the arithmetic Teichmüller group N_Σ/Σ and χ_Σ at the start of a programme of filling punctures, (g,ν) → (g,ν−1). Grothendieck's table continues with "28. … b) passage à un revêtement fini" and "32. Retour sur les aspects arithmétiques du bouchage de trous: relations entre Γ_{g,ν} et Γ_{g,ν−1}". Any such comparison of the Γ_Σ has to respect the multipliers χ_Σ introduced here.
