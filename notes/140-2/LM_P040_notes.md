## 3. Legibility and limits

The main prose is moderately legible, and many large numbered formulas can be read more securely than their surrounding annotations. This is nevertheless a difficult working manuscript with very pale strokes, compressed endings, ambiguous hats and primes, and several layers of revision. The large curled group letters are especially resistant to exact alphabet identification; a recognizable mathematical role has not been treated as evidence for a particular letter.

The least reliable passages are scan 391's normalizer expression and final parenthesis; scan 392's interlinear corrections and closing bracketed argument; scan 393's small comparison diagram; the canceled block and numerical condition at the bottom of scan 395; both bottom NBs and the middle-left choice-of-discretification note on scan 396; the overtraced groups and possible second arrowhead in (9) on scan 399; and the vertical marks, lowest annotation, and final paragraph on scan 400. Some apparent underlines may be long descenders of g, so their status is explicitly doubtful. Repeated inspection clarified some readings, including *bouts*, but did not justify filling the remaining gaps. No accuracy percentage is claimed.
