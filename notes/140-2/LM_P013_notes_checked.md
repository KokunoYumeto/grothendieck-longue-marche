## Notes added in the second pass

**S01. Scan 121. Implication not pursued [second pass].** The reduction to fundamental topoi needs $\sigma^{*}$ and $\rho^{*}$ to send essentially locally constant sheaves to essentially locally constant sheaves. It also needs $F_D$ to be automatically of that kind, which scan 120 asserts through the equivalence with étale sheaves on S. Then the triples $(F_U,F_D,\varphi)$ live entirely in $B_{\pi_1,U}$, $B_{\pi_1,D}$ and $B_{\pi_1,D^{*}}$. Neither step is argued on these pages.

**S02. Scan 125. Implication not pursued [second pass].** The equivariance restored in the transcription has a consequence. Because $T_{\mathcal E}\simeq T_i$ are $\mathcal E_i$-isomorphisms and $\Pi_i$ acts trivially on the $\Gamma_i$-module $T_i$, every inertia group $\rho_i(\Pi_i)$ acts trivially on $T_{\mathcal E}$. So $T_{\mathcal E}$ descends to a local system $T_X$ with an action of $\mathcal E$ modulo the invariant subgroup generated by the $\rho_i(\Pi_i)$, that is of $\pi_1(X)$ by scan 123. This is exactly the parenthesis "(i.e. un $T_X$ sur lequel opère $\widetilde{\mathcal E}=\mathcal E/\ldots$)".

**S03. Scan 129. Claim without proof [second pass].** In the affine-curve example the Proposition needs the comparison in every degree. Both sides vanish for all i ≥ 2, not only for i = 2, provided both have cohomological dimension ≤ 1 at the primes dividing the order of F. The text's reason ("le groupe fondamental premier à p est libre") concerns only the maximal prime-to-p quotient of $\mathcal E$. It still has to be shown that $H^i(\mathcal E,F_{\mathcal E})$ with prime-to-p coefficients agrees with the cohomology of that quotient, or that $\mathcal E$ itself has ℓ-cohomological dimension ≤ 1 for ℓ ≠ p.

**S04. Scan 130. Claim with an implicit hypothesis [second pass].** Case a) (S = ∅) asserts $H^2(\pi,T)\simeq H^2(X,T)\,(\simeq\widehat{\mathbb Z})$ for a connected projective curve over an algebraically closed field. For g = 0 this fails: π is trivial, so $H^2(\pi,T)=0$, while $H^2(\mathbb P^1,T)\neq0$. That is the obstruction of the scan 129 margin ("Non, à cause du genre 0 !!!"). Case a) therefore needs g ≠ 0, which the text states only in case b).

**S05. Scan 130, margin. Claim without proof [second pass].** "Mais dans tous les cas (même si g = 0, du moment qu'on n'a pas ν = 0)" means as soon as there is at least one puncture. In that case $H^2_!(B_{\pi_1,U},\widehat{\mathbb Z})\simeq\widehat T_\pi^{\otimes-1}$ canonically, "d'où une description cohomologique des modules d'orientation, commune au cas sans trous et avec trous". The first pass had reversed the condition ("voire pour ν = 0") and lost the proper supports. The excluded case g = 0, ν = 0 is exactly the one in which π is trivial.

**S06. Scans 126 and 130. Claim without proof [second pass].** $B_{\pi_1,D}\approx B_{\pi_1,S}$ (scan 126) rests on each $D_s$ being henselian, so that it has the fundamental group of its closed point. Over an algebraically closed k each $\Gamma_i$ is trivial. This is why scan 130 can describe $B_{\pi_1,D}$ as "topos discret défini par S = I".

**S07. Scans 128 and 130. Implication not pursued [second pass].** The "calcul habituel … que j'ai un peu oublié" can be written down in the case used on scan 130. The local situation $B_{D,D^{*}}$ is $B_{X,U}$ with (D, D*) in place of (X, U), so its recollement gives a long exact sequence $\cdots\to H^q_{s_i}\to H^q(\Gamma_i,\cdot)\to H^q(\mathcal E_i,\cdot)\to H^{q+1}_{s_i}\to\cdots$. Over an algebraically closed k, $\Gamma_i=1$ and $\mathcal E_i=\Pi_i$. With coefficients $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ (as a limit over $\mathbb Z/n$) this gives $H^2_{s_i}\simeq H^1(\Pi_i,\widehat{\mathbb Z})=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi_i,\widehat{\mathbb Z})$. That is $L_i^{\otimes-1}$ once $\Pi_i$ is identified with $L_i$, which is the displayed isomorphism "$H^2_{s_i}(B_{U,X},\widehat{\mathbb Z})\simeq L_i^{\otimes-1}$".
