## Legibility and limits

The principal prose, headings, and larger displayed formulas are generally readable. Small connectives, terminal letters, abbreviated superscripts, and the distinction between a cancellation stroke and a letter's own horizontal stroke are substantially less secure. Some seemingly familiar mathematical expressions remain explicitly doubtful because their actual letters or indices are not fully legible.

The least reliable passages are the long left margin on scan 141; the successive rewritings of the conjugacy statement on scan 142; the suffix insertion at the foot of scan 144; the second half of the left margin on scan 148; the repeatedly revised hypotheses at the head of scan 149; and the last three cramped lines on scan 150. The isolated interlinear π on scan 150 has no securely identified attachment: it is retained within the added text near its physical position rather than silently moved into an earlier phrase. The last torsor sentence is not fully recoverable.

The duplicate-looking words and syntactically unfinished formulations should not be read as polished French. In particular, the early wording of the theorem on scan 149 and several abandoned proof openings retain visible drafting layers. The principal proof is itself unfinished in the supplied excerpt: scan 147 explicitly proceeds with the topological part assumed proved, and scan 148 explicitly records a remaining anti-involution problem.

## Research notes — English

These notes distinguish what the manuscript states, what it leaves unproved in these scans, and proposed lines of investigation. They do not certify the claims, supply missing manuscript text, or claim novelty for the suggestions. All manuscript quotations and scan references refer to Grothendieck (1981), as identified above; the rest of the manuscript and the external literature have not been used to settle the questions here.

### R01 — Scan 141, lines 6–14 — unproved claim

The fixed lifts over a given point are said to form “un torseur sous” π^{Γ′}, with the verification called “triviale.” A complete argument would identify the deck-transformation action on that fibre and show directly that two Γ′-fixed lifts differ by an element commuting with Γ′. The reference to “hyp. sur a)” depends on preceding material not included in this packet, so its exact scope should be checked before treating the asserted equivalence of a) and b) as unconditional.

### R02 — Scans 141–142, lemma statement — unproved claim

The “Lemme fondamental” asserts a unique fixed point and a cyclic/dihedral description, followed by a topological conjugacy statement, for a faithful finite action on a plane with nontrivial orientation-preserving subgroup. The subsequent proof develops a route to these conclusions but does not complete it in the supplied pages. It is important to distinguish the assertion of the lemma from a proved result available within this excerpt, especially when reading its later applications.

### R03 — Scan 143, lines 4–8 — unproved claim

Starting with an invariant C² structure, the manuscript says “un argument standard” produces an invariant conformal structure. A proof would need to explain what is being averaged or otherwise constructed, how regularity is preserved, and how orientation-reversing elements are treated. This conditional step does not by itself establish the availability of the initial invariant differentiable structure for an arbitrary topological action.

### R04 — Scans 143–144, conformal and compact-subgroup argument — unproved claim

The proof invokes the “th. fondamental de la représentation conforme” and then asserts that a compact subgroup is contained in a conjugate of a displayed maximal compact subgroup. To complete this part one would need the precise conformal or anticonformal automorphism group, the kernel of its chosen matrix realization, and an argument establishing the compact-subgroup conjugacy assertion. The manuscript does not provide those details here, and the initial letters of the matrix-group symbol are themselves doubtful.

### R05 — Scans 142–144, formulas involving τ — own idea

**Confidence: high for the algebraic check, conditional on the literal reading of the symbols; reason: an involution equals its inverse.** Test the written formulas τ^ε with ε = ±1 and Θ_u ∘ τ^{det u} by taking τ to be the named complex conjugation: both powers +1 and −1 then give τ, so those exponents alone do not distinguish the identity from conjugation. This suggests an indexing or drafting issue worth checking against the original sheets and neighbouring pages, but it does not authorize changing the transcription.

### R06 — Scan 143, real-matrix formula — own idea

**Confidence: high for the displayed calculation; reason: multiplying both numerator and denominator by −1 leaves the fraction unchanged.** The visible formula Θ_u(z) = (az+b)/(cz+d) satisfies Θ_{−u} = Θ_u. Any identification of the automorphism group with the displayed matrix set therefore needs a kernel/quotient check; this issue is independent of which uncertain initial letter belongs to the matrix-group notation.

### R07 — Scan 144, lines 7–10 — own idea

**Confidence: high; reason: the proposed maps are explicit inverses and depend only on radius.** For the radial homeomorphism suggested by “[0,1[ → [0,+∞[”, test F(z) = z/(1−|z|) on the open unit disc and F⁻¹(w) = w/(1+|w|). Their radial form makes the required commutation with rotations and conjugation a direct calculation. This supplies a concrete candidate for this one construction, not for the unresolved topological-action classification.

### R08 — Scan 145, paragraph b) — unproved claim

The induction argues that lower-order stabilizers have “la structure dite dans le th.” and concludes that the quotient is a surface and the projection a branched covering. A proof must extract local quotient charts from the induction hypothesis, rather than using only the abstract cyclic structure of the stabilizers. This is also a point at which to check for circularity between the induction's local conjugacy conclusion and the asserted quotient-surface structure.

### R09 — Scan 145, middle of paragraph b) — unproved claim

The assertion of “une unique structure conforme sur D” compatible with the map to U needs a local construction at branch points and a compatibility argument on overlaps. The crossed-out and inserted wording distinguishes holomorphic language from the broader “conforme” formulation. A full proof should specify exactly which oriented or unoriented conformal structures are being used instead of silently identifying the two categories.

### R10 — Scans 145–146, punctured-plane reduction — unpursued implication

Removing the already chosen fixed point and lifting from another hypothetical fixed point is meant to produce a Γ-action on the universal cover commuting with the deck group ℤ. The passage “Γ × ℤ opère” shows that the argument needs a direct-product action, not merely an unspecified extension. This isolates two things to verify separately: the base-point-preserving lifts really form an action, and the induced action on the punctured plane's fundamental group is trivial under the stated orientation assumption.

### R11 — Scans 146–147, auxiliary lemma — unproved claim

The essential remaining assertion is that a finite-order homeomorphism commuting with z ↦ z+1 and fixing the origin must satisfy “u = id.” The excerpt reaches this lemma and sketches consequences of a hypothetical counterexample, but does not prove the assertion. Any proposed completion should address this precise topological lemma rather than assuming an analytic structure that the earlier argument is still trying to obtain.

### R12 — Scan 147, reduction after “OPS” — own idea

**Confidence: high; reason: the order calculation in a finite cyclic group is elementary.** To justify a reduction to prime order, start from a nonidentity u of order m, choose a prime p dividing m, and replace it by u^{m/p}, which has order p and retains both commutation with translation and the fixed origin. A proof that no such nonidentity prime-order element exists would then contradict the original choice of u. This is a proposed justification of the reduction, not additional wording for the manuscript.

### R13 — Scan 147, lines 5–9 — unpursued implication

The inserted observation that the points of ℤ are fixed leads, on the one-point compactification, to an accumulation point of fixed points at ∞. The reaction “décidément très sauvage !” is a diagnosis of what a counterexample would have to look like, not itself a contradiction. A completion needs an explicitly stated local or global fixed-set restriction that rules out this configuration for the finite-order action in question.

### R14 — Scan 147, lines 9–11 — conjecture

“On doit pouvoir terminer par la suite spectrale d'Adams...” records an expected route, immediately followed by “je n'entre pas dans ces détails...”. No spectral-sequence construction, input object, coefficient system, or vanishing argument is supplied in these scans. The research task is to determine what precise construction was intended and whether it proves the needed topological lemma; the manuscript's terminology should not be silently replaced by the name of a different method.

### R15 — Scan 147, paragraph d) — unproved claim

The continuation explicitly begins with the topological part “ainsi supposée prouvée” (the final written endings are doubtful). The normality/invariance argument then transfers the unique fixed point of Γ^∘ to the full group. Its logical status is conditional on the unresolved uniqueness result, and this dependency should remain visible in any later reconstruction of the proof.

### R16 — Scans 147–148, quotient and conformal structure — conjecture

The bracketed remark asks to prove that there “existe tjrs structure conforme invariante” for the more general surface action under discussion. The surrounding text attempts to relate this to the quotient being a surface, apparently with a boundary possibility, although that word is less secure than the main request. A formulation worth investigating would separate interior rotation stabilizers from orientation-reversing stabilizers and specify the appropriate conformal structure on the quotient in each case.

### R17 — Scan 148, bracketed paragraph — unproved claim

“Ici ce qui manque” identifies the local action of an anti-involution near a fixed point as the outstanding issue. A proof would need to describe invariant neighbourhoods and the local fixed set, sufficiently precisely to support the quotient and conformal-structure constructions. This local question should be kept distinct from the stronger global conjugacy proposed in the margin.

### R18 — Scan 148, upper part of the left margin — conjecture

The margin asks to “prouver que” an anti-involution of D can be transported by an isomorphism D ≃ ℂ to z ↦ z̄, and explicitly records the consequence D^τ ≃ ℝ. This is a global straightening expectation, not a completed argument in the supplied material. Investigating it requires both a fixed-set analysis and control of how the complementary regions are interchanged.

### R19 — Scan 148, lower part of the left margin — unpursued implication

The continuation appears to involve a ℤ/2ℤ-action, π₀(U^Γ), and a concluding “est bij.”, suggesting a component-level statement in the reflection case. The codomain and several connecting words are not recoverable, so these scans do not support a precise theorem statement for that map. The passage merits comparison with the surrounding manuscript, but should not be reconstructed merely from the later point-level theorem.

### R20 — Scan 149, theorem a) and c) — unpursued implication

The asserted bijection identifies fixed points with π-conjugacy classes of splittings, and c) says that a splitting forces Γ to be cyclic or dihedral under the theorem's hypotheses. Conditional on the theorem and its unresolved proof obligations, this gives an obstruction to splitting for groups outside those types. The orientation assumptions and the excluded low-order cases are essential parts of that conditional implication, particularly because the hypotheses have several visible drafting layers.

### R21 — Scan 149, theorem b) — own idea

**Confidence: high as a formal consequence of the stated assertion; reason: the stabilizer of a section under π-conjugation is its centralizer in π.** If π^{Γ′} = (1) holds for every section, the conjugation action on actual sections is free. Consequently each conjugacy class has no nonidentity automorphism coming from π in its action groupoid, a stronger rigidity statement than simply counting conjugacy classes. This observation remains conditional on the manuscript's theorem and its hypotheses.

### R22 — Scans 148–149, reflection exception — own idea

**Confidence: high; reason: both the fixed set and the fundamental group in the proposed example can be computed directly.** Test the excluded reflection-only case on U = ℂ with Γ of order two acting by conjugation: U^Γ = ℝ, whereas π₁(U) is trivial and the resulting extension has a single splitting class. A point-level bijection therefore cannot extend unchanged to that example, while a component-level formulation is worth testing. This is an elementary diagnostic for the stated exclusions, not an identification of the unreadable marginal theorem.

### R23 — Scan 150, corollary a)–b) — unproved claim

The corollary says that the stabilizer-associated partial sections “sont maximales” and that the resulting map to maximal nontrivial partial-section classes is bijective. A proof should show that inclusion of such sections forces their lifted fixed points to coincide, and conversely that a maximal partial section is the full stabilizer section of the point it determines. The argument must keep π-conjugacy distinct from conjugacy by the whole extension.

### R24 — Scan 150, left margin — unproved claim

The marginal statement “Cette application commute aux actions naturelles de Γ !” asserts equivariance. To verify it, the action of Γ on π-conjugacy classes of partial sections must be defined through lifts in the extension, with independence from the choice of lift checked explicitly. The phrase identifies a naturality property worth retaining alongside the corollary, not just a count of the underlying sets.

### R25 — Scan 150, corollary and marginal equivariance — own idea

**Confidence: high conditional on the asserted bijection and equivariance; reason: an equivariant bijection induces a bijection of orbit sets.** Passing from U′ to U′/Γ should yield a classification of the exceptional orbits by the corresponding Γ-orbits of partial-section classes. One can then test whether those algebraic orbits are exactly conjugacy classes under the full extension, rather than under π alone. This would formulate an orbit-level consequence not explicitly developed on these pages.

### R26 — Scan 150, paragraph c) and bottom continuation — unproved claim

The equality π^{Γ′} = (1) is interpreted in terms of automorphisms of the universal covering that commute with the stabilizer action, followed by a claim of “un isom. unique” between torsor constructions. A full proof would have to specify those torsors, the twisting action, and the equivariance required of the isomorphism. The bottom-line formula is too uncertain to identify the constructions completely from these images, so no precise twisting identity is asserted here.
