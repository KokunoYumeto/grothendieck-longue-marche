## 4. Legibility and reliability

The continuous prose is often recoverable, especially where the same hand and terminology recur. The brief formulas involving `pf`, inner automorphisms, and the normalization `α=1` are generally easier to follow than the boundary-indexed formulas. Enlarged crops helped distinguish strokes, but did not recover writing obliterated by cancellation.

The least reliable portions are the overwritten opening on scan 61; the auxiliary parenthesis on scan 62; the source of the bottom map on scan 63; the homology argument and cross-reference on scan 64; the canceled block at the bottom of scan 65; the canceled marginal formula on scan 69; and the conjugation exponents, local correction group, and final transporter expression on scan 70. The diagonal correction on scan 64 is clearer at its beginning (“faux tel quel”) than at its end. Some cancellation boundaries and insertion attachment points remain uncertain even where individual words are readable.

This is an explicitly qualified diplomatic transcription, not a verified critical edition. An unmarked mathematical statement has not thereby been proved or checked against the literature. In particular, the Mordell–Weil sentence, the claims of bijectivity, and the final conjugacy equations have not been “repaired” to fit an expected theorem. The table should be consulted before quoting any of the low-confidence passages.

## 5. Research notes — separate from the transcription

These are source-based research leads, not statements of the current status of any conjecture. Brief French quotations retain the author's wording where legible; a quotation involving a doubtful reading is explicitly qualified. “Unproved claim” means that a proof is not given in these ten scans, not that no proof exists elsewhere. Own ideas are marked separately, with confidence and a reason; none is asserted to be new or established.

### R01 — Scan 62, approximately lines 5–8
**Kind: unproved claim.** “Sauf erreur, une réduction facile” introduces the passage to `X'=Spec K` and `E'=Γ`. A proof should explain the change of base field from a general zero-dimensional source and show that the relevant geometric morphisms, diagram morphisms, and their equivalence classes are preserved.

### R02 — Scan 62, approximately lines 14–17
**Kind: conjecture / expectation.** The paragraph says that knowledge of `f`, hence of `pf`, determines `α`, and associates this with “Cent(Γ)={1}”; the status of the nearby canceled “sans doute” is uncertain. The precise hypotheses on the field and on Γ need to be recovered before treating this as a general centre-triviality assertion.

### R03 — Scan 63, approximately lines 1–10
**Kind: unproved claim.** “On peut tjrs décrire un morphisme … avec α=1” is followed by the description in terms of sections modulo `π=Ker p`. The calculation should be checked on representatives: choose a lift of `α^{-1}`, normalize, and then verify that equivalence between normalized representatives uses kernel-valued conjugation rather than unrestricted conjugation in E.

### R04 — Scans 63–64, bottom display and opening
**Kind: conjecture.** The displayed geometric-to-section map is asserted to be “bijective” for an “anabélien” pair, but the next page carries the explicit correction “faux tel quel”. The qualification belongs to the claim: it must not be removed when extracting a section-type conjecture from these pages.

### R05 — Scan 64, upper-left margin
**Kind: conjecture / question.** The note calls for “des conditions supplémentaires sur les f” and refers to “n° 9 ci-dessous”; its closing words are read, less securely, as “Peut-être vrai si I=∅”. The later number 9 is outside the supplied excerpt, so the additional conditions and the scope of the tentative empty-boundary exception remain open here.

### R06 — Scan 64, approximately lines 3–10
**Kind: unproved claim.** “Je sais en tous cas que cette application est injective” is justified by an embedding of U into an algebraic group that is an extension of an abelian variety by a torus, together with the stated Mordell–Weil input. A proof needs the exact field hypotheses, an account of the embedding, and the point-separation step linking the group-theoretic invariant to a rational point.

### R07 — Scan 64, middle paragraph
**Kind: unproved claim.** “Il suffit de connaître la classe de splittage” asserts that an abelianized extension already determines the point. One should identify the map from geometric sections to splittings of this quotient extension and prove the required injectivity, without presuming that injectivity answers the earlier surjectivity problem.

### R08 — Scan 64, last paragraph
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The author explicitly says the faithfulness statement holds under “des hyp. moins draconiennes” than the anabelian hypothesis and writes a numerical condition read as `g=0 ⇒ n≥2` (n/ν remains uncertain). The exact class of curves allowed by this weaker condition, and its dependence on the uncertain preceding cross-reference, should be compared with the hypotheses of the conjectural full correspondence.

### R09 — Scan 64, the sentence about G(K)
**Kind: own idea. Confidence: high for the elementary test; conditional on the scope of the manuscript sentence.** Test the visibly stated finite-generation assertion with `G=G_m` and `K=Q`: distinct rational primes give infinitely many multiplicatively independent elements of `Q×`, so the full rational-point group in that example is not finitely generated. This is a reason to recover the exact earlier hypotheses or the intended meaning of “absolu”, not a license to alter the sentence or to conclude that the intended argument has no valid formulation.

### R10 — Scan 65, opening paragraph
**Kind: conjecture / research program.** “Ceci suggère une approche de la conjecture de Mordell” proposes studying finiteness of π-conjugacy classes of splittings for `g≥2`. A realization of this program must both justify the geometric-to-section comparison and prove the proposed finiteness on the group-extension side; the observation alone supplies neither proof.

### R11 — Scan 65, Fermat paragraph
**Kind: conjecture / research program.** “Idem pour une approche du th. de Fermat” points to an extension involving the displayed punctured-projective-line fundamental group, whose small labels are marked uncertain in the transcription. These scans do not explain how solutions would be encoded, what distinguished sections or maps would be selected, or what property of the extension would exclude the relevant solutions.

### R12 — Scans 65–66, case IV(a)
**Kind: unproved claim.** Constant applications are explicitly allowed, and the continuation describes them by maps “qui sont nuls sur π'”. The claimed full-faithfulness description of this constant part should be derived by the appropriate factorization through a zero-dimensional object, checking the uncertain case numeral rather than silently substituting the expected one.

### R13 — Scan 66, case IV(b), final sentence
**Kind: conjecture / explicit research question.** “Il faudrait essayer de vérifier ce point directement” asks for the group-theoretic triviality on π' of the compatible maps in this boundary case. The manuscript says that this, together with case III, would establish the corresponding bordélique claim; the desired vanishing must therefore be distinguished from the geometric observation that the relevant geometric maps are constant.

### R14 — Scan 66, same vanishing problem
**Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium, because this isolates a necessary test but not the full obstruction.** Try first to identify the map induced on the abelianizations and to show that it vanishes under the exact geometric and field hypotheses. Even a successful abelianized calculation would leave a nonabelian step: it does not by itself rule out an image contained in a commutator subgroup.

### R15 — Scan 67, case IV(d)
**Kind: unproved claim.** The assertion “dominant et fini, génériquement étale” is made after excluding constant maps. A proof should identify which assumptions on the curves imply finiteness and which characteristic or separability assumptions imply generic étaleness, since those hypotheses are not restated at this point.

### R16 — Scan 67, last paragraph, continuing onto scan 68
**Kind: unproved claim / open reconstruction problem.** Faithfulness “résulte aisément” from case III, whereas full faithfulness is called “chose mystérieuse”. The distinction suggests two different proof obligations: detect equality of already existing geometric morphisms, and construct a geometric morphism from the groupoid/extension data; the second is not supplied by the first.

### R17 — Scan 68, first reduction paragraph
**Kind: unproved claim.** “Par des ext. finies des corps K” is used to reduce to surjective structure maps to Γ. The reduction should be formulated with its exact open subgroups and descent data, rather than treating passage to an arbitrary finite extension as automatically reversible for all morphism classes.

### R18 — Scan 68, the sentence beginning “Sauf erreur”
**Kind: unproved claim.** The author tentatively says that case III for extensions “de t.f. de K” implies the general case over K. This is a stronger and different passage from the immediately preceding finite-base-extension reduction; a proof must explain why a section over the relevant finitely generated field supplies a map and why that map extends with the required boundary behavior.

### R19 — Scan 68, same reduction
**Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium, because a generic-point construction gives a natural route but does not solve extension or compatibility.** Apply the case-III description over a source function field to obtain a candidate geometric map at the generic point. Then isolate, as separate steps, extension across the source, agreement of the induced fundamental-group data, and compatibility with the marked boundary; none of these steps should be silently assumed from the existence of the generic point alone.

### R20 — Scan 69, paragraph beginning “Il se pourrait”
**Kind: conjecture.** “Il se pourrait … que … la classe de π-conj. de f suffise” asks whether the underlying outer extension map determines its entire bordélique enhancement when `I,I'≠∅`. This is a reconstruction question for the extra indexing and local data, not merely a choice of representative for f.

### R21 — Scan 69, transporter paragraph
**Kind: conjecture / question.** The explicit “question de savoir si” concerns whether `Trans_E(L_i^d,L_i)` is reduced to `E_i` for every positive d. The convention for the transporter and the meaning of powers of the subgroup should be fixed from earlier definitions before comparing this with any normalizer or commensurator statement.

### R22 — Scan 69, paragraph beginning “Dans ce cas”
**Kind: unpursued implication.** Conditional on the transporter assertion, `E_i` “serait connu” from `L_i⊂π`, and even from the subgroup ray or the homomorphism ray defined by κ_i. A proof should establish that these progressively weaker data really recover the same boundary subgroup, and not just one of its finite-index subgroups or its conjugacy class.

### R23 — Scan 69, transporter problem
**Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium, because this separates two logically distinct requirements.** Analyze d=1 first, then ask whether replacing L_i by a proper power introduces additional transporter elements. This separates a self-transporter problem from stability under passage to the specified powers; it is a diagnostic plan, not a proposed proof of the universal assertion.

### R24 — Scan 70, initial normalization
**Kind: unproved claim.** The surjectivity of each local `E_i→Γ` is used to arrange that the local α-data lie in π as well as to retain the global normalization `α=1`. The simultaneous normalization should be checked against the global and local equivalence relation, particularly because several components of that relation are revised on this page.

### R25 — Scan 70, multiplication condition
**Kind: unpursued implication.** The required local map is characterized by “l'hom de multiplication par d_i”. Recovering the earlier definition of d_i and of `T(bar K)` would clarify which multiplicities are permitted and what geometric information they retain; no positivity, nonvanishing, or ramification theorem is added to the transcription on the strength of expected notation.

### R26 — Scan 70, final paragraph
**Kind: unproved claim.** For fixed `(f,τ)`, the α-data are said to be determined modulo right multiplication by elements of the displayed transporter. This links the ambiguity of local enhancements to the preceding transporter problem, but the heavily revised final formula must be reread before making a precise torsor or uniqueness statement.

### R27 — Scan 70, displayed equivalence formulas
**Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium, because the proposed verification is elementary once the missing conventions and uncertain signs are settled.** Substitute the displayed transformations into the local inclusion and multiplication conditions, keeping track of both global μ and local μ_{i'}. In particular, the visible equation `α'_{i'}=μ_{i'}α_{i'}` has not been supplied with an additional μ or μ⁻¹; any suspected discrepancy belongs in the verification and in a future critical apparatus, not in this diplomatic text.
