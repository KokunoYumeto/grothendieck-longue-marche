## Second-pass notes

**S01 — scan 232 (margin) — claim without proof — second-pass note.**
The margin claims that 𝒢 can be taken with two objects (identity and complex conjugation) and is splittable, even canonically split. A splitting exists because A₀ contains orientation-reversing involutions, so A₀ → {±1} has a section. This matters because H³(ℤ/2; ℤ/2) ≅ ℤ/2, so splitting is not automatic for such a group-category. Canonicity needs a preferred involution up to isotopy. One candidate is the antipodal map, which is central in O(3) ⊂ A₀ = Homéo(S²) and so gives a product decomposition. Another is complex conjugation, once the Riemann-sphere structure X₀ = P¹_ℂ is fixed.

**S02 — scan 233 — claim check, (63) — second-pass note.**
Entrywise conjugation σ of ℂ² is τ-linear, and Λ²σ is conjugation on Λ²ℂ² ≅ ℂ (det σ(v, w) is the conjugate of det(v, w)). So σ ∈ S̃l(2, ℂ), σ² = 1, and S̃l(2, ℂ) = Sl(2, ℂ) ⋊ ⟨σ⟩. No antilinear map fixes every line of ℂ²: if f(e₁) = a·e₁ and f(e₂) = a·e₂, then f(i e₁ + e₂) = a(−i e₁ + e₂), which is not a multiple of i e₁ + e₂. So the kernel of S̃l(2, ℂ) → G̃P(1, ℂ) is the kernel ±1 of the linear part, as (63) states.

**S03 — scan 234 (margin) — claim check — second-pass note.**
An ℝ-algebra automorphism of M₂(ℂ) preserves the centre ℂ, acting on it by id or by conjugation. After composing with entrywise conjugation it is ℂ-linear, hence inner (Skolem–Noether). So Aut_{ℝ-alg}(M₂(ℂ)) = PGL(2, ℂ) ⋊ ℤ/2, which acts on P¹(ℂ) by Möbius and anti-Möbius maps; this is G̃P(1, ℂ), as the margin says. The margin's consequence holds in this sense: twisted forms of M₂(ℂ) correspond to G̃P(1, ℂ)-torsors, which by (64) correspond to sphere bundles. Their centre is a form of ℂ, i.e. a quadratic extension of ℝ with no chosen isomorphism to ℂ, which is the sense of “extension quadratique non précisée de ℂ”.

**S04 — scan 238 — claim check, (69) — second-pass note.**
For G = S̃l(2, ℂ), G° = Sl(2, ℂ). Every continuous automorphism of the real Lie algebra 𝔰𝔩(2, ℂ) is ℂ-linear or ℂ-antilinear. The ℂ-linear ones are inner, since transpose-inverse is conjugation by [[0, 1], [−1, 0]] on SL₂. So Out(Sl(2, ℂ)) ≅ ℤ/2, generated by conjugation, which is not inner because inner automorphisms are holomorphic. Hence (69) Γ → Autext(G°) is an isomorphism here, confirming Grothendieck's “impression” for the semilinear model. For Ã₀ the analogous statement would need rigidity theorems for homeomorphism groups, which are not checked here.

**S05 — scans 236, 239 — claim check — second-pass note.**
A°₀ ≃ SO(3) ≅ ℝP³ = S³/±1, with universal cover S³. So (65) and (66) hold: Π₂ = 0 and Πᵢ(A°₀) ≅ Πᵢ(S³) for i ≥ 2, with Π₃ = ℤ. For G° simply connected (67), Π₂(G°) = 0 as for any Lie group or covering of A°₀, and Π₃(G°) = ℤ ≠ 0. So n = 2 is the largest admissible value. Then B_{G°} is 3-connected and H¹(S, G°) = 1 for CW-complexes of dimension ≤ 3, which justifies the margin “on peut prendre n = 2”.

**S06 — scans 237–238 — implication not pursued (hypothesis check) — second-pass note.**
Grothendieck's examples of G are “(Ã₀, ou G̃P(1, ℂ))” on scan 237 and “les deux cas qui nous occupent (G̃P(1, ℂ) et Ã₀)” on scan 238. G̃P(1, ℂ) does not satisfy (67): its identity component PGL(2, ℂ) ≃ SO(3) has Π₁ = ℤ/2, and its centre is trivial, so μ would be 1. The group in the matrix model that satisfies (67) is S̃l(2, ℂ), whose quotient by μ = ±1 is G̃P(1, ℂ) = ℋ, just as Ã₀/μ₂ = A₀. So in these two places Grothendieck seems to name ℋ where G is meant. The text is left as written.
