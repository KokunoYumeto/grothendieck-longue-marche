## Checker's own notes

**C01 — scan 361 — checker's own note (check of a claim without proof).** The NB says $G\subset\mathcal T^{+}(\pi'_0)$ and that "$\pi_0$ se reconstitue à partir de $\pi'_0$ et de ce sous-groupe" ($G=\pi_0/\pi'_0$). This holds whenever $\pi'_0$ has trivial centre, as surface groups of hyperbolic type do: an extension of $G$ by a centreless group is determined by its outer action, $\pi_0\simeq\operatorname{Aut}(\pi'_0)\times_{\operatorname{Out}(\pi'_0)}G$. Confidence high; it explains why the NB can treat $G$ as a subgroup of $\mathcal T^{+}(\pi'_0)$.

**C02 — scan 363 — checker's own note (computation for R09/R11).** For $\pi=\widehat\Pi_{0,3}$, $(\pi_{\mathrm{ab}})_2\simeq(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$ and the kernel gives the Klein-four cover of $\mathbb P^1$ of degree 4, ramified with index 2 over $0,1,\infty$; Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus 0, and it is the conic $x^2+y^2+z^2=0$ mapped by $(x^2:y^2)$, branched over $0,\infty,-1$. The six points over the branch points ($xyz=0$) are the fixed points of the three commuting involutions, i.e. the vertices of an octahedron, and the normaliser of the Klein group in $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ is the octahedral group: a plausible reason for "revêtement octaédral" (confidence medium for this interpretation, high for the computation).

**C03 — scan 364 — checker's own note (check).** "En d'autres termes" is exact: $J_p\cap J_q=\varnothing$ iff the complements satisfy $\overline J_p\cup\overline J_q=J$ (De Morgan). The ink writes "$J_p,J_q\in J$" where an inclusion is meant; the witnesses' "⊂" is a normalisation.

**C04 — scans 365–366 — implication not pursued (checker).** A connected groupoid is equivalent to the category of torsors under the automorphism group of any object $X_0$, via $X\mapsto\operatorname{Isom}(X_0,X)$; changing $X_0$ changes the equivalence by an inner automorphism. So "s'identifie à la cat. des torseurs sous $\Gamma_0$" needs a reference object, which is what the transitive system of isomorphisms among the $(\widehat\pi_0,\overline\Sigma_{\pi_0})$ on 366 supplies (making the reference object canonical up to unique isomorphism). Confidence high for the general fact, medium for this reading of his intent.

**C05 — scans 367–368 — checker's own note (check of R26–R27).** By the transport formula, $i_{u(\pi_0)}=i_{\pi_0}$ for all $u\in\widehat{\widehat{\mathcal T}}(\pi)_i$ means exactly $\operatorname{int}(u)\circ i_{\pi_0}=i_{\pi_0}\circ u_{\pi'}$: equivariance of $i_{\pi_0}$ for action (1) on $\pi'$ and conjugation on the target. Equivariance gives normality of the image and the stated induced action; the converse needs the action condition on the image *and* injectivity of $i_{\pi_0}$, so normality alone is weaker. Confidence high, given the formula as written.

**C06 — scan 369 — claim without proof (checker).** In (7) $H_{\pi_0}\cap\widehat{\widehat{\mathcal T}}(\pi)_i=H_{\pi_0,i}$ only "⊂" follows from the definitions: an element of $H_{\pi_0,i}$ satisfies the compatibility at $i$, and the equality needs it at every other $j\in I$. (8) $H\supset\widehat{\mathcal T}(\pi_0)$ needs, besides transport of structure for the discrete $\mathcal T(\pi_0)$, that $H$ is closed. Confidence medium, since "dans un sens évident" is not made precise.

**C07 — scan 370 — implication not pursued (checker).** The "structure simpliciale d'extensions successives" for $\nu\ne0$ plausibly comes from the hole-filling maps for subsets $J\subset I$ (forgetting punctures, Birman-type exact sequences), with face maps indexed by the subsets; compatibility of the Galois action with all of them is then a concrete constraint on the image of $\Gamma_{\mathbb Q}$ in $\widehat{\widehat{\mathcal T}}_{g,\nu}$. Confidence medium; it links this paragraph to the hole-filling maps (1) of the same section.
