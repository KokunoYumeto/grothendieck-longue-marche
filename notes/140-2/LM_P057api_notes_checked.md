**R17. Scan 569. Own idea (first pass). Kept; checked in this pass.**
Put $\varphi_A(v)=\det(v,Av)$ on the three nonzero $v\in\mathbb F_2^2$. Then $\varphi_{gAg^{-1}}(v)=\varphi_A(g^{-1}v)$, scalars give 0, and on $(e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2)$ one gets $\varphi_E=(0,1,1)$ and $\varphi_F=(1,0,1)$. The image is therefore the even-weight plane, which maps isomorphically onto $\mathbb F_2^3/\mathrm{diag}$ because $(1,1,1)$ has odd weight. This gives an $\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb F_2)\simeq\mathfrak S_3$-equivariant isomorphism; which bijection $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_2)\leftrightarrow\{0,1,\infty\}$ matches the author's labels is not fixed here.

## Second-pass notes (own)

**S1. Scan 561. Claim without proof (second pass, own).**
"dont l'existence implique déjà que Y est globalmt isom. à $\mathbb P^1_S$". A form of $\mathbb P^1$ with a section is $\mathbb P(E)$ for a rank-2 vector bundle E, and it is isomorphic to $\mathbb P^1_S$ when E is a twist of the trivial bundle. That holds here because $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb Z[1/2])=0$; over a base with non-trivial Picard group the section alone would not suffice.

**S2. Scans 562–565. Implication not pursued, with a check (second pass, own).**
The classical value $j(i)=1728=2^6\cdot3^3$ would give $a=6$ together with the $b=3$ found on 564. With it, (59) becomes the Legendre formula $j=2^8(\lambda^2-\lambda+1)^3/(\lambda^2(\lambda-1)^2)$. These pages do not establish a; the author defers it to the study near 2 (565 margin).

**S3. Scan 565. Slip in a formula (second pass, own).**
As written, "$y^{2}=\sqrt{x(x-1)(x-\lambda)}$" combines the Legendre equation $y^2=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)$ with its solved form $y=\sqrt{\cdots}$. The slip is harmless; it is transcribed as written.

**S4. Scans 565–566. Implication not pursued (second pass, own).**
The configurations differ as the warning on 566 says. In $Y=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}/\mathfrak S_3$, with the normalisation $f_1$, the third section $2^2/3^3$ meets the cusp section 0 at 2 and the section ∞ at 3. On $\widetilde M_{1,1}\simeq\mathbb E^1_{\mathbb Z}$ the cusp is at infinity, and the two anharmonic sections 0 and $2^a3^3$ meet each other at both 2 and 3 (562). So near 2 an elliptic point meets the cusp in the first model but the other elliptic point in the second.

**S5. Scan 567, margin. Claim that needs a qualification (second pass, own).**
"le noyau de l'hom $\mathrm{Gl}(k,A)\to\mathrm{Gl}(k,A/J)$ est tjrs un groupe commutatif … si J est un idéal de carré nul", with the sub-note "et m̄ un $A/J$ … $(A/J)^4$". For $J^2=0$ the kernel is $I+M_k(J)$ with $(I+X)(I+Y)=I+X+Y$, an A/J-module isomorphic to $J^{k^2}$. It is $(A/J)^{k^2}$ only when J is free of rank one over A/J (true for $A=\mathbb Z/4$, $J=2\mathbb Z/4$), and the exponent 4 is $k^2$ for $k=2$.

**S6. Scan 568, margin. Conjecture ("devraient correspondre") (second pass, own).**
The scalar line $\mathbb F_2\subset\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb F_2)$ (dimension 3) has exactly 4 complements, and they form a torsor under the dual of $\mathfrak{sl}/\mathbb F_2=S\Gamma'_{4,2}$. This favours the reading "$V=\mathfrak{sl}/\mathbb F_2$", since the dual of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb F_2)$ has 8 elements. Since $\pi_{0,3}$ is free of rank 2, lifts of $\pi_{0,3}\to\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb Z)'$ through the central ±1 form a torsor under $\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{0,3},\pm1)\simeq(\mathbb Z/2)^2$, so there are also 4 of them. The correspondence itself is not proved; the margin refers back to ms pp. 259–260, which lie in §36 of the author's table of contents.

**S7. Scans 569–570. Implied data and a probable slip (second pass, own).**
In the carte coordinate the degree-24 cover has ramification 4 over 0 (the cusps), 2 over 1 and 3 over ∞; the text names only "$2\{1\}+3\{\infty\}$". Riemann–Hurwitz gives $2g-2=-48+18+12+16=-2$, so g = 0, consistent with the conic (71). The 4-fold cover of the dihedral carte, étale outside the vertices, has 6 vertices of valence 4, 12 edges and 8 triangles: the octahedron named on 569. So "carte octogonale" is probably a slip for an octahedral adjective.

**S8. Scan 570. Question (second pass, own).**
Under a (vertex valence, face size) convention the dihedral carte (three vertices on two edges each, two triangles) has type (2,3) and the octahedron (4,3). The written "$(p,q)=(3,3)$" for the "carte triangulaire pondérée universelle" therefore needs the convention for weighted ("pondérée") cartes, which these pages do not give.
