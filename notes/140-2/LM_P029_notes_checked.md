## Second-pass notes (own)

- **SP-1 · 281 (margin) · implication not pursued.** The marginal condition Π1(A^{0Γ}) ⥲ Π1(A^0)^Γ is the H^0 step of a ladder in H^0, H^1 and H^2(Γ, Π1(A)). The CSG witness prints an "[Intercalaire]" directly after this page, stating the conditions "0-fidèle" to "3-fidèle" in these terms. That sheet is not in this package, so its text needs checking against its own scan before use.
- **SP-2 · 283–285 · implication not pursued.** In the anabelian case to which scan 281 restricts (χ(U) < 0), V → V' is an étale Γ-cover and V = U ∖ S^!. Hence χ(V') = (χ(U) − |S^!|)/|Γ| < 0, so V' is hyperbolic and the degenerate types (0,ν'), ν' ≤ 2, of scan 283 cannot occur. Aut(V')^0 is then contractible (Earle–Eells, Earle–Schatz; lead), so the hypothesis "lorsque Aut(V')^0 est 1-connexe" inserted on scan 285 holds automatically there. Confidence: high for the Euler-characteristic step, medium for the cited contractibility.
- **SP-3 · 288 · own idea (proof sketch).** The "fait" on scan 288 follows from Teichmüller's uniqueness theorem. First choose a complex structure J for which the finite group Γ acts holomorphically, as any finite group of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms allows. If a mapping class [h] commutes with the classes of Γ, let F be the unique extremal quasiconformal self-map of (S, J) in the class of h⁻¹. Then γFγ⁻¹ lies in the same class with the same dilatation, so γFγ⁻¹ = F for every γ ∈ Γ, and F⁻¹ is a homeomorphism in [h] commuting with Γ. Confidence medium, for finite-type surfaces with χ < 0. The argument is probably known but was not checked against the literature.
- **SP-4 · 290 c) · claim without proof, with an argument.** If I'^! = {i'} is Γ-stable, then Γ = Γ_{i'}. Each γ ∈ Γ lifts to E' normalising L_{i'} ≅ ℤ, and because the action is direct (Γ = Γ^+) the lift centralises it. So Z_{i'} maps onto Γ, with kernel the centraliser of L_{i'} in Π', which is L_{i'} for a peripheral subgroup of a surface group. Under 2° (E' torsion-free), Z_{i'} is torsion-free and virtually infinite cyclic, hence ≅ ℤ, so Γ = Z_{i'}/L_{i'} is cyclic. Realisability of finite cyclic actions is Nielsen's theorem (1942; lead). Confidence: medium-high, given 2° and Γ = Γ^+.
- **SP-5 · 286–290 · implication not pursued (lead).** Kerckhoff's solution of the Nielsen realisation problem (1983, after this manuscript) realises every finite subgroup of the mapping class group of a finite-type hyperbolic surface by isometries. Through Dehn–Nielsen–Baer, this covers faithful finite outer actions that preserve the loop structure. For such Π, the "cas réalisable" restrictions on scans 286–290 then exclude nothing, and what remains open in the manuscript's sense is uniqueness and full faithfulness. This was not checked against the corpus in this pass.
- **SP-6 · 283 · claim without proof (hypothesis).** "on sait déjà que A^0 ∩ Γ = (1)" needs U of hyperbolic type: on a torus or an annulus, finite rotations lie in the identity component. For χ(U) < 0, a periodic homeomorphism isotopic to the identity is the identity (classical, Hurwitz/Nielsen; lead). This gives the statement in the anabelian case of scan 281.
- **SP-7 · 289 · claim without proof (lead).** "on sait aussi qu'il est fini": if Π is a hyperbolic surface group, the extension 𝓔 has a torsion-free subgroup of finite index with a finite K(π,1). Groups of this kind have finitely many conjugacy classes of finite subgroups (K. S. Brown, *Cohomology of Groups*, ch. IX; lead not checked), so maximal nontrivial partial sections fall into finitely many Π-conjugacy classes. With realisation (SP-5), these classes correspond to the cone points of the quotient orbifold, which fits the name "pts fixes".
