## Legibility

Much of the main prose is recoverable, but the extract is substantially less secure in its corrections and marginalia than in its continuous sentences. The least dependable areas are the overwritten wording on scan 21, the diagram labels and Tate-group subscript on scan 26, the layered insertions on scan 27, the faint bottom note on scan 28, and the overwritten nodes in the cancelled diamond on scan 29. Two potentially misleading readings deserve notice: the relative-group display on scan 28 shows D_S without an obvious star, and the cancelled text on scan 29 assigns faithfulness to σ and surjectivity to ρ.

## Research notes — English

These notes address only the supplied extract. They do not assess later parts of the manuscript or subsequent literature. Own ideas are proposed checks, not established results or claims of novelty. Scan locators refer to Grothendieck (1981).

### R1. Scan 21–22, §5.4 — unproved claim

“On trouve ainsi un foncteur” asserts functoriality into groupoids over Π₁(K). A proof needs the precise morphisms in that target category and the composition rule for the commutation isomorphisms α; the triangle alone does not specify all coherence data.

### R2. Scan 22–23, opening of scan 23 — unproved claim

The image of the arithmetic group homomorphism is described as “ouverte donc d’indice fini.” Establishing this requires checking the field and finite-type hypotheses and the relation between connected components and the image in Γ; it should not be replaced by a claim of surjectivity.

### R3. Scan 23, Conjecture 5.5 — conjecture

The restricted groupoid functor is asserted to be “pleinement fidèle” for the stated smooth projective schemes of dimension at most one. A proof would require both reconstruction of morphisms and uniqueness, with the manuscript’s anabelian condition and its convention for S; the earlier definition in §5.1 is not supplied here.

### R4. Scan 23, left margin — conjecture — marginal qualification

The note appears to read “C’est un peu [faux?]”, followed by “cf §9.” This may qualify the displayed conjecture, but the doubtful word and the absence of §9 prevent a firm conclusion about either the objection or the eventual corrected statement.

### R5. Scan 23–24, Conjecture 5.5 bis and following paragraph — unproved claim

“Il revient au … de dire ceci” presents the pointed-group formulation as equivalent to the groupoid formulation. The subsequent “classe de chemins” is essential to testing that equivalence: a proof must compare the actual pointed morphisms, including their chosen isomorphisms, rather than substitute strictly base-point-preserving maps.

### R6. Scan 24, middle paragraph — unproved claim

The conjectures “se réduisent à la théorie de Galois” in dimension zero. The reduction should be written with the same groupoid-over-base and pointed conventions, including disconnected objects where the first formulation permits them.

### R7. Scan 24, genus discussion — unproved claim

The condition on geometric components is reformulated using K′ = H⁰(X, O̲_X) and geometric connectedness over K′. A proof should establish the required finiteness and connectedness properties under the preceding, unavailable field hypotheses and then compare the genera of the resulting components.

### R8. Scan 24, last paragraph — unproved claim

“On voit aisément” introduces failure outside the genus restriction, using the projective line and an elliptic curve. A check should identify a specific failure of fullness or faithfulness and the appropriate Hom-set; the displayed ordering X′ = Spec(K) makes the direction of the comparison worth verifying rather than taking for granted.

### R9. Scan 25, opening paragraph — conjecture — expectation

“On trouvera des conjectures” anticipates the genus-one and genus-zero cases with the displayed degree bounds on S. The proposed richer structure must be specified together with its admissible morphisms before the anticipated full-faithfulness assertion can be tested; this extract begins that specification but does not complete it.

### R10. Scan 25–26, local construction and (6.1) — unproved claim

The local diagram carries the labels “surj. sur Hom” and “fidèle,” together with an equivalence from the closed-point groupoid to the local-disc groupoid. The assertions need a comparison of finite covers of the local ring, its fraction field, and its residue field; the permitted replacement of henselization by completion also calls for checking compatibility of the chosen constructions.

### R11. Scan 26, Kummer paragraph — unproved claim

“On le sait par Kummer” and “canoniquement isom.” identify the kernel with a Tate-type group. A proof must establish the identification, its canonicity, and its compatibility with the arithmetic action; the uncertain subscript on the first T and the residue-characteristic hypotheses should be resolved from the manuscript before choosing the exact statement.

### R12. Scan 27, bracketed observation and relative-group formula — unproved claim

The general observation invokes “à noyaux abéliens” and an inserted surjectivity condition on Hom-sets. One proof route is to compare conjugation by two lifts of a morphism: their discrepancy lies in the kernel, so abelianness is the mechanism to examine for independence of the lift and descent to a local system.

### R13. Scan 28, relative-group display — own idea

Compare the displayed D_S without a visible star against D_S* in the preceding definitions and against the relative-group formula on scan 27. This is a consistency check, not a proposed correction to the transcription or a claim about the author’s intention. Confidence: high about the visible absence of a star, low about its explanation; the reason is the contrast between the adjacent displays.

### R14. Scan 29–30, cancelled and rewritten lists — own idea

Audit the axioms version by version before using them as a definition. The cancelled text assigns faithfulness to σ and Hom-surjectivity to ρ, whereas the rewritten list gives the latter role to σ and makes ψ faithful. Confidence: high in the need to distinguish the versions, because the cancellation and the differing labels are visible; the sequence of individual overwritings is less secure.

### R15. Scan 30, bracketed conditions c–d — unproved claim

The bracketed “ρ est fidèle …” and “φ est épi mod. gr. finis sur les Aut …” are not accompanied by proofs here. The first calls for a local-to-global injectivity argument under the relevant hypotheses; the second first requires an exact account of “mod. gr. finis,” rather than an automatic identification with ordinary surjectivity.

### R16. Scan 30, paragraph about α — unpursued implication

The instruction that “il vaut mieux oublier” the identity nature of α in the geometric example points toward treating the commutation isomorphism as genuine structure. This suggests defining morphisms and equivalences of such diamonds with their coherence data, instead of using only strictly commuting diagrams; those definitions are not provided in this extract.

### R17. Scan 23–24, dimension-zero objects and the functor — own idea

Test the full-faithfulness assertion on morphisms from Spec(K) to a curve, tracking the induced map over the arithmetic base groupoid. Comparing this subcase with all allowed groupoid maps could isolate what fullness demands in addition to the reconstruction of nonconstant morphisms. Confidence: medium; the reason is that the source includes dimension-zero objects, but the complete target-morphism conventions still need to be fixed.
