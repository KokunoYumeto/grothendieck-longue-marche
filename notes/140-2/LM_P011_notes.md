## 5. Research notes — English; separate from the transcription

These are reading notes on the supplied pages, not conclusions about what is proved elsewhere in the manuscript or literature. “Conjecture” includes an explicit question or expectation rather than only a formally labelled conjecture. ### R01 — Full faithfulness of the initial functor
**Scan/location:** 101, approximately lines 19–25. **Kind:** unproved claim / expectation.

“Je présume que la démonstration” introduces the expectation that proving the functor fully faithful will present no essential difficulty, using the case `Γ=1`. A proof would need to specify morphisms on both sides and show that the nonequivariant correspondence respects the stated group action, marked points, and orientation-related structure. The passage states this expectation rather than supplying that proof.

### R02 — The cohomological structure in the case with no marked points
**Scan/location:** 101, long left margin beside the target-category paragraph. **Kind:** unpursued implication; reading provisional.

The margin explicitly singles out “cas S=∅” and invokes `H²(π,Z)`, `T`, an action, and “compatibilité”. The precise equivariance requirement deserves separate formulation, including how the character occurring in the orientation condition enters. Because the intervening words are not securely recovered, this should not yet be used as a quoted lemma or a settled definition.

### R03 — Finite-group realization and essential uniqueness
**Scan/location:** 101, last paragraph, continuing on 102, approximately lines 1–7. **Kind:** conjecture / question.

The text calls essential surjectivity “un problème ouvert” and asks whether a finite subgroup of the extended Teichmüller group is realized on the indicated pair “de façon essentiellement unique”. The question has an existence part and a uniqueness part, and these pages do not prove either. This note records the manuscript’s formulation and historical wording only, without asserting that the problem remains open.

### R04 — Contractibility of the identity component
**Scan/location:** 102, approximately lines 8–11. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The parenthetical assertion is “A⁰ est contractile” in the anabelian cases under discussion. A precise statement would need the topology on the homeomorphism or diffeomorphism group and the exact requirements on preservation of the marked data. Those specifications and a proof are not given in this local parenthesis.

### R05 — Lifting versus conjugacy of liftings
**Scan/location:** 102, approximately lines 12–16. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the proposed distinction, not for an asserted solution; the two demands are explicitly different in the text.

The formulation asks both for a lifting to `A` and for two liftings to be conjugate by an element of `A⁰`, with “au sens strict” inserted. It would be useful to separate these into a splitting problem for the preimage extension and a problem about simultaneous conjugacy of group homomorphisms. The preceding contractibility assertion should not be used as a substitute for checking both steps.

### R06 — Why the tensor-dual identification is canonical here
**Scan/location:** 103, approximately lines 1–7. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The aside “il faudrait écrire” distinguishes the tensor inverse of `T` from the displayed occurrence of `T`, then invokes a canonical identification and `T^{⊗2}≃Z`. One should isolate exactly which data make that identification canonical, rather than carry it unchanged into the later arithmetic setting. The pages do not supply a comparison between this self-duality and the later `T(K̄)` notation.

### R07 — Reconstruction of the groupoid/topos diagram
**Scan/location:** 103, approximately lines 8–15. **Kind:** conjecture / reconstruction question.

The program is to recover the diagram “à équivalence définie à isom unique près” from the exterior-group and lacet data. A complete formulation should identify the objects, morphisms, and comparison isomorphisms being reconstructed, not just the individual fundamental groups. The ensuing pages develop this construction, while the equivariant completion is later dismissed without full details on scan 109.

### R08 — Coherence from unique conjugators
**Scan/location:** 104, approximately lines 12–20. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high, conditional on fixing the action and composition conventions; uniqueness gives a direct verification route.

The construction of `i_{u',u}` suggests checking the identity `i_{u'',u'}∘i_{u',u}=i_{u'',u}` by multiplying the unique conjugating elements. This would turn the stated “syst. transitif d'iso” into an explicit coherence calculation. It would also show precisely where triviality of the centralizer is used, rather than treating all naturally isomorphic restriction functors as literally equal.

### R09 — Constructing the extension attached to an outer action
**Scan/location:** 105, approximately lines 7–14. **Kind:** unproved claim with a proposed proof route.

The passage says that the outer action “définit une ext.” of the acting group by `π` when the center is trivial. One possible verification is to form the pullback of `Aut(π)→Out(π)` along the given action, and then identify its kernel `Inn(π)` with `π` using centerlessness. This is a proposed construction to check against the manuscript’s conventions; the profinite version would additionally require the relevant continuity and topology checks.

### R10 — Functoriality of the extensions and classifying topoi
**Scan/location:** 105, approximately lines 15–22, continuing on 106, lines 1–2. **Kind:** question / incompletely checked claim.

“Il faudrait s'assurer” introduces the compatibility of the classifying-topos construction with an equivariant exterior homomorphism whose centralizer is trivial. A conjugation argument is sketched, but compatibility with compositions of exterior homomorphisms and with the induced extension maps is not written out separately. A proof should track those maps and their comparison isomorphisms together.

### R11 — Scope test for the finite-index centralizer remark
**Scan/location:** 105, oblique left margin. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the test calculation; the marginal wording remains provisional.

The margin appears to discuss homomorphisms with finite-index image and a trivial-centralizer conclusion in the relevant groups. As a scope test, take the infinite dihedral group `⟨r,s | s²=1, srs=r⁻¹⟩`: its center is trivial, but the centralizer of its index-two cyclic subgroup `⟨r⟩` contains `⟨r⟩`. This directly shows why the geometric/anabelian hypotheses must not be silently replaced by bare centerlessness; it is not a counterexample to an accurately specified geometric claim.

### R12 — The separate roles of injectivity and self-centralization
**Scan/location:** 106, approximately lines 3–17. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high; this follows from the displayed automorphism-group calculation.

The object calculation gives `Aut(κ)=Centr_π(κ)=κ(T)`, and injectivity is what permits the final identification with `T`. Dropping injectivity while keeping only self-centralization would retain the image group, not necessarily the specified `T`. This distinction is useful when checking exactly which hypotheses must be transported through later compositions.

### R13 — A nontrivial restriction imposed on the integer d
**Scan/location:** 107, approximately lines 8–10. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the stated special case; the argument is elementary subgroup containment.

Test the construction with `f_π=id`, `T=Z`, and an injective self-centralizing `κ`. For `|d|>1`, the subgroup `κ(Z)` centralizes `κ(dZ)` and properly contains it, so the required equality between the centralizer and `Im(κ∘d)` fails. Thus the displayed self-centralizer hypothesis is a substantive restriction on `d`, not an automatic property of scalar precomposition.

### R14 — A variance and composition-order audit before further use
**Scan/location:** 108, first displayed arrow, following square, and final indexed-family display. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** medium because some primes and indices are crowded or overwritten.

The prose arrow is transcribed from `Π_Λ` to `Π_{Λ'}`, whereas the following square starts at `Π_{Λ'}`; the final displayed composition also appears to place the primed lacet map before `Θ`. Before using these formulas in a proof, check whether a convention, a writing slip, or a doubtful reading accounts for the differences. The transcription deliberately does not choose a mathematically convenient correction.

### R15 — What is needed for uniqueness of the indexed data
**Scan/location:** 108, final `NB`, continuing on 109, approximately lines 1–2. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The `NB` links injectivity of `I→Homext(T,π)` to determination of `τ`, and faithful action of `Z` on `T` to uniqueness of the integers `(d_{i'})`. A proof must check injectivity after passage to exterior homomorphism classes, not only equality of maps before conjugation.
