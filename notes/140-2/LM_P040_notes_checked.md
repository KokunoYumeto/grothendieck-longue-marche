## Notes added in this pass (second pass, own)

**SP1. Scan 391. Claim citing a known result (literature link).**
The statement for cyclic G is the cyclic case of the Nielsen realization problem: a finite subgroup of the mapping class group is realized by a finite group of homeomorphisms. Nielsen proved the cyclic case (1942); Kerckhoff proved the general finite case (Annals of Mathematics 117, 1983), after this manuscript. The step "donc … de façon analytique complexe" is the standard passage from a finite group of homeomorphisms to an invariant complex structure, and "algébr." points to the algebraic curve this gives. The hypothesis G ⊂ T(π0) (a finite subgroup of the discrete Teichmüller group) matches that problem.

**SP2. Scans 392–393. Check of the argument.**
Uniqueness of the lift: two lifts of u̇ commuting with G differ by int(h) with h ∈ π0 centralizing G. Since π0^G = 1, the reason the author gives on scan 390 for the injectivity of 𝒢(π0)^G → T(π0)^G, the lift is unique and the stabilizer lifts by a homomorphism. The step "u fixe G, auquel cas … il centralise" holds because G → T(π0) is injective: u g u⁻¹ ∈ G has the same image as g in T(π0), hence equals g. The bracket is therefore correct, and the author's verdict "tautologie" fits.

**SP3. Scan 395, (2)–(4). Check of a formula; slip in the manuscript.**
(3) is the second isomorphism theorem. T̂⁺ ⊲ N (N normalizes T̂⁺), N = N^!·T̂⁺ (the margin) and N^! ∩ T̂⁺ = T̂^{!+} (the square is cartesian) give N^!/T̂^{!+} ≅ N/T̂⁺. In (4) the ink writes the first denominator T̂⁺_a. When T̂⁺_a maps onto 𝔖_I (ν ≥ 2; the parenthesis on this page states this for T_{g,ν}), it is not contained in N^!_a, the kernel in (1). The quotient as written is then undefined, and T̂^{!+}_a, as in (3), is presumably meant. The transcription keeps the ink.

**SP4. Scans 395–396. Implication not pursued.**
"(pas ext!)": capping the hole i turns its conjugacy class of loops into a point, so π'_i comes with a base point and is an honest group. Display (5) and the diagram after it use exactly this, since the composite π'_i → T̂̂_i → 𝒢̂̂(π'_i) is the inclusion by inner automorphisms. The deleted line on 396, "si (g, ν−1) est aussi anabélien", repeats the hypothesis of the 395 margin.

**SP5. Scans 396–399. Status of (6) (correction of the first-pass reading).**
The margin "plutôt : il est vrai que !" upgrades the invariance of the image of (5), not (6). (6) is stated outright and then reduced; only its surjectivity is established on these pages. Its injectivity is replaced by the working hypothesis (f) (FP20, FP33). Results derived from (9) are conditional on (f).

**SP6. Scans 398–399. The label "(f)" (conjecture).**
Read with its "i.e.", (f) is the faithfulness of the Θ_{g,ν}: Γ_ℚ → 𝕀Γ_{g,ν}. Faithfulness of all Θ_{g,ν} gives equal (trivial) kernels, hence the bijectivity of (6) used in (9). It is strictly stronger than what (9) needs, which is only equal kernels for consecutive ν. By (8), Θ_{g,ν−1} factors through Θ_{g,ν}, so faithfulness passes from ν−1 to ν. Suppose Θ_{0,4} is the outer Galois action on the profinite π1 of P¹ minus three points, as the setting of M_{0,4} suggests (the definition of Θ is on earlier pages). Then its faithfulness is Belyi's theorem (1979), which would give (f) for every (0,ν) with ν ≥ 4.
