## 3. Legibility assessment

Much of the larger prose is readable, but these are rapid working pages, not a clean fair copy. Primes, subscripts, abbreviated words, fine accents, and punctuation are appreciably less secure than the main sentences. The least trustworthy passages are the opening and cancelled margin of scan 81; the heading and final multiplicative-group expression on scan 82; the interlinear base-change explanation on scan 83; the group qualifier in the cancelled proposition and the heavily revised bottom of scan 87; the first orientation-twist formula and local-groupoid revision on scan 89; and the compressed category and choice-independence wording on scan 90.

The long diagonal cancellations are clear as cancellations, but the precise attachment and order of some nearby small revisions are not. Illegible counts are estimates of words or, for a mathematical erasure, of the corresponding lost item. This is a provisional diplomatic transcription with explicit unresolved readings, not an independently collated edition. In particular, the manuscript’s complete mathematical assertions must not be inferred from the cleaner-looking parts of a damaged or revised sentence.

## 4. Research notes — English; separate from the transcription

These notes identify questions and proof obligations raised by this excerpt. The category “conjecture” also includes explicit expectations and deferred tasks; it does not imply that every item was stated as a formal mathematical conjecture. “Unproved claim” means that a proof is not supplied in these ten scans; it does **not** mean the statement is unknown in the literature or absent later in the manuscript. Brief quotations follow the provisional reading. Cancelled material is identified as cancelled. Proposed implications and my own ideas are not used to alter the transcription, are not claimed to be new, and have not been checked against later pages or external literature.

### R01. Filling punctures and quotienting

**Location:** Scan 81, approximately 3–10. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The description beginning « boucher les trous » identifies the operation with quotienting by the invariant subgroup generated by the corresponding L_{i'}. A proof would need to specify the relevant topology and the meaning of invariant generation, and to show that the remaining local groups map to the stated images. The excerpt begins mid-argument, so the hypotheses inherited from the preceding page are unavailable.

### R02. A groupoid-level formulation is requested

**Location:** Scan 81, approximately 11–13. **Kind:** conjecture.

« il faudrait exprimer ces opérations aussi au niveau des diagrammes de groupoïdes » explicitly leaves a reformulation to be supplied. It would require compatible quotient or filling operations on all three objects and both connecting maps, not only a description at the level of one group.

### R03. Comparison of the two viewpoints

**Location:** Scan 81, approximately 14–19. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The claim « on s'est ramené à la situation envisagée au début » is followed by a judgment about the relative simplicity of the earlier viewpoint. The actual comparison would need the earlier construction and a precise statement of what data are preserved; neither is fully present in this excerpt.

### R04. Abelian local kernels and their images

**Location:** Scan 82, approximately 3–10. **Kind:** unproved claim.

« les groupes noyaux … sont abéliens » and the subsequent statement about their images are recalled rather than proved here. A proof would have to identify each local kernel, construct the stated Kummer identification, and check the image condition for both displayed groupoid maps; the adjective read as ouverts is not entirely secure.

### R05. Admissibility of sections remains to be defined

**Location:** Scan 82, approximately 13–18. **Kind:** conjecture.

The phrase « en un sens qui sera défini par la suite » explicitly defers the meaning of "section admissible". The stated purpose is to exclude the "triviales" sections coming from the local groups; the present pages do not supply a complete criterion.

### R06. Splitting after passing to an open subgroup

**Location:** Scan 82, approximately 13–18. **Kind:** unproved claim.

Condition a) asserts openness of the image and the existence of a section above an open subgroup. Establishing this in the geometric setting would require the hypotheses on the base and geometric object that make such a section available, as well as compatibility with the deferred admissibility condition.

### R07. Algebraic splittings of the local extensions

**Location:** Scan 82, approximately 19–22. **Kind:** unproved claim.

Condition b) calls the local extension « triviale » and distinguishes a privileged class of « scindages, dits "algébriques" ». The torsor description and its Kummer-theoretic comparison require proof; the final displayed expression is too uncertain to identify its target with a specific completion.

### R08. A deliberately discarded refinement

**Location:** Scan 83, approximately 1–2. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

« on va pas considérer … cet élément de structure supplémentaire » sets aside the algebraic-splitting refinement discussed on the preceding page. It remains to determine which subsequent conditions use only the local extensions and which would change if the privileged class of splittings were retained.

### R09. Reduction by finite extension of the base field

**Location:** Scan 83, approximately 9–21. **Kind:** unproved claim.

« Le cas général s'en déduit par extension finie du corps de base » asserts a reduction to the case where the displayed image subgroups equal Γ. A proof would need to track splitting of punctures, the indicated indices, the connected components of X_{K′}, and the outer homomorphisms under base extension; several interlinear words in this explanation remain uncertain.

### R10. The base-change operation should be expressed for groupoids

**Location:** Scan 83, approximately 21–24. **Kind:** conjecture.

« j'ai la flemme d'expliciter comme il faudrait » concerns the formulation using groupoids rather than profinite groups. The omitted construction should identify the new objects and morphisms and explain how choosing a component recovers the group-theoretic description.

### R11. Check the two index counts simultaneously

**Location:** Scan 83, approximately 15–21. **Kind:** own idea.

Own idea — confidence: medium. A useful consistency test is a finite base change with both more than one geometric component and at least one non-rational puncture, checking the two displayed counts (Γ:Σ) and (Γ:Σ_i) in the same example. The reason for this test is that the text tracks both phenomena but the groupoid construction that should coordinate them is deferred; this is a proposed test, not an established result or a novelty claim.

### R12. Invariance under base extension

**Location:** Scan 84, approximately 1–2. **Kind:** unproved claim.

« seront invariantes par extension du corps de base » states the intended stability of the forthcoming conditions. A proof must check each condition separately, including the local conjugacy classes and the outer action, rather than only the underlying abstract group.

### R13. The conjugating element must lie in the geometric kernel

**Location:** Scan 84, approximately 4–13. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The emphatic « pas seulement α∈E ! » distinguishes conjugation by the kernel π from conjugation in the full extension. This distinction is essential to the actual outer-equivariance condition; replacing π by E would weaken the requirement and is not justified by these pages.

### R14. A tentative normalizer characterization

**Location:** Scan 84, approximately 14–19. **Kind:** conjecture.

The word « probablement » qualifies the inference from normalizing L_i to belonging to E_i. Provided the groups are embedded as the notation suggests, this points toward the assertion N_E(L_i)=E_i, but that formulation is an interpretation and the claim is not proved here. A proof would have to rule out additional normalizing elements, including exceptional cases allowed by the preceding hypotheses.

### R15. From local action to cyclotomic equivariance

**Location:** Scan 84, approximately 16–22. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The paragraph gives part of an argument by choosing β with the same image as g and setting α=gβ⁻¹. To complete it one must justify the identification of the local kernel with T and the asserted action through χ; the text continues on the following scan.

### R16. Separate equivariance from normalizer equality

**Location:** Scan 84, approximately 14–19. **Kind:** own idea.

Own idea — confidence: high as a methodological suggestion. In an attempted proof, isolate the choice of β provided by surjectivity from the separate assertion about the full normalizer of L_i. The reason is that the manuscript explicitly marks the normalizer statement as probable while presenting the choice-of-lift argument directly; treating them as one lemma could accidentally turn the tentative claim into an assumption.

### R17. Strong nontriviality of the outer action

**Location:** Scan 85, approximately 5–15. **Kind:** unproved claim.

« Elle implique que l'opération de Γ sur π est très non triviale » is justified here only by reference to the open image of χ. A precise proof would have to specify what degree of nontriviality is meant and show that the cyclotomic effect on the local maps survives passage to outer automorphisms; the text does not formulate that conclusion as a separate theorem.

### R18. Arithmetic existence versus abstract presentation

**Location:** Scan 85, approximately 11–15. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The remark « sans raisons arithmétiques profondes ! » emphasizes that existence of these actions is not evident from the forthcoming structure of π alone. This suggests separating the abstract group-presentation conditions from the additional realizability problem for an action with the required arithmetic behavior.

### R19. A further Weil-type condition is announced

**Location:** Scan 85, approximately 16–21. **Kind:** conjecture.

« une condition de non-trivialité à la Weil » announces an additional requirement rather than establishing it at this point. The later Frobenius condition should be read as addressing this announcement, while the intervening condition d) concerns generators and relations.

### R20. The profinite presentation

**Location:** Scan 86, approximately 1–7. **Kind:** unproved claim.

Condition d) requires the displayed generators and relation to present π as a profinite group, not merely to give a relation satisfied in π. A geometric proof would need to establish topological generation and that the closed relation subgroup has precisely the required normal generation.

### R21. Recovering the genus from an abelian quotient

**Location:** Scan 86, approximately 8–12. **Kind:** unproved claim.

« g est uniquement déterminé » is supported by a rank statement for the abelianization modulo the local images. To justify the argument one must compute that quotient from the profinite presentation and specify the treatment of the local images and their closure.

### R22. Check the case of no punctures before using the rank formula

**Location:** Scan 86, approximately 8–12. **Kind:** own idea.

Own idea — confidence: high for the conditional calculation; the earlier empty-set reading is also now preferred after enlargement. If ν=0 is permitted, the displayed commutator relation becomes trivial on abelianization and suggests rank 2g, whereas the written formula 2g+ν−1 gives 2g−1. The reason for flagging this is the apparent allowance of I=∅ on scan 82; verify the intended scope rather than correcting the formula.

### R23. Admissible choices of η and the cyclotomic image

**Location:** Scan 86, approximately 13–17. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The statement « si η convient » is extended to χ(α)η after taking conjugates. A proof should distinguish invariance under χ(Γ) from a precise torsor or orbit assertion; the compound read as sous-torseur is itself uncertain.

### R24. A cancelled argument involving simultaneous powers

**Location:** Scan 86, approximately 18–23; continues on 87. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The paragraph beginning « Supposons pour simplifier que g=0 » is crossed out and continues into a cancelled proposition on the next scan. Its cancellation is mathematically relevant: the following surviving discussion distinguishes the original generators from separately conjugated powered generators, so the deleted argument must not be reported as a settled assertion.

### R25. A direct diagnostic for the deleted power assertion

**Location:** Scan 87, approximately 1–8, cancelled. **Kind:** own idea.

Own idea — confidence: high for the elementary word calculation. With three puncture generators and ℓ₃=(ℓ₁ℓ₂)⁻¹, the un-conjugated product at ρ=−1 becomes ℓ₁⁻¹ℓ₂⁻¹ℓ₁ℓ₂, so the deleted assertion would force this commutator to vanish. This explains why taking separately chosen conjugates can matter; it does not prove the surviving general assertion.

### R26. Powered conjugacy classes in a privileged generating system

**Location:** Scan 87, approximately 9–18. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The surviving claim says « on peut trouver des conjugués » of the powered local generators that can again be included in a privileged generating system. A proof must produce the conjugations and the remaining generators and establish the full presentation condition, rather than merely preserve each cyclic subgroup.

### R27. The rational-point existence assumption

**Location:** Scan 87, approximately 10–12. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The phrase « admettant qu'il existe » explicitly assumes a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over Q with the stated genus and ν rational points. No construction is supplied here; the exact range of genus and puncture number for which the argument uses this assumption must be checked before treating the resulting statement as general.

### R28. Even the inverse case is singled out

**Location:** Scan 87, approximately 18–19. **Kind:** conjecture.

« Même pour ρ=−1 ce n'est pas entièrement trivial » singles out a concrete case for further work. An explicit construction for inversion would be a useful test of the required ordering and individual conjugations, without resolving the assertion for all profinite units.

### R29. Recovering a lacet structure on an open subgroup

**Location:** Scan 88, approximately 6–12. **Kind:** conjecture.

« Avec un peu de travail, on doit pouvoir retrouver » leaves the local structure on E′→Γ to be constructed. The construction must identify the new local groups and their images in the abelianized ℓ-part, including the meaning and action on the inserted « la somme ». The marginal assertion that this is elegant in the groupoid setting supplies a proposed framework, not the construction itself.

### R30. The quotient called the VA part

**Location:** Scan 88, approximately 13–15. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The quotient by the local-image submodules is called « la partie "VA" ». A fuller treatment should identify the quotient intrinsically, check that the indicated action descends, and explain the geometric meaning of this designation; those details are not supplied here.

### R31. Purity, integrality, and independence of ℓ

**Location:** Scan 88, approximately 16–20. **Kind:** unproved claim.

Condition e) requires the representation to be « pure de poids 1 » and the Frobenius characteristic polynomials to have integer coefficients and be « indépendants » of ℓ. A precise formulation needs to specify the Frobenius elements under consideration, the meaning of purity, and how the objects for different ℓ are compared. The manuscript states this as a condition on the system, rather than proving it on these pages.

### R32. Canonical orientation after twisting

**Location:** Scan 89, approximately 2–12. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The construction is followed by « X sera donc canoniquement T-orienté ». A proof should define the orientation set, its action, and the contracted product precisely, then establish the claimed canonical element without choosing one of the two orientations. The first formula is too uncertain to use as an authoritative complete definition.

### R33. The local groupoid of punctured germs

**Location:** Scan 89, approximately 13–20. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

The parenthetical description invokes « des germes de rev. universels ». A fuller construction needs explicit objects and arrows, the functor to Π_U, and the additional structure supplied by a T-orientation; the cramped interlinear revision makes it especially important not to treat the current transcription as an unambiguous definition.

### R34. Orientation and the local fundamental-group system

**Location:** Scan 90, approximately 1–5. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The local system of fundamental groups is said to be « can. isom. à T ». A proof should construct the identifications at punctured germs and establish their compatibility with all arrows in the local groupoid, not merely choose a generator at each puncture.

### R35. The proposed 2-equivalence

**Location:** Scan 90, approximately 6–12. **Kind:** unproved claim.

« on trouve une 2-équivalence » states a classification claim between the topological and groupoid descriptions. A proof must specify objects, 1-morphisms, and 2-morphisms on both sides and establish essential surjectivity and the corresponding equivalences on morphisms. The list of conditions is incomplete in the supplied scans, and the overwritten Π_X/Π_U notation should not be silently resolved in formulating the claim.
