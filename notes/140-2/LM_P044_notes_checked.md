## Second-pass notes (Claude)

- **431–434 — the author's withdrawal and its reason — implication made explicit (Claude, second pass).** The "Conjecture provisoire profinie" is marked "Canulé" in the margin, and 433–434 give the reason. Take Γ = π₁(S) for a parameter scheme S of positive dimension. A section of the filled family U(i) → S that meets the section P given by the hole i over some, but not all, points of S is neither a section of U nor equal to P. So the complement of the image of Pt_ad(B_{π,Γ♮}) → Pt_ad(B_{π′,Γ♮}) is larger than {P}: this is "la surjectivité déconne", while injectivity is untouched.

- **431, margin — implication not pursued (Claude, second pass).** The reduction to subsets I′ = I ∖ {j} is qualified "du moins si g ≠ 0" (ink ≠; the CSG prints "= 0"). For genus 0, whether the induced conditions on all π(I′) follow from those on the I ∖ {j} is left open.

- **432 — a slip in a definition (Claude, second pass).** "Une action effective de Γ sur π est dite effective, si l'action ext. est effective, et si elle ne normalise aucun ss-groupe à lacets" is circular as written. The next clause, "Si Γ opère effectivement de façon admissible", shows that "admissible" is meant in both places. The ink says "effective" twice, and the transcription keeps it.

- **435 — conjecture: the section conjecture in germ (Claude, second pass).** Points of S, over finite extensions of the ground field, are said to correspond to germs of splittings of Γ = π₁(S) over open subgroups ℾ′ of ℾ, with Γ seen as an extension of ℾ_Q by its geometric part. This is stated without proof, and is the content Grothendieck later stated as the section conjecture (letter to Faltings, 1983). Here it is used to define "admissible" germs, and that is the circularity he points out.

- **436 — implication (Claude, second pass).** With "p.e." restored, "arithmétiquement fidèle" is wider than injectivity of Γ → ℾ_a, and is wanted only "modulo tt au moins passage à un ss-groupe ouvert". The dimension-0 step of the proposed induction therefore covers every Γ that is faithful on some open subgroup. A definition of faithfulness is still missing on these pages.

- **437 margin and 440 theorem — own idea, medium confidence (Claude, second pass).** The marginal aim, recovering how the points at infinity lie on the components of X^σ from the order-2 outer automorphism, fits the theorem begun on 440. There each component of U^σ should give a π-conjugacy class of order-2 elements of E outside π. A real puncture lying on a component Z_i should then appear as a cusp (loop) subgroup normalised by the corresponding involution σ_i. The distribution of the punctures could be read off from which loop subgroups each σ_i normalises; the pages do not state this, and it needs proof.

- **439–440 — checking the count (Claude, second pass).** The two marginal corrections together give the full count of conjugacy classes of antiinvolutions on a closed oriented surface of genus g.
  - Non-orientable quotient: (γ,μ) with γ, μ ≥ 0 and γ + μ = g, giving g + 1 classes.
  - Orientable quotient: γ ≥ 0, μ ≥ 1 and 2γ + μ − 1 = g, giving ⌊g/2⌋ + 1 classes.

  The total is ⌊3g/2⌋ + 2, the classical number of topological types of real structures in genus g (Weichold, 1883). Harnack's bound μ ≤ g + 1 is reached only in the orientable case with γ = 0. The "exactement g+1" of the Corollaire and of 440 is therefore the non-orientable count alone, as the right margin of 439 says. (Check: g = 1 gives 3 = 2 + 1: Klein bottle, Möbius band, annulus.)

- **433 c) — open question (Claude, second pass).** In c) the target is written π′♮ = R(i)|π(i), with a letter unlike the π of b) and a ♮ exponent that no witness keeps. How π′♮ relates to π′ = π(i) of b) ("par passage au quotient") is not explained on these pages. Settle it before using c) in d).
