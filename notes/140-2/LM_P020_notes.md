# LM_P020 — Editorial and research notes

## Scope and locators

This is a conservative, partially unresolved diplomatic transcription of the ten supplied JPEG images, in file order: `LM_P020_scan191.jpg` through `LM_P020_scan200.jpg`. The source attribution, date, and archive identification are those supplied with the task (Grothendieck, 1981); the research notes below concern this extract, not the status of its arguments elsewhere in the manuscript or literature.

**Numbering.** Scan numbers 191–200 are not the numbers printed in the image footers. The lower-left numerals within the manuscript frames run 190–199; the upper numerals visible on alternate images read 96, 97, 98, 99, and 100. Both visible sets are retained with `\mspage`, without claiming that the lower foliation and the upper pagination have the same authorship or editorial status. The institutional footer outside each manuscript frame is not transcribed.

**Approximate lines.** The `% l. NN` comments in the LaTeX fragment identify approximate main-text baselines, counted separately for each scan. Marginal and interlinear material is located by the nearest main-text line, not assigned a fictitious original line number. Hyphenation is retained where a word has been kept across two such lines.

## 3. Legibility and limits

The main argumentative sequence is appreciably more legible than its revision layers. The broad progression—finite actions, peripheral extensions, a “lemme fondamental,” geometric realization, questions about finite subgroups, and a proposed uniqueness argument—can be followed, but this is not a definitive reading of every word or symbol. Enlarged crops and rotated views of sloping marginalia were used for visual inspection; no OCR or published transcription was used to supply text.

The least trustworthy passages are the lower-left marginal note on scan 191; both margins and the cancelled lower block on scan 194; the name and revised hypotheses on scan 195; the lower-left note on scan 196; most of the overlapping revisions and margins on scan 198; and the overwritten hypotheses of the final lemma on scan 200. The long diagonal strokes on scans 199–200 leave their overall cancellation status uncertain. Insertion attachment is also provisional where a stroke crosses several lines. By contrast, the short warnings “pas vrai!” and “faux!” on scan 200 are legible and must not disappear in a cleaned-up account of the mathematical claims.

### B. Own ideas and tests — not assertions of novelty

**R31 — Scans 192–193. Kind: own idea. Confidence: high for the conditional construction, not for its identification with every handwritten object.** Under the trivial-action hypothesis suggested in the passage, test the proposed extension using $E_\chi=\{(q,\gamma)\in(T\otimes\mathbb Q)\times\Gamma^0:q\bmod T=\chi(\gamma)\}$, with $\chi$ valued in $(T\otimes\mathbb Q)/T$. An element $\gamma\in\ker\chi$ then gives the finite-order element $(0,\gamma)$ in this model. This directly isolates the extra ingredient needed to force injectivity of $\chi$: a justified exclusion of such finite-order elements, not merely the existence of a cohomological parametrization. The confidence assessment rests on this explicit construction; whether it is the intended manuscript construction remains a separate reading/proof question.

**R32 — Scans 193–194. Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium, because the reduction is concrete but its rigidity conclusion is not proved here.** Reformulate the direct finite-order automorphism problem using the semidirect product of the surface group with the finite acting group and the centralizer of a peripheral generator. This may separate the group-theoretic fixed-element obstruction from the geometric realization input. The approach still needs the exact directness and peripheral hypotheses, so it is a proof strategy rather than a replacement proof.

**R33 — Scans 197–198. Kind: own idea. Confidence: high for the elementary edge-case test, low for any intended missing hypothesis.** Test the fixed-subgroup question first with $\Gamma=1$: then $\Pi^\Gamma=\Pi$, so a nontriviality hypothesis is needed unless $\Pi$ itself is trivial. The visible question does not securely state that hypothesis, though it may be implicit in the surrounding work. This test belongs in the notes and does not justify inserting a condition into the transcription.

**R34 — Scans 198–199. Kind: own idea. Confidence: high for the logical distinction.** Separate the assertions that finite subgroups are cyclic, that the torsion elements form a subgroup, and that this subgroup is finite. The additive group $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ shows why local cyclicity of finite subgroups alone does not give a largest finite subgroup: it has cyclic finite subgroups of unbounded order. This is only a test of the proposed inference, not a counterexample to the manuscript's specific automorphism-group claim.

**R35 — Scans 198–200. Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium, because the required simultaneous-realization step is unresolved in this extract.** Investigate whether the finiteness question for conjugacy classes of lifts can be approached through a realization together with a precisely defined equivalence relation on marked fixed-point data. Keep the orientation-preserving and orientation-reversing cases separate rather than importing the same point-counting argument into both. The value of this test is that it targets exactly the distinction between a point, an orbit, and a conjugacy class that becomes problematic at the end of the extract.

**R36 — Scan 199. Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium, conditional on the earlier canonical identification.** Build an explicit naturality square for the isomorphism $\Gamma_0\to T_{n_0}$ under an element of the normalizer. Identity action on $T$ would force the corresponding conjugation action on $\Gamma_0$ to be trivial, whereas sign reversal would produce inversion. This isolates the normalizer–centralizer argument from the stronger, subsequently disputed claim about a unique finite overgroup.

**R37 — Scan 200. Kind: own idea. Confidence: medium; the algebraic mechanism is direct, but suitable geometric examples still have to be constructed.** To test the root-uniqueness assertion, seek a basepoint-fixing homeomorphism $h$ of $(X,S)$, isotopic to the identity relative to that point, that commutes with $u_0$ but not with a proposed root $u$. Then $v=huh^{-1}$ satisfies $v^d=u_0$, and the relative isotopy would make the induced based fundamental-group actions of $u$ and $v$ identical, although $u\ne v$. Constructing such an $h$ under the exact surface and periodicity hypotheses would provide a targeted explanation of the author's “faux!” rather than a generic objection to uniqueness.

**R38 — Scans 195–200. Kind: own idea. Confidence: high as an organizational test, not as a mathematical conclusion.** Maintain three separate proof obligations: existence of a geometric realization, extension of a specified realization to a larger group, and uniqueness of that extension as an actual action rather than only up to conjugacy or isotopy. The manuscript moves among these obligations, and its final warnings occur at the uniqueness step. A dependency diagram using those distinctions would help determine which earlier claims survive without the rejected argument.


**R39 — Scan 200, approximately line 22. Kind: own idea. Confidence: high for the elementary order calculation, not for the exact reading of the final insertion.** Check the concluding order assertion independently of the uniqueness argument: if $u$ has finite order $m$ and $u^d=u_0$ has order $n_0$, then $n_0=m/\gcd(m,d)$. This relation alone does not force $m=n_0d$; for example, $m=3$ and $d=2$ give $n_0=3$. The calculation identifies an additional hypothesis to look for, or another reason the rejected formulation may fail, without changing the uncertain final formula in the transcription.
