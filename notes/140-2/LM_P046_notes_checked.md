16. **456, centres and torsors — claim without proof — correct and settle.** The margin now reads "On ne suppose plus néc. que $D_j$ soit un cercle, seulement que $\partial D_j$ pas trop sauvage". For a round disc the induced torsor agrees with the metric one if and only if $a$ is the centre, since harmonic measure seen from $a$ is uniform exactly then. develop: no.

35. **459–460, "voisin d'un groupe de tresses, et sans doute calculable sans grand mal" — conjecture — keep. develop: yes.** Statement to attack: let $\mathcal M_g$ be the group of isotopy classes of homeomorphisms of $\mathbb RP^2$ that preserve a union of $g$ disjoint closed discs $D_i$ and the $\mathbb U$-torsor structures on the circles $\partial D_i$; describe $\mathcal M_g$ as an extension of a braid group of $\mathbb RP^2$ (permuting the discs, with the orientation data of the holes) by the $\mathbb Z^g$ of boundary twists, and decide whether the orientation-preserving lifts of $\mathcal M_g$ to $X_g$, together with the involutions $\tau_{I'}$ ($I'\subset I$), generate the extended mapping class group of $X_g$. Status: presentations of the braid groups $B_n(\mathbb RP^2)$ are known (Van Buskirk, Trans. AMS 1966; Gonçalves–Guaschi, AGT 2004); mapping classes of a non-orientable surface lift into those of its orientation cover (Birman–Chillingworth 1972); the extended mapping class group is generated by three orientation-reversing involutions (Stukow, C. R. Acad. Sci. 2004). I know of no treatment of this framed $\mathbb RP^2$ group or of this generating statement.

Mapping class groups are generated by involutions (McCarthy–Papadopoulos 1987) and the extended one by orientation-reversing involutions (Stukow 2004); presentations exist by Dehn twists (Hatcher–Thurston 1980, Wajnryb 1983), not to my knowledge by anti-involutions. develop: no (the precise part is folded into 35).

39. **454 and 460, generalized dihedral group — own idea — keep, settled.** On $T_j$, $\dot\tau\rho\dot\tau=\dot\tau\dot\tau'=\rho^{-1}$, so $\tau_\varnothing\rho_j\tau_\varnothing=\rho_j^{-1}$; $\mathcal H\simeq\mathbb Z^g$, and $G\simeq\mathbb Z^g\rtimes\mathbb Z/2$ with inversion. $G$ injects into the extended mapping class group of $X_g$: the twists are independent (*Primer*, Lemma 3.17) and the other coset reverses orientation. develop: no.

C. **453–459, anti-involutions of the cylinder rel boundary — implication not pursued. develop: yes.** Computation: $(\rho^m\dot\tau)^2=\mathrm{id}$ for all $m\in\mathbb Z$; $\rho^m\dot\tau$ is free if and only if $m$ is even (at $t=0$ it is $z\mapsto(-1)^{m+1}z$); $\rho^{2k}\dot\tau=\rho^k\dot\tau\rho^{-k}$ and $\rho^{2k+1}\dot\tau=\rho^k\dot\tau'\rho^{-k}$, where $\rho^k$ is the identity on $\partial T$. Within the ansatz (37), the paths satisfying a)–c) fall into components indexed by $\pi_1(\mathrm{Homeo}^+(S^1))=\mathbb Z$ (the space of fixed-point-free involutions of $S^1$ is weakly contractible), represented by the $\rho^{2k}\dot\tau$; replacing c) by $u_0=\mathrm{id}$ gives the $\rho^{2k+1}\dot\tau$. Statement to attack: every anti-involution of $S^1\times[-1,1]$ that restricts to $(z,\pm1)\mapsto(z,\mp1)$ on the boundary is conjugate, by a homeomorphism that is the identity on the boundary, to exactly one of $\dot\tau$ and $\dot\tau'$. Status: the pieces are Kerékjártó's theorem on periodic homeomorphisms (Constantin–Kolev, Enseign. Math. 1994) and $\mathrm{Mod}(A,\partial A)\simeq\mathbb Z$; I know no printed statement of this rel-boundary version, which would make "C'est bien les deux cas" and the free choice of $\dot\tau_j$ in the Proposition precise.

D. **455–456, the conformal variant — own idea.** The antipodal map is anticonformal, so it carries the torsor of $\partial D'_j$ defined by the centre $a'_j$ to the conjugate torsor of $\partial D'_{\tau j}$ defined by $\tau(a'_j)$; the condition "de façon compatible avec l'involution" ($a'_{\tau j}=\tau(a'_j)$) is exactly what makes the structure descend as a torsor under the twisted group $\mathbb U^{\varepsilon_j}$. Together with note 16 this gives the conformal version of the symmetric situation of 459 without the round-disc hypothesis. develop: no.
